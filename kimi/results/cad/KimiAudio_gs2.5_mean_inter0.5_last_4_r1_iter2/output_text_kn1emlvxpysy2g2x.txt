Sure, let’s recap and compare the wines we’ve discussed:

1. **High-end Pinot Noir**:
   - **Storage**: 55°F (13°C)
   - **Serving**: Slightly below room temp, around 55 to 60°F (13 to 16°C)
   - **Best for**: Cold weather, as it highlights delicate aromas when chilled.

2. **Japanese Hon-Mirin**:
   - **Storage**: 60 to 68°F (16 to 20°C)
   - **Serving for cooking**: Same as storage, 60 to 68°F (16 to 20°C)
   - **Sipping**: Room temp, around 68 to 75°F (20 to 24°C)
   - **Note**: Typically not sipped straight but used in cooking.

3. **Pinot Grigio**:
   - **Storage**: Chilled, around 40°F (4°C)
   - **Serving**: Chilled, around 45 to 50°F (7 to 10°C)
   - **Best for**: Hot weather, as it remains refreshing and crisp.

4. **Chianti**:
   - **Storage**: cellar temperature, around 55°F (13°C)
   - **Serving**: Slightly below room temp, around 60 to 65°F (16 to 18°C)
   - **Best for**: Cold weather, as it’s a robust red that benefits from a cool serving temperature.

5. **Mulled Wine (Red Wine)**:
   - **Storage**: cellar temperature, around 55°F (13°C)
   - **Serving**: Warmed to around 140 to 160°F (60 to 70°C)
   - **Best for**: Cold weather, as it’s a warm, comforting drink.

For hot weather, **Pinot Grigio** and **Mulled Wine** are the best choices, as they remain refreshing and can be enjoyed chilled. For cold weather, **Chianti** and **Pinot Noir** are better suited, as they benefit from a cooler serving temperature.
