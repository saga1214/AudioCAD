At minus ten degrees Celsius, the coefficient of friction for figure skates on ice would likely be around 0.02 to 0.04, not 0.005 or 0.008. The friction would increase because the ice is harder and there's less of that thin water layer. So, while it's still very low, it's not as low as when the ice is around minus five degrees. The exact value would depend on blade sharpness and pressure, but it's generally higher at colder temperatures.
