For color-coding, it's a bit of both. Using different colors can help with memory, making it easier to recall which color corresponds to which task or project. But it also helps reduce mental load by visually distinguishing between different types of work, so you don’t have to think as hard about where something goes.

As for switching between playlists, it's mostly personal preference. Some people find variety keeps them engaged, while others prefer a consistent sound. If you notice your focus wavers when switching, sticking to one might be better. But if you enjoy the change, try experimenting with different intervals—like switching every hour or every two hours—to see what works best for you.

For tackling physical documents, both the environment and breaking tasks into chunks contribute to a sense of progress. A clean workspace can make you feel more in control, while chunking tasks helps you see the progress you're making. It's a bit of a combination, but often, the environment sets the tone, and breaking tasks helps you stay on track.
