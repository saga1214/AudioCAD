Starting YouTube content for kids is a good step!

Before Friday’s show at Sunnyside Assisted Living, here’s a checklist to get you ready:

1. **Equipment Check**: Make sure your phone is in good shape, with enough battery and storage. A tripod can help keep the camera steady, and a small light or natural light can make the video clearer.

2. **Rehearse**: Go through the skits or characters you plan to record. Make sure you’re comfortable with the material and can keep it short and engaging.

3. **Permissions**: Confirm with Sunnyside Assisted Living that it’s okay to record the show. Some places might have specific rules about recording.

4. **Set Up**: If possible, scout a good spot in the venue where the recording will be clear and the audience won’t be too distracting. If not, just find a quiet corner.

5. **Test Run**: If you can, do a quick test recording before the show to make sure everything is working as it should.

6. **Engage the Audience**: Remember, the goal is to entertain kids (or in this case, seniors). Make sure to interact with the audience a bit, and keep the energy up.

7. **Post-Show**: After the show, transfer the recordings to your computer or cloud storage. You can then edit them if needed—YouTube allows basic editing, and you can trim out any parts that are too long or not as funny.

8. **Label and Post**: Make sure to label the video kid-friendly on YouTube and then post it to your channel. Share it with that influencer family or other networks you know.

9. **Consistency**: Try to post within a week or so after the show. Consistency is key to building an audience.

So, just make sure everything is set up, rehearse a bit, get permission, and then you’re good to go. You’ll do great.
