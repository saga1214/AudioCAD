If we give public acceptance a higher weight, the sugar reduction campaign might actually be more appealing. It's easier to get people to agree with the idea of eating less sugar, even if they don't always follow through. The vaccination campaign, on the other hand, can face strong resistance from those who distrust vaccines, which can make it harder to implement effectively. So, depending on how much weight we give to public acceptance, either campaign could be the better choice. It's a reminder that public health campaigns aren't just about the science, but also about how we communicate and engage with the public.
