Here are the best ways to attach fabric without damaging the lining, ranging from ideal to temporary solutions:

### 🧵 1. Professional Tailoring (Best for Durability)
*   **How:** Bring the jacket to a local tailor/seamstress. Explain you need the lining preserved.
*   **Method:** They will likely use a **tiny needle and fine thread** (like upholstery or invisible thread) and stitch *only* the outer fabric layers. They'll carefully avoid the lining entirely, often stitching in a **zig-zag pattern** or **within the seam allowance** to keep stitches hidden. This is the most invisible and durable solution.

### ✂️ 2. Hand Sewing (Temporary/Quick Fix)
*   **Materials:** Sharp **needle** (size 24/26), **invisible thread** (nylon or polyester), small **scissors**.
*   **Steps:**
    1.  **Pin Securely:** Use **fabric pins** (not plastic) to hold the fabric pieces together where you need to attach them.
    2.  **Thread Needle:** Thread the needle, knotting the end.
    3.  **Stitch Tiny & Close:** Bring the needle up through the outer fabric layer at one point, then **push it down *right next to the stitching line* on the inner fabric layer** (the side facing the lining). Pull *just* enough to make a tiny stitch. Repeat, keeping stitches extremely close together and very small.
    4.  **Avoid the Lining:** Never let the needle go through the lining itself. If you accidentally poke it, cut the thread and try again at a different spot.
    5.  **Hide Stitches:** On the right side (outer), try to make stitches fall along existing seams or decorative elements to disguise them. On the wrong side, stitches will be very small and tight.
    6.  **Secure & Trim:** Once attached, gently pull the fabric to ensure it's secure. Trim excess thread ends close to the stitch.

### 🔧 3. Using a Sewing Kit (For Immediate, Very Temporary Fix)
*   **Materials:** Sewing needle kit (often includes small needles/threads), sharp scissors, fabric glue stick (like Aleene's Fabric Fusion - *not* spray glue).
*   **Method (Temporary Only):**
    *   **Glue:** Apply a thin line of fabric glue to the fabric edge where you need to attach it. Press firmly to the other fabric piece. Smooth out bubbles. Let dry completely (follow glue instructions).
    *   **Needle/Thread:** If glue isn't sufficient (or you want reinforcement), use the tiny needle and thread from the kit to make a few *very* small hand stitches along the glued seam for extra security. This is still visible but better than nothing.

### ⚠️ Key Considerations for All Methods

*   **Patience is Key:** Rushing will damage the lining. Take your time, especially with hand sewing.
*   **Sharp Tools:** Use sharp needles and small scissors to minimize fabric damage.
*   **Minimal Contact:** Ensure the needle/threads *only* pass through the outer fabric layers you need to attach, never through the lining.
*   **Test First:** If unsure, practice on a scrap piece of the *outer* fabric first.
*   **Temporary vs. Permanent:** Hand sewing with fine thread is still visible but can be quite durable if done carefully. Sewing kits offer only temporary, visible fixes.

### Recommendation

For best results, **professional tailoring is worth it** if you value the jacket's appearance and longevity. If you need a quick, visible fix *now*, **careful hand sewing with invisible thread** is the most practical DIY solution. The sewing kit method is strictly an emergency option. Good luck!
