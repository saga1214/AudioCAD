You've hit on two absolutely critical issues.

While the connection you have is vital, given the aggression, elopement, clutter, dogs, and lack of supervision, your highest priority within those first20 to 30 minutes actually needs tobe session safety and structure.

First, let's talk about safety. The running dogs and clutter create real hazards. Before you even play music, it might be a good idea to calmly try to guide any dogs outside or have the homeowner clear a small, safe space in the room. This shows you're responsible and helps prevent accidents.

Second, structure helps prevent problems. Instead of Discrete Trial Training cold after music, create a very predictable and short opening routine. For example: Music for 5 minutes, then you do a quick 'check-in' action he knows how to do, like handing you a favorite token, followed by a minute of movement like stretching. This brief structure lets him know what to expect.

Only after that safety and predictability is established should you move into unstructured play based on his interests.

And separately, you are absolutely right to report the lack of proper supervision to your BCBA. That is a serious ethical and safety concern that needs to be addressed first before you can effectively plan learning targets
