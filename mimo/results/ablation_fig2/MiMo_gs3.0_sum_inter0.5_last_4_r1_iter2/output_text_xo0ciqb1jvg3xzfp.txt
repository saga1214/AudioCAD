That's an insightful question. The answer is that it was a very complex situation for both groups.

For the Pilgrims, it was a genuine celebration of their survival and a moment of gratitude.

For the Wampanoah, it was a strategic event. They had recently suffered from a devastating smallpox epidemic, and they were forming an alliance with the English to defend themselves and their harvest from other tribes. So, while cooperation was important, it was also a political arrangement.

It was certainly not a "happy occasion" for everyone. While the shared feast was a positive moment, the relationship between the two groups ended in violence and the loss of Native land. So, it was a moment of cooperation, but not necessarily a "happy" event for both sides in the same way.

The best approach is to encourage the children in the play to focus on the themes of cooperation, harvest, and gratitude, without simplifying the story into a purely positive tale.
