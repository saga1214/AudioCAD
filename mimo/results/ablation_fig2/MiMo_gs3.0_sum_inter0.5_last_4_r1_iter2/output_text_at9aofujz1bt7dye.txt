At temperatures around minus ten degrees Celsius, the coefficient of friction between skate blades and ice is indeed very low, but it's not going to be in the 0.005 to 0.008 range. That’s a little too low, even for ice. At that temperature, the friction usually stays in the 0.01 to 0.05 range, maybe even slightly higher depending on the blade sharpness and pressure. The key thing to remember is that at extreme cold, like minus ten degrees, the ice becomes harder, which can actually increase friction a bit. So yeah, the coefficient is still low, but it’s definitely not that tiny.
