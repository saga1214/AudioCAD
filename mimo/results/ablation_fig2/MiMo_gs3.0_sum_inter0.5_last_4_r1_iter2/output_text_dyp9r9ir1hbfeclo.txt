You're asking about the fourth component in the shoulder for rotation. Let's clarify:

1. **The Three Bones Working Together for Rotation**  
   As I mentioned earlier:  
   - **Humerus** (upper arm bone)  
   - **Scapula** (shoulder blade)  
   - **Clavicle** (collarbone)  

2. **The Fourth Component: The Rotator Cuff**  
   The rotator cuff is a group of **four muscles and tendons** that stabilize the shoulder joint and support rotation. The fourth muscle is:  
   - **Subscapularis** (located on the anterior surface of the scapula).  

   These four muscles work together to enable smooth rotation and prevent dislocation:  
   - **Supraspinatus** (initiates arm lifting).  
   - **Infraspinatus** and **Teres Minor** (external rotation).  
   - **Subscapularis** (internal rotation).  

### Key Relationship:
- The **humerus rotates within the scapula's shallow socket** (glenoid cavity).  
- The **rotator cuff muscles** dynamically stabilize this movement and drive rotation.  

If your friend experiences soreness during dumbbell lateral raises, it could involve:  
- Overuse of the **supraspinatus** or **infraspinatus** (primary movers in this exercise).  
- Poor form or rotator cuff strain.  
**Recommendation:** If pain persists or worsens, advise them to consult a clinician to rule out tears or impingement.
