1.  **Color-Coding Icons: Memory vs. Mental Load**
    *   **Memory:** Color *can* aid memory, especially for quick visual recognition of categories (e.g., red for urgent, green for completed). However, its effectiveness depends on consistency and how distinct the colors are.
    *   **Mental Load:** This is where color-coding shines. A well-chosen, subtle color scheme *reduces* mental load by providing instant visual cues. You spend less time mentally categorizing and more time finding what you need. The key is **subtlety and consistency**. Using too many colors or overly bright ones can actually increase visual clutter and distraction, *increasing* mental load. Focus on 2-4 distinct, harmonious colors applied consistently.

2.  **Switching Background Sounds: Optimal Timing?**
    *   **Primarily Personal Preference:** There's no one-size-fits-all "optimal" timing. It depends heavily on:
        *   **Your Work Type:** Deep focus tasks might benefit from a consistent ambient loop. Tasks requiring more alertness or creativity might benefit from a slight shift in energy (e.g., a brief lo-fi segment).
        *   **Your Sensitivity:** Some people are more sensitive to sound changes.
        *   **Your Goals:** Are you trying to avoid boredom, maintain energy, or avoid distraction?
    *   **Potential Approach:** If you *do* experiment with switching, try shorter intervals (e.g., 30-45 minutes) rather than hourly. Notice how your focus shifts. Sometimes, a very brief "reset" sound (like 10 seconds of nature) between work blocks can be refreshing without being disruptive. Ultimately, **consistency often wins for sustained focus.** Stick to one *good* vibe if it works for you.

3.  **Physical Documents: Progress Source (Environment vs. Chunking)**
    *   **Both are Crucial!** Progress comes from a powerful combination:
        *   **Environment (Clean Workspace):** A tidy, organized physical space *reduces friction*. You're less likely to avoid a task because it feels messy. A clean space can also create a psychological "blank slate" feeling, making starting feel easier. It signals "work mode."
        *   **Chunking (Task Breaking):** Breaking the massive task ("Organize *all* documents") into tiny, actionable steps ("File all receipts from January," "Sort the last 3 months of invoices") is essential. This makes the task feel manageable, provides immediate small wins, and prevents overwhelm. You *feel* progress on each small step.
    *   **Synergy:** The clean environment makes it easier to *start* the chunked task, and completing each chunk reinforces the feeling of order, further motivating you to keep going. The environment sets the stage; chunking provides the path.
