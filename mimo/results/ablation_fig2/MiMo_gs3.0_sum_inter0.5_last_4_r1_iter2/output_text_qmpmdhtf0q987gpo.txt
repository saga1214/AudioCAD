Okay, so you're ready to prime and wondering if one coat is enough. Since you win in auctions and save money, I respect that. But if you're asking if one coat is enough, the short answer is... probably not.

Let's break down what you might be missing. First, are you sure your degreasing was thorough enough? Black cabinets hold onto years of grime. If you didn't scrub every nook and cranny, you could be just painting right over that grease, and it will eventually come back.

Second, did you really sand everything, especially in the corners and around any trim? Sanding is key to getting that adhesion, especially for a big color change like yours. If you just lightly sanded the main surfaces, the paint might peel in those less accessible spots.

But the most important thing here is the primer itself. Are you using a good primer, or just some cheap paint labeled as 'cover-up'? For a dark color like black, you need a primer specifically designed to block stain and provide excellent adhesion. It's not the same as a regular primer. And yes, for that kind of change, you absolutely need two coats of it to be sure.

So, before you start, I'd say you need to do a final, thorough cleaning. Check your sanded spots to make sure they look smooth. Then, and this is the critical part, buy a good quality, stain-blocking primer. Don't try to save money here. And then, apply two full, even coats of that primer before you even think about painting over it.
