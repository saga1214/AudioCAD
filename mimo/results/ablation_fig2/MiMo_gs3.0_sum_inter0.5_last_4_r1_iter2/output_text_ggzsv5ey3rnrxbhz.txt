Okay, so based on that whole conversation, here's a summary of the top products to help minimize forever chemical exposure. 

First, definitely non-stick cookware with what are called PTFE or PFOA coatings. It's much better to use stainless steel, cast iron, or glass instead. 

Second, avoid grease-resistant food packaging. Think of wrappers from fast-food places, microwave popcorn bags, and pizza boxes. Reusable containers are a good idea.

Third, steer clear of things treated with PFAS for stain or water resistance. This includes stuff like carpets, upholstery, some outdoor clothing, and waterproof boots.

Fourth is antibacterial products. Some hand soaps, lotions, and even some dental floss have these chemicals, so choosing the regular versions without the 'antibacterial' label is a good move.

And finally, tap water, since forever chemicals get into water systems. Using a certified water filter at home is the most reliable way to reduce exposure. 

Oh, and a couple of important notes from their chat... bottled water can also have microplastics, so a home filter is still the best bet. And paint isn't usually the source of that chemical smell, that's more from VOCs. While fish do contain these chemicals, they're not the only source, so it's about reducing everything.
