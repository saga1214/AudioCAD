You're absolutely right. The ethical considerations and public resistance are paramount for national departments. If we adjust our thinking to prioritize ethics and public acceptance more, it really complicates things.

For the vaccination campaign, those low scores in ethics and public acceptance become a much bigger problem. Misinformation and debates over personal freedom versus mandates can seriously damage the government's reputation and make the campaign much harder to run. This could easily lead to vaccine hesitancy, which is the opposite of the campaign's goal.

For the sugar campaign, the ethics are generally clearer, and public resistance would likely be less intense. However, you could still face debates about state overreach. Still, the overall challenge in both campaigns becomes very similar when you weight public acceptance so heavily. It really depends on the NHD's risk tolerance for political controversy versus their commitment to long-term health behaviors.
