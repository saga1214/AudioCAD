Of course. Let's recap the wines and temperatures we've discussed.

First, for high-end Pinot Noir: you store it at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius. You serve it slightly below room temp, at about 55 to 60 Fahrenheit, or 13 to 16 Celsius.

Second, Japanese Hon-Mirin: you store it cool, around 60 to 68 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you sip it straight instead of cooking with it, room temp works best, at about 20 to 24 Celsius, or 68 to 75 Fahrenheit.

Third, Chianti: you store it at about 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius, and serve it a bit below room temp, around 60 to Pinot Grigio: you store it at about 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, or 10 to 13 Celsius, and serve it chilled, at about 45 to 50 Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius.

Finally, for mulled wine: you store the wine itself at normal cellar temps of around 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius, and you serve it warm, at about 140 to 160 Fahrenheit, or 60 to 70 Celsius.

So, which is best for hot weather? I'd say the Pinot Grigio is the best for hot weather, since it's served chilled. It's light and refreshing.

For cold weather, the Pinot Noir is the best choice. It's served slightly cool, which is perfect for crisp, cold evenings. It would also be great with mulled wine if you're feeling adventurous.

Hon-Mirin isn’t sipped straight often, but if you are, room temp is fine. Mulled wine is served warm, so it's not ideal for hot weather. And Chianti is served a bit below room temp, so it’s a nice middle ground.
