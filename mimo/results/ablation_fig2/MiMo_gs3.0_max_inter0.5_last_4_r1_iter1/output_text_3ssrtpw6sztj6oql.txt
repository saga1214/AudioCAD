For modern-day security in an art museum, **controlling access at every public entry point remains the definitive first line of defense**. This foundational layer prioritizes *preventing threats from entering the building* over internal countermeasures. Here’s why it’s prioritized first and how it evolves with modern security:  

### Key Components of the First Line of Defense:  
1. **Visible Security Presence**: Guards or patrols deter unauthorized entry and respond immediately to incidents.  
2. **Access Screening**: Mandatory procedures like **bag checks**, identity verification, and X-ray scanning to manage exactly who and what enters. Keycards restrict staff access to sensitive areas (e.g., storage), but public entry relies on broader screening.  

### Why This Is "First":  
- **Proactive Deterrence**: Prevents threats (theft, vandalism, or violence) before they reach vulnerable artworks.  
- **Human Element Control**: Exploiting human error (as in the 1990 Gardner heist, where guards were deceived) is a recurring vulnerability. Access control mitigates this.  
- **Layered Strategy**: Modern security treats entry points as the *initial barrier*; internal systems (cameras, alarms, displays) are *secondary* layers triggered if the first line fails.  

### Modern Enhancements:  
- **Technology Integration**: Automated turnstiles, biometric scanners, and AI-powered cameras at entrances.  
- **Behavioral Analysis**: Guards trained to detect suspicious behavior before entry.
