### 1. Taylor Swift  
   - **Why study her?** Her lyrics *are* the production—they paint vivid scenes, creating space for vocal dynamics and instrumentation. Study how she builds tension in pre-choruses and releases it in choruses (e.g., "Love Story" or "Shake It Off").

### 2. Max Martin  
   - **Why study him?** Martin crafts songs like "cinema." His production (with collaborators like Aaron Dessner) uses layered synths, punchy drums, and vocal harmonies to make melodies *feel* inevitable. Listen to "Bad Blood" or "Shake It Off" for how production amplifies catchiness.

### 3. Ryan Tedder  
   - **Why study him?** His orchestral pop (e.g., "Apologize" or "Viva La Vida") shows how dynamics—building from sparse verses to anthemic choruses—create emotional impact. He proves big production doesn’t sacrifice intimacy.

### Bonus: Quincy Jones/Swedien  
   - **Why study them?** They elevated writing into *experiences*.  
     - **Quincy’s storytelling** (e.g., Michael Jackson’s "The Girl, Part III" shows how production mirrors narrative).  
     - **Swedien’s clarity** (e.g., the crispness of "Billie Jean" makes the bassline *feel* like it’s digging into the floor).  
     - **Takeaway:** Use space and silence to let lyrics breathe. A well-placed drum hit or synth swell can make a lyric hit harder.
