That's a great goal. The key is to balance respect with being specific.

Here are a few pointers for your email. First, introduce yourself and state your purpose clearly. You could write: "Dear Professor [Last Name], I scored a 64 on the recent essay exam and would like to learn how I could improve for future tests to do my best in your class."

Next, focus on learning, not the grade. Say something like: "My other exams have gone well, and I'm particularly interested in understanding what content could have been developed better in my essay."

Finally, be specific about your concern, but frame it as a request for feedback. For example: "Subjective exams can be challenging to understand, and I'd really appreciate any specific feedback you could provide on which areas you felt needed improvement."

This approach should get you the information you need while minimizing any chance of him reacting defensively.
