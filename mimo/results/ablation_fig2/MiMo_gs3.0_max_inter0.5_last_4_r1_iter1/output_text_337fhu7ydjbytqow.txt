Here's a **separate closing gear packing list** for each pacing segment, optimized for your conditions:

### **20-Mile Daytime/Night Transition Segment**  
*(Mid 30s, Hilly, Three Aid Stations)*  
1. **Moisture-wicking base layer** (top & bottom): To manage sweat.  
2. **Wind-resistant mid-layer** (e.g., light fleece or vest): For hilly temperature drops.  
3. **Lightweight rain jacket** (if damp forecast): Even in mid-30s, rain can make you cold fast.  
4. **Gloves** (windproof): Keep hands warm and prevent blisters.  
5. **Wide-brim hat & warm beanie**: Protect head from wind; hat stays on during daylight, beanie for cooling off.  
6. **Trail shoes with grip**: Sturdy, broken-in shoes for uneven/rocky ground.  
7. **Headlamp/handlight** (with spare batteries): For potential daylight-to-dark transition.  
8. Buff or gaiter: Quick-warm neck cover if needed.  

### **Final 12-Mile Night Segment**  
*(Mid 30s to Colder, Full Darkness)*  
1. **Moisture-warning base layer**: Still critical for sweat management.  
2. **Insulated mid-layer** (e.g., fleece or puffy): For sustained warmth at night.  
3. **Waterproof/windproof outer jacket**: Must block wind and light rain.  
4. **Warm hat & gloves** (thermal): Essential for extremities; consider merino wool.  
5. Insulated headband or neck gaiter: Trap heat around face/neck.  
6. **Headlamp/handlight** (with extra batteries): **Non-negotiable**—bring a backup.  
7. Trail shoes: Same as above—grip is vital on dark trails.  
8. **Lightweight pack/vest**: Carry layers for easy swaps.  
9. **Spare socks**: Quick-change if feet get cold.  
10. Buff/gaiter: For added warmth if needed.  

### **Key Notes**  
- **Layer System**: Start light and add/remove layers at aid stations.  
- **Aid Stations**: Use them to swap wet gear (e.g., base layer, gloves).  
- **Footwear**: Broken-in shoes only—no new gear for race day.  
- **Light Priority**: Two lights with batteries cover gaps if one fails.
