Okay, diesel camper vans age, and maintenance is crucial before a long trip like this. First, brakes. The most obvious sign of wear is a soft or spongy brake pedal—it could mean the brake fluid is low or there’s air in the system. Also, listen for any grinding or squealing noises when you brake. Visually, you can check the brake pads; if they’re worn down to the metal backing, it’s time for replacement. For spark plugs, diesel engines use high compression, so the plugs are under a lot of stress. You’ll need a special 17/19 millimeter socket and an extension to get to them. If you’re not comfortable with that, a diesel mechanic can do it for you, but it’s a good idea to check. Finally, diesel fuel filters need to be inspected and changed, as clogged filters can cause issues. It’s a good idea to have a trusted mechanic do a full inspection before you go, just to be safe.
