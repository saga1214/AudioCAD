Got it. Here’s a scenario that highlights a potential rulebook difference.

The Red Team and Blue Team are playing in college. The Red Team is defending their goal. As the Blue Team is about to attack, a Red Team defender deliberately headers the ball backward toward their own goalkeeper. The goalkeeper comes off her line and makes a controlled play on the ball by catching it.

Now, the Blue Team forward, who was in an offside position when the header happened, receives a rebound directly from the goalkeeper's catch and scores a goal.

Here's the analysis: According to college rules, since the goalkeeper made a 'deliberate, controlled play' on the ball, the situation for the attacker is reset, and scoring from the rebound is perfectly legal.

However, high school rules can sometimes be stricter. In high school soccer, a goal might be disallowed in a situation like this because they often interpret any touch by a goalkeeper playing from inside her own penalty area as not being a sufficient 'reset' to allow a rebound to be scored.
