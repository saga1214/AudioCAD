That's a great question, and it's really a matter of perspective. Norman Myers himself has argued that the most important aspect is the ecosystem services, because that's the most direct, tangible link to human well-being. It's a practical, economic argument.

But... many scientists and conservationists would say that the most important aspect is the irreplaceable uniqueness of the life forms themselves. They would argue that the intrinsic value of a species, no matter how useful it is to us, is what really matters. And that's a more philosophical, ethical stance.

So, while Myers might say it's the services, the broader scientific community would say it's the unique life itself. Both are valid and important perspectives.
