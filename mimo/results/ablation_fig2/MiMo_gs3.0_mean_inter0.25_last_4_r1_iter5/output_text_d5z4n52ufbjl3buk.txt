That's a smart way to approach it. Here are some pointers to help you structure your email to strike that balance between being respectful and getting specific.

First, for the subject line, keep it neutral. Something like "Course Name and Topic - Exam Follow-up Request" would work well.

In the body of the email, start by stating your purpose clearly and respectfully. You could write something like: "I'm writing to schedule a brief meeting regarding feedback on my most recent essay exam. I scored a 64 and wanted to better understand how my content could be improved for future assignments."

Next, add a sentence that reinforces your good intentions. For example: "My primary goal is to learn from this experience and perform better in your class."

Finally, ask for the specific information you want in a simple way, like: "Could you share any specific points from the grading that I might have addressed differently?"

End the email by offering flexibility and thanking him in advance. You could say, "Office hours might be difficult with your schedule, so I'm happy to meet anytime that works for you. Thank you for your time and guidance."

This approach focuses entirely on your desire to learn, which minimizes the chance of him reacting defensively. Good luck with it!
