Here’s a safe, fabric-friendly method to stick fabric without damaging the lining:

### Best Method: Double-Sided Tape (Fabric-Specific)
1. **Choose the right tape**: Use **double-sided adhesive tape designed for fabrics** (e.g., Sew-Well or Tacky Fabric Tape). Avoid regular duct tape or cheap tape that peels when wet.
2. **Clean the area**: Wipe the fabric and lining with a damp cloth, then dry completely. Any moisture can weaken the glue.
3. **Peel and apply**: Remove the backing from the tape, align it between the torn layers, and press firmly for 30 seconds.
4. **Secure edges**: Trim excess tape if needed. For larger tears, use a small piece of **interfacing** (a lightweight stabilizer) under the tape for strength.

### If Tape Isn’t Enough:
- **Sewing**: Use a **walking foot** on your sewing machine to handle thick layers without shifting. Stitch ¼-inch away from the tear with a **zigzag or stretch stitch** to allow fabric movement.
- **Temporary Fix**: Use **fabric glue** (like Fusion or Aleene’s) as a backup. Apply a thin layer, press for 30 seconds, and let dry fully (avoid water-sensitive linings).

### What to Avoid:
- Iron-on patches (heat can melt synthetics).
- Staples or needles (punctures weaken fabric).
- Regular tape (peels when damp or stretched).

### Pro Tips:
- Test tape/glue on an inner seam first.
- For down-filled jackets, avoid moisture to prevent clumping.
- If the lining is damaged significantly, consider professional repair at a local tailor (cost: ~$15–$30).

This method keeps the lining intact while providing a durable fix. If the jacket is expensive or sentimental, professional repair is worth the investment! 🧵
