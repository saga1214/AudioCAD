That's an excellent question—and yes, **ultra-processed foods are widely considered the single worst category for health when consumed in excess**. They're defined by the NOVA classification system as industrial foods with multiple additives (like preservatives, flavors, and emulsifiers) that aren't found in traditional home cooking.  

### Why they're so harmful in excess:  
1. **Nutrient Deficiency**: They're stripped of fiber, vitamins, and minerals but packed with calories, unhealthy fats, and added sugars.  
2. **Metabolic Harm**: High sugar/fat/salt content drives obesity, type 2 diabetes, and fatty liver disease.  
3. **Inflammation & Disease Risk**: Regular ultra-processed food intake is linked to heart disease, stroke, and even early mortality (studies show a 15% increased all-cause mortality risk).  
4. **Addictive Properties**: Their engineered taste/texture can trigger dopamine responses, encouraging overconsumption.  

### Examples:  
- Sugary cereals, pastries, and cookies  
- Packaged snacks (chips, candy bars)  
- Frozen meals and instant noodles  
- Sugary sodas and energy drinks  
- Sugary breakfast drinks (e.g., flavored lattes)  

### The Nuance:  
While artificial trans fats and excessive sugar/salt are critical, **ultra-processed foods often *contain* these culprits in dangerous combinations**. For example:  
- A bag of chips has trans fats, salt, and added sugar.  
- A "yogurt dessert" might have full-fat dairy but 30g of sugar per serving.  

### Bottom Line:  
The World Health Organization and major health bodies (like the American Heart Association) consistently highlight ultra-processed foods as the primary dietary threat. Moderation with whole, minimally processed foods (fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, whole grains) is key. If your class is exploring this, focus on *replacing* ultra-processed items—not just avoiding them—to build sustainable habits! 🌱
