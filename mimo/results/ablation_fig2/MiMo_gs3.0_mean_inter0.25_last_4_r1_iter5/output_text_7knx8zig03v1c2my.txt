That's a great set of questions! Let's break down the tools and techniques for training your new puppy:

### 🎯 1. **Training Tools:**
   *   **High-Value Treats:** Since your puppy *likes chicken*, cooked, shredded chicken or chicken broth (in tiny amounts) can be excellent high-value rewards for training. Small, soft, and easy-to-chew bits work best.
   *   **Clicker (or Marker Treat):** This is a small, handheld device that makes a distinct, sharp "click" sound. It's a crucial tool for Clicker Training (explained below).
   *   **Leash & Harness:** Essential for practicing commands during walks and for safety.
   *   **Toys:** Helps keep training engaging and builds focus.
   *   **Crate (if you decide to use one):** See the crate training explanation below.

### 🎲 2. **Clicker Training Explained:**
   *   **The Mark:** The click acts as a "spotlight." When you see your puppy do exactly what you want (even for a split second), you click *immediately*. This tells him, "That behavior was perfect!"
   *   **The Reward:** The instant the click sounds, you give him a high-value treat. The click and the treat become linked in his mind: "Click = Treat!"
   *   **How it Works:** The click precisely marks the *exact* moment he did the right thing, making it easier for him to learn what you want. The treat reinforces that he should repeat that behavior.
   *   **Example (Teaching "Sit"):**
        1.  Hold a treat near his nose.
        2.  Slowly move it up and over his head (towards his tail).
        3.  As his bottom naturally lowers, you click *as his bottom touches the ground*.
        4.  Immediately give the treat.
        5.  Repeat many times, gradually making the movement slower until he sits *on command* before the treat is offered. Say "Sit" as you move the treat up.

### 🐶 3. **Training Techniques:**
   *   **Positive Reinforcement:** This is the gold standard! Reward the behaviors you want (treats, praise, toys, play) when he does them. Ignore or redirect unwanted behaviors (like jumping) without punishment. Focus on what you *want* him to do.
   *   **Short & Fun Sessions:** Keep training sessions short (5-10 minutes), frequent, and always end on a success. Puppies have short attention spans!
   *   **Consistency:** Use the same command words ("Sit," "Come," "Stay") and reward system every time.
   *   **Patience & Practice:** Progress can be slow! Celebrate small wins. Practice commands in different locations and with different distractions as he learns.

### 🐾 4. **Crate Training (The "Cozy Den" Approach):**
   *   **Why it's NOT a Cage:** A crate should be a safe, comfortable retreat, like a doggy bedroom, not punishment.
   *   **Making it Cozy:**
        *   **Comfort:** Line the bottom with soft, absorbent bedding (like an old T-shirt).
        *   **Positive Associations:** Feed meals near or inside the crate. Hide treats inside (start easy, make it fun!). Play hide-and-seek with his favorite toy.
        *   **Short, Positive Sessions:** Start with him already inside the crate. Let him explore. Gradually increase time inside with you nearby, then with you leaving the room for very short periods (seconds to a minute). Reward calm behavior inside.
        *   **Never Punish in the Crate:** Never scold him for accidents or bad behavior inside the crate. Clean accidents thoroughly with enzymatic cleaner to remove odors.
   *   **Benefits:** House training, safe space, reduces separation anxiety, travel safety, provides security.
