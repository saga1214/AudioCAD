Okay, let's break this down. You're asking if, because of saccadic masking, it's correct to say the eye sees continuously. 

First, let's talk about the pronunciation of "saccadic masking." It's pronounced roughly as "suh-KAH-dik MAS-king." The "suh" is like "suh-uh" and "KA" is like "cah" with a hard 'c' sound. 

Now, to answer your question: No, the eye doesn't see continuously in the way you might think. 

Here's why: Your eyes are always moving. They make tiny, rapid jumps to focus on different points, which are called saccades. During a saccade, the brain actually suppresses the visual information you're seeing to prevent you from feeling dizzy or being overwhelmed by blur. So, for a very brief fraction of a second, you're technically blind. This is called saccadic masking. 

The brain doesn't see continuously because it's not receiving a constant, uninterrupted stream of visual data. Instead, it receives a series of snapshots from the eyes, but it's so good at processing and blending them that you perceive a continuous view of the world. It’s like a smooth video feed, but it’s actually made up of tiny, rapidly changing frames. 

So, the eye isn't seeing continuously in the way a camera would. The brain is just so good at making sense of the information it gets that it feels like it is. The continuous perception is an illusion created by the brain.
