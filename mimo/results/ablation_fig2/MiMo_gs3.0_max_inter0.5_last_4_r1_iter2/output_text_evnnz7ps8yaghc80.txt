That's a smart plan. A consistent, scheduled routine is key. Here’s a sample weekly schedule you could follow.

Let's say you practice five days a week, for 20 to 30 minutes each day.

On Monday, you could warm up with two beanbags in your strongest hand for five minutes, then move to three beanbags until you drop one ball at least three times in a row. Finally, end with three minutes of scarf practice.

On Tuesday, focus on that first sequence we talked about—tossing just the first three throws—and spend five minutes juggling with music.

Wednesday could be a lighter day for recovery. Just do five minutes of two one-hand juggles and five minutes of slow, deliberate scarf juggling.

On Thursday, you can try to link your progress. Start with two beanbags, add the third from your other hand, and see how many times you can juggle in a row.

Friday would be a mixed session: five minutes on tennis balls for a new feel, five minutes on scarves, and five minutes back on three beanbags.

Saturday is for a longer session, maybe 30 to 45 minutes, where you can mix everything and really push your progress.

And Sunday is for rest. No juggling at all, just light stretching or watching some juggling videos for fun.

Remember to be patient and kind to yourself. Panic and stress definitely hold you back, so focusing on small, consistent wins will make a huge difference before your son sees you shine.
