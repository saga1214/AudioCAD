Here's a breakdown of your questions, along with actionable advice:

**Switching to Adobe Audition:**
*   **Ease Transition:** Start with Audition's excellent online tutorials. Focus on core functions first (recording, basic editing, noise reduction). Export your Scarlett Solo recordings as WAVs initially to avoid conversion hiccups.
*   **AI Tools:** While Audition has some AI features (like Spectral Frequency Display for noise removal), dedicated AI tools for silence trimming/labeling are often specialized plugins. Reaper's AI silence trim is decent, but Adobe's own AI tools are improving. For labeling, Audition's search/replace or manual tagging is often more reliable than AI for now.

**Vocal Endurance & Health:**
*   **Warm-ups/Cool-downs:** *Essential.* Do lip trills, humming, tongue twisters before recording. Do gentle humming or sighing exercises afterward.
*   **Breathing:** Practice diaphragmatic breathing (belly breathing) daily. Record in shorter bursts (1-3 min) with 20-30 second rests between takes.
*   **Hydration:** Sip room-temp water constantly. Avoid large gulps that cause throat strain. Dehydration is a major cause of fatigue.
*   **Posture & Breaks:** Sit/stand ergonomically. Set a timer for every 20-30 minutes to stand, stretch, and do vocal exercises.
*   **Audiobook Habits:** Pros record sessions in blocks, mark pages, use consistent energy levels per section, and take longer breaks between long sessions. Hydration is non-negotiable.

**Demo Clip Memorability:**
*   **Show, Don't Just Tell:** In 3 seconds, hint at the character's core emotion or conflict (e.g., a weary sigh, a sharp intake of breath, a specific cadence).
*   **Strong Hook:** Start with a distinctive line or sound that grabs attention immediately.
*   **Clear Character Choice:** Even if brief, make it instantly recognizable as *your* voice.
*   **High Energy/Clarity:** Ensure it's technically clean and energetic. Avoid long pauses or mumbling.
