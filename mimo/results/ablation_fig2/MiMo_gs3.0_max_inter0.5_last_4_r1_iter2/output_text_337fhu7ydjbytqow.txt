### **For BOTH Segments (Core Essentials)**

4.  **Warm Hat & Gloves:** *Critical for overnight miles.* Wool or fleece gloves. Warm hat (beanie/fleece) or buff/gaiter.
5.  lightweight trail shoes or road shoes with good grip.
6.  Headlamp/Handheld Light: *Essential for both segments.* Bring extra batteries!

### **For the 20-Mile Segment (Overnight)**
*   **Mid-Layer (Essential):** Fleece jacket, lightweight puffy jacket, or insulated vest. *You WILL get cold fast.* Start light and add layers at an aid station if needed.
*   **Pack/Vest:** To carry layers, water, and snacks between aid stations.
*   **Buff/Gaiter:** Versatile for neck warmth, sweat absorption, or covering face.
*   **Extra Dry Change of Clothes (Optional but Recommended):** Plan to change into clean clothes after the race, but a dry base layer for the final 12 miles is smart.

### **For the Final 12 Miles (Pacing Him Slowest)**
*   **Mid-Layer (Prioritize Warmth):** Fleece jacket or puffy jacket becomes *more critical* as pacing often involves walking slower and stopping more. Choose something packable.
*   **Dry Change of Clothes:** *Highly Recommended.* Pacing is often slower here, and you'll likely want to shower after. Bring a clean base layer, socks, and pants/jacket.
*   **Warm Hat & Gloves:** Still important if it's windy or you stop.
*   **Gaiter/Buff:** Still useful for neck warmth if wind picks up.

### **Key Clothing Strategy (The 3-Layer System)**
*   **Base:** Stay dry (Wick).
*   **Mid:** Insulate (Fleece/Puffy). *Adjust this layer heavily based on aid station temps and your own pace/effort.*
*   **Shell:** Stay dry & manage wind (Windbreaker).

*   **Layer Up at Aid Stations:** Use them to add/remove mid-layers. Don't be afraid to overdress slightly at the start of the 20m segment.
*   **Cover Extremities:** Hat, gloves, and gaiter make a huge difference in preventing rapid cooling.
*   **Pack is Your Friend:** Having a pack/vest lets you easily swap layers between aid stations.
