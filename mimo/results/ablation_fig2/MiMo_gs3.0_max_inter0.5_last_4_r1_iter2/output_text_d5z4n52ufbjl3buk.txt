Of course. Here's a way you could structure the email to strike that balance between respect and being clear.

First, for the subject line, keep it neutral and simple. Something like: Exam Feedback Request.

For the body of the email, you could start with a polite greeting, like "Dear Professor [and his last name],"

Then, introduce your purpose. You could write: "I'm writing to schedule some time to meet with you briefly about my most recent essay exam." 

Next, frame your motivation the way we discussed. Try something like: "As I want to do even better on upcoming tests in your class, I was hoping you might be able to share some insight on what could have been improved in my essay?"

This focuses on your future performance. Finally, close politely: "I appreciate your time and guidance. Please let me know what time next week works best for you." Then sign off with "Sincerely," followed by your name.

This approach is respectful and avoids challenging him directly.
