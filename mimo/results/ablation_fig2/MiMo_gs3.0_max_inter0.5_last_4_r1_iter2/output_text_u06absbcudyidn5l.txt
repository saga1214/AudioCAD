Now, about vasculitis, let me try and explain it differently. It's basically when your immune system mistakenly attacks your blood vessels. This inflammation can happen in vessels of any size, which is why it can affect so many different parts of your body.

Common symptoms you might see are rashes, purple spots, blisters, or sores on the skin. Internally, you might feel fever, fatigue, or have joint pain. Hair and nails can definitely be affected because they need good blood flow to get nutrients, so you might notice them becoming weak or brittle.

The cause is an autoimmune issue, where the body's defenses get confused. Diagnosis usually starts with a doctor's exam, then they might do blood tests or, for a definite answer, a skin biopsy to look at the vessels under a microscope.

Treatment is all about calming the immune response, often starting with high-dose steroids. Over time, a rheumatologist might prescribe other medications to keep the disease in check. The main risk, as I said, is that the inflamed vessels can narrow, which can lead to serious problems like organ damage or tissue death if not managed.
