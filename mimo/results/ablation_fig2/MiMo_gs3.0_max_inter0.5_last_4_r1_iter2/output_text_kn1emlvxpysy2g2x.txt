Of course, wine steward coach at your service. Let's go through what we've discussed.

First, the high-end Pinot Noir: you should store it at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. It's served slightly below room temp, between 55 and 60 Fahrenheit or 13 to 16 Celsius.

Next, Japanese Hon-Mirin: you store it in a cool place at 60 to 68 Fahrenheit or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you taste it directly instead of using it for cooking, room temp works, which is 20 to 24 Celsius or 68 to 75 Fahrenheit.

Then we talked about Italian wines. For Chianti, you store it at 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius and serve it a bit below room temp, between 60 and 65 Fahrenheit or 16 to 18 Celsius. For Pinot Grigio, you store it at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit or 10 to 13 Celsius and serve it chilled, between 45 and 50 Fahrenheit or 7 to 10 Celsius.

And finally, for a hot weather wine, we discussed chilled Champagne or Sauvignon Blanc. You store it at 45 to 50 Fahrenheit or 7 to 10 Celsius and serve it cold at 45 to 50 Fahrenheit or 7 to 10 Celsius.

Now, to answer your question about which is best for hot weather: I would say the chilled Champagne or Sauvignon Blanc. It’s refreshing, crisp, and the cold temperature is very refreshing in the heat. For cold weather, the Pinot Noir is a great choice. It has enough body to be comforting but still light enough to be easy to drink on a cool evening. 

Hon-Mirin is a great choice too, especially if you're cooking, as it adds a wonderful depth of flavor to dishes, but it’s not really sipped on its own.
