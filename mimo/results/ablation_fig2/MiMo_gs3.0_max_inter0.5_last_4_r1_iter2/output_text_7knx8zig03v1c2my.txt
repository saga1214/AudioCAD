That's wonderful you're diving into training! Here's a breakdown of treats and clicker training:

**Treat Recommendations:**
1.  **Small & Soft:** Puppy treats should be tiny and easy to swallow. Hard or large treats are a choking hazard.
2.  **High-Value:** Choose something your puppy *loves* more than anything else (like the chicken mentioned!). Cheese cubes, small bits of chicken, hot dog, or specialized puppy treats work well.
3.  **Safe:** Avoid xylitol (a toxic sweetener) or chocolate.
4.  **Timing:** Give the treat *immediately* (within a second) after the desired behavior, or right as you say the cue word.

**Popular Flavors:** Chicken, beef, salmon, cheese, or peanut butter (check ingredients for xylitol). Many brands offer these in tiny, bite-sized forms.

**Clicker Training Explained:**
Clicker training uses a 3-step process:

1.  **Clicker Sound:** The clicker makes a sharp, consistent "click" sound. It becomes a bridge between the behavior and the reward.
2.  **Timing is Key:** You click *the exact moment* your puppy does the behavior you want (e.g., sits).
3.  **Immediate Reward:** Immediately after the click, you give the treat. The puppy learns: "Click + Treat = Good Thing!"
4.  **Repetition:** Practice this frequently. The clicker helps pinpoint the precise moment the behavior happens, leading to faster learning.

**How it Works for "Sit":**
1.  Hold the clicker in your hand.
2.  Hold a treat near your puppy's nose.
3.  Slowly raise the treat straight up over their head.
4.  As they follow it with their nose, their bottom will naturally lower. **The moment their bottom touches the ground, click!**
5.  Immediately drop a treat into their mouth.
6.  Repeat! Gradually lower the hand less and less, relying more on their understanding of "sit."

**Clicker Training Benefits:** It's objective (click happens at the exact right moment), precise, and builds strong communication between you and your puppy. It makes training sessions more fun and efficient.

**Key to Success:** Be patient, consistent, and keep sessions short and positive (5-10 minutes). Focus on one simple behavior at a time. The clicker is just a tool – the reward (treat or praise) is what teaches the behavior.
