Since this was your **only meal of the day**, the sodium in your portion (roughly 450mg) represents **100% of your daily sodium intake** — not 20%. This is indeed high for a single meal and warrants discussion.

### Why This Sodium Level Matters
1. **Daily Sodium Guidelines**:  
   - Recommended daily limit: **< 2,300mg** (ideally closer to 1,500mg).  
   - Your single meal provided **~20% of the recommended daily limit**.  
   - *For context*: Eating 810mg sodium in one meal is excessive for most people, as it can spike blood pressure and strain kidneys.

2. **Sources in Your Recipe**:  
   - **4g oyster + 4g soy sauce**: This is the primary culprit. Oyster sauce is notoriously salty (≈ 1,000mg sodium per tablespoon). Your 3g likely provided ≈ 300mg sodium alone.  
   - **400g tinned tomatoes**: Most are packed in brine (≈ 500–700mg sodium per 400g). Beef brisket may also have added sodium if not homemade.  
   - Water contributes nothing.

3. **Health Implications**:  
   - **Short-term**: Bloating, water retention, or headache.  
   - **Long-term**: Increased risk of hypertension, stroke, and heart disease.  
   - Protein sources (beef/eggs) and veggies (courgette/onion) are nutritious, but sodium undermines these benefits.

### How to Reduce Sodium Next Time
- **Swap sauces**: Use 1 tsp low-sodium oyster sauce + 1 tsp tamari instead of the original amounts.  
- **Rinse canned tomatoes**: Removes ~30% of sodium.  
- **Boost veggies**: Add mushrooms, spinach, or bok choy for fiber without salt.  
- **Hydrate**: Drink extra water to flush sodium post-meal.  
- **Adjust noodles**: Use 100g whole-grain noodles instead of white for sustained energy.

### Final Note
While the meal provided protein and vegetables, the sodium level is unsustainable for a single meal. If you’re tracking intake, consider **dividing sodium targets by meals** (e.g., ≤200mg per meal). Always check labels on sauces and tinned goods for hidden salt.
