Of course. Let's go through the wines we've discussed and their ideal storage and serving temperatures. 

First, the high-end Pinot Noir: you should store it at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit, or about 13 Celsius. For serving, you want it slightly below room temp, so aim for 55 to 60 Fahrenheit, or about 13 to 16 Celsius.

Next is Japanese Hon-Mirin. You should store it in a cool place at about 60 to 68 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you sip it directly instead of using it for cooking, room temp is best, at about 20 to 24 Celsius, or 68 to 75 Fahrenheit.

Then we have Italian wines. For Chianti, you'd store it at about 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius, and serve it just below room temperature, around 60 to 65 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 18 Celsius. For Pinot Grigio, you'd store it at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, or 10 to 13 Celsius, and serve it chilled at about 45 to 50 Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius.

Finally, for a wine served hot, like mulled red wine, you'd store the base wine at the normal cellar temperature of about 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius, but you'd serve it warm at about 140 to 160 Fahrenheit, or 60 to 70 Celsius.

So, for the best wine in hot weather, I'd say the Pinot Grigio is a great choice because it's light and refreshing. For cold weather, the Pinot Noir is a wonderful option; its bright fruit flavors come out a little more in the cooler air.
