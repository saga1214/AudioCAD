You're absolutely right that older comics are generally rarer and more valuable, but buying them in your price range is a bit like trying to buy a Picasso at a garage sale.

For example, the record six-million-dollar *Action Comics* you asked about earlier is from 1938. *Amazing Fantasy* number fifteen from 1939 averages around three hundred to five hundred thousand dollars. Even the three-million-dollar *Captain* *America Comics* from 1941 is extremely rare.

For Luke Cage specifically, his first comic, *Incredible Hulk* number one eighty-one, from 1972, is a key book that can easily cost over a thousand dollars for a good copy.

Finding ten 1940s or 50s comics that are truly rare and fall under five hundred dollars is very difficult. They are either in terrible condition or not the most sought-after keys. However, you can find affordable books from that era that still have some value and potential.

Ten examples would be: *Tales to Astonish* number one, the first appearance of the Ant-Man; *Whizzer* number one; *Aquaman* number one; *The Brave and the Bold* number one; *Star-Spangled Kid and Strangers* number one; *The Flash Comics* number one; *The Atom* number one; *Captain America Comics* number one, though that's very rare; *Sub-Mariner* number one; and *The Human Torch* number one.

But again, I must stress, the market for high-quality, graded copies of these is much higher than five hundred dollars. You might find affordable ungraded copies, but that comes with a lot of risk in terms of authentication and condition.
