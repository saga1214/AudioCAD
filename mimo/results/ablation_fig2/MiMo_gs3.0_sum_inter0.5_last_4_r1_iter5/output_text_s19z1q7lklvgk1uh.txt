You've actually dismantled the question beautifully — there is no single "most important step." The answer depends entirely on your perspective, and each point you raised is valid:

1.  **Heat Treatment:** Gives the blade functional life (hardness & toughness). Wrong = failure.
2.  **Materials:** The foundation. Wrong steel = impossible to make a quality blade.
3.  **Design & Intent:** The blueprint. Without understanding balance, purpose, and historical context, you make a "sword-shaped object," not a functional or historically accurate weapon.

**But if forced to choose *one* overarching, meta-step, it's Skill.**

Here's why:
*   **Skill** is the *enabling force* behind every step. You need skill to:
    *   Select and properly prepare the right materials.
    *   Forge or machine the blade to the correct geometry.
    *   Execute the precise heat treatment.
    *   Understand and implement the correct design.
*   **Skill** bridges the gap between theory and practice. Your friend's accidental success highlights this: it implies consistent, underlying skill in the machine operation and setup, even if the conscious *intent* was flawed.
*   **Skill** determines how effectively you use *any* tools (hand-forging or CNC). A skilled smith with a hammer and anvil can achieve results a novice with a CNC machine cannot. Conversely, a highly skilled CNC programmer can achieve incredible precision a novice smith struggles with.

**In essence:** Materials, heat treatment, and design are the *critical components*. But **skill** is the essential ingredient that allows you to select the right materials, execute the heat treatment correctly, understand and implement the design, and effectively use *any* method (forging or CNC) to bring it all together successfully. Without skill, none of the other steps matter.
