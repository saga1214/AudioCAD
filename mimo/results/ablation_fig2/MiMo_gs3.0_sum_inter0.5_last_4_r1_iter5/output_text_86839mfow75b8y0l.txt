You're absolutely right to zoom in on this—**Quincy Jones** and **Bruce Swedien** are the masterclasses in how **production choices elevate songwriting**. Here’s why they’re worth deep dives:  

### 1. **Quincy Jones**  
   - **Why study him?** Jones isn’t just a producer—he’s a *song architect*. He blends genres (pop, soul, jazz, funk) into timeless hits and understands how structure, dynamics, and sonic texture tell a story.  
   - **Key lessons:**  
     - How silence, space, and arrangement create emotional weight (e.g., the breath before Michael Jackson’s vocals in *"Billie Jean"*).  
     - The power of layering instruments to build tension/release (e.g., *"Off The Wall"*).  
     - His work with Stevie Wonder and Frank Sinatra shows how production can *reinvent* songwriting.  

### 2. **Bruce Swedien**  
   - **Why study him?** Swedien’s engineering and mixing turned raw songs into *sonic masterpieces*. He captured the intimacy and grandeur of Quincy’s visions.  
   - **Key lessons:**  
     - How clarity and texture make lyrics feel *closer to the listener* (e.g., the crispness of Jackson’s vocals in *"Thriller"*).  
     - Dynamic range: When to let a song breathe vs. when to push it to euphoria.  
     - The *emotional alchemy* of production (e.g., how Swedien made *"Dangerous"* feel like a cinematic moment).  

### Bonus: **Gamble & Huff**  
   While Jones/Swedien worked at higher levels, Gamble & Huff pioneered soul/pop production with Motown and Philly Records. Their hits (like *"Stayin’ Alive"* by Bee Gees) show how groove, reverb, and vocal layering create instant catchiness.  

---

### How to Study Them:  
- **Listen to albums:** *"Off The Wall"* (1979), *"Thriller"* (1982), *"Dangerous"* (1991).  
- **Watch documentaries:** *"The Jacksons: The Making of ‘Thriller’"* (Quincy’s commentary).  
- **Analyze production choices:**  
  - *Example:* In *"Billie Jean,"* the iconic bassline isn’t just a riff—it’s engineered to feel like a heartbeat.  
  - *Example:* In *"Don’t Stop ‘Til You Get Enough,"* Swedien’s vocal reverb makes it sound like a stadium anthem.  

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** When you write, imagine *how it will sound*. Will the chorus explode? Will the bridge feel intimate? Production is the invisible hand that turns a good song into a *hit*.
