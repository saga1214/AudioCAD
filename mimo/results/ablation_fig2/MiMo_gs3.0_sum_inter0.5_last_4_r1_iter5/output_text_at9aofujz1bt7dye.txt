Alright, so at temperatures like minus ten degrees Celsius, the coefficient of friction between skate blades and ice can indeed drop to very low values. If rink temperatures get that cold, the coefficient of kinetic friction can be around 0.005 to 0.1. At the coldest rinks, like in some professional settings, it might even be as low as 0.002 to 0.005. This is because the ice is extremely hard, and the blade pressure creates that tiny bit of melting right under the blade, which helps with gliding. But yeah, it’s a super fine line, and pressure from jumps or turns can affect it too.
