That's an excellent question—and yes, **saccadic masking** (pronounced roughly: *suh-KAH-dik MAS-king*) fundamentally changes how we perceive continuity. Here’s a precise breakdown:

### 1. **What the Eye *Can* See (Physically)**
   - The eye’s retina detects light changes rapidly, but **not continuously**. Photoreceptors (rods/cones) respond to *changes* in light, not steady images.  
   - The **flicker fusion threshold** (the point where discrete flashes merge into a continuous light) is typically **50–60 Hz** for humans. Beyond this, you perceive smoothness.  
   - **Saccadic masking**: During rapid eye movements (saccades), the brain **deliberately suppresses visual input** for ~200ms to avoid motion blur. For 200ms, you’re functionally blind to detail during the jump.

### 2. **What the Brain *Perceives* (Cognitively)**
   - The brain doesn’t receive a "continuous feed" of vision. Instead, it **stitches together** visual data from:  
     - **Sensory input** (retinal signals).  
     - **Prior predictions** (memory, expectations).  
     - **Contextual cues** (e.g., seeing a bird in flight).  
   - This is called **perceptual continuity**. Your brain *creates* the illusion of fluid motion by:  
     - Filling gaps in saccadic masking.  
     - Using predictive coding (anticipating movement).  
     - Leveraging visual scene structure (e.g., edges, motion parallax).

### 3. **The Crucial Difference: Eye vs. Brain**
   | **Eye (Sensor)** | **Brain (Processor)** |
   |------------------|----------------------|
   | Detects light changes (up to ~60 Hz). | Creates *perception* of continuity. |
   | Experiences ~200ms "blind spots" during saccades. | Fills gaps during saccades. |
   | No true "real-time" feed. | Predicts motion (e.g., anticipating where a ball will land). |

### 4. **Why 24 fps "Feels Real" (Despite Saccadic Masking)**
   - Filmmakers exploit this! Your brain **automatically interpolates** between frames during saccades, mimicking the eye’s natural motion blur.  
   - At 24 fps, saccades occur *between* frames, so the brain perceives fluid motion. Higher frame rates (e.g., 60 fps) reduce saccadic gaps, making motion smoother but less "cinematic."  
   - **Hyper-realism**: Modern digital video at 120+ fps captures *more* detail, but it lacks the brain’s "artistic smoothing." That’s why 24 fps feels poetic—your brain *chooses* to fill gaps creatively.

### Key Takeaway
The eye **does not see continuously**—it sees in bursts with blind spots. The brain **perceives continuity** by predicting, predicting, predicting. This is why 24 fps cinema works: it aligns with the brain’s natural "stitching" process. Saccadic masking isn’t a bug—it’s the foundation of how we experience motion as a seamless story.  

For filmmaking: Use **motion blur** (common in 35mm) to mimic the brain’s smoothing. Avoid hyper-smooth high-frame-rate footage unless you want hyper-realism. Art thrives in the gap between eye and brain.
