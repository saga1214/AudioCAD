Using duct tape on fabric requires careful technique to avoid damaging the lining. Here's a step-by-step guide:

### **Best Practices for Duct Tape on Fabric:**
1. **Clean & Dry Surface:**  
   Wipe the area with a damp cloth (no moisture left) to prevent adhesive residue.

2. **Use Non-Woven Tape (Best Option):**  
   - **Avoid** standard duct tape (adhesive residue ruins fabric).  
   - **Use:** **Non-woven duct tape** (e.g., 3M Scotchcast) or **heavy-duty fabric tape** (e.g., Gorilla Grip). These are less toxic and easier to remove.

3. **Cut Small Pieces:**  
   Cut 1–2 inch strips instead of large rolls to reduce bulk.

4. **Apply Strategically:**  
   - **Seam Repair:** Place tape *over* the seam (not under).  
   - **Zipper/Strap:** Align tape parallel to the tear (45° angle reduces bulk).  
   - **Cover Entire Seam:** Overlap pieces by 0.5 inches for security.

5. **Press Firmly:**  
   Rub firmly with fingers to ensure adhesion. Avoid stretching the fabric.

6. **Test First:**  
   Apply a tiny strip to an inconspicuous area (like the hem) to check for damage.

### **Critical Warnings:**  
- ❌ **NEVER use standard duct tape inside the lining** – it melts synthetic fabrics and leaves toxic residue.  
- ❌ Avoid high-heat areas (e.g., under arms) – tape can soften.  
- ⚠️ This is a **temporary fix**. For lasting repairs, visit Arden Fair stores like **Jo-Ann Fabrics** (sewing kits) or **SportChek Sacramento** (technical repairs).

### **Better Alternatives:**  
- **Fabric Glue:** Use **Aleene’s Fabric Fusion** for seams (dry in 1 hour).  
- **Sewing Machine:** A 5-minute stitch is more durable (REI Co-op sells affordable kits online).  
- **Emergency Fix:** **Tape Runner** (adhesive pen) for small tears (e.g., at Arden Fair).
