For modern-day art museum security, the **first and most critical line of defense remains *access control at every entry point***—combining visible security presence and screening procedures (e.g., bag checks, visitor authentication) to manage exactly **who and what enters the building**.  

### Key Rationale:  
1. **Proactive Deterrence**: Guards or surveillance presence at entrances physically deters threats before they enter.  
2. **Threat Mitigation**: Screening (e.g., X-ray bags, ID checks) filters weapons, explosives, or unauthorized items at the outset.  
3. **Strategic Sequence**: This layer stops breaches *before* internal systems (cameras, alarms, secure displays) are triggered.  

### Contrast with Other Layers:  
- **High-tech cameras** (second layer) monitor *after* entry.  
- **Motion sensors/glass-break alarms** (third layer) alert *if* entry is breached.  
- **Secure displays/mounting systems** (fourth layer) physically protect art *inside* the building.  

### Why Human Error Demands Reinforcement:  
The 1990 Gardner heist highlights how human vulnerability (e.g., guards overriding protocols) can bypass defenses. Modern first-line defenses now integrate **automated screening** (e.g., AI-powered scanners) and **biometric access** to reduce reliance on fallible human judgment.  

For your paper, emphasize that the first line is **preventative**—not reactive. It’s the *gatekeeper* layer where threats are intercepted before escalating to internal systems.
