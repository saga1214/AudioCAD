Okay, let's put it all together. Here’s a clear, traditional format that covers everything you've asked about and is very recruiter-friendly.

First, at the very top, centered and using a 12 to 14-point font, you have your name. To the left, indented and using a nine-point font with icons, you can have your email, phone number, city, and expected graduation date for an in-progress degree. Icons are safe now, as ATS systems have improved. I'd also add a link to your LinkedIn profile here, also with a small indent and icon.

Right below your name, have a short two-to-three sentence section called "Professional Summary." This is where you highlight your most relevant skills and career goals.

Next, create a "Relevant Experience" section. Here, you'll list your most important roles, including the company, your job title, and the dates you worked there. For each role, use two to four bullet points to describe your key responsibilities and achievements.

After that, create an "Additional Work History" section. This is where you list your other roles, again with the company, title, and dates, but you can omit the detailed bullet points to keep it brief.

Then comes your "Education" section. List your degrees, the school name, and your expected graduation year. For each degree, add a line underneath to note your honors, like "Graduated with Highest Honors," or any specific scholarships you received.

If you have space, you can add a "Skills" section. You can group them into categories like Technical Skills, Software Proficiency, and Languages.

Finally, at the very bottom, you can have an optional "Certifications" section for any professional certifications you hold.

This structure keeps your resume clean, highlights your most relevant experience first, and presents your entire history in a clear, chronological way.
