 Let's go through the wines we've discussed.

 First, high-end Pinot Noir. For storage, cellar temperature, around 13 degrees Celsius or 55 degrees Fahrenheit. For serving, slightly below room temp.
 Next, Japanese Hon-Mirin. For storage, a cool place at about 16 to 20 degrees Celsius or 60 to 68 Fahrenheit. For serving if tasting alone, room temp at around 20 to 24 Celsius or 68 to 75 Fahrenheit. But mostly cooking with it.
 Then, Chianti. For storage, same as Pinot Noir at about 13 degrees Celsius or 55 Fahrenheit. For serving, just a bit below room temp.
 And finally, Pinot Grigio. For storage, a bit cooler at 10 to 13 Celsius or 50 to 55 Fahrenheit. For serving, chilled at about 4 to 7 Celsius or 40 to 45 Fahrenheit.

 Now, for the best wine for hot weather, I'd say Pinot Grigio is ideal. It’s light, crisp, and refreshing, especially chilled. Hon-Mirin could also work if you sip it, though it’s mainly for cooking. 

For cold weather, Pinot Noir shines. It's a red that’s elegant enough for cooler temps, especially served slightly below room temperature. Hon-Mirin is also great in the cold, as it’s not really sipped straight, so room temp is easy to maintain. 

So to quickly summarize the best choices: for hot weather, Pinot Grigio is best. For cold weather, Pinot Noir is the standout. Hope that helps with your planning!
