For your modern-day security paper, the art museum's **absolute first line of defense** is **controlling access at every entry point** (e.g., doors, windows, visitor entrances). This layer focuses on **preventing unauthorized entry** before threats reach the art itself. Here’s why:  

### Key Components:  
1. **Visible Security Presence**:  
   Guards or patrols at entry points deter potential threats through visibility and authority.  

2. **Screening Procedures**:  
   - Bag checks (e.g., X-ray machines, size restrictions).  
   Membership/ticket verification.  
   Controlled staff keycard access to restricted areas.  

### Why This Is the "First Line":  
- It’s the **primary interface** between the public/threats and the museum.  
- It **manages exactly who and what enters**, reducing the risk of infiltration before internal systems (cameras, alarms, secure displays) can respond.  
- The 1990 **Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum Heist** exemplifies this failure: thieves exploited human error (posing as police officers) to bypass this layer, then overpowered guards inside.  

### Modern Context:  
First-line defenses now integrate technology:  
- Biometric scanners.  
- Automated turnstiles.  
- AI-powered surveillance at gates.  

But the core principle remains: **Stop the threat at the perimeter**. Only after this layer fails do internal systems (alarms, sensors, secure displays) activate.  

For your paper, emphasize that while human error is a vulnerability (as seen in heists), the *design* of first-line defenses prioritizes **proactive deterrence and access control** as the foundational security principle.
