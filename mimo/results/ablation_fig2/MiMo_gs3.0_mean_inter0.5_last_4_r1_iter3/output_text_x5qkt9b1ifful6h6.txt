Based on your biology class exploration and current public health research, **ultra-processed foods** would likely be considered the one type of food **worst for your health if eaten in excess**. Here's why:

### Key Reasons:
1. **Nutrient Deficiency**:  
   Ultra-processed foods are stripped of fiber, vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats. They’re packed with **empty calories** (mostly from refined sugars, oils, and starches) while lacking essential nutrients.

2. **Harmful Additives**:  
   They contain **artificial sweeteners, preservatives, emulsifiers, and colorings** linked to inflammation, gut microbiome disruption, and metabolic dysfunction.

3. **Disease Links**:  
   Excessive consumption is strongly tied to:
   - **Obesity**: High in calories, low in satiety.
   - **Type 2 Diabetes**: Rapid sugar spikes and insulin resistance.
   - **Heart Disease**: High in unhealthy fats, sodium, and trans fats.
   - **Fatty Liver Disease**: Sugar and fructose overload the liver.

4. **Addiction-Like Effects**:  
   Their engineered taste/texture can trigger overconsumption, similar to addictive substances.

### Examples:
- Sugary cereals, chips, cookies, soda, frozen meals, instant noodles, and packaged snacks.

### Why Not Other Contenders?
- **Sugary Foods/Drinks**: While extremely harmful, they’re often *components* of ultra-processed foods.  
- **Trans Fats**: Though devastating for heart health, regulations have reduced their prevalence in many countries.  
- **Processed Cheeses**: A subset of ultra-processed foods, but not as pervasive as the broader category.

### The Bottom Line:
While no single food is "the worst," **ultra-processed foods represent a systemic risk** due to their prevalence, nutritional void, and role in driving chronic diseases. Moderation with whole, minimally processed foods (fruits, veggies, lean proteins, whole grains) is key for long-term health. Your class is studying a critical topic—these insights align with major health organizations like the WHO and NIH.
