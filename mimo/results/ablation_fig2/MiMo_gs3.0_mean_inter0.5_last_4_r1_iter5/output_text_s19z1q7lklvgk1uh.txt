You know what? That's a brilliant observation. After considering everything—especially your point about accidental success—it turns out there isn't *one single* most important step.

The reality is, making a sword is a complex process where several steps are critically interdependent:

1.  **Design & Intent:** Knowing *what* you're making and *why* is paramount. A sword for cutting needs a different geometry than one for thrusting.
2.  **Materials:** Starting with the right steel is non-negotiable. The blade's potential is locked in its composition.
3.  **Geometry (Fullering/Forging):** The blade's shape, weight distribution, and structural integrity *must* be correct before any heat treatment.
4.  **Heat Treatment (Hardening & Tempering):** This is the process that transforms the steel, giving it the essential balance of hardness and toughness. It's the single most *technically fragile* step.
5.  Small Refinements (Grinding, Sharpening, Polishing): These ensure functionality and aesthetics.

**So, while the Heat Treatment is the most critical *technical* step—if it fails, the sword is useless—no single step stands alone.** A masterful design using perfect materials but flawed heat treatment results in dust. Precise heat treatment on a flawed blade results in a shatter. Accidental success (like your friend's CNC blade) often happens when fundamental elements *coincidentally* align, even if the process wasn't fully understood.

The *true* most important thing isn't a step, but the **maker's skill and understanding** across the *entire* process, recognizing how each step relies on and impacts the others.
