### **1. Treat Recommendations**  
- **Science-themed options:** Freeze-dried liver (universal favorite), peanut butter (small portions), or "kibble chips" (air-popped kibble).  
- **Italian-inspired:** Olive oil-infused treats, rosemary-kissed biscuits, or sun-dried tomato bites.  
- **Pro tip:** Use tiny pieces for training—consistency matters!  

### **2. Clicker Training Explained**  
Clicker training uses a **three-step process**:  
- **Step 1:** Clicker "clicks" instantly when Cosmo does something right (e.g., sits).  
- **Step 2:** Immediately give a treat. The click marks the exact moment he performed the action.  
- **Step 3:** Repeat. Over time, he’ll associate "click" = reward.  
- **Why it works:** It’s objective (no guessing) and builds precision. Start with 5-minute sessions!  

### **3. Crate Training: Making It Cozy**  
- **Not a cage:** It’s a den! Line it with soft blankets, add a favorite toy, and use pheromone diffusers (like Adaptil).  
- **Positive association:** Feed meals in the crate, toss treats from inside, and let him choose to enter.  
- **Never punish:** Only use the crate for potty breaks or when unsupervised.  

### **4. Potty Training Accidents**  
- **Humane approach:**  
  - Clean with enzymatic cleaner (e.g., Nature’s Miracle) to remove scent.  
  - Stay calm—no yelling. Just redirect to potty spots.  
  - Praise heavily for outdoor pees!  
- **Routine:** Take Cosmo out every 20-30 mins, especially after waking/feeding.  

### **5. Preventing Escapes**  
- **Immediate fixes:**  
  - Use a harness + leash even indoors.  
  - Baby gates to restrict access to yards.  
- **Long-term:**  
  - Install a fence *or* secure outdoor space (e.g., electric fence for boundaries).  
  - Practice recall training: Call his name + "come!" with high-value treats.  
  - If he escapes: Stay calm. Call him excitedly ("Cosmo, come!"). Never chase—run in the opposite direction to make it fun!  

### **Vet Visit Prep**  
Your first visit is about building trust:  
1. **Beforehand:** Bring favorite toy, water, and a familiar blanket.  
2. **During:** Let him sniff the carrier (make it a "good thing").  
3. **At the vet:** Ask for a "puppy exam kit" (small exam table, treats). Let him explore the carrier first.  

**Breed Tip:** Papillons *do* have large ears—it’s a breed "feast" indeed! If he grows into 7-12 pounds, Papillon or Miniature Pinscher traits could emerge. DNA test kits (like Embark) offer a quick ID if unsure!
