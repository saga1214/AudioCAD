Okay, let's get you back on track with your toolbox. For a severe deep blockage, here's a step-by-step approach using basic tools:

1.  **Hand Auger (Drain Snake):** This is your best bet with a simple toolbox.
    *   **Insert Carefully:** Feed the auger head into the drain, holding it steady and straight. Don't force it.
    *   **Rotate:** Once it's in, start rotating the handle clockwise. Feel for resistance – this is the clog.
    *   **Break it Up:** Keep rotating and pushing. Use your free hand to gently press down on the drain opening to help guide the auger and break up the clog. Don't crank wildly.
    *   **Retrieve:** When you feel resistance stop or the auger starts to bind, stop rotating. Pull the handle back slowly and carefully. You should bring up debris (see step 4).

2.  **Manual Plunger (If Applicable):** If the blockage is further down but you can create a seal (like a sink or tub), try a few more strong plunges. Sometimes it dislodges debris the snake missed.

3.  **Check for Obvious Obstructions:** Look down the drain opening with your flashlight. Can you see a large object like a rag, sock, or a piece of food? If so, try carefully hooking it out with pliers.

4.  **Remove Debris from Auger:** After using the auger, pull it out and use pliers to remove any tangled debris (hair, rags, etc.) caught around the blades. Clean the blades with a rag.

5.  **Flush:** Once you've cleared debris from the auger and broken up the clog, flush the drain thoroughly with hot water. This helps flush out loosened particles.

**Important Safety & Limitations:**

*   **Don't Damage Pipes:** Avoid kinking pipes with the auger. Use a pipe wrench if needed to gently maneuver the drain opening if it's too tight, but be *extremely* careful not to slip and crush the pipe. Simple toolbox might not have a proper adjustable wrench, so pliers are your next best bet for grip, but be cautious.
*   **Avoid Chemical Cleaners:** With only a simple toolbox today, buying chemicals isn't an option, and they often don't work well on deep organic clogs anyway. Stick to mechanical methods.
*   **Know When to Stop:** If the auger gets stuck *deep* in the pipe, don't force it. You risk breaking the auger or damaging the pipe. You might need to call a specialist with a longer machine if this happens.
*   **Calm is Key:** Remember to pause, take that deep breath, and assess before each step. Rushing increases the risk of mistakes.
