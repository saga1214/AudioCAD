First off, it's really important to start when your baby is around six months old and has already shown that they're ready—you know, when they can sit up on their own, have good head and neck control, and are showing interest in food. 

As for what to feed them, you'll want to choose foods that are soft, easy to press, and can be held in their little hand. Think of things like ripe bananas, steamed carrots cut into strips, soft avocado, small pieces of steamed broccoli, or even well-cooked pasta. A good rule of thumb is that the food should be about the size of your baby's thumb. 

And this part is super important: safety first! Always supervise them closely while they're eating. You have to cut any food into tiny, pea-sized pieces, especially things like grapes, cherry tomatoes, or any raw veggies. It's also a good idea to avoid hard or crunchy things like raw carrots or nuts, and to steer clear of choking hazards like whole nuts, popcorn, or anything with a hard shell. 

When you're serving, try using a silicone teether or a soft wooden spoon to encourage them to grab the food. You can also place the food on a high chair tray so they can practice gripping it. And remember, their first bites might be messy, and that's totally okay! Let them explore the textures and tastes. 

Oh, and a final tip: keep offering the same finger foods consistently, even if they don't eat much at first. Repeating is key to building their confidence.
