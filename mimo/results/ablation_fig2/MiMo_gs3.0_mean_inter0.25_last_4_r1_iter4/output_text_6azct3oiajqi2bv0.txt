For hemming a jacket without damaging the lining, follow these steps carefully:

### **Do NOT Use Glue or Iron-On Hemming**
- **Avoid glue sticks/sprays**: They warp synthetics and leave residue.
- **Avoid high-heat irons**: They can melt the lining or fabric.

### **Best DIY Methods**
1. **Hand Stitching (Most Precise)**  
   - **Materials**: Matching thread, needle, or sewing kit.  
   - **Steps**:  
     - Turn the hem inside out.  
     - Fold the edge up ¼ inch, then fold again ½ inch (like a "double fold").  
     - Hand-stitch close to the folded edge using a **running stitch** or **backstitch**.  
     - *Pro Tip*: Use the same fabric from the hem itself as your thread (if available) for invisibility.

2. **Fabric Glue (Temporary Fix)**  
   - **Materials**: Fabric glue (e.g., E6000 or Aleene’s Tacky Glue).  
   - **Steps**:  
     - Apply a thin line of glue to the folded edge.  
     - Press firmly for 30 seconds.  
     - **Test first**: Apply glue to an inconspicuous seam to check for discoloration.  
     - *Note*: Not permanent; may peel over time.

3. **Needle & Thread "No-Sew" Trick**  
   - **Materials**: Needle, thread, scissors.  
   - **Steps**:  
     - Cut a 6-inch strip from the hem’s raw edge (if possible).  
     - Thread the needle, tie the strip to the hem, and pull it taut.  
     - Secure with a knot to anchor the hem.  

### **Professional Help (Recommended for Quality Jackets)**
- **Local Tailors**: Search "tailor near Arden Fair." Many offer hemming for $15–$30.  
- **Online Services**: Sites like **Tailor Mailer** ship jackets for hemming ($25–$50).  

### **Critical Reminders**
- **Wash First**: Clean the jacket before hemming.  
- **Test Everything**: Always test glue/thread on an inside seam.  
- **Fabric Matters**: If your jacket has a **waterproof membrane** (e.g., for rain), avoid sewing/glueing through it—this will ruin the coating.
