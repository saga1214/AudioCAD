Of course, here is a complete breakdown of a standard resume format. Just follow these sections in order.

First, at the very top, is your name in about a fourteen-point font.

Right below that is the contact section. Instead of icons side-by-side, it flows down below your name. You'd start with your email on a new line, then your phone number on the next line, the month and year of birth on the line after that, and finally your city on the line below. Using nine or ten-point font for this part works well.

Next is a professional summary. This is a short, two-to-three sentence paragraph where you highlight your most relevant skills and career goals, especially if you're changing fields.

Then comes the experience section. This is your main area. You'll list your most relevant roles first, with the most recent job at the top. For each job, include the job title, the company name, and the start and end dates. After that, add two or three bullet points describing your key responsibilities and achievements.

After that, you'll have your relevant experience section. This is for older or less related jobs that you still want to include. You can list them simply with the job title, company, and dates, without detailed bullet points.

Next up is the education section. Here, you'll list each degree you earned, along with the school's name and your graduation date. You can add a line for any honors you received, like "Highest Honors."

Then, you can have a skills section. This is optional but very helpful. You can list your key skills, maybe grouping them into categories like technical skills or languages.

Finally, you can add a few optional sections at the end. These could include certifications, relevant volunteer work, or a link to your LinkedIn profile.

This structure gives you a professional and flexible format that effectively showcases your entire career path.
