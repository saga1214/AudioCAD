That's a very good goal. You can strike a balance between being respectful and direct.

In your email, start with a professional subject line, like "Meeting Request: Essay Exam Feedback."

In the body, begin by stating your positive intent. For example: "I'm writing to express my interest in doing even better on future tests in your class. To help me prepare, I was wondering if I could schedule a brief meeting to understand what I could have improved on in my recent exam score of sixty-four."

This approach keeps the focus on your desire to learn. If scheduling an in-person meeting is still difficult, you could add, "Office hours don't seem to work, but perhaps we could meet over lunch or after class?"

This gives you a backup plan if he doesn't respond to the initial request.
