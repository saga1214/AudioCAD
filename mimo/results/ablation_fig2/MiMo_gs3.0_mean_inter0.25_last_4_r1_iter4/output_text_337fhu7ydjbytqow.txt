Here's a clear packing list split between your **20-mile day segment** and **final 12-mile night segment**, prioritizing flexibility since there are three aid stations:

### 🌅 20-Mile Day Segment (Cool, Mid 30s, Hilly, Night Start)
Bring minimal layers but stay prepared. Aid stations help, but you need to be ready for changing conditions.  
1. **Moisture-wicking base layer** (top + bottom)  
2. **Light fleece or windbreaker** (for chillier moments/hills)  
3. **Gloves** (lightweight running gloves for grip/hand warmth)  
4. **Lightweight hat** (for sun protection early, then warmth)  
5. **Headlamp/handheld light** (with **extra batteries**)  
6. **Light pack or vest** (to carry base layers if needed)  
7. **Hiking belt/shoulders** (to secure pack/vest)  
*Why?* Temperatures may dip, but aid stations offer warmth/food. Keep it light and packable.

### 🌙 Final 12-Mile Night Segment
Prioritize warmth and visibility. No aid stations likely—be self-sufficient.  
1. **Moisture-wicking base layer**  
2. **Insulated fleece or puffer jacket** (fleece weighs less than puffy)  
3. **Thicker gloves** (for hand/wrist warmth on descents)  
4. **Warm hat** (beanie or fleece cap)  
5. **Buff or neck gaiter** (for neck/wrists in wind, also sun protection)  
6. **Headlamp/handlight** (with **extra batteries**—critical!)  
7. **Light pack or vest** (to carry layers)  
*Why?* Night gets significantly colder. Layers are non-negotiable.  

### 🎒 Universal Essentials (Both Segments)
- **Trail shoes with aggressive grip** (e.g., Vibram soles)  
- **Quick-dry shorts/leggings** (no cotton!)  
- **Extra socks** ( wool or synthetic)  
- **Zip-off pants** (convertible for versatility)  

### ⚠️ Key Tips
- **Layer smart**: Base + mid + outer should be removable without stuffing.  
- **Test gear**: Wear gloves/hat during training if possible.  
- **Aid station strategy**: Grab their hot drinks (coffee, tea) and dry socks if needed.
