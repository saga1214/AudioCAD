You're absolutely right to focus on that connection—**Quincy Jones** and **Gamble & Huff** are the *primary* songwriters/producer-songwriters who exemplify how production choices directly shape emotion and dynamics. While lyricists like Taylor Swift and melody-focused writers like Max Martin craft the core song, **Quincy Jones** and **Gamble & Huff** were masters of using production as an emotional and dynamic storytelling tool.

Here’s a quick breakdown:

1.  **Quincy Jones:** His collaboration with **Bruce Swedien** (especially on Michael Jackson's *Thriller* and *Off the Wall*) is legendary. They didn't just write songs; they *engineered sonic landscapes*. Their production choices were integral to the songs' dynamics:
    *   **Emotion:** Using silence, space, specific drum sounds (like the iconic 808 kick), meticulous vocal layering, and unique instrumentation to create profound emotional impact (e.g., the vulnerability of "Billie Jean," the epic build of "Thriller").
    *   **Dynamics:** Mastering the art of contrast – huge, bombastic choruses juxtaposed with intimate verses, intricate arrangements that swelled and receded perfectly. The production *was* the dynamic engine.

2.  **Gamble & Huff:** Their work, especially with Donny Hathaway, The Temptations, and Motown acts, was foundational to the "Philly Soul" sound. Their genius lay in:
    *   **Emotion:** Creating lush, warm, and incredibly soulful textures through rich horn arrangements, layered vocal harmonies, and specific drum grooves. The production *felt* emotional and inviting.
    *   **Dynamics:** Building intricate, interlocking parts that created a powerful, driving groove and a sense of communal energy. The production *was* the dynamic foundation and release.

**Why studying them alongside pure songwriters is crucial:**

*   **Modern Context:** While Jones/Swedien's work is classic soul/R&B, the *principles* of using space, texture, arrangement, and sonic detail to build dynamics and evoke emotion are absolutely transferable to pop. Think of how modern pop producers use synth textures, vocal processing, and beat design to create mood and tension.
*   **Holistic Approach:** Pop music is inseparable from production. Studying Gamble/Huff and Jones teaches you to think beyond just the notes and words – to think about *how* the sound itself tells the story and manipulates feeling.
*   **Genre Fluidity:** They mastered blending soul, R&B, gospel, and pop sensibilities – a skill highly relevant to contemporary pop songwriting.

**Taylor Swift & Sia:** While primarily known for their writing, their *productions* are often integral to their emotional impact and dynamics:
*   **Taylor Swift:** Her production choices (especially in *folklore*/*evermore*) heavily shape the narrative tone and emotional shifts within songs.
*   **Sia:** Often collaborates with producers who craft dense, atmospheric, and dynamic sonic palettes that are core to her vocal delivery and the song's emotional journey.

**Max Martin & Ryan Tedder:** Their genius is in crafting *production-ready* songs. Their melodies and structures are often built with the pop production process in mind (e.g., big, anthemic choruses, catchy hooks designed to work over a beat). Understanding their songwriting helps you see how it *serves* the pop production ideal.

**In Summary:** While **Taylor Swift** (lyrics/structure), **Sia** (melody/ hooks), **Ryan Tedder** (structure/hooks), **Max Martin** (melody/structure), **Gamble & Huff** (soulful dynamics/texture), and **Bruce Swedien** (emotional precision) are all essential to study, **Quincy Jones** stands out as the *singular* songwriter whose entire career demonstrates the profound link between songwriting and production choices in creating emotional depth and dynamic range. His work is a masterclass in how sound *itself* tells the story.
