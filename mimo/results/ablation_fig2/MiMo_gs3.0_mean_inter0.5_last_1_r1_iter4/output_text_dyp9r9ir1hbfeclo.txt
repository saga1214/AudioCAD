You're right to double-check! Earlier I mentioned **humerus**, **scapula**, and **clavicle** working together for shoulder rotation. But let's clarify:  

1. **Humerus** Upper arm bone (head rotates in scapula's socket).  
2. **Scapula** Shoulder blade (shallow glenoid socket holds humeral head).  
3. **Clavicle** Collarbone (stabilizes scapula and connects to sternum).  

**The clavicle isn't directly part of the *rotational* joint**, but it’s essential for overall shoulder stability and movement.

### Signs of Poor Rotation (Revisited):  
- **Limited range of motion** (especially inward/outward rotation).  
- **Pain during circular arm movements**.  
- **Weakness** when lifting or rotating away from the body.  
- **Grinding/stiffness** in the joint.

### Regarding Lateral Raise Soreness:  
If your friend has soreness from dumbbell lateral raises:  
- **Normal Muscle Fatigue**: Soreness from working deltoids and rotator cuff.  
- **Concerning Signs**:  
  - Sharp or persistent pain (vs. general fatigue).  
  - Pain *at rest* or limits daily movements.  
  - Weakness or instability in arm movements.  

**Recommendation**: If soreness continues >48 hours or worsens, they should see a clinician to rule out rotator cuff strain or impingement.  

> 💡 **Key Takeaway**: The shoulder’s collaborative bones enable rotation, but poor mechanics or injury can disrupt it. Always prioritize form in exercises like lateral raises!
