### Treat Recommendations
*   **Best Flavors:** Start with high-value treats your puppy *loves* more than chicken right now! Small bits of cooked chicken liver, turkey, cheese (small pieces), hot dog bits, or even tiny bits of peanut butter (xylitol-free) are excellent starting points. Plain boiled chicken breast is also great. Avoid anything overly fatty or spicy.
*   **Size:** Tiny, pea-sized pieces are ideal for training to avoid getting messy.
*   **Value:** Choose treats he can't get *too* excited about, but still really wants. You can switch to lower-value treats (like kibble) once a behavior is reliably learned.
*   **Timing:** Give the treat *immediately* after he performs the desired action.

### Clicker Training Explained
Clicker training uses a simple three-step process:

1.  **Click:** You click the clicker (a small, handheld device that makes a sharp, distinct sound) the *exact moment* your puppy does something you want, even if it's just looking at you or moving slightly.
2.  **Treat:** Immediately after clicking, give him a high-value treat.
3.  **Repeat:** Keep repeating steps 1 & 2 many times. Eventually, your puppy learns that the click sound means "Good job!" and that it's followed by a treat.

**How it Works for "Sit":**
1.  **Lure:** Hold a treat near his nose. Slowly raise it over his head (without letting him lift his head too much). As he looks up to follow it, his bottom will naturally lower.
2.  **Click:** The *instant* his bottom touches the ground, click the clicker.
3.  **Treat:** Give him the treat.
4.  **Repeat:** Do this hundreds of times. Gradually, you can phase out the lure movement and just say "sit" and click/treat when he sits on his own.

**Key Benefits:**
*   **Precision:** The click marks the *exact* moment the behavior happens.
*   **Clarity:** It tells your puppy *exactly* what behavior earned the reward.
*   **Builds Confidence:** It's a neutral sound that becomes a powerful communication tool.
*   **Efficiency:** Often leads to faster learning than verbal commands alone.

**Getting Started:**
*   Get a clicker (any cheap one works).
*   Practice clicking/treating for random actions (looking at you, blinking, moving an ear) until your puppy expects a click/treat.
*   Then, try luring the "sit" as described.

Clicker training is incredibly effective and fun! It builds a strong communication bond and makes training sessions positive and productive. Good luck with Galileo!
