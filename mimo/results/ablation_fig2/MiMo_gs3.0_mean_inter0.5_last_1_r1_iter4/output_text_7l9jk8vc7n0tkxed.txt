I hear you—severe blockages are tough with limited tools. Let's calm down *and* tackle this step by step. Here's a simple, toolbox-friendly approach:

### 1. **Double-Check the Plunger**  
   - Ensure you have a **flat plunger** (not cup-style).  
   - Create a **tight seal**—fill the sink/bowl with 1–2 inches of water.  
   - Pump **20–30 strong, steady plunges** (like you’re pumping a basketball).  
   *If it doesn’t budge, move to the next step.*

### 2. **Check for Debris**  
   - With the drain open, use your **hands or a stick** to pull out loose debris (cotton, hair, etc.).  
   - **Gloves are essential**—avoid skin contact with sewage.

### 3. **Try the Wire Hanger Method** (if plunging fails)  
   - **Step 1:** Bend a **plumber’s wrench** or **pliers** into a hook shape.  
   - **Step 2:** Insert the hook into the drain, hook debris, and pull it out.  
   - **Step 3:** Repeat until the blockage loosens.  
   *Caution: Avoid metal tools if the drain is PVC—use gloves!*

### 4. **Last Resort: Hot Water Flush**  
   - Pour **1–2 gallons of hot water** (not boiling) down the drain.  
   - Wait 30 seconds, then repeat. Heat can melt grease-based blockages.

### Safety First:  
- Wear **gloves** and eye protection.  
- Keep a towel nearby to contain water.  
- If it’s a sewer line blockage, call a pro—don’t risk flooding.
