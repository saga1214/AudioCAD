Of course. Here is a straightforward, traditional format that is very recruiter-friendly. Let's go through it section by section.

First, at the very top, center your first name and last name in about a 12 to 14-point font. Directly below that, on the same line, have your email in the largest text, followed immediately by smaller text for your phone number and city in parentheses with icons.

Right below your name, create a new section for your professional summary. This should be about three to five lines where you describe your background and key skills relevant to the new jobs you're applying for.

Next, you can have a brief "Experience" section. Here, list each job title, company name, and the dates you worked there. For each role, add one or two very brief bullet points highlighting a key achievement or responsibility.

After that, create a dedicated "Skills" section. List your key skills, maybe five to seven for each job you're targeting. You can group them into categories like Technical Skills, Languages, or Soft Skills.

Then comes your "Education" section. List your degrees, the school name, and any honors or awards you received, like Highest Honors or a scholarship.

If you have a lot of older, relevant work history that you don't want to put in the main experience section, you can add an "Additional Work History" section at the end, formatted the same way as your main experience.

Finally, at the very bottom, you can add a single line for references, which usually says something like "References available upon request."

This structure is simple, easy to read, and gets all your important information across very effectively.
