Okay, you want simple, meaningful crafts for Christmas gifts, tailored to different ages. Macramé and cookies have been big topics, but you’re open to other hands-on ideas. Let’s break it down by age group and what might work.

For the little ones, ages 2-5, think super tactile and short attention span friendly. Fingerprint art is great, like I said. They could also do paper plate snowmen or simple cut-out trees with tissue paper snow. You could even have them decorate plain ornaments with stickers or crayons. The key is minimal prep, maximum fun.

For the middle kids, ages 6-10, they can handle more involved projects. Your macramé plant hingers could actually work here if they love them as much as you do. You just break down the steps so younger siblings can help with knots. Or, you could do beaded bracelets—they’re quick, customizable, and you can mix colors. If drop cookies sound like a good fit, that’s another option too.

For the teens, ages 11-15, they might want more autonomy. They could help you with more complex macramé pieces, like bigger wall hangings. Or, if baking is too chaotic, maybe they could design custom cookie cutters and then bake with you. Another idea is upcycling—like turning old jars into ornaments with paint or making mini terrarium hangers.

Across all ages, a good station idea would be to have one area for decorating (like a table with glue, stickers, markers) and another for mixing or shaping. That way, you’re not stuck in one spot. And for the candy globes, if you hate the glue gun, maybe have the older kids glue the pieces while you focus on the filling and assembly.

Christmas cookies could still work drop-style, but maybe let the little ones decorate the ones you’ve already baked. That way, less mess, same fun.

The main goal is to let each kid have their own role, so they feel invested. And since you love crafts, maybe involve them in the planning too—like, “Hey, what do *you* want to make for gifts?” That could spark some ideas they’d genuinely love.
