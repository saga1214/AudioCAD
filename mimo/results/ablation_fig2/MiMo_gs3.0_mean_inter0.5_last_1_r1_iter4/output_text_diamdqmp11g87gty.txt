### For Snowshoeing (Newbie Friendly):
1. **Gear Essentials**:  
   - **Snowshoes**: Start with pre-made ones (rent or buy used) – look for frames around 55-60 inches for a comfortable stride.  
   - **Boots**: Waterproof, insulated boots with crampons for traction.  
   - **Poles**: Adjustable trekking poles for stability.  
   - **Layered Clothing**: Base layer (merino wool), mid-layer (fleece), and a windproof/waterproof shell.  
   - **Safety Gear**: Whistle, map/GPS, and a headlamp (snow can make it dark fast!).  

2. **Beginner Tips**:  
   - Practice walking backward to distribute weight on the snowshoes.  
   - Start on flat, groomed trails (like ski paths) before tackling hills.  
   - Tap trees are a fun "map point" – track your route near them!

### Other Winter Adventures:
- **Fat Biking**: Rent a fat-tire bike and explore snow-covered trails.  
- **Winter Hiking**: Look for frozen waterfalls, ice formations, or wildlife tracks.  
- **Ice Skating**: Find local rinks or frozen lakes (safety first – check ice depth!).  
- **Snowmobiling**: Try beginner trails or rent a sit-and-ride.  
- **Winter Camping**: Experience a cozy tent in snow (insulation is key here too!).  

### Bonus DIY Project: Snowshoe Frame Building  
If you're feeling crafty, you can build your own snowshoes from:  
- A 2x4 frame (cut to 55-60" long).  
- 2x6 cross-beams (4-6" apart).  
- Webbing (nylon or polyester cord) stretched across the frame.  
- Waterproof backing (plastic sheeting) and fabric for comfort.  
*Pro Tip:* Use a tap bit (like the one for your maple tree) to drill holes – it’s designed for wood!  

### Final Cold-Weather Prep:  
- **Seal Attic Insulation film over windows** one last reminder!  
- **Door draft stoppers** you mentioned earlier – double-check those seals.  
- **Fireplace Draft Stoppers**: Close the flue when *not* using it to block escaping heat.
