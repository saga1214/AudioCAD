Of course. Here's a summary of the wines we've discussed, with their ideal storage and serving temperatures in both Celsius and Fahrenheit. I'll also note which ones are best for hot or cold weather.

First, the high-end Pinot Noir: you should store it at cellar temperature, which is about 13 degrees Celsius or 55 Fahrenheit. For serving, it should be slightly below room temp, around 55 to 60 Fahrenheit, or 13 to 16 Celsius. This is best for cool or cold weather.

Next is Japanese Hon-Mirin. Store it at about 16 to 20 Celsius or 60 to 68 Fahrenheit. If you taste it directly, room temp works, at about 24 to 27 Celsius or 75 to 80 Fahrenheit. Since it's mostly for cooking, this isn't really about weather, but tasting it directly would be best at room temp.

Then there's Italian wine, like a Chianti. You'd store it at 13 Celsius or 55 Fahrenheit, and serve it just below room temp, at 16 to 18 Celsius or 60 to 65 Fahrenheit. This is also best for cool or cold weather.

We also talked about a Pinot Grigio, a white wine. You'd store that one at 10 to 13 Celsius or 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, and serve it chilled, between 7 and 10 Celsius or 45 to 50 Fahrenheit. This is the best choice for hot weather.

Finally, for a hot wine, you'd have something like mulled wine. You store the wine itself at 13 Celsius or 55 Fahrenheit, but you serve it warmed up to between 60 and 70 Celsius, or 140 to 160 Fahrenheit. This is also for cold or cool weather.

So, to quickly summarize: for hot weather, the Pinot Grigio is the best choice because it's light and crisp. For cold weather, the Pinot Noir or Chianti are great for their warmth and structure. Hon-Mirin is versatile and really depends on how you're using it. Hope this helps with your planning!
