That's a very smart observation. Older comics are generally much rarer and can be a fantastic investment because of their scarcity. I can definitely give you a list of 10 highly sought-after comics from the 1940s and 1950s.

Here are 10 highly valuable comics from that era. First, the ultimate one is *Action Comics* number one, Superman's debut from 1938, but it's astronomically expensive. So, moving into the 40s and 50s, you have *Superman* number one, which is the first full appearance of Superman. Then, *The Flash Comics* number one, which is The Flash's first appearance. Another classic is *The Batman* number one from 1940. From the 50s, a very popular one is *Detective Comics* number 27, where Batman and Robin first team up. *Captain America* number one is another key issue. *The Amazing Spider-Man* number 30 is significant because it's the first appearance of the Green Goblin. *The Amazing Spider-Man* number 39 is a key for the first appearance of Doctor Octopus. *The Amazing Spider-Man* number 43 is the first appearance of Norman Osborn as the Green Goblin. And finally, *Fantastic Four* number one, which is the first appearance of the Fantastic Four and Galactus. These are all major keys from the Golden Age of comics.
