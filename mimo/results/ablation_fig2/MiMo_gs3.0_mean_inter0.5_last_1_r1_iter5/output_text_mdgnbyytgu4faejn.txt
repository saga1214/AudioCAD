Of course. Here is a clear breakdown of a standard professional resume format that you can follow. 

First, at the very top, is your name in about a fourteen-point font. To its right, on a separate but aligned line, you'll have icons along with your contact info: email, phone number, city. I still recommend removing those icons just to be safe with application tracking systems. It's also great if you have and want to include a link to your LinkedIn profile here.

Right below your name and contact info, you'll have a short two-to-four sentence "Professional Summary." This is where you highlight your most relevant experience and state your career goals.

Next is the "Experience" section. This is where you list each job, including the company name, your title, the start and end dates, and a brief summary of your key responsibilities and achievements for that role. If you have very long work history, you can create a smaller "Additional Work History" section below this, where you just list job title, company, and dates for less relevant roles.

After that comes the "Education" section. Here you'll list your degree, the university, and your graduation year. You can also add any honors, like "Summa Cum Laude," or specific awards, like a scholarship, right under the degree where you earned them.

Then, you should have a "Skills" section. This is very important. You can list your technical skills, like software you know how to use, and your soft skills, like leadership or communication. A great tip is to tailor this list to the specific job description you're applying for.

Finally, you can add optional sections like "Certifications" or "Projects" if they are relevant to your application. Just list the name of the certification or project, and provide a brief description.

This structure should give you a clear, professional, and effective resume to get you started.
