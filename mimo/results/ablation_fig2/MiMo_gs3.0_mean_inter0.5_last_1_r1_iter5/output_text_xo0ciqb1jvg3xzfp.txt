You're touching on a very important point. For the Pilgrims, it was a joyous celebration of their survival and a successful harvest.

For the Wampanoag, the event was more complex. It was a diplomatic gathering to strengthen an alliance that was important to both groups, but it was also a time for them to witness the arrival of a new and powerful force on their land.

So, for the children putting on the play, you might want to focus on cooperative elements, like the harvest, learning from each other, and sharing a meal. It's a good idea to avoid implying that the event was universally happy or that the story is simple.
