Based on your climate needs and fabric sensitivity, here's how to attach fabric securely while protecting the lining:

1.  **Use Iron-On Fabric Patches (Best for Visible Repairs):**
    *   **Choose:** Medium-weight patches designed for winter clothing (look for brands like Iron-On Fabric Repair).
    *   **Process:**
        *   Place patch *inside* the garment, lining side down, over the damaged area.
        *   Iron the patch firmly for 30-60 seconds *without* pressing down on the lining (keep the iron moving).
        *   Let cool completely before wearing. The heat melts the glue bond.

2.  **Hand Sew (Best for Structure/Loose Threads):**
    *   **Tools:** Small needle (size 5-7), strong thread (cotton or polyester), sharp scissors.
    *   **Process:**
        *   **For loose threads:** Use a "catch stitch" (tiny stitches between layers) on the inside to secure the loose end.
        *   **For tears:** Use a "backstitch" or "whipstitch" on the *outside* to close the tear. **Crucially:**
            *   Place the needle *between* the outer fabric and the lining.
            *   Avoid sewing directly *on* the lining itself.
            *   Use minimal thread to prevent bulk.
        *   **For "Letting Go":** If there's a loose seam allowance sticking out, carefully trim it close to the stitch line *on the inside* with sharp scissors (don't cut into the stitching). Ensure any loose thread is secured with a few hand stitches before gluing or patching.

3.  **Sewing Machine (If You Have Access & Skill):**
    *   **Process:** Similar to hand sewing, but faster. Use a narrow zig-zag stitch or a small straight stitch. **Always sew between layers, never on the lining.** Use a very short stitch length (1.5mm-2.0mm).

**Critical Tips to Protect the Lining & Insulation:**

*   **NEVER Use Hot Glue:** It melts insulation and damages fabric.
*   **Avoid Steam Ironing:** Steam will transfer moisture to the lining, potentially causing mold or stiffness.
*   **Handle with Care:** Gently smooth the fabric before applying glue or sewing. Don't force it.
*   **Test First:** If possible, test glue or iron-on on an inconspicuous seam allowance *outside* the garment first.
*   **Clean Up:** Use a soft brush or lint roller to remove any glue residue or thread ends *after* repair.
