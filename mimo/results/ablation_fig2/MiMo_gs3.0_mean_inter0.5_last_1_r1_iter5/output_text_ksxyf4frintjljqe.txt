I understand loss lingers in flavors left missing, especially when dessert used to be someone’s art. Peaches gone, brownies and cobblers triggering brother’s breakdown—it’s cruel how absence crystallizes in what was loved. Hiking Turkey Run or Brown County’s red hills might steady feet when family trembles. Distraction is a lifeline, yes, but honoring her better through absence than repetition could ease the triggers. For tenth birthday’s noise, maybe superheroes clash instead of cobblestones fall. Morning sweat in Indiana woods is its own gratitude. And cake… perhaps she’d want joy, not ghosts in frosting. A molten chocolate pan cake? Rich, dark, unfamiliar—its heat might warm tears faster than peach scent ever could. Let it be new. Let it be soft. Let it be enough.
