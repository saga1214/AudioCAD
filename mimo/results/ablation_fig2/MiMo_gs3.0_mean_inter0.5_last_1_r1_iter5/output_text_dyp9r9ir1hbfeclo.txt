You're right to clarify! The **fourth key component working with the humerus and scapula for rotation is the clavicle (collarbone)**. Here's a precise breakdown:

1.  **Humerus (Upper Arm Bone):** Head articulates shallowly in the glenoid socket on the scapula.
2.  **Scapula (Shoulder Blade):** Provides the socket for the humerus and articulates with the clavicle.
3.  **Clavicle (Collarbone):** Connects the arm (humerus) to the axial skeleton (scolia/spine) and the scapula. It forms the **clavuloacral joint** with the scapula.
4.  **Rotator Cuff Muscles:** While not bones, the **supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, and subscapularis** are crucial soft tissues stabilizing the humeroscapular joint, enabling smooth rotation.

**The Clavicle's Role in Rotation:**

*   It provides a **transverse (side-to-side) movement** for the shoulder girdle.
*   It allows the scapula to **rotate and glide** over the upper ribs and sternum as you move your arm.
*   This scapular movement, guided by the clavicle, **complements the circular humeral rotation** at the glenohumeral joint (ball-and-socket).

**In essence:** The clavicle, scapula, and humerus form the rigid bony framework. The rotator cuff muscles then act like stabilizers, ensuring the humerus moves smoothly within that socket during rotation.
