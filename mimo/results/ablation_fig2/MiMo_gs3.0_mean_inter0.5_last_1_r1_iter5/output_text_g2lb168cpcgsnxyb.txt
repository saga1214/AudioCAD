Those are two extremely important, but separate, issues.

Let's address them one by one.

First, connecting with your client remains absolutely critical. It's the bedrock of your work. The aggression and elopement often decrease significantly when he feels safe and valued by you.

Second, and this is non-negotiable, you must raise the environmental and safety concerns.

One: The dogs running around are a liability. You should request that they are secured during sessions, either by being leashed or put in another room. A pet can be set off and could escalate a situation with the client.

Two: The clutter itself is a trigger. Clutter can be over-stimulating and unpredictable. Request that the house be kept as clean as possible during your sessions. You can even suggest simple ways, like clearing a small corner for you to work in.

Three, and this is the most urgent: You need to formally document and report that there is no proper supervision. As a technician, you shouldn't be alone with a client in a challenging environment without an authorized supervisor present. This is a safety and ethical issue. You should write down specific examples of elopement attempts or aggression that happened while you were alone, and report this to your supervisor and the BCBA immediately.

Connecting with him is your power, but you cannot be effective if you aren't safe. Ensuring proper supervision and a safer environment are the most important first steps you need to take right now.
