Here's your **clothing gear packing list**, separated for the daytime 20-mile segment and the nighttime final 12-mile pacing section:

---

### **Daytime 20-Mile Segment (Running at Dusk/Dark)**
Bring adaptable, breathable layers since temps are cooling but not yet freezing:  
- **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking top and bottom (synthetic or merino wool).  
- **Mid-Layer**: Fleece jacket or lightweight insulated vest (for chill hilly air).  
- **Outer Layer**: Wind/water-resistant shell jacket (for unpredictable weather).  
- **Hands**: Lightweight gloves (for grip and sun protection).  
- **Head**: Cap or baseball hat (for sun; remove at night).  
- **Footwear**: Trail running shoes with aggressive tread (for hills).  
- **Extras**: Buff or neck gaiter (for face warmth), lightweight pack to carry layers.  

*Aid stations will provide warmth items (sweaters, hand warmers), so focus on moisture control here.*

---

### **Nighttime 12-Mile Pacing (Running Fully in Darkness)**
Prioritize warmth, visibility, and foot protection:  
- **Base Layer**: Same as daytime (keep dry).  
- **Mid-Layer**: Fleece or lightweight puffy jacket (20–25°F rating).  
- **Outer Layer**: **Waterproof/windproof shell** (critical for rain/cool temps).  
- **Hands**: **Warm gloves** (consider touchscreen-compatible).  
- **Head**: **Headlamp/handheld light** (with extra batteries) + **warm hat** (wool or fleece).  
- **Footwear**: Same trail shoes, but bring **extra pair of warm socks** (wool or synthetic).  
- **Extras**: Buff/gaiter (over ears/neck), lightweight pack (if needed for extra layers).  

---

### **Key Notes for Both Segments**  
1. **Layer System**: Wear thin-to-thick, so you can add/remove layers as needed.  
2. **Visibility**: Wear reflective gear or a light-colored outfit (aid stations may have reflective tape).  
3. **Foot Care**: Change socks if feet get wet (aid stations provide extras).  
4. **Weather Flexibility**: Check forecasts close to the race—wind or rain could require the waterproof shell at both segments.
