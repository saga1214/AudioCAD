Alright, here is a suggested weekly practice schedule to help you make steady progress without stress.

First, aim to practice for about 15 to 20 minutes each day. Consistency is more important than long, stressful sessions.

Here's how you could break it down:
On Monday, you could start with five minutes of two-ball one-hand drills, then spend five minutes adding the third scarves, and finish with five minutes catching beanbags.

Tuesday, focus on the transition we talked about first: the first three throws method, plus five minutes with three beanbags and five minutes with tennis balls.

Wednesday, stick with the first method for transitioning, again with beanbags and tennis balls.

On Thursday, switch to the master-one-hand-two-balls approach, practicing that skill.

Friday, try practicing to music, again focusing on the transition from two to three using whichever method feels best.

Saturday would be a great day for a longer session, maybe 20 minutes, to mix everything together and focus on catching.

Finally, Sunday could be a light day. You could do five minutes of just two beanbags for fun, five minutes of slow-motion throws with scarves to review rhythm, and five minutes of gentle stretching.

Remember, stress hinders learning, so if a day feels frustrating, just stop and come back to it. Be proud of every success, no matter how small. Teaching him will be ten times sweeter once you've taught yourself. You can absolutely do this.
