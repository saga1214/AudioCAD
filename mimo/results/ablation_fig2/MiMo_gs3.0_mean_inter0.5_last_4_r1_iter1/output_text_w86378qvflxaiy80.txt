Think of it this way: every species that goes extinct in a hotspot is gone forever. It's a unique piece of the Earth's evolutionary history that can never be recovered. The loss is permanent. This is what Norman Myers himself has called the "eternal loss" of a species.

And it's not just the species themselves. The entire web of life that depends on them, you know, the whole complex ecosystem, is also lost. The potential for scientific discovery, medical breakthroughs, and ecological understanding is also wiped out.

So, while we can sometimes replace the services an ecosystem provides, or find alternatives for medicine, we can never, ever bring back a unique species that has been lost forever. That's the most fundamental reason for why we have to preserve them.
