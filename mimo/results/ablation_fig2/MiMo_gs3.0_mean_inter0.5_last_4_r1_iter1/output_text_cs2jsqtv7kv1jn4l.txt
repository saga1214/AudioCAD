I understand feeling cautious as a parent! Here's a clearer approach:

*   **Cilantro Dislike vs. Allergy:** Your daughter's strong dislike for cilantro is **very likely taste-related, possibly even genetic** (some people taste soap). It's **not** the same as a true allergic reaction (which involves an immune system response causing hives, swelling, breathing difficulties, dizziness, or vomiting).
*   **True Allergies Starting Later:** Yes, people can absolutely develop true, serious food or environmental allergies **at any age**, including adulthood. It's more common in children, but adults can develop new allergies too.
*   **Testing Frequency:** **You do not need to test for allergies routinely.** The key is observation. **Monitor her for any physical reactions** after eating:
    *   **Watch for:** Hives (red, itchy welts), swelling (especially of the face, lips, tongue, or throat), difficulty breathing or swallowing, stomach cramps, vomiting, dizziness, or a rapid heartbeat.
*   **Action if Symptoms Occur:** **If she ever experiences ANY of these symptoms after eating a specific food, stop eating that food immediately and consult her pediatrician or an allergist.** They can recommend testing (like skin prick tests or blood tests) to confirm an allergy and guide management. **No need for frequent tests otherwise.** "Better safe than sorry" testing for taste dislikes isn't necessary or helpful.
