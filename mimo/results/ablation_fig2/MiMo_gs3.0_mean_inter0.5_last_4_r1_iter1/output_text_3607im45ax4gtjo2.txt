Dealing with difficult people can be emotionally draining, but here are some practical strategies to help you cope and protect your well-being:

### 1. **Prioritize Your Safety & Boundaries**
   - **Physical Safety:** If the neighbor’s behavior feels threatening, document incidents (dates, times, witnesses) and report to authorities (CPS/police if necessary). Avoid direct confrontation.
   - **Emotional Boundaries:** Limit exposure if possible (e.g., supervise kids closely near her property). Politely decline to engage—short, neutral responses like "I’ve heard your concerns" can shut down arguments.

### 2. Focus on What You Can Control
   - **Your Response:** Practice saying "no" firmly. Redirect conversations: "I need to focus on [school/Halloween] right now."
   - **Community Support:** Rally other affected families privately. Share resources (e.g., neighborhood safety tips) without escalating conflict.

### 3. Practice Self-Care
   - **Manage Stress:** Your mention of Red Ribbon Week and Halloween shows small joys helping—lean into those! Exercise, music (like Forrest Frank!), or hobbies can reset your mood.

### 4. Limit Rumination
   - Stress lingers when we replay scenarios. Set a "worry timer"—dedicate 10 minutes/day to think about it, then consciously shift focus to positive tasks (dress-up, Halloween planning).

### 5. Seek Perspective
   - Ask yourself: *"Does this person’s behavior reflect on me/my family, or is it about them?"* Their actions likely stem from their own issues, not your worth.
   - Talk to trusted friends/family—verbalizing stress can lessen its weight.

### 6. When to Escalate
   - If CPS/police involvement deepens, cooperate fully and follow their guidance. If the behavior persists despite reports, explore legal options (e.g., restraining orders in extreme cases).

### Remember:
> "You can’t control others, but you can control how much their behavior impacts you."
