Okay, back to the problem. Since it's a *deep* blockage and the plunger isn't working, here's a step-by-step plan using your simple toolbox safely:

1.  **Safety First:** Put on gloves and eye protection. Ensure good ventilation if you suspect grease or chemical cleaner might be involved.
2.  **Locate the Clog:** If you know where it *might* be (e.g., under the sink, in the drain pipe itself), try to isolate it. Sometimes a deep clog can be near the drain opening.
3.  **Basic Drain Snake (Auger):** If you have even a basic drain snake in your simple box, this is your best bet for a deep clog.
    *   **Insert Carefully:** Feed the snake into the drain, pushing it straight down as far as it will go. Don't force it sideways.
    *   **Find the Clog:** Once the head is past the bend and near the blockage, start cranking the handle *gently but firmly*. You'll feel resistance as you hit the clog.
    *   **Wrap & Hook:** Keep cranking until the snake head starts to turn and hook into the debris. You'll feel it catch.
    *   **Recover the Snake:** Once hooked, slowly and carefully pull the snake back out, using the handle to guide it. The debris should come out with it.
    *   **Repeat:** You might need to do this several times, or even go back in for a second pass. Don't give up!
4.  **Liquid Drain Cleaner (Use with Caution):**
    *   **Last Resort:** Only use if you *absolutely* have to, and if it's compatible with your pipes (PVC or CPVC are generally okay for liquid, but check labels!).
    *   **Follow Instructions:** Pour the correct amount *directly* down the drain. **Do NOT use a plunger with this.**
    *   **Wait:** Let it sit for the full recommended time (usually 15-30 mins).
    *   **Flush:** Pour a bucket of boiling water down the drain to flush the cleaner and hopefully dislodge the clog.
    *   **Caution:** Can damage pipes over time or with misuse. Avoid if possible.
5.  **Baking Soda & Vinegar (Gentle Alternative):**
    *   Pour 1 cup of baking soda down the drain.
    *   Follow with 1-2 cups of white vinegar.
    *   Cover the drain immediately with a plug or cloth to contain the reaction.
    *   Let it fizz for 15-30 minutes.
    *   Pour boiling water down the drain.
    *   **Note:** This is often more effective for grease clogs than a truly deep, solid blockage.
6.  **Check for Visible Debris:** Sometimes, a simple blockage can be caused by a small object or excessive hair. Look down the drain with a flashlight if you can safely access it.

**Key Tips for Success:**

*   **Patience is Crucial:** Deep clogs take time and persistence. Don't get frustrated again.
*   **Don't Force It:** If you feel the snake slipping or hear grinding, STOP. Forcing it will damage your pipe.
*   **Focus on Technique:** Using the snake correctly (finding the clog, hooking it, recovering) is more important than brute force.
*   **Take Breaks:** If you hit a wall, step away for 5-10 minutes. Come back refreshed.
*   **Know When to Call for Help:** If you've tried the drain snake carefully multiple times and it's not working, or if you suspect a broken pipe, it might be time to grab your full toolbox tomorrow or call a colleague/fellow plumber for assistance.
