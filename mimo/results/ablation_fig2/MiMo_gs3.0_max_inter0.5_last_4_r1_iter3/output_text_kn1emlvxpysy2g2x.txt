Of course, wine steward coach here. Let me summarize the wines we've discussed and their ideal temperatures.

First, the high-end Pinot Noir. You should store it at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. The serving temperature is slightly below room temp, so aim for 55 to 60 degrees Fahrenheit, or 13 to 16 Celsius.

Next, Japanese Hon-Mirin. You should store it in a cool place at about 60 to 68 degrees Fahrenheit, or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you taste it directly instead of using it for cooking, room temp works best, around 68 to 75 degrees Fahrenheit, or 20 to 24 Celsius.

Then we have Chianti. For storage, it's 55 degrees Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius. For serving, you want it just a bit below room temperature, so 60 to 65 degrees Fahrenheit, or 16 to 18 Celsius.

And the Pinot Grigio. You should store it at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, which is about 10 to 13 Celsius. Serve it chilled at 45 to 50 Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius.

Finally, for mulled red wine. The storage temperature is the normal cellar temp of about 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. But the serving temperature is hot, around 140 to 160 Fahrenheit, or 60 to 70 Celsius.

Now, to compare them. For hot weather, the best choice is the Pinot Grigio, served chilled. It's light and refreshing. For cold weather, the Pinot Noir is a great choice, served a little cooler to highlight those deep flavors. 

And for the best wine for a hot dinner party, definitely the Pinot Grigio. It’s light, crisp, and refreshing. Hope that helps!
