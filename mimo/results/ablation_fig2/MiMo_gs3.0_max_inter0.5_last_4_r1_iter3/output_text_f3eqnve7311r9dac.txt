Yes, vintage comics from the 1940s and 50s are very rare and valuable.

Three record-selling examples recently were a nearly perfect copy of *Action Comics* number one for six million dollars, *Amazing Fantasy* fifteen for three-point-six million, and *Captain Comics* one for just over three million.

If you're looking to start with older comics, Heritage Auctions or reputable dealers are your best bet. Here are ten significant books from that era, keeping Luke Cage and Defenders in mind. Be aware, the price for any of these will be well over five thousand dollars, and you'll need to find a copy in good condition, preferably with a professional grade like 'Very Fine' or 'Fine'.

First, *Marvel Super-Heroes* number one from 1964, which is the first appearance of the Fantastic Four. Second, *The Incredible Hulk* number one from 1962, the start of the Hulk's most famous story. Third, *Fantastic Four* number one from 1963, the first appearance of Spider-Man as an adult.

Since you like the Defenders and Power Man, *Fantastic Four* number forty-nine from 1967 is a great choice; it's the first appearance of the Defenders. *Fantastic Four* number fifty is also key, as it features the first appearance of Power Man and Luke Cage.

For your favorite, *Luke Cage, Hero for Hire* number one from 1972 is essential. You can also look for *Marvel Spotlight* number five, which is the first comic to feature both Luke Cage and Power Man.

A few more from the 50s and 40s: *The Avengers* number one, the first comic to feature the entire team. *Captain America Comics* number one from 1941. And finally, *Strange Tales* number one hundred and ten from 1963, which is the first appearance of Namor the Sub-Mariner.

As you can see, these are all major investments, so it's wise to start with the ten to twenty-dollar comics I mentioned earlier and learn your way into the high-end market.
