First, address the fin debate once more for Costa Rica specifically since that's your dream trip debate point. Long fins shine where Costa Rica excels: strong currents near the Pacific coast (like Cenizo or Caño Island) and long outreaches needed for Mitiyo or offshore spots. Short fins offer compactness – crucial since you're flying – and ease for calm areas like Cocos Island's calmer southern side or Tulemar's lagoons. For your first few Costa Rican dives, if you plan mostly in calm lagoons or for short distances, short fins are practical. If your itinerary includes known current zones or long swims, long fins provide efficiency. A compromise? Mid-length fins. They offer more propulsion than short but are less taxing than full long fins. Great middle ground for building strength while gaining benefits.

Now, planning the trip! Costa Rica's prime dive areas are Cocos Island, the Pacific Coast (Guanacaste & Osa Peninsula), and the Caribbean Coast (Tortuguero & Limón). Each offers distinct experiences.

Let's start with Cocos Island. It's remote and dramatic. Dives here are often long outreaches, sometimes 2-3 miles each way, to incredible wrecks like the Sardina and El Remolino, or to submarine canyons. Strong currents are common, especially on the Pacific side. This is where long fins (or strong legs!) are advantageous for efficient travel. The marine life is phenomenal – seals, sea lions, dolphins, and large pelagic species like tuna and hammerheads are frequently seen. It's an adventure in itself getting there, but the diving is world-class and feels wild and untouched. Best for experienced divers comfortable with long swims and currents. Ideal if your Costa Rica dream includes that "epic" offshore feeling.

Key things to remember for planning:
- Weather: The best diving season is typically from December to April, when the water is warmest and most stable. Avoid the rainy season (May-October).
- Certification: Ensure you're both current with certification, especially if doing technical or deep dives. Cocos Island can have deeper wrecks.
- Liveaboards vs. Shore: Boat dives offer more variety and access to remote sites, while shore dives are great for calmer beaches and easier access. A mix is often best.
- Fins decision: Based on likely activities in Costa Rica (mix of reefs and possible currents), mid-length or long fins might be your best investment over short fins for efficiency and power. But pack your short fins too – you'll appreciate them on calm days or for travel!
