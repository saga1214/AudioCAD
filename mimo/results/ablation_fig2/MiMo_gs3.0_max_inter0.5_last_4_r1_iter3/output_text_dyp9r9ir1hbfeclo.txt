You're absolutely right to circle back—the clavicle is the fourth key component working with the humerus and scapula for shoulder rotation, though its role is more about **stability and guiding movement** rather than direct rotation. Here's the breakdown:

1. **Humerus** (upper arm bone): Rotates *within* the shallow socket on the scapula (glenoid fossa).  
2. **Scapula** (shoulder blade): Provides the socket for the humerus and moves laterally/medially to change the joint's position.  
3. **Clavicle** (collarbone): Connects the scapula to the sternum and sternum to the humerus. It **stabilizes the shoulder girdle** and enables upward/lateral arm movement.  
4. **Rotator Cuff Muscles** (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis): Act as a "ligament" to hold the humerus in place and actively control rotation.  

### Key Functions in Rotation:
- **Upward Rotation** (e.g., raising arm overhead): Requires scapula to elevate + clavicle to move upward.  
- **External/Internal Rotation** (e.g., throwing a ball): Primarily driven by rotator cuff muscles.  
- **Stability**: Clavicle prevents scapula from dislocating; rotator cuff prevents humerus from slipping.  

### Signs of Poor Rotation:
- **Stiffness** in abduction/external rotation.  
- **Weakness** when lifting arm away from body.  
- **Pain** during overhead movements or reaching.  
- **Grating/creaking** sensation during motion.  

### Rotator Cuff Soreness & Exercises:
If your friend felt soreness during **dumbbell lateral raises**, it’s likely due to:  
- **Normal muscle fatigue** (especially supraspinatus).  
- **Overuse** or poor form.  
- **Early-stage rotator cuff strain** (if pain radiates to neck/back or worsens with movement).  

**Recommendation:** If soreness persists >48 hours or worsens, advise your friend to:  
1. Rest the exercise.  
2. Ice the shoulder.  
3. See a clinician if pain is sharp or limited movement.
