### Key Email Structure:
1. **Subject Line:**  
   Keep it neutral and professional. Example:  
   > *"Meeting Request: Exam Feedback"*  
   *(Avoid phrases like "Grade Appeal" or "Questioning My Score" which sound confrontational.)*

2. **Opening (Focus on Respect & Context):**  
   > *"Dear Professor [Name], I hope you're doing well. I scored a 64 on the recent exam and wanted to sincerely thank you for the material you taught. I’m committed to doing even better in this course and would appreciate your guidance on how I could have improved my essay."*  
   *(This sets a collaborative tone.)*

3. **Ask for Specifics (Be Specific & Objective):**  
   > *"Based on the content and structure of the exam, could you share which aspects of my work fell short? For example, were there issues with argumentation, sourcing, or clarity? I’d love to understand the rubric or criteria you used to provide feedback."*  
   *(Focus on "criteria" vs. "unfairness." This frames the ask as a learning opportunity.)*

4. **Offer Flexibility (De-escalate Pressure):**  
   > *"If you’re available, I’d be grateful to discuss this briefly during your office hours or at your convenience. Please let me know a time that works for you."*  
   *(Shows deference and avoids demanding his time.)*

5. **Close Professionally:**  
   > *"Thank you for your time and mentorship. I look forward to applying your feedback to future work."*  
   *(Reinforces respect.)*

---

### Critical Pitfalls to Avoid:
- ❌ Don’t say: *"You graded me unfairly."*  
  → This triggers defensiveness.  
- ✅ Instead, say: *"I’d like to understand how the content could have been better assessed."*  
- ❌ Don’t imply his ego is a barrier upfront.  
  → Wait to see how he responds before adjusting tactics.

---

### If the Meeting Goes Poorly:
- **Stay calm and solution-focused.** If he shuts down, say:  
  > *"I understand your perspective. I’ll focus on improving for the next assignment."*  
- **Follow up in writing** summarizing key points to create a record.  
- **Consider T-34 (Tertiary Prevention of Academic Misconduct)** if you suspect bias—this is a formal process for appeals, but use it sparingly and only if other options fail.
