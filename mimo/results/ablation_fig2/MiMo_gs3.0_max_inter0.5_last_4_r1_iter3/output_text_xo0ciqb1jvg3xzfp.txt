For the Pilgrims, it was a moment of relief and survival after surviving both starvation and disease. It was a harvest feast, just like they would have celebrated back in England.

For the Wampanoag, the relationship was strategic. They had recently united their tribes against a common enemy and saw the settlers as a potential ally. The feast was part of that political negotiation.

So, at that specific moment in 1621, both groups were likely happy and grateful. But it's important to remember that this was a fleeting moment of cooperation, which would eventually give way to centuries of conflict and tragedy for the Wampanoag people.
