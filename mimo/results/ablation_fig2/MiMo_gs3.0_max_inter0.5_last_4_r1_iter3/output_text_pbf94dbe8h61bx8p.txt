Okay, you're looking for simple, meaningful Christmas gifts where your macramé passion and cookie baking love can still play a part, but also fit your busy life with five kids of different ages. Let's break this down.

First, for macramé plant hingers, absolutely double down on that love! Age-specific stations is a brilliant idea. Little ones (2-5) can do sprinkling decorations or pre-cut dough assembly. Middle kids (6-10) can handle measuring and simple knotting. Teenager (15) could manage the whole station setup. Quick wins like wall hangings instead of hingers every day might save time.

For cookies, drop cookies or chocolate crinklers are your best bet to avoid rolling out. To get the kids cooperating across all ages, stations are key again. Have a prep station for pre-baking dough, a decorating station with cookie cutters and toppings, and a baking station with a timer. Play good music and make it a race to see who makes the most festive cookies! And yes, for Santa's globes, Walmart or even local farm stores can be cheaper than PetSmart. Just be mindful of Peanut's pickiness with treats.

Since candy canes are out due to the story, how about other sweet projects? Fingerprint holiday cards are super simple and personal. Or maybe tie-dye fabric squares with festive colors? Kids can design their own mini blankets or bandanas. Another fun one is making wooden ornaments with a hot glue gun, like stars or trees, and then painting them. The glue gun part is still there, but it’s more contained.

And for the bird feeders, you're right to think about the peanut butter. It’s fine outside if you use it right before gifting, but maybe use a different binder like melted white chocolate or honey for a cleaner look. Or skip the binder altogether and just use the birdseed. 

The main goal is to lean into what you love making (macramé, cookies) but adapt it to fit your time and your kids' involvement. What feels like the most manageable starting point for Christmas?
