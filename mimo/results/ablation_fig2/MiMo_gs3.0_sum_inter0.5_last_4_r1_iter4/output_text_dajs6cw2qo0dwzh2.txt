That's a very insightful question. You're absolutely right that the Portuguese were already pursuing a sea route independent of the overland hubs. However, the fall of Constantinople was a huge catalyst because it signaled that the *existing* overland routes, which had been the foundation of European wealth for centuries, were fundamentally threatened. This created immense pressure to complete the sea route, turning a long-term project into an urgent necessity. So, while the timing might have been similar, the event dramatically accelerated and intensified the drive for discovery.
