Let's talk about high school and college offside rules. The main difference is how they define the defender used for the offside check. 

In high school soccer, you use the very last defender. College uses the second-to-last defender.

Here’s a scenario to illustrate that. Let's say Red Team is attacking Blue Team's goal. Red number 9 is behind Blue number 5, who is behind Blue number 2. Red number 10 plays a through ball. Red number 9 runs past both defenders and gets the ball to score.

For the high school rulebook, you would look at the last defender on the field, which is number 2. Since Red number 9 is closer to the goal line than both ball and number 2 when the ball is played, that's an offside offense.

However, for the college rulebook, you use the second-to-last defender, which is number 5. Since Red number 9 is ahead of number 5 and not closer to the goal line than both the ball and number 5, they were not in an offside position. Therefore, no offense. 

So, the outcome of the play can completely change depending on which rulebook you're using.
