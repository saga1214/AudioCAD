That's a great attitude. Here are a few tips for writing that email.

Start with a professional subject line, like "Meeting Request: Essay Exam Feedback - Student [Your Name]".

In the email, introduce yourself and state your purpose clearly. You could say: "I scored a 64 on the recent exam about [the course topic] and would like to improve on future tests. I'd appreciate your feedback on what I could have written better in my essay."

To be specific without being confrontational, you could add: "Subjective grading can be hard to understand, and based on the content I worked on, I'm curious what factors led to this score."

Then, ask for a brief meeting by saying: "Would you have time to meet briefly sometime this week? Office hours seem difficult with both our schedules."

Finally, end politely with: "Thank you for your time and guidance. I look forward to your feedback."

This approach focuses on your desire to learn and avoids direct accusations, which should give you the best chance for a constructive conversation.
