### Treat Recommendations for Puppy Training
*   **Best Types:** Choose small, soft, easy-to-chew treats that your puppy can swallow whole. Common options include:
    *   Small pieces of cooked chicken, turkey, or hot dog (plain, no seasoning).
    *   Cheese cubes (mild cheddar or cottage cheese).
    *   Dog-specific training treats (like Zuke's Mini Nibbles, Wholesome Essentials, or the same brand of training treats you might find at the store).
    *   Small bits of peanut butter (check for Xylitol-free and no added sugar).
*   **Best Flavors:** Flavors are less critical than the size and quality. Chicken, beef, cheese, or just plain peanut butter are usually appealing. Avoid anything overly salty or sugary. Since he likes chicken, that could be a good starting point!
*   **Key Tip:** Use *only* treats during training sessions so he knows the value. You can gradually phase out treats and use praise or a clicker as markers instead.

### Clicker Training Explained & How It Works
Clicker training is a highly effective, positive reinforcement method. Here's how it works:

1.  **The Clicker:** It's just a simple, handheld electronic device that makes a sharp, clear, consistent sound ("click!"). Think of it as a bridge between the dog's action and the reward.
2.  **The Process:**
    *   **Step 1: Condition the Clicker:** First, teach your puppy that the click means he *will* get a treat. Hold the clicker, shake it slightly, and wait for a random behavior (like looking at you, sniffing, or turning his head). The *instant* he makes *any* sound or movement (even the click itself), click it and immediately give him a treat. Repeat this 5-10 times, clicking and treating for the first thing he does, then the next, then the next. Within a few tries, he'll start doing things *just* to get the click and treat.
    *   **Step 2: Mark the Correct Behavior:** Now, use the clicker to mark the *exact* moment he does the behavior you want. For example, to teach "sit":
        *   Hold a treat near his nose.
        *   Slowly raise it over his head (without letting him bite it).
        *   As his bottom touches the ground, click! *Instantly* give him the treat.
        *   Repeat this sequence many times.
    *   **Step 3: Fade the Clicker:** Once he reliably performs the behavior on cue (e.g., sits when you raise the treat), you can start fading the clicker out. You can:
        *   **Phase it Out:** Click, then immediately say "Yes!" or "Good!" and give the treat. Over time, say "Yes!" a tiny bit later, then even later. Eventually, you can just say "Good!" or "Sit!" and give the treat.
        *   **Use a Marker Word:** Replace the clicker entirely with a word like "Yes!" or "Good!" which becomes his conditioned marker.
3.  Galileo sounds like a perfect candidate for clicker training! It makes learning incredibly clear and fun for him and gives you precise timing.

**In short:** The clicker marks the *exact* moment he does the right thing, telling his brain "That's it! Good job!" and signaling the treat is coming. It becomes a powerful communication tool between you and your puppy.
