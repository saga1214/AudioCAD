You're absolutely right to bring that up, as ethics and public acceptance are central to national departments operating within a democratic framework.

Given that, the vaccination campaign's lower public acceptance score becomes a much bigger deal. You would have to be very careful not to overstep personal freedoms while trying to protect public health. The ethical debate and misinformation could undermine the campaign's success regardless of how good it is. So, while it's medically the better choice, the political and ethical challenges make it much riskier.

In that case, the sugar campaign, despite being slower, might actually be the more prudent choice. It's generally ethically sound and faces less public controversy. So yes, considering public resistance and the delicate balance a national health department must strike, the vaccination campaign's high risk in those areas could lead to choosing the sugar consumption campaign instead.
