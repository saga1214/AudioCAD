For winter chicks on a concrete floor while avoiding straw/shavings to keep rodents away, focus on **insulation, moisture control, and safety**. Here’s a breakdown of your best options:

### 1. **Large Flake Pine Shavings (Deep Litter Method for Chicks)**
   - **How to use:** Line the bottom of the brooder with rubber mats (if using) or directly on the concrete. Add fresh shavings weekly on top of composting material. The decomposing bottom layer generates **natural heat**.
   - **Why it works:** Insulates well, rodent-uninviting if layered fresh often, and avoids mold/moisture buildup.
   - **Tip:** Seal cracks high on the coop to block drafts but ensure **upper ventilation** to prevent moisture/frostbite.

### 2. **Sand (Chick Bedding)**
   - **Ideal for:** Tiny chicks (5–8 weeks). Spread a 2–3 inch layer on rubber mats or directly on concrete.
   - **Why it works:**  
     - Dries quickly (no rodent nests).  
     - Provides cushioning for chicks’ feet.  
     - Easy to scrape clean daily.  
   - **Use with:** Combine sand with rubber mats for insulation from the cold floor.

### 3. **Heavy-Duty Rubber Stall Mats (Brooder Area)**
   - **How to use:** Cut mats to fit the brooder area. Place the heated plate on top.
   - **Why it works:**  
     - Rubber insulates cold concrete.  
     - Sand or a thin layer of wood shavings on top can cushion chicks’ feet.  
     - Easier to clean rodents away than shavings.  
   - **Tip:** Seal gaps around mats with hardware cloth to prevent rodent entry.

### 4. **Paper-Based Bedding (For Spot Cleaning)**
   - **Use:** Paper towels or recycled newsprint for daily cleaning around the chick area.  
   - **Why:** Non-toxic, moisture-absorbent, and rodent-unattractive. Replace as needed.

### Critical Winter Chicks Tips:
- **Ventilation is non-negotiable:** Even with heat, chicks need airflow to prevent suffocation or frostbite. Use a vent cover you can adjust seasonally.
- **Seal cracks:** Caulk gaps around the brooder plate, coop door, and walls to block drafts.
- **Monitor temps:** Aim for **90–80°F** (32–27°C) in the brooder box, dropping to **75–70°F** (24–21°C) as they mature.
- **Hygiene:** Change wet bedding daily to prevent chilling or disease.

### Rodent Prevention Recap:
- **Avoid loose bedding** (straw/shavings/sand deep layers) in storage areas.  
- **Use rubber mats** under bedding for insulation without attracting pests.  
- **Seal entry points** with metal hardware cloth (1/2-inch mesh) under the coop and around walls.

By combining rubber mats, sand, and sealed insulation, you’ll create a warm, rodent-resistant environment for both your adult flock and winter chicks. Stay vigilant about ventilation and cleanliness! 🐔❄️
