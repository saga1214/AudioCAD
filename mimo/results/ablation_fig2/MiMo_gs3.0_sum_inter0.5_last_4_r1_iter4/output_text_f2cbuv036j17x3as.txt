Okay, diesel camper vans age, and maintenance is crucial before a long trip. First, brakes. Some basic things you can check yourself: look for any red dust on the brake pads, which indicates wear. Also, listen for any grinding or squealing noises when you brake. If the van stops smoothly, that’s a good sign. But for a real, thorough inspection, you’d want to take it to a diesel mechanic to check the pads, rotors, and fluid levels. Next, spark plugs. Diesel engines use compression ignition, so they don’t have spark plugs—they use something called glow plugs. Glow plugs heat up the combustion chamber to help fuel ignite during cold starts. They usually last a long time, but if you’re concerned, you can check for any corrosion or soot buildup. Replacing them can be tricky because they’re located in the cylinder head, so unless you have experience, it’s best to leave it to a professional. Either way, a good pre-trip tune-up with a trusted diesel specialist is the safest bet.
