Certainly, the Pilgrims survived a deadly winter with the help of the local Native people. To celebrate their first successful and abundant harvest, they held a large feast and generously invited the Wampanoag to join in sharing the bounty.

However, for the Wampanoag, the arrival of the English settlers marked the beginning of profound and devastating changes. They faced the spread of diseases that killed many of their people, and their land and resources were gradually taken from them.

So, it's accurate to say that while the event itself was a moment of cooperation and gratitude, the long-term consequences for the Wampanoag were overwhelmingly negative and tragic.
