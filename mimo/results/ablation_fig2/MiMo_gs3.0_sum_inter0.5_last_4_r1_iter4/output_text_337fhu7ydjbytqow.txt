Here's a **clothing gear packing list broken down for your 20-mile daytime segment** and your **12-mile nighttime final-mile segment**:

---

### **20-Mile Daytime Segment (Tomorrow Night, Hilly, Cool Mid-30s)**
Bring **lightweight, adaptable layers** since temperatures might shift, and aid stations allow easy adjustments.  
- **Base Layer:** Moisture-wicking shirt & shorts (avoid cotton).  
- **Mid-Layer:** Fleece jacket or light puffy vest (for chillier spots).  
- **Outer Layer:** Wind/water-resistant shell (e.g., lightweight rain jacket).  
- **Hands:** Gloves (lightweight for warmth/hills).  
- **Head:** Cap or baseball hat (for sun/shade).  
- **Extras:** Headlamp (handheld, in case it gets dark early), buff/gaiter (for wind).  
- **Optional:** Lightweight pack or vest (to carry layers if needed).  

---

### **12-Mile Nighttime Segment (Final Miles, Running After Dark)**
Prioritize **warmth, visibility, and reliability**—no aid stations here, so pack extra layers.  
- **Base Layer:** Thermal top/leggings (fleece or synthetic).  
- **Mid-Layer:** Insulated puffy jacket (down/synthetic fill).  
- **Outer Layer:** Wind/water-resistant shell (for weather protection).  
- **Hands:** Warm gloves (glove liner underneath if extra cold).  
- **Head:** Warm beanie or skullcap, plus buff/gaiter (neck warmth).  
- **Visibility:** Brightly colored shirt/shorts (reflective strips if allowed), headlamp (with **extra batteries**).  
- **Extras:** Lightweight pack/vest (to carry layers), extra socks (in case feet get cold).  

---

### **Key Tips for Both Segments**
- **Shoes:** Trail running shoes with grip (hilly terrain). Bring **extra socks** (change if feet get cold).  
- **Modularity:** Pack layers separately so you can add/remove them easily.  
- **Visibility:** Night gear is non-negotiable—reflective gear or bright colors prevent accidents.
