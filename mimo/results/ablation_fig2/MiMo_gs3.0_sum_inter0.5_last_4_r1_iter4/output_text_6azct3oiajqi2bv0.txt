For repairing fabric without damaging the lining, here's a practical method using **fabric glue**:

### Recommended Method:
1. **Clean & Dry Fabric**: Wipe the torn area with a damp cloth, then let it dry completely.
2. **Test Glue First**: Apply a small amount of **fabric-specific glue** (like E6000 or Aleene’s Fabric Tacky Glue) to an inconspicuous area (like an inner seam) to check for color/lining damage.
3. **Apply Glue Carefully**:
   - Use a toothpick or fine-tip brush to apply glue *only* to the torn edges (avoid the lining).
   - Place a weight (like a book) on top of the fabric to hold layers together.
4. **Reinforce (Optional)**: For larger tears, place a small piece of matching fabric as a "patch" before gluing to prevent fraying.
5. **Wait 24 Hours**: Let glue fully cure before wearing.

### Tips:
- **Avoid Sewing Machines**: If you try to sew, use a **hand-sewing needle** with thin thread and stitch *between* the outer fabric and lining (like "whipping stitches") to avoid damaging the lining.
- **Temporary Fixes**: For small tears, **iron-on patches** (test heat first!) or **fabric tape** (like Washi tape) work quickly.
- **Brand Recommendations**: For Sacramento’s variable climate, REI’s Co-op brand offers mid-range pricing with functional, durable outerwear. Their online store offers shipping, and Arden Fair area stores often have in-store pickup options.

### Why Not Sewing?
 Sacramento’s transitional climate means jackets get frequent use. Sewing through the lining can weaken seams, while glue is faster and preserves the lining’s integrity for longer wear.
