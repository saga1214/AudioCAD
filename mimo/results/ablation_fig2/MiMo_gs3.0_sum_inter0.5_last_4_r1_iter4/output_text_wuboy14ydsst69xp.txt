You've hit on the complexity of cybersecurity! While **building and maintaining a strong security culture** is widely recognized as the *single most important aspect*, your coworkers are absolutely right that **prevention (technology)**, **detection**, **response**, **testing**, and **outsourcing** are all *critical enablers*.

For your presentation, I recommend this approach:

1.  **Acknowledge the Interdependence:** Start by stating that cybersecurity is a holistic program where people, processes, and technology are all vital. No single aspect works in isolation.
2.  **Highlight the "Most Important" Aspect (Your Core Focus):** Dedicate significant time to explaining **why security culture is paramount.** Emphasize:
    *   **Employees are the Human Firewall:** Technology protects systems, but people interact with them. An alerting user spotting phishing is the first line of defense.
    *   **Mitigates the Human Element:** Technology can't stop a malicious insider or someone falling for a sophisticated social engineering attack.
    *   **Enables Other Controls:** A well-trained team makes detection more effective (reporting incidents), response faster, testing more realistic, and technology more valuable.
    *   **Aligns Security with Business:** Culture fosters a mindset where security is everyone's responsibility, not just the IT department.
3.  **Briefly Cover the Critical Enablers (Addressing Your Coworkers):** Dedicate short segments to explain how the other aspects support the core culture:
    *   **Prevention (Technology):** The essential tools (firewalls, EDR, MFA, patching) that create the foundation. *But emphasize it's a tool, not a solution.*
    *   **Detection:** Monitoring and alerting systems (SIEM, EDR alerts) and user reporting channels. *Crucial, but detection is useless without response.*
    *   **Response:** The Incident Response Plan (IRP), team readiness, communication protocols, and recovery procedures. *The safety net.*
    *   **Testing:** Regular vulnerability scans, penetration tests, and IR drills. *Proves the program works and finds gaps.*
    *   **Outsourcing:** Leveraging specialized expertise (MSSPs, managed security service providers) to augment internal capabilities. *A strategic choice, not a replacement for ownership.*
4.  **The "Single Most Important" Conclusion:** Conclude by reiterating that while all elements are necessary, **without a strong security culture, the effectiveness of technology, detection, and response is significantly diminished.** People are the variable that makes or breaks the program. Investing in training, awareness, clear policies, and empowering employees is the bedrock upon which the entire cybersecurity program is built.

**Why this works for your presentation:**

*   **Clear Focus:** You highlight the "most important" aspect as requested.
*   **Holistic View:** You demonstrate a deep understanding by acknowledging the interplay of all critical components.
*   **Actionable Insight:** It provides leadership with a clear priority: invest in people and culture to maximize the value of all other security investments.
*   **Addresses Concerns:** You validate your coworkers' points about prevention, detection, and response, showing you understand their importance within the larger framework.

By framing culture as the essential multiplier that makes all other technical and procedural controls effective, your presentation will be both insightful and impactful. Good luck!
