You're absolutely right to revisit this—it *is* a layered question. After considering design, materials, tools, and technique, the answer isn't a single step but a **mindset**: **the maker's intention and mastery**.  

Here’s why:  
- **Design & Understanding**: A sword isn’t just steel. Its geometry (taper, distal taper, fullers) dictates balance, cutting efficiency, and purpose. Without this knowledge, even a perfect heat-treated blade is a blunt instrument.  
- **Execution**: Precision in forging (forged vs. CNC), heat treatment, and grinding requires decades of skill. A misstep at any stage cascades into failure.  
- **Materials**: Steel selection (1095, 52100, O1), heat treatment parameters, and tempering aren’t guesswork—they’re science requiring deep material understanding.  
- **Purpose**: Is it a display piece, a functional training sword, or a historical recreation? Intent shapes every decision.  

**Heat treatment** remains the technical linchpin (giving steel life), but **the maker’s holistic mastery**—balancing art, science, and purpose—is what truly defines a sword. Without it, even the best CNC program or rare steel becomes a hunk of metal.  

So, if forced to pick: **The heat treatment is the most *technical* critical step**. But **the craftsperson’s skill and intention** are the *ultimate* determinant of success.
