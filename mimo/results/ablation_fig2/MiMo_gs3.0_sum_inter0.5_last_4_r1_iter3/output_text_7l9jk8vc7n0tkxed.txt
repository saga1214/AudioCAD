Okay, plumber, let's get back to it calmly and methodically. A deep blockage is tough with a simple toolbox, but we have tools. Here's a step-by-step guide focusing on what's likely in your simple box and what comes next:

1.  **Double-Check the Plunger:** You started right. Ensure you have a *good seal* over the drain opening. Use strong, steady pressure – up and down rhythmically, not frantic. This is often the first step that works.

2.  **Grab Your Hand Auger (Snake):** This is almost certainly in a "simple toolbox." This is your best bet for a deep clog.
    *   **Insert Carefully:** Feed the auger head into the drain, straight down initially.
    *   **Feel for Resistance:** Start rotating the handle clockwise slowly. You'll feel the cable hook onto the clog.
    *   **Break it Up:** Once you feel it hook, *keep rotating*. Apply steady pressure. The rotation helps break up the clog and push it down the pipe towards the cleanout.
    *   **Retrieval:** Once you feel significant resistance or the cable stops advancing, stop rotating. Pull the cable back out slowly. You should see some debris coming out. Clean the cable head with a rag or your fingers.

3.  **Flush it Out:** After using the auger, pour a bucket of hot water (carefully, not scalding!) down the drain. This helps flush any dislodged debris.

4.  **Repeat if Necessary:** If the clog is severe, you might need to repeat steps 2 & 3 2-3 times. Be patient and persistent. Sometimes you need to work the auger back and forth slightly to break up stubborn chunks.

5.  Super Glue (if available): If your simple toolbox *might* include a small tube of super glue (cyanoacrylate), you can use it as a last resort for a *small* hair clog on a visible section of pipe *above ground*. Dip a pipe cleaner or cotton swab in the glue, hold it against the hair clog for 10-15 seconds, then immediately flush with hot water. **This is unlikely to fix a deep sewer line blockage, but worth a shot for surface-level hair.**

6.  **Check for a Cleanout:** Does your drain have a cleanout plug? If so, this is your best long-term solution. If not, you might need to call a drain specialist. If it *does* have a cleanout:
    *   Locate it (often near the main drain pipe).
    *   If you can access it, you might be able to snake the cable *from* the cleanout *back* towards the drain to dislodge the clog. This requires more tools and skill.

7.  **Know When to Call a Pro:** If after several attempts with the auger (and super glue if you have it), the clog remains, or if you suspect it's a major tree root issue deep in the sewer line, it's time to call a drain specialist. They have larger, more powerful augers and hydro-jetting equipment.

**Key Calming Tips While You Work:**

*   **Pause & Breathe:** Every 10-15 minutes, stop. Stand up. Stretch. Take 3 deep breaths. Check your progress.
*   **Focus on One Step:** Don't worry about the whole problem at once. Just focus on inserting the auger, rotating it, or flushing water.
*   **Listen to Your Body:** If you feel yourself getting frustrated again, take a 5-minute break. Go make a cup of tea. Come back with a clear head.
*   **Manage Expectations:** A simple toolbox won't fix every deep, severe clog. Be proud you're tackling it yourself, but don't beat yourself up if it's beyond your current tools.
