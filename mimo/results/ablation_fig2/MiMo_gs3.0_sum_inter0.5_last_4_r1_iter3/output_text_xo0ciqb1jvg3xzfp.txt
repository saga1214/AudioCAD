That's a great question. The 1621 feast was a moment of cooperation and celebration for both groups at that specific time.

For the Pilgrims, surviving a near-extinction winter was a miraculous event.

For the Wampanoag, the book suggests the relationship was complex. The harvest feast was part of their own traditions, but the English settlers were also a source of instability in their region.

So, while the feast itself was a positive event, it was part of a larger, more complicated relationship that ultimately led to significant loss for the Wampaneag people.
