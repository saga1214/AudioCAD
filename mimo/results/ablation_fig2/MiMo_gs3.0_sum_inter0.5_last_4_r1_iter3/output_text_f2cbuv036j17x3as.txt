Okay, diesel camper vans age, and maintenance is crucial before a long trip. For brakes, a simple visual inspection is the first step. Look for the brake pads themselves—do they still have some thickness left? If they’re worn down to the metal, that’s a problem. Also, check the rotors for any warping or deep grooves, which would require replacement. If you hear any grinding noises or feel a pulsation in the brake pedal, that’s a red flag. For spark plugs, diesel engines use high compression, so the plugs can be tough. You’d typically need to remove the diesel injector pump first, then the head bolts, and then pull the old plugs. New ones are installed, and the gaskets are replaced. It’s a process, and a diesel mechanic can do it for you. But you should at least check the van’s service records to see if it’s due for an oil change or other fluid flushes. A van that’s well-maintained will handle a long trip much better.
