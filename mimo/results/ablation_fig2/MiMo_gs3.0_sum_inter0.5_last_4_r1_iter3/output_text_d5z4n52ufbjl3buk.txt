That's a great goal. Here’s a framework you could use to write an email that strikes that balance.

First, start with your desire to improve. You could write, "Dear Professor [Last Name], I scored a 64 on the recent essay exam and wanted to schedule some time to better understand how I could improve on my next test."

Second, compliment his work. You could say, "I found the subject interesting and worked hard on the essay. I was hoping you could share any feedback that would help me refine my work going forward."

Third, be specific but neutral. For example, "Based on content alone, could you point me toward any areas where I could have contributed more effectively to the discussion?"

By framing everything as a quest for learning, you reduce the chance of him becoming defensive. Good luck with the scholarship application.
