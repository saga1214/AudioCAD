No problem! Here’s a clear recap of all the measurements I recommended, categorized for your small tailoring business:

### **I. Core Measurements (Take for EVERY Fitting)**  
1. **Bust** (under arms, fullest part)  
2. **Natural Waist** (smallest part of the torso, not the belt line)  
3. **Hips** (widest part, usually 8–12 inches below waist)  

---

### **II. Basic Functional Measurements (Add for Better Fit)**  
- **Inseam** (distance from crotch to ankle)  
- **Sleeve Length** (shoulder to wrist)  
- **Shoulder Width** (across upper back from shoulder to shoulder)  

---

### **III. Advanced Measurements (For Fitted/Garments)**  
| Garment Type | Key Measurements |  
|--------------|------------------|  
| **Tops/Shirts/Jackets** | Neck (collar size), bicep, upper back (SAG), shoulder seam allowance |  
| **Pants/Skirts** | Thigh (inside leg), back rise (critical for tall clients!), front rise, hip ease |  
| **All Pants** | Thigh, knee (if needed), crotch gusset width (for flexibility) |  

---

### **IV. Key Tips for Your Client**  
For your 6'4" neighbor:  
1. **Back Rise**: Must be 2–3 inches longer than front rise to prevent sagging.  
2. **Hip & Thigh Ease**: Add 1–2 inches for comfort when sitting.  
3. **Inseam**: Use **37"** (his measurement) → **shorten slightly** (to ~34–35") if he wears sneakers/low-profile shoes. Test by having him stand on the shoes and pin the hem!  

---

### **Practical Reminders**  
- **Use a Tape Measure + Notepad**: Always bring your tools to fittings.  
- **Consistency**: Keep a measurement chart for repeat clients.  
- **Ease**: Add 1–2 inches of extra fabric at hips/thighs for stretchability.
