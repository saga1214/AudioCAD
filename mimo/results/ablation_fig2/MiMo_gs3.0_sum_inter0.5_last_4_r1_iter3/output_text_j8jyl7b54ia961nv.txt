You are correct to point out the importance of public resistance. Ethically, forcing vaccination is controversial because it touches on personal autonomy versus public duty. Misinformation and free speech concerns can also get in the way. Public resistance can be so strong that it can even lead to restrictions on personal freedoms. Public acceptance is also key to a national department's reputation; constant controversy can damage trust.

Given those added points, the vaccination campaign becomes much more challenging. The high ethical controversy and the very low public acceptance make it a risky choice. Therefore, after giving public resistance and ethics more weight, I would now choose the national campaign to reduce sugar consumption. While results might be slow, it faces fewer direct and intense ethical debates and public opposition.
