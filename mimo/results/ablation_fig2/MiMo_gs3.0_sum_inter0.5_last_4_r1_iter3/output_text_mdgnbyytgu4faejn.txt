Okay, let's put it all together. Here's a clear, traditional format that's perfect for scanning and covers everything you've asked about.

First, at the very top, large 14-point font: your name.

Right below that, on a new line for better readability, start with a period before your contact info. You could have a period before email, another before phone, and a third before city. Icons should be omitted. Font-wise, keep your email and phone at nine-point like you had, but consider making your city ten or eleven-point. This looks clean and professional.

Next, a short, powerful professional summary of two or three sentences. This is where you'll briefly restate your most relevant skills and career goals.

Then, list your Relevant Experience. For each role, include the job title, the company name, and the years you worked there. Use the exact dates. Follow each entry with one or two very brief bullet points that highlight the most important achievement or skill from that role.

After that, create a separate section for Additional Work History. Here, you can list older roles without dates or with just the year range. Again, keep it to just the job title and company name.

For your education, list your degrees in reverse chronological order. For each degree, include the school name, your expected graduation date, and any honors you received, like 'Highest Honors' or the name of a scholarship.

Finally, create a Skills section. List your most relevant hard and soft skills, maybe grouped into categories like 'Technical Skills' or 'Languages', and include a few key certifications.

So, to recap: put contact info centered below your name for clarity. Group older, relevant experience together without dates in the main 'Relevant Experience' section. Omit icons to ensure compatibility with tracking software. And place your skills section after your experience to make them easy to find. This format presents your career transition effectively.
