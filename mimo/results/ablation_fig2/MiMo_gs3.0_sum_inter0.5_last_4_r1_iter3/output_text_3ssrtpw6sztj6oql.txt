For modern-day art museum security, **controlling access at every entry point remains the definitive first line of defense**, *regardless of human unreliability*. Here's why this principle is foundational and how it addresses your paper:  

### Key Layers Explained:  
1. **Visible Presence & Screening (Guards + Bag Checks)**  
   - Controls physical entry by managing exactly who and what enters the *building*. Deterrence, identity verification, and bag screening prevent threats before they breach the museum’s perimeter.  

2. **High-Tech Surveillance (Second Layer)**  
   - Cameras monitor interiors *after* entry. They deter internal threats but don’t replace access control.  

3. **Motion Sensors & Alarms (Third Layer)**  
   - Trigger alerts if someone bypasses internal barriers. They detect breaches but don’t prevent initial entry.  

4. **Secure Displays (Final Layer)**  
   - Reinforced cases/pedestals protect individual artworks *inside* the museum. They add resistance but don’t stop a breach at the entrance.  

### Why Access Control is Unshakeably First:  
- **The Gardner Heist (1990)** exploited human error (guards tricked into opening doors) but *still* relied on the thieves gaining entry first. The vulnerability was at the **access point**, not deeper layers.  
- **Modern Threats** (drones, cyber intrusions) often start by targeting entry points. Robust access control is the only layer that can stop a breach *before* sensors, alarms, or displays are overwhelmed.  
- **Risk Management**: Even if guards make mistakes, strict protocols (e.g., no unsearched bags, biometric access for staff) create a baseline of control.  

### Key Takeaway for Your Paper:  
While layered tech is vital, the **first line of defense must be the gateway itself**. Modern upgrades include:  
- AI-powered facial recognition at entrances.  
- Automated bag screening with X-ray/MRT.  
- Multi-factor authentication (keycards + PINs) for restricted areas post-entry.  

This foundational control minimizes the attack surface, buys time for internal systems to respond, and remains the most critical failure point to fortify.
