You got it. Let's look at a scenario involving a corner kick to illustrate a key difference in how the defender's touch to the offside reset is applied.

In this situation, the Red Team is attacking the Blue Team's goal. A Red Team attacker takes a corner kick. As they kick it, the second-to-last defender makes a deliberate, controlled play on the corner kick, intercepting it and clearing it away from the goal.

Now, here's the crucial part: the ball deflects and rebounds off that same defender, landing right in front of Player number nine, who was in an offside position but not involved until then.

This is where the rules differ. 

According to college and high school rules, Player nine can no longer be called offside. Since the ball simply deflected or rebounded off the defender, not made a deliberate play, Player nine is still in an onside position and it's not an offense.

However, in some versions of the Laws of the Game, like those used internationally, a deliberate defender's touch that results in a rebound can still reset the situation and make the attacker offside. 

So, in our study help for certification, referees working college and high school games should call the goal, because the rebound doesn't count as a deliberate touch that resets the offside.
