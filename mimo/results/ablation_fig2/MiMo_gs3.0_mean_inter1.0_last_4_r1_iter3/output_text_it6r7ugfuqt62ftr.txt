Here’s a detailed itinerary for Big Sur and Morro Bay, designed for warmth, adventure, and celiac safety.

The theme is Warm Beaches, Coastal Wonders & Easy Swimming. It's great for swimming, avoids heights, and has plenty of gluten-free options.

For your accommodation, I'd suggest a cozy beachfront cabin or a nice Airbnb in Morro Bay. Morro Bay is a charming, walkable town with beaches perfect for swimming and is the gateway to Big Sur. It's a great base for both days. You can find comfortable places to stay for around two hundred to three hundred dollars a night.

Now for the travel plan. For Day One, you'll drive from Northern California to Morro Bay. It's about a three to three-and-a-half-hour drive. You should leave early, maybe around 7 AM, to beat the traffic. You can pack a light breakfast and snacks. I'd suggest stopping for a quick gluten-free breakfast in Santa Barbara if you have time. Once you arrive in Morro Bay, you can check into your place, relax, and then enjoy a wonderful dinner at a dedicated gluten-free restaurant called The Fishery. It's a cozy spot right on the harbor, and their menu is designed for people with celiac, so it's very safe. They serve fresh seafood and have great gluten-free desserts. You can just tell them it's for celiac, and they'll handle it perfectly.

On Day Two, you'll head to Big Sur. It's a scenic drive, about an hour and a half to two hours from Morro Bay. You'll want to leave early again, around 7 or 8 AM. The drive itself is beautiful, but you should definitely stop at Pfeiffer Big Sur State Park to see the iconic McWay Falls. There's a short, easy walk down to the falls, and you can even swim in the calm, beautiful pool right below it! It's perfect for your bathing suit and it's not a height thing. After the falls, you can visit the Pfeiffer Beach Picnic Area. It's a lovely spot to have a packed lunch and enjoy the ocean views. Then, you can drive along the coast to check out some of the iconic viewpoints, like the Bixby Bridge. You can stop whenever you want for photos. In the afternoon, you could take an optional tour to see the elephant seals at Piedras Blancas, which is a short drive north. It's usually free and a really cool experience. For lunch, you could grab something from a deli or a market along the way.

For Day Three, you'll drive back towards Morro Bay, which takes about an hour and a half. You can start by visiting the famous Hearst Castle. It's a bit of a drive, but it's iconic Northern California. You'll need to book tickets online well in advance. You can tour the magnificent mansion and gardens. After that, you can drive down to the beautiful Nepenthe restaurant, which has stunning ocean views. Now, they have a limited gluten-free menu, so you'll want to call ahead and mention your daughter's celiac diagnosis to make sure they can accommodate her safely. It's worth it for the amazing food and views!

On your last day, you can either relax in Morro Bay or drive a bit further south to Cambria or San Simeon for more beach time and cypress tree views. You can pack a picnic and swim at one of the lovely beaches along the way. For a final special dinner, you could go back to The Fishery or find another great spot in Morro Bay, like the Harbor House Restaurant, which also has excellent gluten-free options.

For packing, remember your two bathing suits and cover-up as before. Also pack light clothes like dresses, shorts, and tank tops. Sunscreen, sunglasses, hats, and light layers are a must. And for celiac, remember to pack your own safe snacks and maybe a gluten-free raincoat just in case.

Overall, this trip hits all your points: warm weather, swimming, no heights, a birthday celebration, and a relaxing vacation for your family. Enjoy your trip!
