For the Pilgrims, it was a relief after barely surviving starvation. For the Wampanoag people like Ousamequin, who led them, it was a strategic alliance to help their tribe survive. While there was gratitude, it was also a political act.

It was certainly not a happy occasion for the Wampanoag people as a whole, because many of their people had already died from European diseases before the feast even happened.
