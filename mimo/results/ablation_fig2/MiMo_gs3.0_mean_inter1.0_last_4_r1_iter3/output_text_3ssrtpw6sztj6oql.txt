For modern-day art museum security, the **first line of defense remains controlling physical and digital access at every entry point**. This foundational layer prioritizes managing exactly *who and what* enters the museum before threats can materialize internally. Key components include:  

1. **Visible Security Presence**: Trained guards to deter intrud bag searches, coat checks, and ID verification to mitigate risks like smuggling or explosive devices.  
2. **Automated Screening**: AI-powered scanners, X-ray machines, or RFID systems to inspect items beyond manual checks.  
3. **Access Control Systems**: Biometric scanners (e.g., facial recognition) and encrypted keycards for restricted areas (e.g., storage, server rooms).  

### Why This Is the First Layer:  
- **Preventive Deterrence**: Criminals often target entry points first (e.g., the 1990 Isabella Stewart Gardner heist exploited guard error). Robust access control disrupts initial breach attempts.  
- **Layered Strategy**: While internal systems (cameras, sensors) and secure displays (reinforced cases, anti-tamper alarms) are critical, they operate *after* entry. First-line defenses buy time for these systems to activate.  
- **Modern Threats**: Includes cyberattacks (e.g., disabling alarms via network intrusion) and insider threats—making perimeter control essential.  

### Evolution for Modern Context:  
- **Smart Perimeters**: Laser grids, drone patrols, or seismic sensors around the building.
