### 1. **Pronunciation of "Saccade"**  
   - **Correct Pronunciation:** /səˈkeɪd/ (suh-KAYD)

### 2. **Does the Eye See Continuously?**  
**No—this is a critical distinction.**  
- **The eye (retina):**  
  - Acts like a biological camera. It captures light in discrete "snapshots" during each eye movement.  
  - During a **saccade** (rapid eye movement), the eye briefly "fixates" on a point, capturing a snapshot.  
  - The eye has a **"blind spot"** during saccades because it’s not processing the full visual field.  

- **The brain:**  
  - Receives signals from the eye but **doesn’t perceive the world directly**.  
  - It **stitches** these snapshots into a continuous motion.  
  - **Saccadic masking** happens: The brain *temporarily suppresses* visual input during saccades to avoid motion blur.  

---

### 3. **Key Scientific Concepts**  
- **Flicker Fusion Threshold:**  
  - The maximum frame rate your eye can perceive as "continuous."  
  - **Typical threshold:** ~60 Hz (higher in bright light, lower in darkness).  
  - Example: A 60 Hz strobe light appears solid to most people.  

- **Saccadic Masking:**  
  - During a saccade, the brain **blocks** the visual signal for ~100 milliseconds.  
  - You *don’t consciously see* what happens during the saccade.  

- **Perceptual Smoothing:**  
  - The brain fills gaps between snapshots to create fluid motion.  
  - This is why 24 fps film feels continuous: The brain matches its "snapshots" to the eye’s.

### 5. **Why 24 fps Feels "Real"**  
- **Alignment with Brain Processing:**  
  - 24 fps matches the brain’s ability to stitch snapshots.  
  - Higher frame rates (e.g., 60 fps) don’t *feel* "more real"—they feel "smoother" but lack the cinematic "dreaminess" of 24 fps.  
- **Saccadic Masking Explained:**  
  - At 24 fps, the brain’s "snapshots" align with the eye’s natural sampling.  
  - At 60 fps, the brain perceives each snapshot as distinct, leading to smoother motion but no added "reality."

### 6. **Can the Eye See *Everything*?**  
- **No.** The eye can detect:  
  - Motion up to ~60-70 Hz (flicker fusion threshold).  
  - Extremely fast events (e.g., a bullet’s trajectory) as blur.  
- **The brain fills gaps** in motion that the eye can’t resolve.
