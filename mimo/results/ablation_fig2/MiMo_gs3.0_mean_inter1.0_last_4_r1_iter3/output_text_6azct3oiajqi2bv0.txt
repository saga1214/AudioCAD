For attaching things to a jacket lining without damage, here are the safest methods:  

### 🛠️ Best Options:
1. **Fabric Glue** (e.g., Aleene's Fabric Fusion):  
   - Apply a small dot of glue to the tag/label, press firmly onto the lining, and hold for 30 seconds.  
   - **Pros:** No heat, no residue, works on most fabrics.  
   - **Cons:** Requires drying time (check glue instructions).  

2. **Small Safety Pins** (for temporary fixes):  
   - Insert pins through the lining and body fabric to hold the tag in place.  
   - **Pros:** Instant, no heat, invisible from outside.  
   - **Cons:** Not permanent; avoid sliding pins on delicate fabrics.  

### ⚠️ Methods to AVOID:  
- **Ironing/Steam:** Heat can melt synthetic linings (polyester, nylon) or warp tags.  
- **Double-Sided Tape:** May leave residue on the lining and damage fabric over time.  

### 🔥 If You *Must* Use Heat (e.g., Sewing Machine):  
- **Low-Heat Ironing:** Place a thin cloth between the tag and lining. Press *briefly* (1–2 seconds) to flatten the tag.  
- **Sewing:** Use a **hand-sewing needle** with a **synthetic thread** (e.g., polyester) to stitch through the lining only.  
  - **Thread Tip:** Cut thread into small pieces to avoid tangling.  
  - **Stitching:** Use a simple running stitch on the inside of the lining.  

### 💡 Pro Tips:  
- **Remove Tags First:** If possible, detach the tag entirely (sew a patch or use fabric glue instead).  
- **Test First:** Always test glue/heat on an inconspicuous area (like the hem).  
- **Arden Fair Stores:** REI’s Co-op brand or H&M (for affordable basics) carry mid-range jackets with removable tags.
