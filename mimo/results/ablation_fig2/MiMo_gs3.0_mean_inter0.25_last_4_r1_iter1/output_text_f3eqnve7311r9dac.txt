That's a very astute point. Older comics are generally much rarer and have a higher potential for significant growth because of scarcity. Investing in pre-1990s comics, especially from the Golden and Silver Ages, is a major trend.

Here are ten highly sought-after comics from the 1940s and 50s that command serious money at auction. I'll describe what they are and an example of what they recently sold for.

First, the ultimate comic book: *Action Comics* number one, Superman's first full appearance, recently sold for six million dollars as we mentioned.

Second, *Amazing Fantasy* number fifteen by Stan Lee and Steve Ditko: Superman first here too, but Ditko did the art. One recently sold for three point six million.

Third, *Captain America Comics* number one from 1941, Jack Kirby's first work for Marvel, sold above three million in 2022.

Fourth, *Tales to Astonishment* number 4, which introduced the first full appearance of the Cosmic Horror genre. A copy sold for just over one and a half million.

Fifth, *The Silver Surfer* number one, the first appearance of the Surfer, sold in 2024 for over a million dollars.

Sixth, *Fantastic Four* number one, Jack Kirby's first work for Marvel and the start of the Silver Age, sold in 2021 for over a million.

Seventh, *The Incredible Hulk* number one, Stan Lee and Jack Kirby's first Hulk story, sold in 2021 for one point two million.

Eighth, *Ghost Rider* number one, the first appearance of the character, sold in 2022 for over a million.

Ninth, *The Amazing Spider-Man* three hundred, the first full appearance of Venom, sold in 2021 for over one point four million.

And finally, *Amazing Spider-Man* two fifty-two, the first appearance of the Symbiote suit, which is the origin of Venom. A copy of that sold for over a million dollars in 2022.

All of these are incredibly rare and highly sought-after by collectors.
