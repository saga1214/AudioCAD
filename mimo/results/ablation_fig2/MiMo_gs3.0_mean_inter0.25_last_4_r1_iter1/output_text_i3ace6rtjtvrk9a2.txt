The saccade is a jump from one focal point to another. During that jump, the brain temporarily suppresses visual processing to prevent you from seeing blur, so there’s a tiny, almost imperceptible gap in the visual stream.

However, the brain fills in this gap with visual memory and context. So, you don’t consciously notice the gap. This is what makes it feel like you’re seeing continuous motion.

So, while the eye is technically processing in short bursts, the brain integrates that information so you perceive a continuous visual experience. It’s a bit like how a film camera records at 24 frames per second: each frame is separate, but your brain blends them into a smooth motion.
