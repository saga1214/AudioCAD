Okay, I can definitely give you a clear, step-by-step breakdown of a traditional resume format. Here's how you can put it all together.

First, at the very top, is your name in about a fourteen-point font. To its right—without icons—place your email, phone number with the city, expected graduation date if you're a student, and any relevant links like your LinkedIn or portfolio. Using nine-point font for this secondary information can create a nice visual contrast.

Right below your name goes a short Professional Summary. This is just two or three sentences that highlight your most relevant skills and career goals.

Next is your Experience section. Here you'll list your jobs, starting with the most recent one at the top. For each job, write your job title, company name, and the start and end dates. Then, add two or three bullet points that briefly describe your key responsibilities and achievements.

After Experience, you'll have a Skills section. This is a great place to list your key skills, maybe grouped into categories like Technical Skills or Soft Skills. Tailor this list to match the job description you're applying for.

Then comes your Education section. List your degrees, the school's name, your expected graduation date, and any honors you received, like 'Highest Honors'.

You can also have optional sections at the end. A Certifications section is good for any important professional certifications. An Additional Information section can be used for things like languages you speak or relevant volunteer work. And if you have a long work history, you can use the 'Additional Work History' section we talked about to list less relevant roles very concisely.

Just remember to keep it clean, consistent with your font and spacing, and always tailor it for each job you apply for. Would you like me to put together an example that combines all of these parts?
