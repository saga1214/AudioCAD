That's a really interesting counterfactual to think about. The technological advances in shipbuilding and navigation were definitely the core enablers. And the intense national rivalries and the drive for wealth and religion were powerful ongoing forces. However, the Ottoman expansion, especially the fall of Constantinople, acted as a major catalyst and a key trigger. It created a specific, urgent problem that pushed explorers like the Portuguese to act faster and with greater urgency. So, while the groundwork was being laid, that specific geopolitical event dramatically accelerated the timeline and focused the efforts. It was a crucial catalyst.
