First, three established players you might have heard of:

1.  PU Leather: dates back to the **1960s**.
Intro: The long-standing plastic-based staple, offering a vast array of colors and textures at an affordable price, forming the backbone of many mass-market vegan leather goods but raising sustainability concerns.
Market Date Started: 1960s.

2.  Piñatex: came onto the market around **2015**.
Intro: An innovative material crafted from the natural fibers found in the leaves left after harvesting pineapple crops, providing a unique texture and a sustainable way to utilize agricultural waste, backed by brands like H&M.

3.  Cactus Leather: developed from nopal cactus and introduced in **2019**.
Intro: A rapidly gaining premium alternative made by extracting the cactus pulp, resulting in a sturdy, flexible, and naturally water-resistant material popular with eco-conscious luxury and streetwear brands.
