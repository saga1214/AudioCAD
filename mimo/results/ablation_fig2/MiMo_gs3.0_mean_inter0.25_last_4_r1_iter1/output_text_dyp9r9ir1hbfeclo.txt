You're right to double-check that! While there are three *primary* bones, the clavicle is actually the fourth critical structure that **stabilizes** the shoulder for rotation. Here’s a clearer breakdown:

1.  **Humerus:** Upper arm bone (head rotates within scapula).
2.  **Scapula (Shoulder Blade):** Houses the socket for the humeral head.
3.  **Clavicle (Collarbone):** Connects the arm (humerus) to the main torso skeleton (sternum & ribs). It provides **stability** to the shoulder joint.
4. **Rotator Cuff Muscles:** (Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres Minor, Subscapularis) - The fourth major group is these muscles, which *control* the humeral head's position and movement within the scapula.

**So, working *together* for rotation includes:**

*   The **bones**: Humerus, Scapula, Clavicle (providing structure and stability).
*   The **soft tissues**: Rotator Cuff Muscles (providing active movement).

**The Clavicle's Role:** While rotation itself primarily happens at the humerus-scapula joint, the clavicle is essential for overall shoulder stability, especially during movements that involve lifting the arm away from the body or rotating it externally. Without the clavicle's connection, the shoulder joint would be much less stable during rotation.

**Regarding your friend's soreness:**

*   **Soreness vs. Pain:** Mild soreness during/after *any* new exercise (like lateral raises) is often just muscle fatigue (Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness - DOMS) and is normal.
*   **Rotator Cuff Concerns:** Sharp, localized pain, especially during specific movements (like overhead raising), or pain that radiates down the arm, *could* indicate rotator cuff irritation or strain, particularly if it's persistent or worsens.
*   **Advice:** Advise your friend to:
    1.  **Stop the exercise causing the pain.**
    2.  **Ice the shoulder** for 15-20 minutes.
    3.  **Modify form** if soreness is mild (e.g., use lighter weight, reduce range).
    4.  **If pain persists, worsens, or doesn't improve after 3-5 days, they should see a doctor or physical therapist** to rule out injury and get proper guidance.

Rotator cuff issues are common in overhead athletes and lifters, but proper form and gradual progression are key! Study hard for that med exam!
