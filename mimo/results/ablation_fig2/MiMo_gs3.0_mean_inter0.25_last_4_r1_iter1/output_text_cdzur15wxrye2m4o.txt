The steps for an oil change on a car draining from the top are the same as draining from the bottom, but the *method* matters. Since you're using ramps for your own car, here's a clear, safe process:

1.  **Prepare the Ramps & Surface:** Ensure the driveway is **completely level**. Place the ramps securely on solid ground. *Never* use a slope – it's extremely dangerous.
2.  **Lift Safely:** Use a jack to lift the car just enough so the front wheels clear the ramps. Do NOT rely solely on the jack for support – place jack stands under the vehicle's frame rails *immediately* for safety.
3.  **Drain the Old Oil:** Place the oil pan under the engine. Remove the drain plug and let all the old oil drain out. Wipe the plug clean.
4.  **Replace the Oil Filter:** Lubricate the new filter's rubber gasket with a little new oil. Screw it onto the engine's oil filter housing by hand until snug. You may need a wrench, but be careful not to overtighten.
5.  **Add New Oil:** Pour the correct amount and type of new oil through the fill hole (usually on top of the engine) into the oil pan. Start with half the amount, then check the dipstick after a minute or two to ensure it's at the correct level.
6.  **Lower & Dispose:** Carefully lower the car off the ramps using the jack and jack stands. Remove the drain plug. Collect the used oil in a sealed container for proper disposal at an auto parts store.

**Critical Safety Note:** The level surface requirement (Step 1) is non-negotiable. A tiny slope can cause a car to roll, leading to serious injury or death. If the driveway slope is even slightly significant, do *not* use the ramps there.
