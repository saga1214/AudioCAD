That's an excellent question—and based on current scientific consensus, **ultra-processed foods (UPFs) are arguably the broadest category of foods most detrimental to health when consumed in excess**. Here's why:

### Key Reasons UPFs Stand Out:
1. **Toxic Combinations**:  
   UPFs often contain multiple harmful ingredients at once:  
   - **Added sugars** (linked to diabetes, heart disease, obesity).  
   - **Unhealthy fats** (trans fats, saturated fats).  
   - **Excess sodium** (raising blood pressure).  
   - **Artificial additives** (colors, preservatives, emulsifiers).  
   - **Low fiber/nutrients** (empty calories, no vitamins/minerals).

2. **Engineered Overconsumption**:  
   UPFs are designed to be hyper-palatable—making it easy to eat far more than your body needs. This drives weight gain and metabolic disorders.

3. **Chronic Disease Links**:  
   - **Obesity**: UPFs displace whole foods in diets, leading to calorie overload.  
   - **Type 2 Diabetes**: High sugar/fat combinations disrupt insulin sensitivity.  
   - **Heart Disease**: Sodium and unhealthy fats contribute to atherosclerosis.  
   - **Inflammation**: Additives and excess sugar trigger systemic inflammation.

4. **Global Impact**:  
   Unlike artificial trans fats (now banned in many countries), UPFs are embedded in modern diets—from packaged snacks to fast food—making them a pervasive public health threat.

### Context Matters:
- **Trans fats** remain uniquely harmful (raising "bad" cholesterol while lowering "good" cholesterol), but they're less common today.  
- **Sugar/salt** are major culprits but are often *components* of UPFs.  
- **Processed meats** (like bacon, hot dogs) are also high-risk due to nitrates and saturated fats.

### Bottom Line:
While foods like trans fats or sugary drinks are exceptionally harmful individually, **ultra-processed foods represent the most widespread, multi-faceted risk**. Reducing UPF intake—even by 10-20%—can significantly improve health outcomes. Your class is studying a critical topic: whole, minimally processed foods (vegetables, legumes, whole grains) remain the foundation of long-term well-being.
