Now, let's break down the two interpretations. According to older, more common interpretations of the Laws of the Game, an offside player can only reset the situation if they make a deliberate, controlled play on the ball. In our scenario, the keeper's pass went directly to midfielder number ten, who was clearly offside. Since midfielder number ten simply received the ball directly without making any deliberate play on it, the offside offense still applies to the forward, number seven, who scored. That would be an offside offense.

However, college certification exams almost always follow the吕萨规则, which is the Laws of the Game from FIFA. Under吕萨规则, a defender's touch on the ball automatically resets the offside situation regardless. So, in the same scenario, as soon as the keeper touches the ball, forward number seven is no longer considered offside, and the subsequent play is legal. No offense.
