Let's look at a scenario that highlights a key difference based on which soccer rulebook you're using. In college and high school US certifications, they teach you to follow either the Laws of the Game from the International Football Association Board, or FIFA's Laws. These two rulebooks have a slight difference in how they define deliberate deflections resetting offside.

Here’s the scenario: The Red Team is attacking the Blue Team's goal. Defender number three is in an offside position. The Blue Team's goalkeeper kicks the ball forward. The ball takes a clear, controlled deflection off Defender three's chest before going to Player A from the Red Team, who then shoots and scores.

Now, here's the difference:

According to the International Laws, Defender three has made a 'deliberate play on the ball.' Defender three now controls the situation, Player A is no longer offside, and Defender three should have played the ball back to their goalkeeper.

However, FIFA's Laws use the word 'touch.' They say that any touch, whether deliberate control or an involuntary rebound, does not reset the situation. So, in this case, Player A would still be in an offside position and the goal would be disallowed.

For your college ref certification specifically, you absolutely must follow the International Laws. So in this situation, Defender three's touch deliberately resets the offside situation, and Player A is not offside.
