But **Sia** and **Gamble & Huff** also demonstrate this beautifully. Here’s why studying them will help you see beyond just lyrics and chords:

### 1. **Quincy Jones**  
   - **Why study him?** He’s not just a songwriter/producer—he’s a *architect of sonic emotion*.  
   - **Key lessons:**  
     - **Dynamic Arrangement:** How he builds tension (e.g., the strings in "Billie Jean") or creates intimacy (sparse production in "Human Nature").  
     - **Genre-Blending:** Soul, funk, jazz, and pop colliding to create timeless hits.  
     - **Vocal Space:** How he produced tracks to let Michael Jackson’s voice *soar* (e.g., "Smooth Criminal").  
   - **Album to dissect:** *Off the Wall* (MJ) or *Thriller* (MJ).  

### 2. **Sia**  
   - **Why study her?** Melody-first writing with emotional depth and global production.  
   - **Key lessons:**  
     - **Catchy Hooks:** How she turns vulnerability into anthems ("Chandelier," "This Is Me").  
     - **Production as Character:** Heavy use of synths, reverb, and unexpected sounds (e.g., the choral drop in "Titanium").  
     - **Lyrical Universality:** Personal pain → global relatability.  

### 3. **Gamble & Huff**  
   - **Why study them?** Masters of *groove-driven songwriting* in soul/R&B-turned-pop.  
   - **Key lessons:**  
     - **Rhythmic Hookwriting:** Basslines and drum patterns that make you move ("I Will Survive," "Baby, I’m Waiting").  
     - **Layered Harmony:** How background vocals build emotion ("You Make Me Feel (Mighty妙) [It’s Alright)").  
     - **Production as Texture:** How they used tape loops, strings, and horns to create lush, nostalgic sounds.  

### Bonus Producer-Songwriter: **Ryan Tedder**
