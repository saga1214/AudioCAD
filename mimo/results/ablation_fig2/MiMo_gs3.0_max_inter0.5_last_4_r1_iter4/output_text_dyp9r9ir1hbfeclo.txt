Great question! Let's clarify the fourth component working with the other bones for rotation in the shoulder:

1.  **Humerus:** Upper arm bone (head fits into the socket).
2.  Sc**apula:** Shoulder blade (holds the socket).
3.  **Clavicle:** Collarbone (connects the scapula to the sternum, stabilizing the shoulder).

**The fourth, crucial component isn't a bone, but a group of muscles and tendons: the Rotator Cuff.** As mentioned earlier, this consists of four muscles/tendons:
*   Supraspinatus
*   Infraspinatus
*   Teres Minor
*   Subscapularis

**What it does when it comes to rotation:**

*   The humerus and scapula form the **glenohumeral joint** (ball-and-socket where the humeral head fits into the scapula's socket). This allows most of the wide rotation.
*   The clavicle connects the scapula to the rest of the skeleton, providing overall stability.
*   **The Rotator Cuff muscles and tendons surround the glenohumeral joint.** Their primary job is to:
    *   **Stabilize** the humeral head firmly within the shallow socket of the scapula.
    *   **Guide** the humerus during all movements (especially rotation), preventing it from dislocating.
    *   **Provide the muscle power** needed for rotation (internal and external rotation).

**So, working TOGETHER for rotation:** The humerus (moving part), scapula (socket), and clavicle (stabilizer) physically interact at the glenohumerval joint. **The rotator cuff muscles/tendons are essential partners that make this stable and functional rotation possible.** They are the fourth key component enabling smooth, controlled shoulder rotation.
