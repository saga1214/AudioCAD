Okay, let's break this down into a clear, step-by-step format you can follow. Here's a complete template you can adapt.

First is your name, large 14-point font. To the right of that, aligned with your email, phone number with icons in nine-point font, and finally your city. Icons might cause ATS problems as we discussed, so you might want to skip those. Also, definitely add your LinkedIn profile link right here.

Next, a short and punchy Professional Summary. This is where you'll highlight your most relevant skills and career goals.

After that, the Contact Information section. This should include your email, phone, city, LinkedIn URL, and any other relevant links like your portfolio.

Then comes the Experience section, divided into two parts. The first part is 'Relevant Experience,' where you list only the jobs that directly relate to the new roles you're targeting. Keep it brief with just the company, title, dates, and a single line to describe the role. The second part is 'Additional Work History,' where you list all your other jobs, again just with the company, title, dates, and a very brief description. This neatly handles long work histories or career changes.

After Experience, you have the Education section. Here you list your degrees, the school name, and any honors or awards you received for that degree.

Following that is the Skills section. This is very important. List your key skills, separating them into categories like Technical Skills, Soft Skills, and Languages, if you have any.

Finally, you can have an optional section for Awards and Certifications, where you list things like scholarships or professional certifications.

This structure works well because it prioritizes the information recruiters want to see first, it helps you strategically highlight your most relevant experience, and it's flexible enough for you to tailor it for different jobs. Just remember to keep it to one or two pages maximum and focus on relevance.
