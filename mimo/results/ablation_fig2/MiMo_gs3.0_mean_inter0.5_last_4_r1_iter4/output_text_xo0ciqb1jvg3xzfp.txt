Yes, for both groups, it was a time of survival and gratitude, but for very different reasons. For the Pilgrims, it was relief after barely surviving their first winter. For the Wam-panoag, it was a strategic moment of cooperation, recognizing the settlers as potential allies.

However, the traditional Thanksgiving story often focuses only on the Pilgrims' gratitude and completely ignores the Wampanoag people's role, the broader history of colonization, and the suffering that followed.

The Wampanoag perspective is essential to tell the full story. So, while it was a moment of cooperation, it's inaccurate to call it a 'happy occasion' for both sides, as the long-term consequences for the Wampanoag were devastating.
