For modern-day art museum security, the **most fundamental and foolproof first line of defense is the physical barrier of the building itself**—specifically, **reinforced entry points and automated access control systems**.  

This includes:  
1. **Automated Gateways**: Turnstiles, biometric scanners, or pressure-sensitive floors that prevent unauthorized entry without human intervention.  
2. **Reinforced Materials**: Blast-resistant glass, steel doors, and anti-ram barriers at all entrances to deter forced entry.  
3. **24/7 Surveillance Integration**: Cameras and sensors linked to central control systems that *immediately* alert security if entry is compromised.  

### Why this is the *true* first line:  
- **Human error is inevitable** (as seen in the Gardner heist, where guards were deceived). Technology at entry points eliminates reliance on personnel.  
- **Speed matters**: Automated systems provide instant response, buying time for human defenders to react.  
- **Layered deterrence**: Modern materials and sensors make forced entry extremely difficult even for determined attackers.  

After this physical layer, secondary defenses (staff patrols, alarms, secure displays) engage. But the building’s architecture and automated barriers are the **ultimate first line**—they stop threats at the threshold before they reach the art.
