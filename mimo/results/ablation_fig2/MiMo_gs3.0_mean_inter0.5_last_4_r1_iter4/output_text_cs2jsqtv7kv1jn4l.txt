**Regarding Allergies:**

1.  **Cilantro Sensitivity:** This is almost certainly **not an allergy**. It's a **genetic taste sensitivity** where some people metabolize a compound in cilantro into a soap-like substance. She can "taste it on everything" even if it's not visible – she's just tasting that compound. No freak out needed! Monitor for *actual* allergy symptoms.
2.  Nut Allergies & When They Start/Stop:
    *   **Childhood:** Very common to develop new food allergies (including nuts) during the first few years of life.
    *   **Older Childhood/Adulthood:** People absolutely **can and do develop new allergies to things (including nuts, shellfish, eggs, etc.) at any age**, including adulthood. This is sometimes called "adult-onset allergy."
3.  How Often to Test?
    *   You typically **do not need to proactively test** for allergies.
    *   Testing is done **when specific symptoms arise**. If you ever notice your daughter having reactions like hives (especially around the face/swelling), difficulty breathing, stomach problems (like severe vomiting/diarrhea) immediately following a food, after she eats, that's when consult her doctor.
    *   The doctor may then recommend patch testing (skin prick test), blood tests (like IgE tests), or oral food challenges to identify the specific allergen.
    *   Focus on observation for symptoms, not regular testing. Keep a basic food diary if you start to suspect something.

The key is vigilance for *actual allergy symptoms* (not just dislike of taste) and open communication with her doctor if symptoms occur.
