Of course. Here is a clear, traditional format that's very recruiter-friendly. I'll walk you through each section.

First, at the very top is your name in about a fourteen-point font.

Right below that is your main section for contact information. Instead of to the side, you'll have just your name. Then on the next line, starting flush with the left margin, you'll put your email address. The next line will be your phone number. Followed by your city on the next line. You could even add icons again here since contact info issues mainly apply to automated *experience scanning*, not contact details. The font can stay nine-point if you like. A pro-tip is to add your LinkedIn URL here as well.

Next, a short two-to-four sentence "Professional Summary." This is where you'll quickly recap your most relevant experience and state your career goals.

After the summary, comes the main "Work Experience" section. Here, you'll list each job you've had, in reverse chronological order. For each job, include the company name, your job title, the city and state, and the dates you worked there, for example, from June 2010 to May 2015. Under each job, add two to four bullet points that highlight your key accomplishments and skills using strong action verbs.

Then, a "Skills" section. List your most relevant hard and soft skills, like programming languages, software, or leadership abilities. It's helpful to group them into categories like 'Technical Skills' and 'Soft Skills'.

Following that is "Education." List each degree you've earned, the school's name, and the year. You can add your honors or awards right next to the degree.

If you have a lot of older, relevant experience, you can create a "Relevant Experience" section. This would follow the "Work Experience" section and would be where you list those jobs, just like in the main experience section.

An optional "Additional Work History" section can go after that, for jobs that aren't as relevant.

Finally, you can add a "Certifications" section for any relevant professional certifications, and a "Projects" section if you have any that demonstrate your skills.

This structure is clean, easy to scan, and strategically presents your experience to make a great first impression.
