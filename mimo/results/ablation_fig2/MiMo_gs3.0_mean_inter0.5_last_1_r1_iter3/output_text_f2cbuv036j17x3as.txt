Okay, diesel camper vans are workhorses, but they definitely need some TLC before a long trip like this. First off, brakes. A good way to check is to see if the van pulls to one side when you brake lightly—this can indicate uneven wear. Also, listen for any grinding or squealing noises when you stop. If the brake fluid looks dark or smells burnt, that’s a sign it’s time for a change. Now, for spark plugs—those are inside the diesel engine, which is a bit different from a gasoline engine. Diesel engines don’t use spark plugs, they use glow plugs to help ignite the fuel-air mixture when it’s cold. So, you’d look for those instead. You’d typically check the condition of the glow plugs, maybe clean or replace them if they’re corroded or have a high resistance. Diesel engines are pretty robust, but they do require regular maintenance. It might be a good idea to have a trusted mechanic do a quick inspection before you go, just to be safe. And of course, top up all your fluids—diesel, brake fluid, coolant, and oil—and check your tires for good tread depth and proper pressure. A well-maintained van will give you a much smoother and safer trip.
