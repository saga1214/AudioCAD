You're right to double-check that! Let's clarify the initial answer:

The three primary bones working together **directly for rotation** at the shoulder are:
1.  **Humerus** (Upper Arm Bone)
2.  **Scapula** (Shoulder Blade) - socket location
3.  **Clavicle** (Collarbone) - girdle stabilization

**The Fourth Component - The Ball-and-Socket Joint:**

While the clavicle stabilizes the shoulder girdle, the *primary mechanism* for rotation and movement is the **ball-and-socket joint** formed by:
*   The **head of the humerus** (the "ball") articulating with the **glenoid cavity** (a shallow socket on the scapula).

**This joint allows circular (circular) rotation.** The clavicle's main role here is to position the scapula correctly and stabilize the shoulder girdle, enabling the humerus to move freely within the socket.

**Summary:**

*   **For Rotation:** Humerus + Scapula (via the ball-and-socket joint).
*   **Stabilizing the Girdle:** Clavicle works with the scapula.
*   **Rotator Cuff:** The four muscles/tendons (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) stabilize the humeral head in the socket and assist with rotation/lifting.

So, the clavicle is the fourth bone involved in the overall shoulder complex, but the direct rotation mechanism is between the humerus and the scapula.
