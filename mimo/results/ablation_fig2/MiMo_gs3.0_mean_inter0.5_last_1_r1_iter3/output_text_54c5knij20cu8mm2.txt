Okay, let's break this down.

The right-brain versus left-brain idea is mostly a myth. The brain doesn't really separate creative and logical functions that way. Both sides work together. However, the brain does have different areas that specialize in different tasks, like language or spatial reasoning. But creativity and logic aren't strictly divided; they're more like a spectrum.

For your writing transposition issue, it’s often motor-based dysgraphia or just sequencing under pressure. It doesn’t mean spelling knowledge is the problem.

Tinnitus mostly comes from physiological issues, so focus on hearing protection and stress management if stress makes it worse.

Now, for balancing concrete measurement with creativity, here are some ideas:

First, Information Design or Data Visualization. This uses data to create visually appealing and clear representations, great for your structured and measurable side, with a creative flow.

Second, Architecture or Industrial Design. Building structured, solid forms that serve a purpose. It’s creative, objective, and tangible.

Third, Programming or Game Development. You can be creative with code, but it requires exact, measurable logic and problem-solving.

Fourth, Technical Illustration. Combining precise technical knowledge with artistic expression.

And fifth, Robotics or Engineering with an arts integration. Combining hard science with creative applications.
