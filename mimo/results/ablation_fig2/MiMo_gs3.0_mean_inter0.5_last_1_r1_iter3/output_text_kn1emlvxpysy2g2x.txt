Of course. Here’s a summary of the wines we've discussed and their ideal storage and serving temperatures.

First, high-end Pinot Noir: It should be stored at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. It's served slightly below room temp, so aim for 55 to 60 degrees Fahrenheit, or 13 to 16 Celsius.

Next is Japanese Hon-Mirin. It should be stored in a cool place at about 60 to 68 degrees Fahrenheit, or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you sip it directly instead of using it for cooking, room temperature is fine, around 68 to 75 degrees Fahrenheit, or 20 to 24 Celsius.

Then we have Italian wines. For Chianti, a red wine, you store it at about 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius and serve it below room temp, between 60 and 65 Fahrenheit or 16 to 18 Celsius. For Pinot Grigio, a white wine, you store it at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, or 10 to 13 Celsius, and serve it chilled, around 45 to 50 Fahrenheit or 7 to 10 Celsius.

Finally, for the best wines for temperature extremes. For cold weather, a full-bodied red like a Cabernet Sauvignon is great, served at about 60 to 65 degrees Fahrenheit, or 16 to 18 Celsius. For hot weather, a crisp white like a Sauvignon Blanc is ideal, served chilled at about 45 to 50 degrees Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius.

To quickly compare them: The Pinot Noir is best for cool to warm weather. Hon-Mirin is unique since it's not sipped alone, but room temp works if you must. Chianti is good for moderate weather. Pinot Grigio is perfect for hot weather. Hon-Mirin can also be a good choice for hot weather since it's light. And a Cabernet Sauvignon is the best choice for cold weather.
