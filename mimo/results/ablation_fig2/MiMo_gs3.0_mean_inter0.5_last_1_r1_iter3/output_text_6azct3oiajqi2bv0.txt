Here's how to attach Velcro securely without damaging fabric or lining, with methods ranked by reliability:

**Best Method: Sew-On Velcro (Most Secure & Damage-Free)**
1.  **Choose the Right Velcro:** Use **flat sew-on strips** (e.g., 1" x 1") instead of hook-and-loop patches. They're less bulky and easier to sew.
2.  **Prep the Fabric:**
    *   Mark the placement on the *outer* fabric with chalk or fabric marker.
    *   Pin the strip in place.
3.  **Sew Securely:**
    *   Use a **straight stitch** on a **medium or slightly long stitch length** (2.5mm-3mm).
    *   Sew **along the edge of the strip**, staying close to it but *not* directly on top of the Velcro loop itself (this prevents pulling through the fabric).
    *   Sew **across the top and bottom** of the strip to anchor it firmly.
    *   **Key:** Use a **heavy-duty or denim presser foot** if possible, as it spreads the pressure more evenly than a standard foot.
4.  **Trim Excess:** Carefully snip the excess fabric threads and Velcro loop ends close to the stitching.

**Alternative Methods (Use with Caution):**

*   **Adhesive Velcro Strips (Temporary/Fragile):**
    *   Use **heavy-duty fabric glue** or **double-sided tape** designed for fabrics (like E6000 or Aleene's Tacky Glue). Apply sparingly, press firmly, and let cure fully. **High risk of damaging cheap fabrics over time.**
    *   **Test First:** Always test adhesives on an inconspicuous seam or hem.

*   **Velcro Patches with Strong Adhesive Backing:**
    *   These are bulkier than sew-on strips but designed to stick to fabric.
    *   Press firmly for several minutes. **Still risks damaging cheap fabric over time.**

**Critical Tips:**

*   **Fabric Matters:** REI Co-op pieces are generally built with more durable fabric and seams, making them more forgiving for sewing. Avoid Velcro on very thin or fragile fabrics.
*   **Placement:** Attach Velcro to existing seams, pockets, or areas where the fabric is thickest for maximum strength and to minimize stress on the lining.
*   **Stitching is Key:** If you sew, **always sew along the edge of the Velcro strip, not directly on the fabric**, and use a strong needle (like a 90/14 or 100/60) and appropriate thread (all-purpose or polyester).
*   **Avoid the Lining:** Never attach Velcro directly to the lining, especially if it's thin or stretchy. Always attach it to the *outer* fabric and anchor it securely to the *main body* of the jacket.

**Recommendation:** For a jacket you care about, the **sew-on flat strip method** is definitely the safest and most reliable way to add functional Velcro without ruining it. If sewing isn't an option, be prepared that any adhesive method will likely damage the fabric eventually on a garment that's already described as defective or torn.
