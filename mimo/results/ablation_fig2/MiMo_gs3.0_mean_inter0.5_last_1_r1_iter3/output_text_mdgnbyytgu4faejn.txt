Okay, let's put it all together. Here is a clear, step-by-step guide for a traditional and effective resume format.

First, at the very top, center your name in about a fourteen-point font. To its right, aligned under your name, create your contact header. This can include icons along with your email, phone number, city, and expected graduation date if you have one all using nine-point fonts. Adding "Open to Work in your location" to this header is a great touch. And like we discussed, it's better to place this contact section directly below your name vertically.

Next is your professional summary. This is a short, powerful paragraph at the top that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals.

After that, a standard section for your education. Here, list your degrees, expected graduation dates, and any honors or awards you received for that degree.

Then comes the experience section. This is the most important part. You'll want to list your most relevant jobs first, even if they are older. For each job, write down the company name, your title, the dates you worked there, and then one or two bullet points that briefly describe your key achievements.

If you have a lot of experience you want to include but it's not the main focus, you can add an "Additional Work History" section. For these roles, just list the job title, company, and dates, without the detailed bullet points.

Now, about the skills section: it's highly recommended. You should list your key skills, maybe separated into categories like technical skills or soft skills. Tailor this list to the specific job description you're applying for.

Finally, you can add a section for other relevant information, like certifications, languages you speak, or volunteer work. Just make sure to include only things that add value to your resume.

This structure should give you a clear and professional resume that effectively showcases your career change.
