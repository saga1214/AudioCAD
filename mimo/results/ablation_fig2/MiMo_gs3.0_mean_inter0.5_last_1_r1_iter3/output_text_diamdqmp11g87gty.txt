You're in for a treat—winter in Wisconsin is perfect for outdoor adventures! Here are some great DIY-friendly winter activities beyond ice fishing and mudroom projects:

### 🌲 **Maple Syrup Tapping**  
- **Tools Needed**: Drill (1¼-inch bit), spile (metal spout), bucket, tubing.  
- **How To**: Tap sugar maples in late winter (when nights freeze but days stay above 0°C/32°F). Drill a shallow hole, insert the spile upward, and collect sap. Boil it down (1:10 sap-to-syrup ratio) for delicious homemade syrup!  

### 🛷 **Snowshoeing**  
- **Gear**: Waterproof boots, snowshoes (rent or DIY with wooden frames and webbing), poles.  
- **Tips**: Start on groomed trails, layer clothing for warmth, and explore local parks or cross-country ski trails.  

### 🚲 **Fat Biking**  
- Attach wide tires to your bike for deep snow adventures. Look for fat-bike-friendly trails or frozen river paths.  

### 🛷 **Snowmobiling**  
- Rent a snowmobile or join guided tours in state parks (like Kettle Moraine).  

### ❄️ **Winter Hiking & Ice Skating**  
- Pack a thermos for hot drinks, wear waterproof boots, and check trail conditions.  
- Find frozen lakes for ice skating (always test ice thickness first!).  

### 🎨 **Snow Sculpting**  
- Get creative with shovels and brushes—great for family fun!  

### ⛷️ **Skiing/Snowboarding**  
- Check local resorts or cross-country ski trails for rentals and lessons.  

### Safety First:  
- **Layer Clothing**: Moisture-wicking base, insulating middle, waterproof outer.  
- **Foot Protection**: Insulated boots with traction (e.g., microspikes).  
- **Emergency Kit**: Include a whistle, gloves, and a compact shovel.  

Winter in Wisconsin is all about embracing the cold with warmth and adventure. Happy tapping, snowshoeing, and exploring! ❄️
