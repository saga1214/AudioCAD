That's an insightful question—and yes, **ultra-processed foods (UPFs)** are widely recognized as the most broadly detrimental category if consumed excessively. Here’s why:

### Key Reasons Ultra-Processed Foods Are Harmful:
1. **Nutrient Deficiency**:  
   They’re stripped of fiber, vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats while being packed with **added sugars, refined carbs, unhealthy fats, and sodium**. This displaces nutrient-dense whole foods (like vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins).

2. **Metabolic Harm**:  
   Excess sugar and unhealthy fats drive **obesity, type 2 diabetes, and fatty liver disease**. The "empty calories" in UPFs contribute to overeating without satisfaction.

3. **Blood Pressure & Heart Disease**:  
   High sodium and trans fats (in fried foods短语) raise blood pressure and LDL ("bad") cholesterol, increasing heart attack and stroke risks.

4. **Addictive Properties**:  
   UPFs are engineered to overstimulate dopamine receptors (e.g., hyper-sweetened sodas, salty snacks), making them hard to consume in moderation.

5. **Gut Health Disruption**:  
   Lack of fiber harms gut microbiota, linked to inflammation, obesity, and autoimmune conditions.

### Why Not Just One Food?  
While **sugar** (e.g., in sodas/baked goods) and **trans fats** are exceptionally harmful, UPFs represent a **comprehensive threat** because they combine multiple toxins (sugar + fat + salt + additives) in a single package. The World Heart Federation estimates that **ultra-processed foods contribute to 50%+ of daily calories in many countries**—far exceeding the damage of any single food.

### Practical Takeaway for Your Class:  
Focus on whole, minimally processed foods (fruits, veggies, legumes, whole grains). When in doubt, follow the **"if it has 20+ ingredients or you can’t recognize them, it’s likely UPF."** Moderation matters, but limiting UPFs is key for long-term health.
