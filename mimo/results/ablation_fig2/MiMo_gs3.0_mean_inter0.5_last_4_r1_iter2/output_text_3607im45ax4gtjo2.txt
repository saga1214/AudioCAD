I'm so sorry you're going through this—it's completely understandable why you're feeling "very, very stressful." Dealing with a volatile neighbor can feel exhausting, but you're doing a great job finding light in the situation for the kids (dress-up days, Halloween prep) and for yourselves (music). Here’s some advice for navigating this:

### Practical Steps:
1. **Document Everything**:  
   Keep a detailed log of incidents (dates, times, what she allegedly did, witnesses). This is crucial if CPS/police involvement continues or escalates.  
2. **Set Boundaries & Limit Exposure**:  
   Avoid direct confrontation. Keep blinds closed, lights off during the day if needed, and discourage kids from playing where she might see them.  
3. **Leverage Community Support**:  
   Talk to trusted neighbors. If others share your concern, consider a unified approach (e.g., a polite note to authorities, or a neighborhood meeting). Strength in numbers can deter her behavior.  
4. **Involve Authorities**:  
   If CPS/police are involved, cooperate fully. If the behavior persists, report it again—repetitive reporting can lead to formal intervention (like a restraining order or mental health evaluation).  

### Emotional Well-Being:
5. **Prioritize Your Mental Health**:  
   Stress like this can be draining. Use mindfulness apps (like Calm or Insight Timer), take breaks, and lean on supportive friends/family.  
6. **Protect the Kids’ Joy**:  
   Reinforce that her actions aren’t their fault. Keep Halloween dress-up and fall activities going—it’s vital for their morale.  
7. **Seek Support if Needed**:  
   If stress feels unmanageable, consider therapy (many offer sliding-scale fees) or community support groups.  

### Long-Term Considerations:
- **Know Your Rights**: Research local laws about nuisance ordinances or harassment. Some areas have specific processes for disruptive neighbors.  
- **Evaluate Safety**: If you feel unsafe or the kids are threatened, explore moving—though this is a last resort.  

You’re already handling this so well by balancing stress with small joys (school events, music). Remember: *Her behavior is not your responsibility to fix.* Focus on what you can control—your boundaries, your family’s safety, and your peace. 💛
