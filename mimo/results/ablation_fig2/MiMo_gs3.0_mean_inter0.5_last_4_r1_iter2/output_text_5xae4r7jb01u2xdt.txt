Let's look at a scenario that highlights a key difference between college and high school rules, specifically regarding throw-ins.

In this situation, the Red Team has just defended a corner kick and cleared the ball into their own throw-in area. A Red Team defender takes the throw-in. As the defender prepares, the Red Team forward, number nine, is level with the second-to-last defender. A Blue Team defender, number ten, is also in an offside position, but slightly behind the Red forward. The Red Team defender throws the ball directly to a teammate, number eleven, who is onside. The Red forward, number nine, then sprints ahead of number eleven and scores.

In the study for your college certification, you would call this an offside offense. The player who scored was in an equal offside position to the second-to-last defender and received the ball directly from the throw-in. That violates exception four of the college/high school rulebook.

However, for high school soccer, this would not be an offense. The high school rule, as you might find in your study help, is more liberal. It states a player cannot be offside when receiving the ball directly from a goal, throw, or corner. Since the Red forward received the throw-in directly, he was onside, and the subsequent play is legal.

So, the key difference is that the college and high school rules interpret the phrase "directly from" differently. College soccer is stricter, while high school is more lenient, and that's a crucial distinction to know for both certifications.
