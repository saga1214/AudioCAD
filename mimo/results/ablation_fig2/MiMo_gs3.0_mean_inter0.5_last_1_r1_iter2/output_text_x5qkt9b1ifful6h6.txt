Based on your biology class discussion and public health research, **ultra-processed foods are arguably the one type of food that would be the *worst for your health if eaten in excess***. Here’s why:

### Key Reasons:
1. **Cumulative Harm**:  
   Ultra-processed foods (like sugary drinks, packaged snacks, fast food, and frozen meals) often combine multiple harmful ingredients:  
   - **Added sugars** (linked to diabetes, obesity).  
   - **Unhealthy fats** (like trans fats or saturated fats).  
   - **Excessive sodium** (raising blood pressure).  
   - **Artificial additives** (colorings, preservatives).  
   They also lack fiber, vitamins, and minerals.

2. **Hyper-Palatability**:  
   They’re engineered to be addictive—high in sugar, fat, salt, and texture—leading to overconsumption without satiety.

3. **Nutritional Void**:  
   They displace whole foods (fruits, vegetables, lean proteins) in your diet, causing micronutrient deficiencies.

4. **Disease Links**:  
   Strongly associated with:  
   - Obesity  
   - Heart disease  
   - Type 2 diabetes  
   - Fatty liver disease  
   - Early mortality  

### Comparison to Other "Worst" Foods:
- **Artificial Trans Fats** (e.g., in fried foods shortening): Directly raise LDL ("bad") cholesterol and increase heart disease risk.  
- **Added Sugars** (e.g., soda, candy): Drive metabolic disorders like diabetes.  
- **High-Sodium Foods**: Contribute to hypertension.  

But ultra-processed foods *encompass* these risks while adding their own unique dangers. A 2022 Harvard study found that ultra-processed foods alone accounted for **50%+ of daily calories** in Western diets and were linked to a **14% higher risk of death**.

### Bottom Line:
While excess sugar or trans fats are highly damaging, **ultra-processed foods represent the worst overall category** due to their pervasive role in modern diets and their compounding health hazards. For your class, focusing on reducing ultra-processed food intake could be the most impactful health takeaway.
