Here's a safe way to tackle that sticking fabric without damaging your jacket lining, focusing on methods that minimize heat and pressure:

**The Safest & Most Recommended Method: The "Cold Iron" with a Pressing Cloth**

1.  **Clean the Stuck Area First:** Gently wipe the stuck section with a dry cloth to remove any dust, oils, or residue that might be causing friction.
2.  **Test Heat (CRUCIAL):** *Always* test your iron on an inconspicuous area first (like an inside seam). Set the iron to its **lowest possible heat setting** (usually Synthetics or Delicate). Hold it there for 10-15 seconds. If it feels too hot for the fabric or lining, switch to the *lowest setting* below that (like "No Heat" if your iron has it).
3.  **Use a Pressing Cloth:** This is non-negotiable for protecting the lining.
    *   Place a thin, clean cotton cloth (like an old T-shirt or handkerchief) over the stuck area.
    *   Dampen the pressing cloth *slightly* with water using a spray bottle. You want it damp, not soaking wet.
4.  **Apply Gentle Heat & Pressure:**
    *   Place the damp pressing cloth over the stuck spot.
    *   Gently place the *cold* iron face down onto the cloth.
    *   Apply **very light pressure**. You're using the warmth from the cloth, not the direct heat of the iron.
    *   Hold for 10-15 seconds. The friction and slight heat from the damp cloth usually release the fibers.
5.  **Repeat & Check:** If it's still stuck, repeat steps  wooden spoon method (see below) if the iron isn't working. *Do not use steam unless you know the lining can handle it.*

**Alternative Methods (Use with Caution):**

*   **The Wooden Spoon Method:**
    1.  Clean the area.
    2.  Place a thin, dry cotton cloth over the stuck spot.
    3.  Hold a clean wooden spoon (or similar smooth, heat-resistant utensil) against the cloth.
    4.  Place the cold iron face down onto the spoon.
    5.  Apply **very light pressure** and gently rub the iron over the spot. The friction should release the fibers without heat. *Avoid this if the fabric feels stiff or synthetic.*
*   **Hand Steaming (Best for Minor Creases/Residue):**
    1.  Fill a kettle with water.
    2.  Hold the kettle *just* below the stuck area (don't pour!).
    3.  Use a clean white cloth as a barrier between the kettle and the jacket.
    4.  Gently pour the steaming water over the cloth, letting the steam permeate the fabric. The moisture often softens and releases fibers. Let it sit for a few minutes before gently trying to pull the fabric free. Repeat as needed.

**Key Tips to Protect Your Lining:**

*   **Lowest Heat Possible:** Never use the "Cotton" or "Wool" setting directly on the lining. Synthetics, down, or fleece linings are especially vulnerable.
*   **NO Direct Heat:** Never place the hot iron *directly* on the fabric or lining. Always use a pressing cloth or spoon.
*   **Avoid Steam Unless Necessary:** Steam can damage synthetics, cause water spots, or make linings shrink. Use the cloth/barrier method with steam only if you know the lining is safe.
*   **Gentle Pressure:** You're trying to release fibers, not flatten them. Use minimal force.
*   **Check the Label:** If your jacket has a care label, follow its specific instructions. It might say "Do Not Iron" or specify a heat setting.
*   **Patience:** Sometimes letting the area steam or sit damp for a few minutes before gently trying to pull it free works better than immediate heat.
