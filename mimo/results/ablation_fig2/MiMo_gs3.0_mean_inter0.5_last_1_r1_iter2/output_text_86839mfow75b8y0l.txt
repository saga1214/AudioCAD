You're absolutely right to zoom in on that point—**production isn't separate from songwriting in pop**; it's the invisible hand that amplifies emotion, energy, and dynamics. Here’s who to study specifically for that blend of **songwriting + production mastery**:

### 1. **Quincy Jones**  
   - **Why?** His work with Michael Jackson (e.g., *Off the Wall*, *Thriller*) redefined pop as an *audio experience*.  
   - **Key Takeaway:** Study how he used arrangement, instrumentation, and sonic space (like the haunting silence before "Billie Jean’s" bass drop) to build tension and emotion. His collaborations with Bruce Swedien (e.g., *Thriller*) teach how production serves the song’s narrative.  
   - **Actionable Lesson:** How silence, reverb, and dynamic shifts (loud/soft) shape a song’s emotional arc.

### 2. production + songwriting  
   - **Why?** Gamble & Huff pioneered the *soulful, groove-driven pop* sound of the 70s/80s (e.g., "Kiss You" by the Commodores, "September" by Earth, Wind & Fire).  
   - **Key Takeaway:** Notice how they layered instruments (horns, strings, clavinet) to create lush, danceable textures that made lyrics feel bigger. Their work proves melody *needs* sonic texture to land.  
   - **Actionable Lesson:** How to write a song that *sounds* as good as it feels—using production to elevate relatable lyrics.

### 3. Ryan Tedder  
   - **Why?** OneRepublic’s frontman writes anthems that *demand* big production (e.g., "Stop and Stare," "Apologize").  
   - **Key Takeaway:** Study how he crafts vocal melodies that soar, then uses synth swells, drum builds, and vocal harmonies to match that energy. His work with Swazey on *Transistor* (OneRepublic’s debut) is a masterclass in pop dynamics.  
   - **Actionable Lesson:** How to build tension/release in a chorus—sonically *and* lyrically.

### Bonus: Max Martin’s Secret Weapon  
While Martin is known for crafting melodies, his *real* genius is in **arrangement minimalism**.  
- **Example:** "Dance Monkey" by Tones and I uses just a few synth layers, a driving beat, and space around the vocals—but every element is *purposefully placed* to maximize impact.  
- **Study:** How he strips back production to highlight the core emotion, then adds layers only when it serves the song’s energy.

### Pro Tip:  
Listen to the **remixes** of your favorite songs. Notice how Martin, Tedder, or Jones can take the same lyrics and transform the production to fit a new mood (e.g., a slow R&B version of a pop hit). This teaches you how to make one song *feel* like 10 different experiences.

Start by dissecting albums like *Off the Wall* (Quincy), *Transistor* (Tedder), and the *OTM* series (Gamble/Huff). You’ll hear how songwriting and production are inseparable forces in pop magic. Good luck—you’re asking the right questions! 🎶
