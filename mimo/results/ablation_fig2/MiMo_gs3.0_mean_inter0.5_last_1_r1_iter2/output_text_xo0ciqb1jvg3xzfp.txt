You're right to question if it was a happy event for everyone. The traditional story often portrays it as a happy occasion for both Pilgrims and Wampanoag. However, a more complete historical understanding shows that the event was complex and likely very different for each group.

For the Pilgrims, it was a celebration of their survival after nearly all of them had died the previous winter. For the Wampanoag, it was a diplomatic gathering to solidify an alliance that would bring some short-term benefits.

But the story doesn't end there. This alliance eventually led to Wampanoag lands being seized, and they suffered greatly from European diseases and the loss of their way of life. So, while the 1621 feast itself may have been a moment of cooperation, the story of Thanksgiving as we know it carries a painful legacy for Native peoples.
