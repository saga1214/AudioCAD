Of course, let's just quickly go over them. So... a regular bit is that light switch, on or off. A qubit is the actor, who could be hero, villain, background—all at once—that's superposition. Taking a measurement is when the director says "Action!" and the actor finally commits to one role.

Entanglement is those linked actors whose roles correspond instantly. Uh, budgets of superposition are the moments an actor can hold all potential states. Noise causing decoherence is that random noise that breaks their focus. Error correction, or editing, is post-production where you fix mistakes. A quibble... well, that's your actor, the resource you use to build complexity. And finally, taking measurements is like the final cut, getting a single clear answer. The main thing to remember is that a quantum computer explores a huge number of possibilities at the same time, but you only see the answer when you take that final measurement. Hope that helps.
