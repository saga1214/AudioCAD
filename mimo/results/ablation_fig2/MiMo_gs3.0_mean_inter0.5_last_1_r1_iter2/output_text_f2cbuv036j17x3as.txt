Okay, diesel camper vans age, and maintenance is important before any long trip. For brakes, a key sign of wear is a soft or spongy brake pedal—like when you press it down, it doesn’t feel firm right away. That could mean the brake fluid is low or air has gotten into the system. You should also check for any squealing or grinding noises when you brake. If you’re at a mechanic, they can do a full brake inspection and recommend if pads or rotors need replacing. For spark plugs, diesel engines don’t use spark plugs like gas engines do. Diesel engines use glow plugs to help the fuel ignite when it’s cold. So replacing spark plugs isn’t something you’d typically do on a diesel. The mechanic should check your glow plugs, though. They can test the resistance and recommend if they need to be replaced. Also, diesel engines are heavy on oil, so getting an oil change and a fluid check is a good idea. Just make sure everything is in good shape before you hit the road.
