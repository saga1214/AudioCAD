That's a great way to approach it. You can definitely balance your desire for information with a respectful tone. Here are a few key things to include in your email:

First, introduce yourself and state the purpose of your email clearly. You could write: "Hi Professor [Last Name], I'm [Your Name], a student in your class. I scored a 64 on the essay-style exam we recently took and would like to improve on future tests. I was hoping to briefly understand what areas in my essay could have been developed better."

Second, compliment your performance on other aspects of the class. This helps build a positive connection. For example: "I've found your class on [Topic] to be very insightful, and my other assignments have been graded very fairly."

Third, focus on your growth. You could add: "I'm particularly interested in understanding how subjective grading might have played a role in this one exam, so I can better tailor my writing to your expectations next time."

Finally, make a low-pressure call to action. Instead of scheduling a meeting yourself—which could feel confrontational—you could say: "Would you be open to briefly discussing this over email or during a quick chat if you have time? No pressure either way."

This way, you keep the focus on learning and avoid challenging his ego directly. Good luck!
