That's a profound question. While it's hard to pick just one, the most critical aspect is probably the preservation of irreplaceable evolutionary history and genetic diversity.

Think of it this way: every species is the result of millions of years of evolution. A species that goes extinct in a hotspot is a unique evolutionary story that will never be told again. And within those species, there's often genetic diversity that could one day be crucial for us—say, for breeding disease-resistant crops or developing new medicines.

So, by protecting these hotspots, we're safeguarding the planet's entire biological heritage, and we're ensuring that we have access to its potential for our own survival in the future.
