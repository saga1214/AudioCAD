Of course, I can give you a clear breakdown. Just imagine your resume as having four main sections.

First, at the very top, you'll have your name in about a fourteen-point font. To its right is a Contact Header. This can contain your email, phone number, city, icons—which I still recommend removing—and maybe a link to your LinkedIn in slightly smaller nine-point font. Putting this below your name instead of to the side creates a cleaner flow.

Second is the Professional Summary. This is a short paragraph at the top that highlights your most relevant experience and goals, which is perfect for framing a career change and explaining any gaps.

Third is Experience. The primary section here, called 'Relevant Experience', lists your most relevant jobs with dates. For older or less relevant work, you can have a smaller 'Additional Work History' section that just lists the job title, company, and dates.

Fourth is Education. Here you'll list your degrees from the same school, along with details like your expected graduation year for a current degree and any honors or awards you received.

Finally, you'll have a Skills Section. This is crucial. You should list your key skills, and it's a good idea to tailor them to the specific job description you're applying for.

So, to summarize: put contact info directly below your name, use 'Relevant Experience' for key roles and 'Additional Work History' for others, list education details, remove icons for ATS compatibility, and make sure to tailor your skills section for each application. This structure is professional, clear, and easy for recruiters to scan.
