Great question - let's clarify this for your exam prep. The **three primary bones** collaborating for *rotational movement* in the shoulder are indeed:  
1. **Humerus** (upper arm bone)  
2. **Scapula** (shoulder blade)  
3. **Clavicle** (collarbone).  

**Important note:** While muscles like the rotator cuff *support* rotation, they are **not bones**. The clavicle's role is stabilizing the shoulder girdle but not directly enabling rotation.  

### If you're thinking of a "fourth component" for rotation, it's likely a muscle/tendon, not a bone:  
The **supraspinatus tendon** (part of the rotator cuff) is often injured during shoulder rotations, but it’s not a bone.  

### Signs of Poor Rotation (for your exam):  
- **Reduced mobility** (e.g., difficulty raising your arm overhead).  
- **Pain at the shoulder joint** (especially during rotation).  
- **Weakness** when lifting or rotating the arm outward.  
- **Grinding/stiffness** during movement.  

### Regarding your friend's soreness from dumbbell lateral raises:  
This is likely **normal muscle fatigue** from working the deltoids, supraspinatus, and trapezius. However, if soreness is:  
- **Persistent** (beyond 48 hours)  
- **Sharp or localized** (over the rotator cuff)  
- **Accompanied by weakness**  
...it could signal rotator cuff strain. Advise rest, ice, and gentle mobility exercises. If pain persists, they should see a clinician.  

**Key takeaway for your med exam:** Focus on the **three bones** (humerus, scapula, clavicle) for shoulder rotation mechanics. The rotator cuff muscles/tendons *support* this motion but aren’t bones. Good luck! 🩺💪
