Okay, so we're talking about skate blades on ice at minus ten degrees Celsius. At that temperature, the coefficient of friction is really low, but it might actually be a little higher than at typical rink temperatures because the ice is harder and that thin layer of water that helps with gliding isn't as present. So, a reasonable estimate would be somewhere in the range of 0.005 to 0.01. It really depends on the sharpness of the blades and how they apply pressure, but it’s definitely one of the smoothest surfaces out there!
