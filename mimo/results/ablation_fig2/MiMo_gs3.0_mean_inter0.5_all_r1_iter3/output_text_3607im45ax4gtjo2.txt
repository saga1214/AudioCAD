Dealing with a difficult neighbor requires a mix of strategy, self-care, and community awareness. Here’s practical advice tailored to your situation:

### 1. **Prioritize Safety & Documentation**
   - **CPS Involvement is Serious**: Since CPS is now active, document *everything*. Keep a detailed log (dates, times, incidents) and save all evidence (police/CPS reports, texts, photos). Share this with school counselors or a trusted teacher—they may help advocate for the kids.
   - **Safety Plan**: If calls to CPS are escalating, discuss safety steps with CPS/social services. They may investigate her behavior or offer resources.

### 2. **Avoid Direct Conflict**
   - **Don’t Engage**: Responding to her escalates tension. Keep interactions brief, polite, and factual ("I’m not comfortable with that") if unavoidable.
   - **Use Written Communication**: If you must contact her, do it via email or certified letter. This creates a paper trail and limits misunderstandings.

### 3. **Leverage Community Resources**
   - **Form Alliances**: Talk to affected families and neighbors. A unified approach (e.g., a group complaint to the police or housing authority) shows this isn’t isolated behavior.
   - **School Involvement**: Dress-up/Halloween events are great for distracting kids, but also involve their teachers/school counselors. They can monitor the kids’ well-being and report patterns to authorities if needed.

### 4. **Protect Your Mental Health**
   - **Set Boundaries**: Limit news/social media exposure about her to avoid spiraling stress. Designate "worry time" (e.g., 10 minutes/day) instead of constant rumination.
   - **Self-Care**: Your stress is real—prioritize activities that recharge you (like listening to Forrest Frank!). Even short walks, journaling, or talking to a friend can help.
   - **Seek Support**: Consider therapy or a stress-management group. Stress accumulates; professional help normalizes your feelings.

### 5. **Escalate Appropriately**
   - **Housing Authority/Police**: If local authorities aren’t helping, file formal complaints. Document every call.
   - **Mediation**: Request a mediated conversation (many communities offer free services). It creates a neutral space to address issues without blame.
   - **Legal Options**: Research local tenant/neighborhood laws. If her behavior violates ordinances (e.g., harassment), consult a lawyer.

### 6. **Focus on the Positives**
   - **School Fun**: Lean into the kids’ dress-up/Halloween excitement—it’s a tangible bright spot. Let them lead the energy!
   - **Music as Therapy**: Forrest Frank’s uplifting vibe is perfect for mood resets. Create Halloween playlists together to build family traditions.

### Remember:
- **You’re not alone**: Many neighborhoods face similar stressors. Your awareness is the first step.
- **Small Wins Matter**: Documenting incidents, rallying neighbors, or even a single positive school moment is progress.
- **CPS/Psychiatric Help**: If her behavior seems extreme (e.g., mental health crises), suggest she seek help. You can’t control others, but you can model calm and advocacy.

This takes time—be patient with yourself. You’re doing a great job balancing stress with joy for your kids. 💪🎃
