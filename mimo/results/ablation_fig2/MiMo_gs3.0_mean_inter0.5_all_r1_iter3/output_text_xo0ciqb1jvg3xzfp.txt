That's an excellent question. From a Wampanoag perspective, it's highly unlikely that the three-day feast was a happy occasion for everyone involved.

For the Pilgrims, it was a celebration of survival after nearly all of them had died the previous winter.

For the Wampanoag, it was a diplomatic event to solidify a political agreement. The relationship was complex and, in the long run, very damaging to their way of life.

So, it was probably a moment of gratitude and celebration for the Pilgrims, but also one of strategic necessity for the Wampanoag. It was certainly not a uniformly joyful event for both groups.
