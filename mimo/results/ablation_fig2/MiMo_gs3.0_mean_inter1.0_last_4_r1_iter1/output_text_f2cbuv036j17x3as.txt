Okay, diesel camper vans can be tricky before a long trip. For brakes, the most obvious signs of wear are a soft pedal, meaning you have to press harder to stop, or a grinding sound when you brake. If you see the pads are thin and you can see the metal backing plate, that’s a definite sign to replace them. For spark plugs, diesel engines use high-compression ignition, so the plugs are different. They’re usually more robust, but they still need to be changed. You’ll need a special socket and wrench. The process involves draining the oil first, then getting to the plugs, which are usually high up and under the van. You’ll want to use the correct heat range and gasket, then reassemble carefully. It’s a bit of work but not impossible. If you’re not comfortable, a diesel mechanic can do it for you. Most importantly, get a pre-trip inspection from a diesel specialist—check the engine, transmission, and exhaust system for anything unusual. Vibration or unusual noises can be a sign of bigger problems. Your old diesel van should be fine if it’s well-maintained, but long trips put a lot of stress on the vehicle.
