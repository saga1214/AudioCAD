Here’s a **modular packing list** tailored for both segments, prioritizing warmth, versatility, and nighttime needs:

### **For Daytime (20 Miles, Mid-30s, Three Aid Stations)**
1. **Base Layer**:  
   - Moisture-wicking top & leggings (synthetic or merino wool).  
2. **Mid-Layer**:  
   - Fleece jacket or light puffer vest (adjustable warmth).  
3. **Outer Layer**:  
   - Windproof/water-resistant shell (lightweight, packable).  
4. Aid Station Essentials:  
   - Gloves (lightweight, for grip/hand warmth).  
   - Cap or baseball hat (sun protection).  
   - Trail running shoes with grip (e.g., Vibram soles).  
   - Headlamp + spare batteries (critical for night start).  
   - Small daypack (to carry layers as you warm up).  

### **For Night Final 12 Miles**  
1. **Insulation**:  
   - Fleece jacket (replaceable with a heavier layer if needed).  
   - **Heavy insulation**: Fleece jacket, down vest, or insulated puffer.  
2. **Headwear**:  
   - Warm hat (beanie) + gloves (thicker, windproof).  
   - **Optional**: Balaclava or neck gaiter (extra warmth).  
3. Aid Station Essentials:  
   - Extra hat/gloves (for swapping).  
   Handheld light (brighter than day segment).  
   - Small pack/vest (for water layers).  
   - Extra socks (if needed).  

### **Key Notes**  
- **Modularity**: Bring items you can swap between segments (e.g., gloves, hat, fleece).  
- **Aid Stations**: Leverage them for water/sweat management—don’t overpack.  
- **Visibility**: Wear reflective gear if dark early.  
- **Packability**: Prioritize lightweight, packable items (e.g., foldable windbreaker).  

### **Avoid**  
- Heavy rain gear (hilly segments may have aid stations for dry clothes).  
- Excessive layers (you’ll shed/wear them between segments).
