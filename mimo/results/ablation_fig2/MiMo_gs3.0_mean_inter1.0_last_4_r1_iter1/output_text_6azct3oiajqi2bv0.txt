Based on your situation and the Segmental climate, here's a method to use duct tape on fabric without damaging the lining, using Nordstrom Rack as your suggested source:

1.  **Prepare the Surface:** Thoroughly clean the area where you need the tape. Wipe away dust, dirt, oils, or moisture (use a dry cloth or slightly damp cloth if needed, then dry completely) to ensure good adhesion. This prevents the tape from peeling off later.
2.  **Cut the Tape Neatly:** Use sharp scissors to cut a piece of duct tape that is slightly larger than the area you need to cover. Try to cut it as straight and smooth as possible. Avoid jagged edges.
3.  **Apply the Tape Strategically:**
    *   **For Small Tears/Loose Stitching:** Cut a small strip (about 1-2 inches wide) of tape. Stick the *backing* of the tape onto a piece of cardboard, a credit card, or even a piece of plastic wrap. Peel off the backing material.
    *   **Apply the Tape:** Press the *backing* (cardboard/credit card/plastic wrap) firmly onto the fabric *just below* the tear or loose area. Smooth it down completely.
    *   **Peel Off the Tape:** Carefully peel the backing material *away* from the fabric, keeping it as straight as possible. The tape should now be firmly adhered to the fabric *through* the backing.
    *   **Smooth:** Gently press down the tape on top of the backing to ensure full contact.
4.  **Secure the Edge (Optional but Recommended):** If the tear is significant, you can use the tape strip method above to create a small patch over the hole before applying the main tape strip through the backing.
5.  **Reinforce the Edge:** If the tear is along a seam, you can run a small strip of tape *along the seam* through the backing to help secure the loose fabric, but be very gentle near the seam allowance.

**Key Tips to Protect the Lining:**

*   **Use the Backing:** The cardboard, credit card, or plastic wrap acts as a buffer, preventing the rough texture of the duct tape itself from directly rubbing and potentially snagging or damaging the delicate lining fabric.
*   **Short Strokes:** When applying the tape through the backing, press down firmly with short, smooth strokes instead of dragging it.
*   **Test First:** If you're unsure, test the technique on an inconspicuous area of the jacket first.
*   **Temporary Fix:** Remember, duct tape is a strong temporary repair. It's ideal for holding a jacket together until you can replace it with something quality like REI Co-op from Arden Fair/Nordstrom Rack online.

**Alternatives if Duct Tape Feels Too Rough:**

*   **Heavy-Duty Fabric Glue (like E6000 or织物胶水):** Apply glue carefully to the *back* of the fabric *through* a backing (like cardboard) and press it down. This is smoother but still not perfect for delicate linings.
*   **Seam Tape (like Duck Tape for Sewing):** Specifically designed for fabric, it's much softer and smoother than regular duct tape. Look for "seam tape" or "fabric tape" at REI or a sewing store if you find duct tape too abrasive.
