Okay, let's put it all together. Here's a clear and traditional resume structure that should make the process much easier for you.

First, at the very top, is your name in about a fourteen-point font. To its right—still to the side—your contact info in nine-point font. This could include icons for your email dot symbol, phone number, current city, and state. I'd still advise removing those icons just to be safe with tracking software. And as I mentioned, below your name instead of to the side would look very clean. Oh, and definitely add a link to your LinkedIn profile here. If you have multiple numbers, just list the most important ones, like your phone and email, and say something like "Additional contact info available upon request."

Next up is a professional summary. This is a short, two or three-sentence paragraph that highlights your most relevant skills and experience, and states your career goals. This is key for bridging any gaps in your work history.

Then, you can have a section for Relevant Experience. Here, you list the jobs that are most important for the new roles you're targeting. For each job, include the company name, your title, the dates you worked there, and a few bullet points that focus on achievements, not just duties. You can use action verbs like 'managed', 'developed', or 'increased'.

After that, you can have an Additional Work History section. This is for jobs that aren't as directly relevant, but you still want to show your complete career path. For these, just list the job title, company, and dates. You don't need detailed bullet points.

For your education, list your degree, the school name, and your expected graduation date if you're a student. If you have multiple degrees from the same school, just list them in chronological order. And as we discussed, add any academic honors or awards right under the degree where you earned them.

If you have any, you can also add a Skills section. A good way to do this is to list your skills in categories, like Technical Skills, Soft Skills, or Languages, with keywords from the job description.

Finally, you can have optional sections for things like Certifications, Volunteer Work, or Projects. Just include only the ones that are most relevant.

So, to quickly recap: put contact info below your name, list relevant experience first, and keep everything concise. This structure should help you create a strong resume that effectively showcases your career transition. Good luck!
