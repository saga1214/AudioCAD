Of course. Here's a summary of the wines and their temperatures for you.

First, the high-end Pinot Noir. It should be stored at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. It's served slightly below room temp, around 55 to 60 Fahrenheit or 13 to 16 Celsius.

Next is Japanese Hon-Mirin. It should be stored in a cool place at about 60 to 68 Fahrenheit or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you sip it directly instead of using it for cooking, room temp is best, around 20 to 24 Fahrenheit or 68 to 75 Celsius.

Then we have Italian wines. For a Chianti red, you store it at about 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius and serve it below room temp, around 60 to 65 Fahrenheit or 16 to 18 Celsius. For a Pinot Grigio white, you store it at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit or 10 to 13 Celsius and serve it chilled, around 45 to 50 Fahrenheit or 7 to 10 Celsius.

For a Champagne or Sauvignon Blanc, the best served cold, you store them at about 50 Fahrenheit or 10 Celsius and serve them cold, around 45 to 50 Fahrenheit or 7 to 10 Celsius.

And for mulled wine, which is served hot, you store the wine itself at normal cellar temps of about 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius, but you serve it warm, between 140 and 160 Fahrenheit or 60 to 70 Celsius.

To answer your last question, for hot weather, the best choice would be the Pinot Grigio or a Sauvignon Blanc. For cold weather, a Pinot Noir or a Chianti would be excellent.
