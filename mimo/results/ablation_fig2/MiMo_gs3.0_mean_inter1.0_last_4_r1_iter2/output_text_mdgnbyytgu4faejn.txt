Okay, let's put it all together. Here's a clear, traditional format that works really well.

First, at the very top, bold and centered, is your name in about a fourteen-point font.

Right directly underneath, centered, is a single line with your contact info on one line: email, phone number, city. I'd suggest removing those small icons just in case automated tracking software has trouble with them. If you want, you can add a link to your LinkedIn profile just below this section.

Next, a clear, centered heading like "Professional Summary" or "Summary." Underneath that, write a short paragraph of three to five sentences. This is where you'll highlight your most relevant skills and career goals, which is a great way to address any long gaps in your work history.

After the summary, you'll have a section for "Relevant Experience." Here, you'll list your most important jobs first. For each one, write the job title, the company name, and the years you worked there. Then, add two to four bullet points that focus on your key achievements and skills, using strong action words.

Below that, you can have a smaller section called "Additional Work History." In this part, you can list your other jobs in a similar way, just with the job title, company, and dates, without the detailed bullet points.

Then comes the "Education" section. You can list your degrees, starting with the most recent one. For each degree, write the name of the degree, the school, and the year you graduated. You can also add a brief note here, like "Highest Honors" or the name of a scholarship you received.

Finally, you can add a "Skills" section. This can be a simple list of your key hard and soft skills, tailored to the job you're applying for.

This structure is professional, easy to scan, and effectively showcases your entire career path, even as you're making a change.
