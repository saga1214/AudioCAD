Okay, diesel camper vans age, and maintenance before a long trip is critical. For brakes, a common sign of wear is a soft or spongy pedal feel—like the brake pedal sinks when you press it, then comes back up. Another sign is a grinding noise when you brake, or a vibration that travels through the brake pedal. If you see brake dust or debris accumulating on the wheels, that’s also a good indicator to get them checked. As for spark plugs, diesel engines use high compression, so they can be a bit tricky. The process involves removing the air intake ducts and the battery to get access. You’ll need to disconnect the battery first to avoid shorts. Then, you remove the coil packs or injectors, depending on the setup. The spark plugs themselves are usually located high up, so you’ll need a long extension on your ratchet to reach them. After you’ve removed the old plugs, you’ll need to torque them down to the manufacturer’s spec, which is usually pretty tight, around 30 to 40 foot-pounds. Then you’ll reassemble everything and make sure there are no leaks or issues. It’s a process, but it can be done. It’s always a good idea to have a mechanic check it out before you go.
