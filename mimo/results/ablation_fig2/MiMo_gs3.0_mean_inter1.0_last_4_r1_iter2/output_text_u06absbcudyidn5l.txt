Of course, let's break it down. Chronic redness has many potential causes. Besides rosacea and sun damage we mentioned before, it could be eczema, allergies, contact dermatitis from something you're reacting to, or even heat from exercise. It could also be a sign of a more systemic condition like lupus.

Now, for vasculitis, let's go over it again. It's inflammation of your blood vessels. Think of it as your body's immune system getting confused and attacking the tubes that carry blood to your organs. This inflammation is what causes the symptoms. Common signs are fever, fatigue, and rashes. As I mentioned, because the inflammation can happen in the small blood vessels that feed your hair follicles and nail beds, it can definitely weaken them, leading to thinning hair and brittle nails. The biggest danger is that in larger vessels, the inflammation can narrow them, which can lead to serious issues like kidney or lung damage.

As for treatments, doctors usually start with high-dose steroids to calm the immune system down. If that's not enough, they might use other immunosuppressants or even biologic drugs to target the inflammation more precisely.

And to be clear, ibuprofen is for general pain and inflammation, but it doesn't treat the underlying cause of vasculitis. That's why a proper diagnosis and treatment from a specialist is so important.
