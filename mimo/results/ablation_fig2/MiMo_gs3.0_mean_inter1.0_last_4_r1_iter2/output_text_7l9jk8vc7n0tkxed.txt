### Step 1: Plunger (if not already tried)
- Use a **plunger** (even a simple rubber one) with a *tight seal* over the drain. Pump vigorously for 20-30 seconds.  
- If it doesn't work, try again after 5 minutes of letting water drain.  

### Step 2: Drain Snake (Auger)
- If the plunger fails, grab your **hand auger** (the small, manual drain snake).  
- Insert the tip into the drain, twist gently, and slowly push until you feel resistance.  
- **Rotate clockwise** while pulling back to break up the clog.  
- If you hit a solid object (like a pipe or debris), stop and reassess.  

### Step 3: Chemical Drain Cleaner (Use with Caution)
- If you have **basic chemical drain cleaner** (not heavy-duty), pour it down the drain.  
- **WARNING:** Avoid mixing with other chemicals! Wait 15-30 minutes.  
- If it works, flush with hot water.  
- If it doesn’t, skip to Step 4.  

### Step 4: Manual Breakup (If Tools Allow)
- If you have a **hand auger or pipe wrench**, carefully disassemble the drain trap (the U-shaped pipe under the sink).  
- Remove the P-trap, check for debris, and clear it.  
- Reassemble and test.  

### Step 5: Hot Water + Baking Soda/Vinegar
- Pour **hot water** down the drain to loosen debris.  
- Add **1 cup baking soda**, then **1 cup vinegar**. Wait 10 minutes.  
- Flush with **hot water**.  

### Safety & Calming Reminders:
- **Pause every 10 minutes** to stretch, breathe deeply, and recheck progress.  
- **Accept that deep clogs take time**—don’t force tools if they’re stuck.  
- If you hit a pipe obstruction, call a pro with bigger tools.
