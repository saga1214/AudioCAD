For the Pilgrims, the feast was likely a moment of relief and gratitude after barely surviving their new life.

For the Wam-panoag side, the situation was more complicated. While the book David Silverman suggests that some Wampanoagers likely appreciated the hospitality and shared harvest, it was also a time of great change and hardship for their people, largely due to the arrival of the English. So, while the shared feast may have been a positive moment for some, it existed within a much larger and more difficult context for the Wampanoag.
