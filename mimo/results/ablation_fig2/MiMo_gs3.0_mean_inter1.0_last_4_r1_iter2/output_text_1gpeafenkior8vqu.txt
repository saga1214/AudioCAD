Okay, let's create a rich art-focused holiday schedule while maximizing sightseeing for both deep exploration and presentation research. Since Japan art history could anchor your coursework, we'll weave research opportunities into your itinerary naturally.

For Tokyo (5 days), here's a sample:
Day 1: Check out Edo-Tokyo Museum - their art history exhibits are perfect content research. Visit Senso-ji Temple to study Japanese architectural design principles.
Day 2: Imperial Palace East Gardens (Buddhist sculpture) + Ueno Park museums (calligraphy and ceramics galleries). Use museum brochour images for slide design ideas.
Day 3: Asakusa for traditional kimono patterns (color scheme inspiration) + Tsukiji Outer Market woodblock print shops.
Day 4: Work out early AM. Morning: Meiji Shrine forest art. Afternoon: Ghibli Museum (modern design influence) or workout session.
Day 5: Check out early AM workout. Morning: Shibuya for contemporary street art. Afternoon: Narita Airport transfer (could include Nara if time allows).

For Osaka (4 days) with Kyoto/Nara day trip:
Day 1: Arrive, check hotel. Evening: Dotonbori to see ukiyo-e-inspired neon signage colors.
Day 2: Morning: Osaka Castle (perfect topic choice!). Afternoon: Work out. Evening: Kuromon Market.
Day 3: Full day trip: Nara (Todai-ji Temple giant Buddha sculpture, Nara Park stone carvings) + Kyoto (Kinkaku-ji golden pavilion color scheme, Ryoan-ji rock garden layout study).
Day 4: Morning: Kyoto Gion district (courtly art era). Afternoon: Osaka Universal Studios (Hokusai-inspired Frozen ride). Evening: Departure.

Design tips for slides: Ukiyo-e often used flat colors with nature motifs - consider emulating Hiroshinge Hiroshige's color palettes. Layout could mimic traditional scroll composition with asymmetry. Fonts might pair traditional calligraphy brushstrokes with modern minimal type.

This schedule balances holiday time, workout commitments, and presentation prep. The art exposure will help all aspects of your marking criteria - literacy content through temple studies, images from historical sites, layout inspiration from scroll compositions, color from kimonos, and font from calligraphy.
