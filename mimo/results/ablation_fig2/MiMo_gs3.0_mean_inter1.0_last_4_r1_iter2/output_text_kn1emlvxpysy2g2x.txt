Of course. Let's review the wines we've discussed and their ideal temperatures. 

First, for high-end Pinot Noir: it should be stored at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit. It's served slightly below room temp, so aim for 55 to 60 degrees Fahrenheit.

Next, Japanese Hon-Mirin: it should be stored in a cool place at about 60 to 68 degrees Fahrenheit. If you sip it instead of using it to cook, room temp of 68 to 75 degrees Fahrenheit is fine.

Then there's Pinot Grigio: you should store it at about 50 to 55 degrees Fahrenheit, and serve it chilled, around 45 to 50 degrees Fahrenheit.

For Italian Chianti: it's stored the same as the Pinot at 55 degrees Fahrenheit, but it's served just a bit below room temp.

Finally, for wines served hot, like mulled wine, the serving temperature is much higher, around 140 to 160 degrees Fahrenheit. However, the wine itself is stored at normal cellar temps of about 55 degrees Fahrenheit before it's warmed up.

So, to answer your question about the best for hot weather, the Pinot Grigio is the best choice because it's served cold. For cold weather, the Pinot Noir is the best, as it highlights delicate aromas slightly above freezing. Hope that helps you plan for the weather.
