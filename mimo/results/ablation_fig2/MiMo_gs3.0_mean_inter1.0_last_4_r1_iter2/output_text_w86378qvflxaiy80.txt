The most important aspect of preserving biodiversity hotspots is **their irreplaceable role as Earth's evolutionary libraries**. These regions harbor unique species lineages shaped over millions of years—many of which hold untapped potential for medicine, agriculture, and climate resilience. Their loss isn't just ecological; it's a permanent erasure of genetic diversity and evolutionary solutions we may never discover.  

### Key Aspects Driving Preservation:  
1. **Irreplaceable Genetic Diversity**:  
   - Each hotspot contains plant species found *nowhere else on Earth* ( Meeting the 1,500-endemic-plant threshold).  
   - Example: The Cape Floristic Region (Africa) has 6,000 endemic plant species—each a unique genetic blueprint.  

2. **Ecosystem Services for Human Survival**:  
   - As you noted, hotspots provide **35% of ecosystem services** vital for vulnerable communities (e.g., clean water, pollination, flood control).  
   - Example: The Sundaland Shelf (Southeast Asia) supports rice paddies and fisheries feeding 200+ million people.  

3. **Preventing Extinction Cascades**:  
   - Losing a single keystone species (e.g., pollinators in the Indo-Burma hotspot) can collapse food webs, threatening crops and livestock.  

4. **Climate Resilience**:  
   - Forest hotspots like the Atlantic Forest (Brazil) act as carbon sinks and regulate regional rainfall patterns.  

5. Norman Myers’ Legacy:  
   - Introduced hotspots in **1988** in his book *Biodiversity: Earth’s Living Resources and the Future of Human Society*. CI formalized conservation strategy in 1989.  

### Why This Matters Beyond Ecology:  
> "Hotspots are not just about saving pretty animals—they are about securing the biological infrastructure that makes human civilization possible."  
> — *Conservation International, 2022*  

Preserving hotspots is ultimately about safeguarding **our own future**—from undiscovered medicines to stable climates and food security. Their destruction would erase solutions to crises we haven’t yet faced.
