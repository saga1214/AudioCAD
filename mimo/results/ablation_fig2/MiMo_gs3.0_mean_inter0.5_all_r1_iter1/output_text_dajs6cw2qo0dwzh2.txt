Here's a breakdown of the interplay:

1.  **The Portuguese Route *Was* the Decisive Innovation:** The development of superior ship designs (caravel, lateen sail), advanced navigation techniques (astrolabe, quadrant, compass use), and aggressive state-sponsored exploration (Prince Henry, da Gama) *were* the fundamental drivers. These innovations made finding and sailing new routes possible. Without them, no Age of Discovery would have happened, regardless of Ottoman actions.

2.  **Constantinople's Fall (1453) Was a Major Catalyst, Not the Sole Cause:**
    *   **Disruption, Not Instant Closure:** As you noted, trade didn't vanish overnight. The Ottomans controlled key nodes, imposed high taxes, and created instability. Venetian and Genoese merchants still operated in the Black Sea and Mediterranean ports, but their access to spices via the *traditional overland routes* was significantly hampered and became more expensive and risky.
    *   **Creating the "Need":** This disruption made the dream of a direct sea route to Asia (via the Indian Ocean) incredibly attractive to powerful kingdoms like Portugal, Spain, England, France, and the Netherlands. It accelerated the quest for a new route.
    *   **Symbolic and Strategic Blow:** The fall of Constantinople, the ancient Christian capital of Europe, was a massive shock. It symbolized the decline of the old Mediterranean world order and the rise of Ottoman power, making Europe desperate for new sources of wealth, glory, and spreading Christianity *independently*.

3.  Age of Discovery Happened *Because* of the Portuguese Route *and* the Catalyst of Ottoman Disruption:
    *   **The "Why":** The desire for spices, wealth, and glory (driven by competition among kingdoms) was amplified by the *perceived* loss of control over the spice trade routes due to Ottoman dominance.
    *   **The "How":** The Portuguese innovations in shipbuilding and navigation provided the *means* to find and sail the new routes (like around Africa and across the Atlantic).
    *   **The "When":** The disruption caused by Ottoman control of Constantinople and the Mediterranean created the *urgency* and competitive pressure that made Portuguese exploration efforts seem viable and necessary *at that specific time*.

**Conclusion:**

You're absolutely right that the Portuguese establishment of the African sea route was the *primary technological and organizational driver* of the Age of Discovery. **However, the Ottoman capture of Constantinople didn't *cause* the Age of Discovery; it *catalyzed* it.** It created a powerful *need* and *urgency* that made the Portuguese innovations seem like the only viable solution, accelerating the process significantly. Without the Portuguese route, the Age of Discovery wouldn't have happened. Without the Ottoman disruption creating the context, the *timing* and *intensity* of the Portuguese (and later other European) efforts might have been very different. It was the combination of the new technology *and* the geopolitical catalyst that ignited the Age.
