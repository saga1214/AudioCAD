You're very close with the pronunciation—very close! It's pronounced roughly like "suh-kah-dee." Now, to your question: because of saccadic masking, the eye does not see continuously. Instead, it sees in short, rapid bursts, or jumps, between points of focus. It's not like a camera shooting at a constant rate. The brain then takes these little snapshots and fills in the gaps to create the perception of a continuous scene. 

So, while the eye is technically seeing in short bursts, the brain’s processing makes the experience feel like a continuous, flowing stream of information. It’s a bit of a paradox, right? The eye is moving around, but the brain makes it all seem like a smooth, stable picture.
