For the Pilgrims, surviving the brutal winter and achieving a successful first harvest was a miraculous event, and sharing the feast was a powerful expression of their gratitude to God.

For the Wampanoag, the situation was much more complicated. While they did participate in the feast, it was a strategic decision made by their leader in an effort to protect their own people from English attacks.

So, while cooperation did happen, it was not a universally happy event for both groups. For the Wampanoag, it was also a moment of survival and diplomacy in a rapidly changing and often hostile world.
