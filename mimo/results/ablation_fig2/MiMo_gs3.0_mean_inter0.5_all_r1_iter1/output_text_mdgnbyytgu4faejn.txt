Of course, I can give you a clear breakdown. Here’s a straightforward format that works for most situations.

First, at the very top, center your name in about a fourteen-point font. Right directly underneath it, on the same line, place your email, phone number with country and area code, and city. Icons are best to skip entirely. You could also add a link to your LinkedIn profile here. Just remember to remove icons if using automated resume systems gives you trouble.

Next, have a short professional summary of about three to four lines right below your name. This is where you briefly highlight your most relevant skills and career goals, especially if you have a career change.

After that, have a section for your Relevant Experience. Here, you'll list your key roles first, including the company, your job title, and the specific dates you worked there. For these, you can use brief bullet points to describe your most important achievements.

Then, create a separate section for Additional Work History. Here you can list other jobs, but again, just the company, title, and dates, without detailed descriptions. This is a good way to show a full work history without cluttering the resume.

For Education, list your highest degree first. Under each degree, write the school's name and the expected graduation month and year. Right below that, you can add details like your graduation honors, any scholarships you received, or a link to your academic portfolio.

You should also have a dedicated Skills section. Here, list your hard skills, like software programs or technical abilities, and your soft skills, like communication or leadership. It's a good idea to tailor this list to the specific job description.

Finally, you can add other sections like Certifications, Awards, or Volunteer Work if they're relevant to the jobs you're applying for. And at the very bottom, you can include a link to your personal website or portfolio if you have one.

Just remember to keep everything concise and tailored to each job you apply for. Does this overall structure help you get started?
