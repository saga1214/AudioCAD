That's a great way to approach it. Here are a few pointers to help you craft a balanced email.

First, start with your goal: "I scored a 64 on my latest essay-style exam and wanted to learn how I could improve on future assignments to do better."

Second, express respect and gratitude. You could say, "I appreciate the opportunity to learn from your course."

Third, state your purpose clearly but neutrally. For example: "I was surprised by my score and would be grateful to understand what factors the grading considered."

Fourth, keep it specific. You might add, "Since the exam resembled an essay, I didn't know how definitively a grade could be assigned solely based on content."

Finally, end with a low-pressure call to action, like, "Would you have 10 to 15 minutes available during your office hours sometime this week to discuss this briefly? If not, I completely understand."

This structure focuses on your growth and shows respect, which might be more effective with an ego-driven professor.
