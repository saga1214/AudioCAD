Here's a concise summary of solutions:

### For Feeding/Hunger Prevention:
- **Controlled Feeders:** Use microchip-activated or timed feeders to release food slowly, ensuring fussy eaters can’t monopolize it.
- **Room Separation:** Confine fussy cats to a separate room for feeding *before* you leave home at 2 pm.
- **Stretch Meals:** Mix small amounts of quality food with cheap fillers (e.g., plain cooked rice) to make meals last longer. Vet-approved first!

### For Poverty-Stricken Care:
- **Seek Free Help:** Search for local pet food banks, shelters, or community groups offering surplus food.
- **Buy in Bulk:** Purchase larger bags of quality food for lower cost per serving (prioritize nutrition).
- **Homemade Supplements:** Safely add unseasoned cooked meats (chicken, fish) to cheap kibble when affordable.
- **Prevent Illness:** 
  - Maintain clean litter, bowls, and environment.
  - Brush cats to reduce skin issues/hairballs.
  - Play with DIY toys (string, crumpled paper) to combat obesity (a major health cost).

### For Health Management:
- **Obtain Essentials:** Prioritize low-cost spaying/neutering and vaccines.
- **Monitor Closely:** Watch for early illness signs (lethargy, weight loss) to act fast if needed.
- **DIY Basic Care:** Learn simple first aid (e.g., wound cleaning) online—many free resources exist.

**Key Takeaway:** Combine controlled feeding with strategic budgeting (bulk buys, supplements) and proactive prevention (cleanliness, activity) to keep cats fed and healthy despite financial constraints. Always prioritize fussy cats’ inclusion in feeding plans.
