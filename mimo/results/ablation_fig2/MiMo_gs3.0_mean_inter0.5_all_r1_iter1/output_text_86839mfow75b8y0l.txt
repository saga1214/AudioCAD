You're absolutely right to connect songwriting with production—these are inseparable in pop music! The key songwriter you mentioned who embodies this **fusion of songwriting and production genius is Quincy Jones**. Here’s why studying him is especially valuable:

### 🎹 **Quincy Jones: The Master of Storytelling Through Sound**
- **Why He’s Worth Studying**:  
  Jones didn’t just write songs—he *architected entire sonic worlds*. Albums like *Off the Wall* (Michael Jackson) and *Thriller* (Jackson) show how melody, rhythm, and production work together to create emotional impact.  
  - **Emotion Through Production**: Swedien’s minimalist drum sounds on "Billie Jean" make the bassline feel like a heartbeat. The sparse arrangement lets Jackson’s vocal ad-libs feel explosive.  
  - **Dynamic Contrast**: Songs like "Human Nature" use smooth, layered synths and subtle basslines to create a hypnotic groove—proof that production *is* part of the songwriting.

### 🎤 **Honorable Mentions Who Blend Songwriting & Production**
1. **Sia**:  
   Her producers (like Justine Frischmann and Sean Lopez) use lush synths and unconventional arrangements to amplify her lyrics about resilience.  
2. **Arif Martin**:  
   Known for working with Aretha Franklin and Whitney Houston, he crafts arrangements where every instrument tells a story (e.g., the gospel choir on "Greatest Love of All").  
3. **Sylvia Massy**:  
   A producer who collaborates with songwriters (like Taylor Swift early on), she teaches how to make lyrics "feel" through sonic textures.

### 📚 **What to Study from Quincy Jones**
- **Albums**:  
  - *Off the Wall* (1979)  
  - *Thriller* (1982)  
  - *Back on the Block* (1987)  
- **Key Techniques**:  
  - **Space & Silence**: How negative space in production makes melodies feel bigger.  
  - **Layering**: Combining organic instruments (piano, horns) with electronic beats.  
  - **Dynamic Range**: From whisper-quiet verses to thunderous choruses.  

### 💡 **Actionable Advice**
1. **Listen to raw demos vs. final mixes**:  
   Compare Taylor Swift’s early acoustic demos (like "Love Story") to Max Martin’s polished productions. Notice how vocal doubling, reverb, and percussion transform a simple idea.  
2. **Study "in the studio" interviews**:  
   Quincy’s documentary *Quincy Jones: An Interview* (2017) shows how he crafts songs *in the moment* with collaborators.  
3. **Embrace imperfection**:  
   Gamble & Huff’s "Sound of Philadelphia" basslines were *improvised*—sometimes the best production choices come from spontaneity.  

> “A song is not just about the notes. It’s about the silence between them, the breath before the chorus, the way the hi-hat hits at 3:15.”  
> — Quoted from Quincy Jones’ work with Swedien on *Thriller*

Start with Quincy’s approach: **write like a poet, but imagine the recording studio as your co-writer**. Pop music lives in the space where melody, lyric, and sound design collide! 🎶✨
