No problem! Here's a clear, categorized list of all the measurements we discussed, organized by when you'd typically take them:

### **I. EVERY Fittings (Core Measurements):**
1. **Bust** (underarm to fullest part of chest)  
2. **Natural Waist** (smallest part of the torso, not the belt line)  
3. **Hips** (widest part of the hips, usually 8-12 inches below the waist)  

---

### **II. For More Fitted Garments (Tops, Jackets, etc.):**
1. **Sleeve Length** (shoulder to wrist)  
2. **Neck** (for collars/cuffs)  
3. **Shoulder Width** (across upper back, prevents pulling)  
4. **Bicep** (mid-bicep circumference)  

---

### **III. For Fitted Pants/Skirts:**
1. **Inseam** (inner thigh from waist to ankle)  
2. **Hip** (already in core list, but double-check here)  
3. **Thigh** (most important for comfort, measure 1" below the crotch)  
4. **Front Rise** (crotch to waist)  
5. **Back Rise** (crotch to waist at the back)  
6. **Foot Opening** (waistband to ankle, critical for length)  

---

### **IV. For Length Adjustment (Pants/Skirts):**
**Always test the client wearing the shoes they'll use!**  
- Measure from **waist to shoe top** (not ankle!).  
- Example: If inseam is 37", but the client wants pants to touch sneakers, you’ll likely need to **shorten by 1-3"** (depending on shoe height).  

---

### **Pro Tips for Your Fittings:**
- **Natural waist** = Use a belt or drawstring to mark the narrowest point.  
- **Back rise** = Crucial for tall clients! Measure from crotch to waistband.  
- **Ease** = Always add 1-2" of extra fabric to the hips/thigh for sitting comfort.  
- **Test with shoes** = Never guess pant length—pin the hem to the shoe first.
