Here’s a streamlined clothing list split by segment, prioritizing warmth, versatility, and packability:

### **20-Mile Segment (Daytime into Night, Mid-30s, Three Aid Stations)**  
1. **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking top (short/sleeve) and bottom (shorts/leggings).  
2. **Mid-Layer**: Fleece or light puffy jacket (removable if needed).  
3. **Outer Layer**: Wind/water-resistant shell (hooded or zippered for layering).  
4. **Extremities**: Gloves (grip for hills), cap/hat (for sun), buff/gaiter (wind protection).  
5. **Night Essentials**: Headlamp/flashlight + **extra batteries** (critical!).  
6. **Footwear**: Trail shoes with aggressive tread (hills + uneven ground).  
7. Utilize aid stations for dry tops if needed!  

### **Final 12 Miles (Nighttime, Slower Pace)**  
1. **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking top (short/sleeve).  
2. Walking/Warmth: Insulated jacket (fleece or down) for slower pace chill.  
3. **Head/Neck**: Warm beanie + buff/gaiter (night gets colder).  
4. **Gloves**: Thicker gloves (wind chill affects hands fast).  
5. **Headlamp**: Ensure it’s fully charged (battery pack if possible).  
6. **Footwear**: Trail shoes (same as above).  
7. **Extras**: Small pack for carrying layers if needed.  

### **Key Tips**  
- **Test Layers**: Wear your outer layer during the day to gauge warmth.  
- **Aid Station Strategy**: Use them for dry tops, gloves, or batteries.  
- **Packability**: Bring layers in zip bags for quick swaps.  
- **No Rain?** Skip the shell; prioritize insulation for night.
