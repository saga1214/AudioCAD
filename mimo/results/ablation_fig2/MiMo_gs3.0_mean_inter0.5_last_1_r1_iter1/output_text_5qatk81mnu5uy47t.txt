**Step 1: Costa Rica Dive Locations**
Costa Rica is scuba diving heaven. The key areas are:
*   **Cocos Island:** Famous for its strong currents, deep walls, huge pelagic wildlife (sharks, rays, marlin), and dramatic underwater landscapes. Best in spring (Feb-Apr) and fall (Aug-Oct) when currents are calmer. Requires more experience.
*   **Guanacaste:** The north coast, especially around **Playa Conchal** and **Puerto Viejo**, offers more accessible reefs, coral gardens, manta ray hotspots (especially around **Malpasa** and **Playa Hermosa**), and generally calmer conditions. Great for beginners and medium-level divers. Spring and Fall are good.
*   **Puntarenas:** Home to **Sirena** and **Dona Ana** dive shops. Sirena is known for its volcanic rock formations, lava arches, and good visibility. Dona Ana offers deeper walls and drift dives. Can get busier.

**Recommendation:** For a first dream trip debating fins, **Guanacaste (especially Playa Conchal/Puerto Viejo area)** is likely the most accessible and forgiving location, while still offering fantastic visibility, diverse marine life (turtles, reef fish, mantas), and beautiful coral. Cocos Island is an epic adventure but demands more experience and calmer conditions for the best dive.

**Step 2: Diving Experience Level**
*   Short fins being a better *investiture* for beginners suggests you might be Open Water Certified (PADI or NAUI).
*   Cocos Island's strong currents and drift dives mean you should ensure you're at least **Advanced Open Water** certified and comfortable in moderate currents. If you're true beginners, focus on Guanacaste.
*   **Action:** Confirm your certification levels. If Advanced, you can seriously consider Cocos Island trips. If Open Water, stick to the shallower, reef-based dives in Guanacaste.

**Step 3: Dive Shops & Live-Aboard Options**
*   **Live-Aboard (Lagoon Boats):** Offer the most diving, longest days (often 10-12 dives in 5-7 days), and the chance to visit remote sites like Cocos Island (if certified). More immersive but pricier and requires travel to the boat base. Great for serious divers.
*   **Day Boat (Flyboard/Brilliant):** Offer shorter dives (often 6-8 dives in 4-5 days), include hotel transfers, and are generally more convenient and affordable. Good for exploring the coast around Guanacaste.
*   **Action:** Research reputable dive shops in your chosen area (e.g., **Dive Conchal** in Playa Conchal, **Tropicat** in Sirena). Look at dive schedules, boat size, crew quality, and reviews. Contact them to confirm requirements for your chosen itinerary.

**Step 4: Beyond Diving - Make it Unforgettable**
Costa Rica is more than just diving!
*   **Land Adventures:** Zip-lining through cloud forests (Monteverde or Arenal), hiking active volcanoes (Arenal, Poás), wildlife spotting (sloths, monkeys, toucans - Manuel Antonio NP or Tortuguero), snorkeling in lava tubes (Arenal), visiting a coffee plantation.
*   **Costa Rica Experience:** Yoga retreats, eco-lodges, relaxing on pristine beaches (Tamarindo, Santa Teresa, Playa Conchal), enjoying the local cuisine and coffee.
*   **Action:** Plan a mix! Aim for 5-7 full days of diving minimum. Add 2-3 land adventure days. Look at itineraries combining diving with land activities. Book accommodations early, especially near dive sites or in popular eco-resorts.

**Step 5: Packing & Logistics**
*   **Fins:** Your winning investment! Pack your chosen short fins.
*   **Certification Cards:** Bring physical copies and digital backups.
*   **Dive Gear:** Shop rental gear in Costa Rica (BC, regulator, weights, mask, fins, snorkel) to save luggage space unless you specifically want to bring your own.
*   **Travel:** Book flights early. Consider a domestic flight from San José to Liberia (LGA) in Guanacaste or Santa Maria (SJO) for the south (Puntarenas). Research airport transfers.
*   **Action:** Start booking flights and dive shops now. Research accommodation options near your dive base.
