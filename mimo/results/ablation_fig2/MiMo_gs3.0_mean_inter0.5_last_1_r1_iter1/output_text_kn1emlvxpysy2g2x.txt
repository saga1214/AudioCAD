Of course. Let's recap the wines we've discussed and their ideal temperatures for both serving and storing.

First, the high-end Pinot Noir. You should store it at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. When serving it, it's best slightly below room temp, so aim for 55 to 60 Fahrenheit, or 13 to 16 Celsius.

Next, Japanese Hon-Mirin. You should store it in a cool place at about 60 to 68 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you're sipping it directly instead of cooking, room temp is fine, around 20 to 24 degrees Celsius, which is about 68 to 75 Fahrenheit.

Then we have Chianti. For this Italian red, you should store it at the same cellar temperature as the Pinot Noir, about 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. Serve it just a bit below room temperature, between 60 and 65 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 18 Celsius.

And finally, the Pinot Grigio. You should store this white wine a little cooler, between 50 and 55 Fahrenheit, or 10 to 13 Celsius. Serve it chilled, at around 45 to 50 Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius.

Now, to answer your other question: the best wine for hot weather is definitely the Pinot Grigio. It's light and refreshing, and it stays crisp even in the heat. The best wine for cold weather would be the Pinot Noir or the Chianti. They are full-bodied reds that really warm you up on a cool day. Hon-Mirin is interesting, but it’s not typically sipped on its own, so it doesn’t really fit into a specific weather category.
