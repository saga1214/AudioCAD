Okay, diesel camper vans age, and maintenance is critical before a long trip. For brakes, a professional inspection is the only real way to know for sure—look for worn pads or rotors. You can also watch for signs like a metallic grinding noise when braking, or a vibrating pedal when pressing down. A mechanic can do a brake fluid flush too, which is a good idea for diesel vehicles. For spark plugs, you can change them yourself if you're comfortable. Diesel engines use high compression, so the plugs need to be high-torque. You’ll need a special socket, a wrench, and some diesel-specific lubricant. It’s not a simple task, but if you’ve done it before, you can do it again. If not, a mechanic can do it for you. Just make sure the van is warm, disconnect the battery, and check the oil before you start.
