Here are the best ways to attach Velcro without damaging fabric or lining, depending on your needs:

**Best Methods:**

1.  **Hand-Stitching (Most Durable & Recommended):**
    *   **Positioning:** Mark the exact spot on the lining where you want the Velcro to attach. Trace the *hook* side of the Velcro onto the lining.
    *   **Thread:** Use a strong, thin cotton or polyester thread (like a quilting thread). Avoid heavy-duty thread if the lining is thin.
    *   **Stitch Type:** Use a small, tight **backstitch** or **whipstitch**. This creates a strong, neat line that won't pull through.
    *   **Technique:** Start and end stitches *on the wrong side* of the fabric to keep them hidden. Sew *only* through the lining fabric. *Do not* stitch through the outer shell if possible – this is what damages it.
    *   **Velcro:** Secure the Velcro's *loop* side first. Once the loop is firmly stitched down, flip the garment over and stitch the *hook* side onto the lining, ensuring it lies flat against the loop side.

2.  **Invisible Stitching Machine (Cleanest Look):**
    *   If you have a sewing machine with an **invisible stitch** or **blind stitch** foot, this is ideal.
    *   The stitch is so small it disappears from the right side of the fabric, leaving a very clean attachment on the lining.
    *   Follow the same positioning and stitching technique as hand-stitching, but use the machine.

3.  **Adhesive (Use with Caution - Best for Temporary/Heavier Liners):**
    *   **Choose Wisely:** Use a high-quality, heavy-duty **fabric glue** specifically designed for sewing or leather. Avoid cheap craft glues.
    *   **Test First:** ALWAYS test the glue on an inconspicuous area (like an inside seam) to ensure it doesn't damage the fabric or lining over time.
    *   **Application:** Apply a *very thin* layer to the *loop* side of the Velcro. Press firmly onto the lining for several minutes. Let it cure fully (often 24+ hours) before handling or wearing.
    *   **Potential Issue:** Adhesives can sometimes weaken the fabric fibers over time, especially on synthetic linings. It's less durable than stitching.

**What NOT to Do:**

*   **Don't Stitch Through the Outer Shell:** This will tear the fabric, ruin the lining, and look terrible. Stitch *only* on the lining.
*   **Avoid Heavy Threads or Needles:** They can easily pull through thin linings.
*   **Don't Use Cheap Adhesives:** They are likely to fail, damage the fabric, or leave residue.

**Temporary Solution (While You Wait):**

*   **Safety Pins:** Use strong safety pins to temporarily secure the Velcro's loop side to the lining fabric. This is a quick fix but not permanent.

**Recommendation:** For lasting quality, **hand-stitching** using a small, strong thread and stitching *only* through the lining fabric is your best bet. If you have access to a sewing machine, the **invisible stitch** method offers the cleanest result. Test any adhesive carefully first.
