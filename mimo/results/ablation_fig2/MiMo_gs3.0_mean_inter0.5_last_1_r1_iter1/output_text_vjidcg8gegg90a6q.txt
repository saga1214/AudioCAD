For your winter chicks on a concrete floor with rodents to avoid, here's the best approach:

### Bedding Options:
1. **Large-Flake Pine Shavings on Rubber Mats**  
   - Cut rubber stall mats into strips to line the coop floor/chick area.  
   - Add 6–8 inches of large-flake shavings on top.  
   - Use the **deep litter method**: Replace old shavings layer-by-layer throughout winter. As decomposition occurs, heat will be generated naturally. Sand underneath keeps bedding dry and deters rodents.  

2. **Sand + Rubber Mats**  
   - Layer rubber mats for insulation.  
   - Cover with a thin layer (2–3 inches) of sand. Sand repels rodents, is easy to clean, and prevents moisture buildup.  

### Why This Works on Concrete:
- Rubber mats create an air gap, blocking cold concrete from chilling the chicks.  
- Deep litter/sand insulates better than loose materials rodents love.  
- Sealing cracks (with copper wire or hardware cloth) prevents drafts AND rodent entry.  

### Ventilation Reminders:
- Install vents **high on the coop walls** with a screen to release moisture and prevent chicks/frostbite.  

### Chick Heat Source:
Use a **radiant heat panel** (not a lamp) mounted on the wall. Chicks will huddle near it for warmth. It’s energy-efficient, safe, and avoids rodent risks.  

This setup keeps chicks warm, rodent-proof, and on concrete—without straw or lamps! 🐔🔥
