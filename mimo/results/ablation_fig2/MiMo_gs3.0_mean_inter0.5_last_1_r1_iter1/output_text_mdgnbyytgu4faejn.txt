Of course, I can give you a clear breakdown of the standard resume format.

First is your Header. Center your large 14-point name. To its right, starting with email in paragraph format and icons off, is better than to the side. So the order should be Name, then Email, Phone, City below those. Using paragraph style instead of icons can help automated tracking systems.

Next, a short Professional Summary of about three to four lines right under your name. This should highlight your most relevant skills and career goals.

After that, your Experience section. The best way to handle this is to have two parts. First, a "Relevant Experience" section for the older jobs that are most relevant to the new roles you want, making sure to include dates. Then, an optional "Additional Work History" section for more recent roles that aren't as relevant, where you can just list the job title, company, and dates.

Then comes Education. List your degrees from the same school in chronological order. For each one, include the degree, the school name, your graduation year or expected graduation year, and any academic honors or awards you received, like a scholarship or graduating with highest honors.

If you have a skills section, it's best to place it right after your experience or education. Just list your most relevant skills clearly.

Finally, you can add other optional sections like Certifications or Projects if they are relevant to the jobs you're applying for.

So, to quickly summarize the ideal structure: it starts with your name as the header, followed by a professional summary. Then comes the relevant experience section, followed by additional work history, education, skills, and any other optional sections.
