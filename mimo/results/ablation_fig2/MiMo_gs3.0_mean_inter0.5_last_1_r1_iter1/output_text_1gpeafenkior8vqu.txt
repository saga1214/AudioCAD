Here is a suggested itinerary for a deep exploration of Tokyo and the Kansai region, including a day trip to Nara and a visit to Kyoto.

First, let's talk about Tokyo, which you could spend four to five days on.
Day one could be for your arrival and the Asakusa area. In the morning, you could check out Asakusa, see the Senso-ji Temple, and explore Nakamise Street. In the afternoon, you could visit Ueno Park, which has museums and the Ueno Zoo. For the evening, you could have dinner in Ameya-Yokocho.

Day two could focus on Shinjuku and Shibuya. In the morning, you could visit the Meiji Shrine in Harajuku and then explore Omotesando or Takeshita Street. In the afternoon, you could wander through Omoide Yokocho and Golden Gai in Shinjuku and visit the Metropolitan Government Building for a view. In the evening, you could experience Shibuya Crossing and maybe have dinner at a ramen spot.

On day three, you could do Akihabara and the Imperial Palace. In the morning, you can explore Akihabara for its anime and electronics. In the afternoon, you can tour the outer grounds of the Imperial Palace and visit National Museum of Tokyo. For the evening, you could go to the teamLab digital art museum or Shinjuku’s bars.

For day four, you could plan a day trip. You have two options: Nikko or Kamakura. Both are about two to two and a half hours by train. You can visit Toshogu Shrine in Nikko or the Great Buddha in Kamakura.

If you have a fifth day, you could explore more of an area like Ginza for luxury shopping, or the Tsukiji Outer Market and 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art if you're interested in modern art for your school presentation.

Now, for the Osaka and Kansai region, you could plan three to four days.
On day five, you can arrive in Osaka. In the morning, visit Osaka Castle and the park around it. In the afternoon, explore Dotonbori for food and the Shinsekai area. In the evening, you could walk along the canal near the Osaka Aquarium.

Day six could be a full day trip to Nara. In the morning, you can take the train to Nara and visit the Todai-ji Temple with its giant Buddha and feed the friendly deer. In the afternoon, you can see the Kasuga Shrine and explore Nara Park. You can return to Osaka in the evening for some street food.

Day seven could be for Kyoto. In the morning, take a train to Kyoto and visit the Fushimi Inari Shrine with its famous red gates. In the afternoon, you can explore the Gion district and see Kiyomizu-dera Temple. You could stay in Kyoto for the night or return to Osaka.

On day eight, you could take a day trip from Osaka to Kyoto again. In the morning, visit Arashiyama with its Bamboo Forest and Tenryu-ji Temple. In the afternoon, you can see Kinkaku-ji, also known as the Golden Pavilion. You can return to Osaka in the evening.

Finally, day nine would be for your departure. You can do some last-minute shopping in Shinsaibashi or Kuromon Market, and then check out in the afternoon.

This itinerary gives you a good balance between Tokyo's modern energy and the historic charm of the Kansai region, including deep dives into Osaka, Nara, and Kyoto. You can adjust it based on your interests, like adding more art-focused spots if needed for your presentation. Enjoy your holiday!
