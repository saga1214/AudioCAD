A "Brownie Olympics" format in the cafeteria is a fantastic idea! Here's why it works and how to execute it:

1.  **Cost-Effective & Flexible:** Uses your existing meeting space (cafeteria) – no extra venue costs. Activities can be simple relay races (egg & spoon, sack race, three-legged race), scavenger hunts (around the cafeteria or nearby park), or skill challenges (tying knots, basic first aid scenarios like bandaging). Keep equipment minimal (sack bags, eggs, string, scavenger list).

2.  **Dietary Friendly:** Easily accommodate chips & dip (plantain chips if you need), sundaes (ice cream sandwiches or dairy-free options), pizza (small slices), and other small snacks. Focus on simpler, less dairy-heavy options.

3.  **Inclusive for Parents:** Parents can easily join in as "Olympians" or cheer from the sidelines. You can designate specific parent helpers for setup/cleanup or to run one station.

4.  **Makes Snacks Simple:** Instead of a big meal, plan small, fun snack stations:
    *   **"Build Your Own" Stations:** DIY sundae bar (ice cream sandwiches, yogurt, sprinkles, fruit), DIY pizza station (pre-sliced cheese/pizza toppings for assembly), or a big chips & dip spread.
    *   **Game Prizes:** Offer small, fun prizes (stickers, mini toys, craft supplies) for winners. This is often more exciting than a big meal.

5.  **Keeps it Short & Sweet:** A 2-3 hour event fits perfectly into a regular meeting slot. End with a quick awards ceremony (for fun, not just competition) and a group photo.

**Key Tips for Your Celebration:**

*   **Communicate Early:** Tell parents about the plan, the activities, and the snack options/prizes so they can prepare (especially sashes/dietary needs).
*   **Delegate:** Assign simple tasks to parents (setting up snack stations, helping with one relay, cleaning up).
*   **Focus on Fun & Achievement:** Celebrate the girls' growth, badge progress, and teamwork throughout the Olympics, not just competition winners.
*   **Keep it Light:** Emphasize participation and laughter over intense competition. Ensure everyone feels included.

This approach directly addresses your concerns about cost, dietary needs, logistics, and involving parents, while creating a memorable and fun end-of-year event celebrating your daughters' involvement! You've got this – being apprehensive is normal, but you'll make it okay.
