### Switching Software & AI Tools
*   **Switching Software:** Start by learning Audition's core features (noise reduction, EQ, comping) *while still using Audacity*. Practice one task at a time. Consider Audition's free 7-day trial first. The learning curve *is* real, but the efficiency gains (especially with multi-track editing and advanced effects) are significant long-term.
*   **AI Tools:** Yes, reliable options exist:
    *   **Trim Silence:** Audition's built-in tool is excellent. Alternatives: iZotope RX (paid, industry standard), Descript's "Edit Gaps" (free, great for dialogue editing), Audacity's "Noise Reduction" (free, manual).
    *   **Labeling:** Descript (free tier) is fantastic for automatic transcription and labeling (find, replace, edit). Otter.ai (paid) is also strong for transcription. Adobe Audition's "Speech to Text" is improving but less consistent than Descript/Otter for complex audio.
*   **Organization:** Use consistent folder structures: `ProjectName/Master > Recording > Takes > Edit > Final > Deliverables`. Use project notes/docs. Leverage Audition's "Projects" window or Descript's organization features. Batch process similar tasks.

### Vocal Endurance & Burnout
*   **Warmups/Cooldowns:** Non-negotiable. 10-15 mins daily: lip trills, humming, tongue twisters, scales. Cool down with gentle humming.
*   **Breathing:** Practice diaphragmatic breathing ("belly breathing"). Record in shorter, focused bursts (e.g., 10-15 mins max). Hydrate well (room temp water!).
*   **Rest:** Take 5-10 min breaks *every* 20-30 minutes. Eat lunch away from the mic. Set hard start/end times for sessions. Prioritize sleep.

### Making Your Demo Memorable
*   **Strong Hook:** Start with a compelling character voice or line that grabs attention immediately.
*   **Show Range:** Within the short clip, demonstrate 2-3 distinct vocal qualities (e.g., warm narrator, sharp villain, playful character).
*   **Clarity & Polish:** Flawless delivery is paramount. No stumbles, clicks, or background noise. High-quality recording and editing are essential.
*   **Punchy & Engaging:** End with a strong, memorable line or sound. Make listeners want to know what happens next.
