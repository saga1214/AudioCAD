Okay, mom-to juggler mom, I've put together a weekly practice plan for you. It's designed to be manageable and to reduce that panic by focusing on steady, stress-free learning. The main goal is three throws with beanbags.

Here’s the schedule for a week. Aim for about six to eight short sessions, about fifteen to twenty-five minutes each, spread out so you don't get overwhelmed. Let's break it down by day:

On Monday, you could start with a warm-up of about five minutes. Toss one ball from your dominant hand, then when its peak, toss the one from the other, peak that one, then add the last ball. Don't catch yet, just focus on the rhythm. Then, spend about ten to fifteen minutes just practicing that first sequence we talked about. Finally, switch to scarves for five minutes to feel that slow, deliberate motion.

Tuesday could be a rest day, or you could do a light warm-up and use scarves to practice your one-hand two-bag rhythm with music if you like.

On Wednesday, you could do a warm-up of five minutes juggling your one-hand two-bags skill, then spend about fifteen minutes tossing that first three-throw sequence, still just focusing on the rhythm and not catching.

Thursday is another good day for scarves, maybe fifteen minutes to really focus on the slow timing and catching.

For Friday, you could try to catch. Warm up, then spend fifteen to twenty minutes trying to catch the balls as they come down. Be gentle and patient.

Saturday is for three throws with beanbags. Start with short bursts, like two or three throws, then take a break. Gradually increase the number of successful throws in a row. End with five minutes of scarves to relax.

Finally, Sunday is a full rest day. It's important to let your brain and hands recover to avoid frustration.

Just remember a few key tips: start small, focus on rhythm over speed, and be kind to yourself. Panic comes from pressure, so creating a gentle, consistent routine will help you and fulfill that promise to your son. You can do this!
