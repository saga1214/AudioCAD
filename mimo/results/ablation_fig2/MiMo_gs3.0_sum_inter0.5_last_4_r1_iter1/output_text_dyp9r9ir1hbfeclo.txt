You're right to double-check! While three bones work *together for rotation*, there's actually a fourth component that completes the shoulder girdle: **the scapula itself**.  

Here’s the breakdown:  
1. **Clavicle (collarbone)**: Connects your arm to your sternum and stabilizes the shoulder joint.  
2. **Scapula (shoulder blade)**: Houses the socket for the humerus and moves to position the arm.  
3. **Humerus (upper arm bone)**: Rotates within the scapula’s socket.  
4. **Rotator Cuff Muscles**: Act as a dynamic stabilizer for all these bones during movement.  

**Key Takeaway**:  
- **Bones for rotation**: Humerus + Scapula + Clavicle.  
- **Rotator Cuff’s role**: Stabilizes the humerus in the scapula’s socket during *all* motions (lifting, rotating, reaching).  

If your friend feels soreness during lateral raises:  
- **Normal soreness**: Muscle fatigue (eases with stretching/exercise).  
- **Rotator cuff concern**: Sharp pain, weakness, or "pinching" that worsens with rotation. **Stop the exercise and consult a doctor if this persists.**
