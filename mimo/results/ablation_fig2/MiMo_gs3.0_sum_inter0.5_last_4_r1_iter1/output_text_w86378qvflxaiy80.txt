That's a really important question. While it's hard to say there's just one single most important aspect, there are a few key reasons why preservation is so critical.

First, there's the sheer irreplaceability of these species. As Norman Myers himself later put it, extinction is forever. Once a species is gone, it's gone for good. There is no 'do-over.'

Second, as we've discussed, they are the foundation of critical ecosystem services. They are vital for the water, food, and climate stability that billions of people depend on.

Third, they are like our planet's living heritage. Hotspots are home to plant families that are millions of years old. They're like a living fossil record of our planet's evolutionary history. Losing them is like losing a piece of our own past.

And finally, they hold a kind of potential we haven't even discovered yet. Many of the unique species in hotspots could hold the key to new medicines or technologies that could one day save lives or solve major problems for humanity.

So, really, it's about protecting irreplaceable, ancient life that is fundamental to our own survival and holds a future we can't even imagine yet.
