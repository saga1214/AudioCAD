Alright, let's break down those two analogies for you.

First, there was the 'throwing a party' version. In that one:
Milk was the big, empty room.
Good bacteria were the party guests.
Keeping it warm energized the guests.
Sugar in the milk was their party food.
Thickness and tanginess came from everyone linking into a Group Hug.
And overheating the yogurt was like making the room too hot, which kills the bacteria and leaves you with just warm milk.

Then, the second analogy was about yogurt as an organized net:
Milk was the disorganized crowd.
Bacteria were the tiny team coaches.
Warming it up energized the coaches.
Flavoring was like the team's uniforms.
And the net itself was a mesh of milk proteins that the coaches get everyone to hold hands and form. This net traps the liquid, turning the runny milk into a solid yogurt.

Basically, both analogies get to the same technical points: bacteria eating sugar in warm milk causes proteins to bond, which results in a thick, solid product. The first one uses a social gathering idea, and the second uses a sports team or net idea. Hope that helps clear it up!
