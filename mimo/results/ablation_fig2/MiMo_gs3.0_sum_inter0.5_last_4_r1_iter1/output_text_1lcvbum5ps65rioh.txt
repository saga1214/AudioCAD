**1. U-Turn Rules in British Columbia & Penalties:**

*   **General Rule:** U-turns are generally permitted, but **only** when:
    *   You have a clear and unobstructed view of both directions of traffic for at least 150 meters (about 500 feet) in each direction (or a distance proportional to the road width).
    *   You are not interfering with traffic already in the intersection or crossing the intersection.
    *   You are not on a curve, hill, or near an intersection (unless explicitly permitted by a sign).
    *   You are not in a no U-turn zone (check road signs).
    *   You are not on a highway or restricted roadway (specific rules apply there).
*   **Signage is Key:** Many roads have signs prohibiting U-turns at intersections or during certain times. **Always obey road signs.**
*   **Penalties for Illegal U-Turns:**
    *   **Ticket:** A ticket for an illegal U-turn in BC typically costs around **$116** (as of late Guarantor: 2023, subject to change). This includes a 1 point on your driver's license.
    *   **Insurance Impact:** Accumulating too many traffic violations (including points) can potentially lead to increased insurance premiums.
*   **"If you forget or make a wrong one":** If you accidentally perform an illegal U-turn, the penalty is based on the violation, not intent. The standard fine applies. It's always best to avoid the illegal turn if possible.

**2. Spaghetti Carbonara Ingredients:**

Here's a classic, minimal-ingredient list for Spaghetti Carbonara:

*   **Pasta:** Spaghetti (or other long pasta like Bucatini)
*   **Eggs:** Large eggs (typically 3-4 per person, depending on appetite). **Room temperature eggs blend better.**
*   **Cheese:** **Pecorino Romano** (the traditional choice, sharp and salty) or a mix of Pecorino Romano and Parmesan. Grated finely. (About 1/2 cup grated per 4 servings).
*   **Cured Pork:**
    *   **Guanciale:** The *ideal* ingredient (cured pork cheek), but often hard to find outside Italian delis. If unavailable...
    *   **Pancetta:** A good Italian substitute (cured pork belly).
    *   **Bacon:** A common non-Italian substitute (use thick-cut, smoked bacon). *Avoid regular pork belly bacon for this dish.*
*   **Black Pepper:** Freshly ground, generously.
*   **Salt:** For seasoning pasta water and (if needed) the cured pork.
*   **Optional but Recommended:**
    *   **Freshly Ground Black Pepper:** A lot of it!
    *   **Parsley:** Chopped fresh parsley for garnish (traditional but optional).
    *   **Red Pepper Flakes:** A pinch for a subtle heat.

**Key Tips for Cooking Carbonara:**

1.  **Cook Pasta Al Dente:** Reserve about 1 cup of the starchy pasta water before draining.
2.  **Render the Pork:** In a large skillet over medium-low heat, cook the diced guanciale/pancetta/bacon until crispy and the fat has rendered out. Season lightly with salt if needed. Remove from heat (or lower heat significantly).
3.  **Temper the Eggs:** In a large bowl, vigorously whisk the eggs, grated cheese, and a *generous* amount of black pepper together. While still over the *warm* (but not hot) rendered pork fat (or just off the heat), slowly drizzle in the egg mixture while whisking constantly. This helps prevent scrambling.
4.  **Combine:** Add the drained pasta (and a splash of the reserved pasta water) directly to the egg/cheese mixture in the skillet with the pork fat. Toss quickly and vigorously over very low heat (or off the heat) until the pasta is evenly coated and the eggs are warmed through and slightly creamy. The residual heat is key – don't let it cook on high heat!
5.  **Finish & Serve:** Remove from heat immediately. Stir in chopped parsley (if using). Serve immediately, topped with extra cheese and black pepper.

Enjoy your delicious Spaghetti Carbonara!
