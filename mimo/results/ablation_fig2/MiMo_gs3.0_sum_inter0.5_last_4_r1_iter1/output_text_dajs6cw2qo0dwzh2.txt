That's a very insightful counterfactual. But no, I don't think it would have happened at the same time regardless of Epirus.

The Portuguese were indeed developing new sea routes, but the Ottoman conquest was a massive catalyst that dramatically accelerated everything. It removed the legitimacy and stability of the old system, it intensified the competition between kingdoms, and it created an immediate, urgent need to bypass the Ottomans. Without that event, the push might have been slower and less focused. So, the conquest was the spark that lit the fire.
