You're absolutely right to question that assumption! At rink temperatures of **minus 10°C**, the coefficient of friction is **not** as low as 0.005 or 0.008. Instead, it typically increases slightly to a range of **0.02–0.05**. Here's why:

### Key Factors at Extreme Cold:
1. **Harder Ice Surface**:  
   Below -10°C, ice becomes more brittle and rigid. The blade's sharp edge presses into the ice more directly, increasing friction as it "grinds" rather than glides smoothly.

2. **Reduced Thin-Film Lubrication**:  
   At warmer rink temps (e.g., -5°C), a thin layer of liquid water forms between the blade and ice, acting as a lubricant. At -10°C, this layer **disappears**, removing critical low-friction support.

3. **Pressure Effects Diminish**:  
   Sharpness enhances pressure, but at very low temperatures, the blade's ability to "cut" the ice is reduced. Friction rises as the blade drags along the surface.

### Comparison to Warmer Rinks:
- **-5°C (typical rink)**: μ ≈ **0.01–0.02** (optimal for gliding).  
- **-10°C**: μ ≈ **0.02–0.05** (noticeably higher friction).  
- **-20°C (near freezing point)**: μ ≈ **0.05–0.1** (poor gliding conditions).

### Practical Implications for Skaters:
- **Pushing Power**: At -10°C, skaters need more force to accelerate (higher rolling resistance).  
- **Control**: Friction increases make sharp turns or stops slightly harder.  
- **Safety**: Higher friction reduces slipping risk but demands precise balance.

### Why Not Lower Friction?
Contrary to intuition, **extreme cold does not magically reduce friction**—it often does the opposite. The "superlubricity" effect (μ < 0.01) only occurs in highly specialized conditions (e.g., liquid-metal coatings or ultra-cold vacuum systems), not in real-world ice skating.

For reference, studies (like those from *Journal of Sports Sciences*) confirm that **consistency is key**—competition rinks maintain -6 to -7°C for optimal μ ≈ 0.015, balancing glide and control. If you’re testing blades at -10°C, expect **2–5× higher friction** than at typical rinks! 🧊⛸️
