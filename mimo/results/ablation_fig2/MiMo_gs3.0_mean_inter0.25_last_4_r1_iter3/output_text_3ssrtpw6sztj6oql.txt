For your paper, the clearest and most defensible answer for an art museum's **first line of defense** is:  

**Controlling all physical entry points through visible security presence (e.g., guards) and screening procedures (e.g., bag checks).**  

### Why this is the primary answer:
1. **Proactive Prevention**: It aims to *prevent* unauthorized access before threats enter the building.  
2. **Deterrence**: A visible security team discourages opportunistic theft.  
3. **Manage Access**: Screening exactly who and what enters is foundational to all museum security.  

While high-tech cameras, alarms, and secure displays are critical *secondary* layers, they all operate *after* an intruder has bypassed the initial access control. Modern security prioritizes stopping the threat at the perimeter first. For famous cases like the Gardner heist, human error occurred at an *entry* point (guards letting in disguised intruders), reinforcing that the first line must focus on managing access.
