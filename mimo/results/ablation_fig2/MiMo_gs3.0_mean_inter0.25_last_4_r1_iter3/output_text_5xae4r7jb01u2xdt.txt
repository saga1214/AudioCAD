Got it. This scenario will highlight a key difference between college and high school rules, specifically regarding indirect free kicks. In college, an offside player who isn't involved can have an indirect free kick placed on the spot. In high school, it's often a penalty kick.

Here’s the scenario: Red Team and Blue Team are in the attacking half. Red Team defender number two deliberately touches the ball. The ball then deflects or rebounds off him, and Blue Team forward number nine, who was already in an offside position, gets the rebound and scores.

In a college certification exam, you need to know that in this situation, because the defender's deliberate play reset the situation for attacker number nine, there was no offside offense. Play continues.

However, because a defender's touch occurred, a referee would place the ball on the spot where the deliberate play happened and have a direct free kick taken. Since this happened in the attacking half, it's an indirect free kick. The attacking Blue Team would then have possession and play from that spot.

In a high school game, the rule might differ. The offside offense would likely still apply, and a penalty kick would be awarded to the non-offending team, Red Team, to be taken from the penalty spot. 

Knowing this distinction is a great point to include on your college referee certification.
