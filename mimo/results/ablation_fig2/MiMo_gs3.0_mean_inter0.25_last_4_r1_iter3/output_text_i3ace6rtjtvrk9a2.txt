Now, to your main question: can the eye see continuously? 

In a way, yes, but with some important caveats. The eye is always processing information, but the brain actively fills in the gaps between those moments of high focus, which are the saccades. So, what we perceive as continuous vision is actually the brain's interpretation of the eye's data. 

During a saccade, the eye’s central focus is briefly shifted, and the brain temporarily suppresses that visual information to prevent motion sickness or blurring. This means that during a fast saccade, your brain might not fully register what’s happening in that tiny moment. However, it does process the images between saccades to create a smooth, continuous experience. 

So, the eye itself isn’t seeing in a continuous, unbroken stream like a camera. It’s more like a series of snapshots that the brain then seamlessly stitches together into a continuous, fluid experience.
