Of course. Here is a clear, standard resume format you can follow. Just remember to replace the examples with your own information.

First, at the very top, is your name in about a fourteen-point font.

Right below your name is the contact information section. I suggest putting this directly below your name rather than to the side. It reads clearly: Job Title, followed by Company, Dates of Employment, Location, Job Title, Company, Dates, Location, and so on. You can include icons, email, phone number, city, and LinkedIn, using nine-point font.

Next is the Professional Summary. This is a short paragraph of two or three sentences where you highlight your most relevant skills and experience, and state your career goals.

Then, you have the Experience section. Here you list each job, including the company, your title, the dates you worked there, and a few key bullet points describing your main responsibilities and achievements.

After that comes Education. You list your degree, the school, the dates, and any relevant awards or honors you received, like a scholarship or graduating with highest honors.

If you have many older roles you want to highlight, you can create a section called "Relevant Experience" and list them there.

You can also add a Skills section. This is very important. List key skills relevant to the job you're applying for, such as technical skills, software you're proficient in, and languages you speak.

Finally, you can have an optional "Additional Information" section for things like your certifications, professional memberships, or volunteer work.

This structure gives you a professional format that clearly shows your relevant experience and skills, no matter how long ago you earned them.
