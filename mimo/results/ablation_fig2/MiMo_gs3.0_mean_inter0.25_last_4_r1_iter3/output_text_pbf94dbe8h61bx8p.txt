Okay, so you want simple, meaningful Christmas gift ideas that you can all craft together, tailored for different ages. Macramé and cookies have been part of the mix, but you're looking to expand a bit while keeping things manageable. Let’s brainstorm some ideas that are hands-on, inclusive, and fit different skill levels.

First, building on your macramé passion, you could do mini plant hanger "workshops" for friends or family who love plants too. Maybe package them in a cute kit with pre-measured cord and small plants. That way, you love making them day-in/day-out without opening a store pressure.

For the cookie situation, drop cookies or no-bake options like chocolate-dipped pretzels with sprinkles could be a lifesaver. Minimal rolling, minimal mess, but still festive.

Getting the kids on board is key. Stations are a great idea. For macramé, the little ones could do simple knots while older kids assemble hangers. Pine cone bird feeders are a solid alternative. You’re right to question the peanut butter—using birdseed alone or a dry glue (like Mod Podge) would be safer and avoid any ickiness. It’s also a great outdoor activity.

Other simple, inclusive crafts:
- **Fingerprint holiday cards**: Each kid can create their own card with their fingerprint "drawing," then write a message. Meaningful and no tools needed.
- **Wreath-making**: Use pipe cleaners, buttons, and yarn. Older kids can build the wreath, younger ones can decorate sections. You can even make it religious-themed if appropriate.
- **DIY ornaments**: Like paper snowflakes cut and hung with string, or painted pine cones (no sticky peanut butter).

The goal is to let each kid contribute in a way that suits their age and interests, so everyone feels involved. Minimal stress, maximum holiday spirit. Does one of these ideas feel like a good starting point?
