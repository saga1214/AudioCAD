Dealing with a difficult person can be incredibly draining, but these strategies might help you manage the stress and protect your peace:

### 1. **Set Firm Boundaries**
   - **Limit interactions**: Avoid direct engagement if possible. A simple "No comment" or brief acknowledgment suffices.
   - **Document incidents**: Keep a record (dates/times of reports) if CPS/police get involved. This could be useful later.

### 2. **Focus on What You Control**
   - **Prioritize safety**: Keep kids indoors during known triggers, ride bikes in less visible areas.
   - **Support the community**: Coordinate with affected neighbors to report harassment collectively (if applicable).

### 3. **Practice Emotional Detachment**
   - **Reframe the situation**: Remind yourself: *"Their behavior reflects their issues, not my worth."*
   - **Limit exposure**: Reduce news/social media about the issue if it fuels anxiety.

### 4. **Channel Energy Positively**
   - **Lean into joy**: Use school events (Halloween/Costumes) and music (like Forrest Frank) as emotional anchors. These small wins build resilience.
   - **Community connection**: Bond with other parents over shared frustrations or solutions.

### 5. **Seek Support**
   - **Talk it out**: Vent to trusted friends/family—sharing reduces isolation.
   - **Professional help**: Stress/anxiety can be overwhelming; therapy can provide tailored coping tools.

### 6. **Know When to Escalate**
   - **Involve authorities**: CPS involvement means the system is now engaged—focus on supporting kids while professionals handle it.
   - **Legal options**: If reports persist, consult a lawyer about nuisance laws (varies by location).

### Remember:
> "You cannot control their actions, but you can control your reactions. Protect your peace like it’s the rare gem it is." 💎
