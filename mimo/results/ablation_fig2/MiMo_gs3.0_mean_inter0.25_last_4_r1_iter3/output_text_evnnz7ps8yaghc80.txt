It sounds like you're feeling stressed specifically because juggling three objects feels elusive after nailing two. Stress blocks learning for anyone, so easing up is key. Transition methods depend on where your two-bag game is weak. If your two-ball throws from one hand are solid, add the third ball incrementally like I mentioned. If your rhythm is shaky, scarves are perfect because their slow descent forces you to focus on timing, not speed. Beanbags are forgiving on the catch, but scarves demand better control. Using both actually complements each other well. Now, for the schedule... Here's a balanced plan: Aim for five to six practice days a week, maybe fifteen to twenty-five minutes each session. On Monday, Wednesday, Friday, and Sunday, you could focus on beanbags, alternating between one-hand two-ball practice and transitioning to three bags. On Tuesday and Thursday, dedicate those sessions to scarves, focusing purely on rhythm and pattern. If you have a son to teach, adding short 'teaching' sessions even when things go wrong can be motivating and realistic. The most important thing is to be consistent and kind to yourself. Panic is normal, but small, scheduled wins add up.
