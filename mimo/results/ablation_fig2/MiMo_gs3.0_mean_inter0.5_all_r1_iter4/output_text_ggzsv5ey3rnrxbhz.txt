Okay, so for the five products to avoid to prevent getting Forever Chemicals, the top products would be... non-stick cookware, grease-resistant food packaging, stain-resistant or waterproof items like carpets and outdoor clothing, certain detergents and cosmetics, and tap water. Using a certified water filter helps a lot. PFAS stands for per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, and no, they're part of a large family of chemicals, not just one. And you're right to be cautious, bottled water can also contain them, so a home water filter is best. Detergents can be a source, look for 'PFAS-free' labels. Regarding fish, yes, they can concentrate in certain fish in polluted water. Toilet paper can have trace amounts from processing. House dust can also contain them. And yes, some electronics coatings may contain them, but direct exposure is low. For your question about removing them, there's no sure way to remove PFAS from your body, but reducing ongoing exposure is the best you can do. A healthy diet and staying hydrated are good for overall health.
