Okay, deep breath. Let's break this down methodically. With a simple toolbox tackling a deep blockage, patience and the right technique are key. Here's a step-by-step approach:

1.  **Visual Inspection & Confirmation:** Before diving in, confirm it's a *deep* blockage. Look down the drain. Is there a visible solid object (like a sock, rag, or piece of foil) partially blocking it? Or is it a slow, grime-heavy drain that just won't flush? The plunger *might* dislodge a partial blockage near the surface, but for a deep one, it's usually ineffective.

2.  **Cable Drain Snake (Auger):** This is your most likely tool in a plumber's simple box.
    *   **Start Shallow:** Insert the cable tip gently. Don't force it.
    *   **Find the Bowl:** Rotate the handle slowly until you feel resistance or the cable hits the "bowl" (the U-shaped curve inside the drain pipe). This is crucial.
    *   **Scrape & Retrieve:** Once you hit the bowl, push the cable tip *through* it. Keep applying steady, moderate pressure. As you push, the cable's hook will likely catch the blockage. Keep pushing until you feel it dislodge or the cable catches firmly on the object.
    *   **Retract Carefully:** Slowly pull the cable back out. You should feel resistance or see debris coming out. If it's stuck, try rotating the handle a bit while pulling.
    *   **Clear the Cable:** Once the cable is mostly out, use the pointy end or a small hand tool to scrape any remaining grime or debris off the cable's hook and shaft. Wipe it clean.

3.  **Liquid Drain Cleaner (Use with Caution):** If the snake didn't fully clear it and you have a liquid cleaner:
    *   **Ventilation is CRITICAL:** Open any nearby windows or doors for ventilation. Plumbers hate working in toxic clouds.
    *   **Read the Label:** Follow the instructions *exactly*. Most require pouring it down the drain, letting it sit for 15-30 mins, then flushing with very hot water.
    *   **Gloves & Eye Protection:** Wear rubber gloves and safety glasses. This stuff is harsh.
    *   **Flush Thoroughly:** After the dwell time, flush with a large amount of hot water. Don't use too much cold water initially, as it can slow the reaction. Flush again once more to ensure everything is gone. Be prepared for some unpleasant smells and potential pipe corrosion over time (though simple box cleaners are usually less aggressive).
    *   **Check:** Flush again slowly with just water to see if it's clear.

4.  **Manual Removal (Last Resort with Simple Tools):**
    *   **Safety First:** Wear gloves. The water will be cold and dirty.
    *   **Dipper/Hand:** If you can see the blockage and it's accessible (e.g., in a small sink or tub), use a sturdy spoon, a wire hanger bent into a hook, or even your fingers (gloved!) to try and pull out any visible solid debris.
    *   **Pipe Wrench (Use Extremely Carefully):** If the blockage is *very* deep and nothing else works, and you suspect it might be a large object (like a piece of cloth or a toy), *very gently* try to use a pipe wrench on the drain opening to twist and dislodge it. **WARNING:** This risks damaging the drain pipe or the fixture's drain flange. Only do this if you're confident and know it's likely a large object. Forcing it can cause leaks.
