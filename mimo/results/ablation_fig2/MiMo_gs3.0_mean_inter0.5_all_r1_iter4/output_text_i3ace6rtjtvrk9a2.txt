### Eye vs. Brain: The Perception Divide
1. **Eye as a Sensor:**  
   - The eye captures light via photoreceptors (rods for low light, cones for color) and transmits signals via the optic nerve.  
   - It **does not** "see" continuously. During saccades (rapid eye movements), the fovea (central vision) is momentarily "blind" for ~200 milliseconds.  

2. **Brain as a Processor:**  
   - The brain’s visual cortex **constructs** perception from eye signals.  
   - It uses **motion interpolation** and **temporal smoothing** to create the illusion of continuous motion.  
   - During saccades, the brain suppresses blur (saccadic masking) to prevent dizziness.  

---

### Why "Continuous" Vision is a *Perceptual Illusion*
- **Reality:** The eye samples motion in discrete "frames" (like a camera), but the brain fills gaps between samples.  
- **Example:**  
  - If you watch a 24 fps film, your eye sees 24 frames per second, but your brain **perceives** fluid motion because it interpolates in between frames.  
  - If you move your eyes rapidly (a saccade), your brain **still** perceives continuous vision because it masks the "gaps" in visual input.  

---

### Key Takeaways for Filmmaking
1. **35mm at 24 fps** leverages the brain’s interpolation. This creates the "cinematic" dreamlike quality.  
2. **Video at 30/25 fps** relies on the same brain mechanics but feels "more real-time" due to higher sampling.  
3. **Saccadic masking** explains why:  
   - High frame rates (60+ fps) feel smoother because they reduce the brain’s need to "fill in" gaps.  
   - Low frame rates (e.g., 16 fps minimum) start to feel jerky because the brain struggles to interpolate.  

In short:  
- **Eye:** Samples discrete frames.  
- **Brain:** Generates the *illusion* of continuity.  

This distinction is crucial for your work! Understanding how the brain processes frames helps you choose the right medium for emotional impact.
