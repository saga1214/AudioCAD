Alright, let's break down both analogies we used.

First, the party analogy: 
Milk is the empty room, good bacteria guests, keeping it warm energizes them. They link-armed group-hugging turns the milk tangy and thick into yogurt.

Second, the net analogy:
Milk is the disorganized crowd, bacteria are tiny coaches. Making it warm energizes the coaches, who get the crowd to hold hands and form a tight net. That net traps the liquid, turning the runny milk into a thick yogurt. The flavoring is like the team's uniforms that give it an identity.

Now, for the technical explanation based on all that:
Good bacteria eating sugar causes everyone to form group hugs in the milk, making it thick yogurt.
Or, the bacteria energizing sugar turns everyone into a tight net that traps liquid, making thick yogurt.

Basically, both analogies describe the same technical process: bacterial fermentation thickens milk by either forming a group hug or a net.
