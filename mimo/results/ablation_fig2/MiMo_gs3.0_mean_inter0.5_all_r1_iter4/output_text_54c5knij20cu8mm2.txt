### 2. Reasoning Styles: Deductive vs. Inductive  
- **Deductive (Top-Down)**:  
  - Starts with general principles → reaches specific conclusions.  
  - *Example*: "All mammals breathe air. Whales are mammals. Therefore, whales breathe air."  
  - **Physiological basis**: Associated with prefrontal cortex activation for logical deduction.  

- **Inductive (Bottom-Up)**:  
  - Starts with specific observations → infers general patterns.  
  - *Example*: "I saw 10 swans; they're white. Therefore, all swans are white."  
  - **Physiological basis**: Linked to pattern-recognition networks in the temporal lobes.  

- **Other Reasoning Styles**:  
  - **Abductive**: Forming the best *explanation* from incomplete data (e.g., doctors diagnosing symptoms).  
  - **Analogical**: Drawing conclusions by comparing dissimilar things (e.g., "The universe is like a clock").  
  - **Critical Thinking**: Integrates deductive/inductive/abductive reasoning.  

> **Is the brain wired differently?**  
> Neuroimaging shows overlapping brain regions for all reasoning styles, but *tendencies* may develop through experience. Genetics play a role, but environment shapes neural pathways. It’s not a rigid "left vs. right brain" dichotomy—both hemispheres collaborate.

### 3. Dyslexia Clarifications  
Using your "laugh" example:  
- **Phonological dyslexia**: Struggles to sound out "laugh" (k-uh-f).  
- **Surface dyslexia**: Relies on sounding out, so irregular words like "laugh" (which *doesn’t* sound like "l-uh-g") confuse them.  
- **Visual dyslexia**: May see "laugh" as "laguh" due to letter distortion.

### 4. Your Writing Quirk  
The habit of inserting letters from the next word is called **letter transposition**. It’s common in dysgraphia or developmental dyslexia but isn’t a standalone diagnosis. It often stems from:  
- Rapid motor planning during writing.  
- Visual-spatial processing differences.
