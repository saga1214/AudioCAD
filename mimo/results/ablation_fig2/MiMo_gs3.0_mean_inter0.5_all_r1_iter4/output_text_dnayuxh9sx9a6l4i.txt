   - *Hybrid Option*: Poodle mixes (Goldendoodles, Cockapoodles).  

3. **Bichon Frise**  
   - *Traits*: Cheerful, adaptable, minimal barking. Easy to groom. Great with kids and other pets.  

4. **English Cocker Spaniel**  
   - *Traits*: Sweet-natured, low-shedding. Quiet indoors; crate training helps during absences.  

5. **Bulldog/Pug Mixes**  
   - *Traits*: Low-energy, affectionate, minimal barking. Avoid Pug-specific mixes (e.g., French Bulldog mixes) if noise is a concern.  

6. **Labrador Retriever** (or **Lab Mix**)  
   - *Traits*: Friendly, trainable, great with kids. Size: 40–60 lbs (within "not too big"). Noise: Usually quiet when alone if well-exercised.  

7. **Golden Retriever**  
   - *Size*: 55–75 lbs (slightly over landlord’s "big," but worth checking if spouse is flexible).  
   - *Traits*: Outgoing, patient, highly trainable. Quiet when left alone if exercised.  

### Rescue/Hybrid Focus  
- **Search Terms**: Use "low-shedding," "apartment-friendly," or "easy-going" in rescue ads.  
- **Rescue Directories**: Petfinder, Adopt-a-Pet, or breed-specific rescues (e.g., Cavalier Rescue, Poodle Rescue).  
- **Hybrid Rescues**: Organizations like "Doodle Dads" (for Goldendoodle/Pembroke Corgi mixes) or "Bichon Rescue" networks.  

### Steps to Locate Candidates  
1. **Define Non-Negotiables**:  
   - Size: Max 40 lbs? Noise level scale ( landlord said "not too noisy").  
   - Age: Adult dogs (1–3 yrs) are often calmer, trained, and less high-maintenance.  

2. **Research & Connect**:  
   - Filter shelters/rescues by location, size, and traits (e.g., "social with kids").  
   - Contact breed-specific rescues (e.g., Cavalier Rescue of [State]).  
   - Use apps like Petfinder with filters for "apartment-friendly" or "low-energy."  

3. **Meet & Evaluate**:  
   - Visit 3–5 potential dogs. Observe:  
     - Barking: Does it bark at strangers/doors?  
     - Energy: Does it calm down quickly?  
     - Play style: Does it jump on kids?  
   - Ask about separation anxiety history.  
   - Test crate training or confinement briefly.  

4. **Trial Period**:  
   - Arrange a weekend trial with a foster or temporary home to assess behavior when alone.  

5. **Finalize & Prepare**:  
   - Dog park access? Use it for socialization.  
   - Grooming: Set up a schedule (e.g., Bichons need clipping; Bulldogs need weekly brushing).  
   - Training: Enroll in obedience classes (spouse’s predictability goal).  

### Pro Tips  
- **For spouse’s concern**: Many mixed-breed rescues have purebred parents (e.g., a Lab mix from a Lab parent).  
- **For noise when alone**: Crate training + puzzle toys (e.g., Kongs) can reduce barking.  
- **Cat chasing**: Avoid terriers, hounds, or herding breeds.
