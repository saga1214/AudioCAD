Based on your balcony space, facing, height, and the need for lightweight plants you can carry up five flights of stairs, here's a refined plant selection guide with key things to consider:

### Key Considerations Recap
- **East-facing balcony fifth/f twentieth floor building**: Bright morning sun, moderate wind exposure.
- **Space constraints**: Only a chair + table fit → prioritize **compact, floor-friendly plants**.
- **Weight**: Must be **lightweight and portable** (no heavy, large pots).
- **Temporary stay**: Month-long onsite → choose **forgiving, low-maintenance plants**.
- **Goal**: Make it feel homeier → focus on **color, texture, and ease of care**.

---

### Recommended Houseplants & Why
1. **Bird's Nest Snake Plant (Sansevieria hahnii)**  
   - **Why it fits**:  
     - Compact vertical growth (stays under 12 inches tall, 1–2 feet wide).  
     - Sturdy, architectural leaves (silver-green with dark speckles, "nest"-like center).  
     - **Lightweight**: Small nursery pots (under 2 lbs each).  
   - **Placement**: Perfect for the **floor** of the balcony.  
   - **Care**: Thrives on neglect—water only when soil is bone dry (every 3–4 weeks). Tolerates low light but loves east-facing sun.  

2. **Small Succulents (e.g., Echeveria, Haworthia, or Burro's Tail)**  
   - **Why it fits**:  
     - Delicate, colorful, and add whimsy.  
     - **Lightweight**: Tiny pots (under 1 lb each).  
   - **Placement**: Great on the **balcony table** for visual interest.  
   - **Care**: Needs bright morning sun (east-facing is ideal). Water sparingly—let soil dry completely between waterings.  

3. NYC-Friendly Additions:  
   - **Pansies/Violas**: Compact, colorful, and love morning sun.  
   - **Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum)**: Trailers gracefully, tolerates moderate wind, and thrives in bright light. **Lightweight** (small pots).  

---

### What to Avoid
- **Large Pots/Big Plants**: Geraniums, mint, or pothos can grow large and become unwieldy.  
- **Heavy Plants**: Peace lilies, monstera, or fiddle leaf figs (too bulky for stairs).  
- **Sensitive Plants**: Calatheas or ferns ( NYC humidity/dust can stress them).  

---

### Critical Tips Before Buying
1. **Pot Size Matters**:  
   - Choose **small nursery pots** (4–6 inches diameter) to keep weight low.  
   - Use **lightweight containers** (plastic or unglazed terracotta).  

2. **NYC-Specific Factors**:  
   - **Air Pollution**: Opt for hardy plants (snake plants, succulents).  
   - **Wind**: Ensure plants have sturdy stems (snake plants, geraniums).  

3. **Watering in City Heat**:  
   - East-facing balconies can get hot—water in the evening to prevent scorching.  
   - NYC summer humidity may require slightly more water for succulents.  

4. **Stress-Testing Plants**:  
   - Buy from local nurseries (not grocery stores)—they acclimate better to NYC conditions.  
   - Check for pests (mealybugs, spider mites) before bringing them home.  

---

### Final Plant Suggestions
- **Table Displays**: Pansies, small succulents, spider plants.  
- **Floor Plants**: Bird's nest snake plants (2–3 for coverage).  
- **Total Weight**: 3–5 lbs total (easy to carry up stairs).  

These choices balance aesthetics, resilience, and practicality for your month in NYC! 🌿
