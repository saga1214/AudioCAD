That's wonderful to hear you're looking forward to spring again! Based on your love for that pond and embracing each season so fully, here are a few spring ideas that might spark joy:

1.  **Extend Your Painting:** Obviously! Resume painting the unfrozen pond. You could focus on the vibrant colors of the blooming flowers you mentioned originally, the movement of water, or even the ice patterns melting. Maybe even paint different times of day.
2.  **Pond Scavenger Hunt:** Pack a basket with your paints and brushes, but also a small notebook, pencil, magnifying glass, and a thermos of hot chocolate. Explore the pond area specifically looking for things like:
    *   Specific spring wildflowers or buds.
    *   Early frogs or dragonflies.
    *   Interesting textures on ice if it's just thawing.
    *   Bird nests or tracks.
    *   The perfect spot for a picnic painting.
3.  **Watercolor Exploration:** If you enjoy painting by the pond, maybe try watercolors! They are fantastic for capturing the softness of spring light, the colors of flowers, and reflections on water. It's portable and perfect for that scene.
4.  **"Seasonal" Photography:** Treat your painting spot like a photography subject. Capture the changing light, the first crocus pushing through, the color of the water at different times. You could even sketch *while* you photograph to combine the two.
5.  **Nature Journaling:** Beyond painting, bring a small notebook. Sketch plants, write down sounds you hear (birds, wind), note textures, or simply jot down feelings about the space coming back to life. It deepens your connection.
6.  **A "Spring Renewal" Walk:** Before you paint, take a dedicated walk around the pond area. Observe the changes since winter. Notice what's different, what's new. It sets a wonderful mindful tone for your painting session.
7.  Thermos & Blanket Picnic:** Pack a simple picnic – lemonade is a great idea! – and a cozy blanket. Paint *from* your picnic spot, enjoying the view and the company (even if it's just you).
