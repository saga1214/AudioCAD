You are correct. The ethical aspect of a national department campaigning must heavily weigh public resistance. So, let's re-evaluate vaccination acceptance with a higher ethical weight.

For the sugar campaign, ethics are generally strong, cost is moderate, results are difficult to see, and public support is generally high. 

For the vaccination campaign, while highly effective and cost-efficient, the ethical debate over freedom versus mandate is a major issue, public acceptance is currently low, and misinformation is widespread.

So, if we give the vaccination campaign's public acceptance and ethical weight much more importance, its overall score drops significantly. In that case, the choice would likely shift to the sugar consumption campaign, simply because it avoids such intense ethical controversy and public backlash. The main cost for the vaccination campaign becomes managing public resistance.
