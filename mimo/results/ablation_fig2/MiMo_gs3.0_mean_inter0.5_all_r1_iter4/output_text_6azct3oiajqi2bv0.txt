Here's a reliable method to attach Velcro without damaging the lining, focusing on a no-sew approach:

**Best Method: Iron-On Adhesive Hook & Loop Strips (Velcro)**

1.  **Choose the Right Velcro:**
    *   Get **Velcro brand hook-and-loop strips** (not generic). Look for **high-quality** strips.
    *   Choose the **hook side** (the rough, scratchy side) for attaching to the jacket. The loop side will face the inside against the lining.

2.  **Measure & Cut:**
    *   Carefully measure where you want the Velcro to attach on the jacket *outside* (away from the lining).
    *   Cut the hook strip to the precise length needed. **Crucially, cut it *slightly shorter* than the measured length.** You'll trim the excess after applying.

3.  **Prepare the Jacket Fabric:**
    *   Turn the jacket *inside out* to expose the area you're attaching to.
    *   Iron the area where you'll apply the Velcro. This removes wrinkles and makes the fabric smoother for adhesion. *Do not iron the lining itself directly.*

4.  **Apply the Adhesive:**
    *   Peel off the backing paper from the Velcro strip. You'll see a clear adhesive layer underneath.
    *   **Press FIRMLY** the Velcro strip onto the *inside* of the jacket fabric (the part facing the lining) for **at least 30-60 seconds**. Use a warm iron over a thin cloth pressing down if you have one, but firm hand pressure alone works well. The goal is to create a strong bond.

5.  **Trim Excess:**
    *   Flip the jacket back to the right side.
    *   Carefully cut off the excess Velcro strip sticking out from the *outside* of the jacket using sharp scissors. **Do not cut near the lining.**

6.  **Press Again:**
    *   Flip the jacket inside out one last time and press firmly over the Velcro strip again for 30-60 seconds to ensure maximum adhesion.

**Key Tips for Success & Protecting the Lining:**

*   **Test First:** If you have a spare scrap of the jacket's outer fabric (like a seam allowance), test the adhesive on it first.
*   **Press Firmly & Evenly:** Don't rush the pressing step. Apply even pressure over the entire strip.
*   **Avoid Lining Directly:** Never iron or press the Velcro adhesive directly onto the lining fabric. Always apply it to the *outer* jacket fabric layer.
*   **Quality Matters:** Cheap Velcro adhesive often fails. Velcro brand is reliable.
*   **Alternative (Less Ideal):** You *could* use high-strength double-sided fabric tape or a small amount of fabric glue (like E6000) applied *only* to the *outer* fabric layer, but iron-on adhesive is generally stronger and more permanent for this purpose.
*   **Reinforce (Optional):** If attaching something heavy, a few small, tight stitches through the jacket fabric *around* the Velcro strip (not through the lining) can add security without tearing.

**Why this works:**

The adhesive bonds the Velcro strip firmly to the *outer* jacket fabric. When you press it down, the hook side is secured to the jacket, and the loop side gently adheres to the *inside* of the jacket fabric layer, which is adjacent to the lining but not glued directly to it. The lining remains untouched and intact.

This method provides a strong, permanent attachment without sewing or damaging your warm winter garment.
