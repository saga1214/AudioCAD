Of course. Here is a complete, step-by-step breakdown of a standard resume format that you can use as a template.

First, at the very top, is the Header Section. Center your large 14-point name. To its right—perhaps using icons, though removing them is safer for automated systems—you'll list your email, phone number with the city, expected graduation date if you're a student, and any links to your LinkedIn or personal portfolio.

Right below your name is the Summary Section. This is a short, powerful paragraph of two or three sentences that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals, especially if you're changing fields.

Next is the Experience Section. This is the most important part. Here you'll list your jobs in reverse chronological order, meaning the most recent job comes first. For each role, include the company name, your job title, and the dates you worked there. Then, use bullet points to describe two or three key achievements, focusing on what you accomplished rather than just your duties.

After Experience comes the Education Section. List your highest degree first. For each degree, include the university name, your expected graduation year, and any honors you received, like 'Summa Cum Laude' or a specific scholarship. If you have multiple degrees from the same school, you can list them sequentially.

Then, you have the Skills Section. It's very important to tailor this list to the specific job you're applying for by highlighting the skills you have that match the job description.

If you have significant volunteer work or relevant projects, you can include a Volunteer Experience or Projects section.

Finally, you can add an optional section for Certifications, Awards, or Languages. This is a good place to put any other achievements that don't fit neatly into the other sections.

Just remember to keep the formatting clean and consistent with a readable font size. This structure is clear, traditional, and easy for recruiters to find the information they need.
