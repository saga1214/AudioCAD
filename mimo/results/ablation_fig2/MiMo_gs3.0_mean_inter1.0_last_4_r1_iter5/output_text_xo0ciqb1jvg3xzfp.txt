For the Pilgrims, the feast was definitely a moment of relief and celebration after barely surviving the colony's most brutal winter. They were grateful for the harvest and for the help they had received from the Wampan-og people.

For the Wampanoag, the story is more complicated. They participated in the feast, but they were also facing a growing colonial presence and the spread of European diseases that were wiping out their communities. The relationship between them and the Pilgrims was one of mutual dependence at first, but it did not last.

So, it was a very complex moment. It was a celebration for the settlers, but not necessarily a celebration of the same kind for the Wampanoag, who were already dealing with immense challenges.
