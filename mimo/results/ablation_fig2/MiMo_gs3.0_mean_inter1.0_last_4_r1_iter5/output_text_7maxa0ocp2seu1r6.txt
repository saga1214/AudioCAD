Here's a complete, categorized list of measurements based on our conversation, designed for clarity so you can easily reference it:

### **Core Body Measurements (take EVERY fitting)**  
1. **Bust** (under arms, fullest part)  
2. **Natural Waist** (highest point of the waistband, not belt)  
3. **Hips** (widest part of the hips, usually 8–10 inches below the waist)  

### **Basic Measurements (for most garments)**  
1. **Inseam** (inner leg length from crotch to ankle)  
2. **Sleeve Length** (center-back neck point to wrist)  
3. **Shoulder Width** (across upper back, from shoulder seam to shoulder seam)  

### **Additional Measurements (for fitted garments/jackets/shirts)**  
1. **Neck** (circumference at the base of the neck)  
2. **Bicep** (at the widest part of the upper arm)  
3. **Shoulder Seam to Seam** (back of neck to hem of sleeve)  
4. **Thigh** (between hip and knee, for tailored pants/skirts)  

### **Height-Related Adjustments (for tall clients)**  
- **Back Rise** (crotch to top of waistband; critical for comfort in long pants)  
- **Front Rise** (crotch to hip seam)  
- **Shoe Length** (client should try on shoes to set inseam/sleeve length)  

### **Practical Tips for Your Client**  
- His inseam (37") is long, so **shorten the pattern** if he wants pants touching sneaker tops.  
- Add **extra ease in hips/thighs** (by 1–2 inches) for sitting comfort.  
- Always measure **back rise separately**—it’s often longer than front rise.  

> 💡 **Key Reminder**: For length (sleeves/pants), *always have the client try on shoes first*. Pin the hem where they want it, then use that measurement.
