It sounds like you're feeling overwhelmed, but determined. Breaking juggling into a routine can definitely help. Here’s a sample weekly schedule to get you and your son started.

First, let's talk about your daily practice. Aim for about 20 to 30 minutes a day, maybe right after school or before dinner. The key is consistency.

Here’s how you could structure that time. Start with a five-minute warm-up by tapping two or three balls in each hand. Then, dedicate about ten minutes to practicing two things: one, perfecting that sequence I mentioned earlier for transitioning to three with your dominant hand; and two, practicing juggling two beanbags just in one of your hands.

After that, spend another ten minutes on scarves. For beginners, a good way is to throw one or two scarves at the same time. As you get better, you can try throwing one from each hand.

Finally, take the last five minutes to juggle all three beanbags together. Don't worry if it's messy; the goal is just to get a feel for the rhythm and make sure all three are in the air at the same time.

So, how does that fit into a week? You could practice every day. On Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, you could focus on beanbag transitions and one-hand practice. On Tuesday and Thursday, maybe focus more on one-handed two-ball juggling and scarf practice. Saturday could be a long, relaxed practice session, and Sunday is a rest day.

Remember, stress gets in the way, so try to make this fun. Celebrate the small wins, like when all three beanbags finally go in a circle. You promised your son something, and that kind of dedication makes it worth every practice session. You'll get it figured out.
