It's smart to get your diesel van in good shape before a long trip. Here’s a rundown of what to check and how to handle things like brakes and spark plugs:

### **Brakes**
- **Check Brake Pads**: Look for wear indicators (metal tabs that scrape the rotor when worn) or reduced pad thickness (use a gauge if unsure). If pads are worn, replace them before the trip.
- **Inspect Rotors/Discs**: Look for cracks, warping, or excessive grooves. If they’re damaged, they may need resurfacing or replacement.
- **Test Fluids**: Ensure brake fluid is at the correct level and free of air or contamination.
- **Test Pedal Feel**: If the pedal feels spongy or sinks slightly, check for air in the system or a fluid leak.

### **Spark Plugs (Diesel Engines)**
Diesel engines use spark plugs differently than gasoline ones, but here’s how to check/replace them:
1. **Access**: Remove the air filter and access the engine bay. Diesel plugs are often under the valve cover.
2. **Check Condition**: Remove the plug (using a special socket) and inspect for wear, oil fouling, or excessive carbon buildup. Diesel engines can be dirty.
3. **Replace if Needed**: If the plug is old (typically 100,000–200,000 miles) or worn, replace it with the correct type (e.g., copper or platinum). Torque it to spec (consult your manual).
4. **Check Compression**: If you suspect issues, a compression test can help diagnose if the plug or cylinder is failing.

### **Other Diesel Maintenance Tips**
- **Fuel System**: Clean the fuel filter (if external) or replace it if needed. Ensure your diesel has the right additive for cold weather (to prevent gelling).
- **Oil & Filters**: Change the oil and diesel fuel filter before the trip. Use high-quality diesel oil (e.g., API CJ-4 or higher).
- **Tires & Tires**: Check pressure, tread depth, and alignment. Diesel vans are heavy, so tires wear faster.
- **Propane (if equipped)**: Test the tanks, lines, and regulator. Ensure the camper’s furnace/oven works.
- **Water System**: If your camper has a fresh/wastewater tank, empty them and flush with fresh water before the trip.

### **Where to Get Help**
- Visit a diesel specialist (not just a generic mechanic). Diesel vans need specific care.
- Check for pre-trip inspection services at a trusted shop.

A well-maintained diesel van will handle long trips better than older gas models, especially with stops. If anything feels off, get it checked before you go. Safe travels!
