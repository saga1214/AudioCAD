Deep blockages are tough, but you can handle this with your toolbox. Here’s a calm, step-by-step approach—take your time, no rush:

### 🧂 1. **Boiling Water Test**  
   - Fill a kettle with water, bring to a *full boil*, and pour it slowly down the drain in stages.  
   - Wait 1–2 minutes. If it clears, you're done! If not, repeat.  

### 🔍 2. **Visual Inspection**  
   - Turn off the water supply valve.  
   - Remove the drain cover (if possible) and check for visible debris (hair, food, etc.).  
   - Use a flashlight to see how far down the pipe the clog goes.  

### 🛠️ 3. **Manual Removal**  
   With your simple tools:  
   - **Tweezers or pliers:** Try to grab and pull out any visible blockage.  
   - **Wire coat hanger:** Bend a hook at the end. Insert it into the drain, hook debris, and pull it out.  
   - **Zip ties:** If the drain is partially open, use a zip tie to scoop out gunk.  

### 🧯 4. **Boiling Water + Dish Soap**  
   - Pour boiling water, then add ½ cup of dish soap. This lubricates and helps break up the clog.  
   - Wait 10 minutes, then flush again with boiling water.  

### ⚙️ 5. **Hand auger (drain snake)**  
   If you have one:  
   - Insert the cable into the drain, crank the handle clockwise.  
   - When it hits resistance, crank slowly to hook the clog.  
   - Pull it out slowly—don’t force it!  

### 💧 6. **Salt + Baking Soda**  
   - Pour ½ cup baking soda, then 1 cup white vinegar down the drain.  
   - Cover with a plug or wet cloth to contain the fizzing.  
   - Wait 15 minutes, then flush with hot water.  

### ⚠️ Key Tips:  
   - **Stay calm:** Deep clogs take patience. If you feel overwhelmed, pause for 2 deep breaths.  
   - **Protect yourself:** Wear gloves and eye protection.  
   - **Call for backup if needed:** If the clog persists after these steps, it might be beyond your simple toolbox.
