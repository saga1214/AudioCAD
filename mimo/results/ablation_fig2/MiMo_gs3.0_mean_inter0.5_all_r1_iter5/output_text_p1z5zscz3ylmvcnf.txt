Okay, so you're thinking about a fun way to get your father-in-law interested in investing in classic cars. That’s a great idea—it connects with his American-car loyalty and old-school vibe while giving him a tangible asset that could appreciate. Here are a few classic cars that are known to hold or increase in value, especially in the Arizona market where car culture is big.

First, there's the Ford Mustang, especially from the first-generation, 1964 and a half to 1966 models. They’re iconic American cars, so he’d appreciate that, and they’re always in demand. A well-maintained one can double or triple in value over time.

Second, the Chevrolet Camaro, specifically the first-generation from 1967 to 1972. It’s a muscle car, just like the Mustang, and it’s a great alternative. They’re collectible and often outperform in value compared to banks.

Third, a Porsche 911, especially the early models from the 1960s and 70s. Now, this might stretch his pride a little since it’s not American, but the investment potential is huge. They’re highly sought after, and a well-restored one can be worth much more than it was new.

Fourth, a Dodge Challenger, particularly the 1970 to 1976 models. They’re part of the muscle car era, and collectors love them. A clean one can appreciate significantly, especially if it has low miles.

And fifth, a classic Maserati Ghibli from the 1970s or 80s. These are rare and often undervalued at first, but they can appreciate nicely if maintained well. It’s a bit of a niche investment, but it could be a smart long-term play.

You could frame it as a way for him to keep his independence but also grow his wealth in a hands-on way. Maybe suggest he start with one or two and enjoy the process of restoring or collecting. It’s a great way to bond with the family, too, especially with the grandson already into cars.
