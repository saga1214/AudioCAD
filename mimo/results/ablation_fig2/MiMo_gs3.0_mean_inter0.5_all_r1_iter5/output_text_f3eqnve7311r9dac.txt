That's a very smart point. Older books are often rarer and can appreciate more, but they require more expertise to evaluate. I'll give you ten important books from the 1940s and 1950s that are good starting points, along with a few things to keep in mind.

First, there's *Captain America Comics* number one from 1941.
Then *Tales of Suspense* number 92, which is Captain America's first appearance on the cover.
*Amazing Spider-Man* number 50 is a key issue where Spider-Man joins the Avengers.
*The Incredible Hulk* number one from 1962 is a must-have, introducing the Hulk.
*Fantastic Four* number 15 is a big one, as it's the first appearance of Galactus and the Fantastic Four's first team-up with the Avengers.
*Thor* number 160 is the first appearance of Beta Ray Bill.
*Adventure Comics* number 250 is the first appearance of the Legion of Super-Heroes.
*Detective Comics* number 227 is the first appearance of the Joker.
*Warrior* number 3 is the first appearance of the Warlord.
And finally, *Adventure Comics* number 252, which is the first appearance of the Legion of Super-Heroes' Saturn Girl.

And just a few things to remember: First, the condition is everything. Second, getting books graded by a company like CGC can significantly increase their value and add credibility. Third, it's a great idea to start small, maybe buy a few modern books to learn the ropes before jumping into the more expensive vintage market. And finally, comic collecting is a passion, so having Luke Cage and the Defenders in your collection will make it even better.
