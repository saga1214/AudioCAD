You're right to double-check! The fourth key component working for shoulder rotation is the **clavicle (collarbone)**. While it doesn't directly articulate with the humerus or scapula in the same shallow socket, it plays a critical role in stabilizing the entire shoulder girdle. Here’s how it integrates:

1.  **The Humerus & Scapula:** Form the primary ball-and-socket joint (glenohumeral joint), enabling circular internal and external rotation.  
2.  **The Rotator Cuff:** Provides dynamic stability around the humeroscapular joint, preventing the humerus from slipping out of the scapula's shallow socket during movement.  
3.  **The Clavicle:** Acts as a **stabilizer** for the scapula itself.  
    *   It connects the scapula to the sternum (breastbone) and ribs.  
    *   It provides an anchor point for muscles that move the scapula (like the trapezius and serratus anterior).  
    Poor stability or injury to the clavicle/scapular stabilizers can indirectly affect shoulder rotation mechanics and increase strain on the rotator cuff.

**In Summary:**  
For optimal, pain-free rotation:  
*   **Bones:** Humerus, Scapula, Clavicle.  
*   **Stabilizers:** Rotator Cuff Muscles (Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres Minor, Subscapularis).  
The clavicle ensures the scapula is positioned correctly to allow the humerus and rotator cuff to function effectively.
