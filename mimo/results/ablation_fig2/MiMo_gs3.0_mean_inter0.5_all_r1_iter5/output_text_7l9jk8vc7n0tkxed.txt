1.  **Safety First:** Put on gloves. Protect your hands.
2.  **Forceful Plunging:** This is still your best bet.
    *   Ensure water covers the plunger cup.
    *   Create a tight seal over the drain.
    *   Use strong, steady up-and-down motions (like you're pumping a siphon). Do 10-15 pumps, then rest for 30 seconds. Repeat several times.
    *   *Why it works:* Creates significant suction and pressure to dislodge debris deep in the pipe.
3.  **Manual Removal (If Possible):**
    *   **Check for a Loose Cap:** Is there a drain cover or plug? Remove it carefully.
    *   **Use Pliers/Grips:** If you have pliers or needle-nose pliers in your toolbox, carefully grab any visible chunks of debris (like hair, gunk, or a rag) coming up with the water and pull them out.
    *   **Avoid Forcing Tools:** Don't force a pipe wrench or screwdriver into the drain. You risk scratching or breaking the pipe. Pliers are safer for grabbing debris.
4.  **Boiling Water (If Safe & Applicable):**
    *   **Only for PTrap Drains (Sinks, Tubs):** If you know it's a PTrap, carefully pour a kettleful of boiling water down the drain in stages (1/3, wait 30 secs, 1/3 more, wait 30 secs, final 1/3). **WARNING:** Do NOT use this on toilet drains, hot water heaters, or drains where boiling water could cause damage or a dangerous scald. **Only use if you're absolutely sure it's a standard PTrap.**
5.  **Salt & Baking Soda (For Minor Residues):**
    *   Pour 1/2 cup baking soda down the drain.
    *   Follow with 1 cup white vinegar. It will fizz. Let it sit for 15-20 minutes.
    *   Flush with very hot (not boiling) water. This helps break down mineral deposits or sticky residue.
6.  **Patience & Persistence:** Deep blockages take time. Don't get discouraged if it doesn't clear instantly. Repeat plunging and the manual removal steps consistently. Sometimes it takes multiple attempts over hours.
