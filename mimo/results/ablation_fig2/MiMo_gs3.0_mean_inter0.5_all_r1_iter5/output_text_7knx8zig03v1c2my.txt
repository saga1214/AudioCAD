### Treat Recommendations
*   **Best Flavors:** High-value treats for training include small pieces of chicken, turkey, cheese, hot dogs, peanut butter (xylitol-free!), or freeze-dried liver. Start with high-value treats (he'll work harder for them), then gradually phase to lower-value ones.
*   **Size:** Tiny, bite-sized pieces are ideal so they can be eaten quickly during training.
*   **Timing:** Give the treat *immediately* after he performs the desired action.

### Clicker Training Explained
1.  **The Click:** The clicker is a noise-maker. You press it to make a sharp, consistent "click" sound. This sound is neutral until you start training.
2.  **Marking the Behavior:** When your puppy *does* the exact behavior you want (e.g., sits), you immediately press the clicker. This click *marks* that precise moment in time.
3.  **Delivering the Reward:** The instant the click sounds, you give him the treat.
4.  **The Association:** Your puppy learns that the click sound means a treat is coming *right after* he did the right thing. The click becomes the bridge between the behavior and the reward.
5.  Sit Example:
    *   Puppy looks up at you.
    *   You say "Sit" and hold a treat near his nose, moving it slowly over his head.
    *   The moment his bottom touches the ground, you **click**.
    *   Immediately after the click, you give him the treat.
    *   Repeat many times. Gradually, you can add the verbal cue ("Sit") before the click/treat, then eventually say "Sit" alone after he sits, clicking and treating.

### Crate Training Explained (Making it Cozy)
Crate training is about providing a safe, den-like space, **not punishment**. Here's how to make it cozy:
1.  **Make it a Happy Place:** Start by placing treats, toys, and his bed *outside* the crate. Let him explore it voluntarily.
2.  **Encourage Entry:** Gently guide him in, close the door briefly (a few seconds), open it, and praise/treat him. Repeat.
3.  **Short & Positive Sessions:** Keep initial sessions very short (5-10 minutes) and always positive (treats, praise, play *outside* the crate).
4.  **Gradual Increase:** Slowly increase the time he spends closed in the crate. Always end sessions on a positive note (open the door, praise/treat).
5.  **Never Punish in the Crate:** Never crate him for long periods (boredom leads to destruction), make him go to the bathroom in it, or use it for punishment. It should be his safe haven.
6.  **Supervise or Use a Gate:** If you can't watch him, leave the door open or use a baby gate to keep him out of the crate if he's alone.

### Accident Potty Training
*   **The Goal:** Teach him *where* to go, not just punish mistakes.
*   **The Process:**
    1.  **Take him Out Frequently:** Take him out every 15-30 minutes, especially after waking, eating, playing, and before bed.
    2.  **Watch for Signs:** Look for sniffing, circling, or whining – these are potty signals.
    3.  **Reward Potty Outdoors:** The *instant* he eliminates outside, use an enthusiastic "Yes!" or a clicker, then give a high-value treat and lots of praise.
    4.  **Clean Accidents Thoroughly:** Use an enzymatic cleaner *specifically for pet urine* (like Nature's Miracle). Regular cleaners won't remove the odor molecules that trigger him to go there again. Baking soda/vinegar mixes can help mask residual smells initially.
    5.  **No Punishment:** Never yell, scold, spray water, or make him wait for the accident to finish. This creates fear and anxiety, not learning. He won't know *why* he's being punished.
    6.  **Consistency is Key:** Everyone in the household must follow the same rules.

### Getting Him Back if He Escapes
*   **Prevention First:** Secure doors/windows. Use baby gates strategically. Keep the door to your home closed when you leave (unless you have a secure outdoor area). **Always use a leash in unsecured outdoor areas.**
*   **If He Runs Out:**
    *   **Stay Calm:** Panicking will make him run further or hide.
    *   **Call His Name:** Use a happy, excited, high-pitched voice. "Galileo! Come! Come here, buddy!"
    *   **Use His Favourite Toy/Treat:** Hold it up, wave it, and call him. "Come! Come get your ball/treat!"
    *   **Create a Path:** Stand near the door/window he escaped from. Call him, hold the reward, and gently guide him back. Don't chase him.
    *   **Positive Reinforcement:** The *moment* he comes back to you, shower him with praise, kisses, and high-value treats. Make coming back the best thing ever!
    *   **Practice Recall:** Practice the "Come!" command in your yard *before* he knows he can escape. Reward heavily every single time.
