Okay, diesel camper vans can be older and definitely need a pre-trip check. For the brakes, one thing you can look at is the brake pads—some of them have wear indicators that are little metal tabs that grind against the rotor when the pads are thin. If you hear a high-pitched squeal or feel a vibration, that’s a sign. Also, check the brake fluid level—it should be between the minimum and maximum marks. For spark plugs, those are inside the diesel engine, which is a bit different from a gasoline engine. Diesel spark plugs are usually more durable, but they do wear out over time. You’ll need a socket that’s the right size and a ratchet to take them out. The process involves unscrewing the old ones and installing new ones, but it’s definitely a mechanical task. I’d recommend having a mechanic do it unless you’re comfortable with it. Also, check your diesel fuel filter and oil level before you go. It’s always better to be safe than sorry when you’re on a family trip in an old diesel van.
