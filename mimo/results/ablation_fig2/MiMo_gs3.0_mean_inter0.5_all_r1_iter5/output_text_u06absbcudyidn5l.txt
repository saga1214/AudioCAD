Okay, absolutely. Chronic skin redness can have many causes. Besides rosacea and sun damage we mentioned before, it could also be eczema, contact dermatitis from something you're sensitive to, or even heat or physical irritation.

Now, about vasculitis, let me try to explain it differently. It's basically an autoimmune condition where your body's immune system mistakenly attacks its own blood vessels. These vessels can be any size—large, medium, or small. When they get inflamed, they can swell up, which restricts blood flow.

Common symptoms are often systemic, meaning they affect the whole body, like feeling feverish, tired, and having muscle or joint pain. Skin symptoms are very common, showing up as rashes, small purple spots, or even sores. Hair and nails can definitely be affected because poor blood flow means less nutrients get to them, leading to weakness and slow growth.

Diagnosis is done by looking at symptoms, running blood tests, and often taking a skin biopsy for a definitive look under a microscope. Treatment focuses on suppressing the immune response, usually with medications like steroids. The main risk is that this ongoing inflammation can cause permanent damage to the organs or nerves if it's not managed.

So, I'd really recommend seeing a rheumatologist to check for vasculitis, or a dermatologist to look at your skin. They can figure out what's really going on.
