For modern-day art museum security, your observation about human error is critical—it highlights why the "first line" must be designed to minimize vulnerabilities. While technologies like secure cases and keycards are vital, they belong to **later layers of defense**. The **true first line of defense remains *access control at all entry points***, but it must be enhanced to counter human unreliability. Here’s why:

### 1. **Guarded by Visibility and Screening (Visible Presence)**  
   - Includes trained security personnel (guards) to deter intruders and monitor entry points.  
   - Enforces **physical screening** (e.g., X-ray bag checks, prohibited items enforcement) to control *what* and *who* enters.  
   - *Why it’s first*: This layer aims to **prevent threats from entering the building entirely**. Human error (like the Gardner heist) is mitigated by a visible, active presence that discourages opportunistic attacks.

### 2. **Bag Checks and Identity Verification**  
   - Modern systems use automated scanners (e.g., RFID for high-risk bags) or staffed checkpoints.  
   - For staff/students, **laser-scanned keycards or biometrics** (fingerprint/retina) restrict access to non-public zones *after* the public screening.  
   - *Why it’s first*: Keycards are **secondary access controls** for internal areas. The first line stops the initial breach; keycards compartmentalize *post-entry* risks.

### 3. **Why Other Layers Aren’t the "First Line"**  
   - **Cameras (second layer)**: Deterrents inside galleries, but they detect threats *after* entry.  
   - **Alarms (motion/break-in sensors)**: Reactive; they trigger alerts only if someone bypasses the first line.  
   - **Secure cases/pedestals (third layer)**: Physical barriers against theft *inside* the museum. They assume entry has already occurred.  

### Modern Context: Layered Security Philosophy  
Museums now treat security as a **hierarchy**:  
1. **Prevent entry** (first line: access control).  
2. **Detect intrusion** (second line: cameras/alarms).  
3. **Deter/impair theft** (third line: secure cases).  
4. **Restrict internal access** (e.g., keycards/biometrics).  

### Heist Lessons Applied  
The Gardner heist (1990) succeeded because guards **failed to verify police credentials** (human error). Modern first-line defenses now include:  
- **Automated verification** (e.g., facial recognition for staff).  
- **AI-driven behavior analysis** at entry points.  
- **Zero-trust protocols** (e.g., requiring secondary authentication for bags).
