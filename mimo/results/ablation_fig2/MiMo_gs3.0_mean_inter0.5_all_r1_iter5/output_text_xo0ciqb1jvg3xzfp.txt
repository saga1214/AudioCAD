It was not a happy occasion for both sides. The Pilgrims survived a deadly winter because of the help they received. The Wampanoag, however, were already losing a large number of people to European diseases like smallpox.

This feast marked the beginning of a relationship that, while initially cooperative, would eventually lead to war and the loss of their land. So, while the Pilgrims celebrated abundance, the Wampanoag were likely aware of the difficult future that was ahead.
