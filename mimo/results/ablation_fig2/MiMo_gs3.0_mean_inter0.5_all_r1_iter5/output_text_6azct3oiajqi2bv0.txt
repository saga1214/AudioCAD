Here's a step-by-step guide to sewing interfacing onto a jacket lining without damaging it, focusing on precision and care:

1.  **Access the Inside:** Flip the jacket completely inside out. Find the seam you want to reinforce (e.g., the shoulder seam, a chest seam, or a hem).

2.  **Measure & Mark Precisely:** Measure the width of the interfacing you need (usually 1-2 inches wide for a seam). Mark this width along the *inside* edge of the seam allowance where you want the interfacing. Use chalk or a water-soluble pen for accuracy.

3.  **Secure the Interfacing (Carefully):**
    *   **Option A (Seam Ripper - Recommended):** Gently use a seam ripper to undo *only* the stitches on the very edge of the seam allowance where your marking line is. This creates a small opening large enough for the interfacing to slip underneath. Don't rip into the lining itself!
    *   **Option B (Needle Poking):** If you don't have a seam ripper, carefully use a needle to poke tiny holes along your marking line in the seam allowance fabric *only*. This gives you something to slide the interfacing under.

4.  **Position the Interfacing:** Slide the interfacing piece flat and smoothly under the seam allowance fabric, right up to the marking line. Ensure it's centered along the seam allowance edge.

5.  **Stitch Securely:**
    *   Use a **low thread tension** on your machine (check the manual) to prevent puckering.
    *   Set a **longer stitch length** (around 3.0mm or 1/8 inch) for easier control.
    *   Sew *very* close to the edge of the interfacing (about 1/8 inch) with a straight stitch. Go slowly and carefully.
    *   **Crucially:** Sew *only* the seam allowance stitches. Do *not* stitch through the lining fabric itself. Your stitches should be hidden *within* the seam allowance.

6.  **Press & Secure:** Once stitched, gently press the seam allowance down *over* the interfacing using a pressing cloth (a thin cotton scrap) between the fabric and the iron. This helps set the interfacing and the stitches without melting or damaging the fabric. Press firmly along the seam.

**Key Tips to Protect the Lining:**

*   **Test First:** If possible, test your technique and tension on a scrap piece of similar fabric and interfacing.
*   **Avoid High Heat:** Don't use high heat or steam directly on the interfacing if it's a synthetic type, as it can scorch.
*   **Gentle Handling:** Be extremely gentle when manipulating the lining fabric. Avoid pulling or stretching it.
*   **Lightweight Interfacing:** Choose a lightweight woven interfacing (like "fusible woven" or "lightweight fusible") for the best results with delicate linings. Avoid heavy fusible interfacing which can be difficult to sew in place.
*   **Match the Stitch Pattern:** If the seam has a specific pattern (like a buttonhole stitch), try to replicate it when stitching down the interfacing for invisibility.

By following these steps carefully and focusing on gentle handling and precise stitching within the seam allowance, you can effectively add reinforcement to the lining without damaging it. This is a great temporary fix until your Arden Fair shopping arrives!
