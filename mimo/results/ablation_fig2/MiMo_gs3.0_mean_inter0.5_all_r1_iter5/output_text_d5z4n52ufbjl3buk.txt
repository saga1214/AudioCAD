Here are some pointers for your email, focusing on respect, specificity, and a constructive tone:

### Key Email Structure & Phrasing
1. **Subject Line**:  
   `Meeting Request: Understanding Recent Exam Performance`  
   *(Neutral and professional)*

2. **Opening (Respectful Tone)**:  
   > "Dear Professor [Name],  
   > I hope this email finds you well. I’m writing to request a brief meeting about my recent exam performance on [Course Name/Topic]. I’ve always valued your guidance in this class and wanted to respectfully understand what contributed to my score of 64."

3. **Focus on Improvement, Not Challenge**:  
   > "As someone eager to excel in this subject, I’d genuinely appreciate understanding what I could have improved in my essay to better align with expectations. This feedback would help me refine my approach for future assessments."

4. **Acknowledge Exam Nature (Without Accusation)**:  
   > "Since the exam was more essay-based, I found grading can sometimes be subjective. If you have general insights on criteria you prioritized, I’d find that incredibly useful."

5. **Address Scheduling (If Office Hours Conflict)**:  
   > "I understand your office hours may be busy. If possible, I’d be grateful for even 10–15 minutes during a time that works for you—whether in person, virtually, or during a gap."

6. **Closing (Gratitude & Flexibility)**:  
   > "Thank you for considering this request. I’m happy to accommodate your schedule. Sincerely, [Your Name]"

### Critical Tips
- **Avoid Accusations**: Never say "unfairly" or "eagle." Stick to neutral language like "understanding the rationale" or "seeking feedback."
- **Be Specific**: Mention the exam’s nature (essay-like) and content focus to show you’ve reflected.
- **Keep It Short**: Evasions or long paragraphs can feel defensive. Aim for 3–4 concise paragraphs.
- **Attach the Exam Rubric (If Available)**: If there’s one, reference it: "Could you clarify how my responses aligned with the rubric provided?"
- **No Agenda**: Frame this purely as a learning opportunity—no hint of a grade appeal.

### If You Don’t Get a Response
- **Follow Up Once**: "I haven’t heard back and wanted to reiterate my request for feedback..."
- **Alternative Path**: If ignored, document the exchange and consider escalating to the department head *only if* the behavior persists across multiple courses. Focus on documented concerns (e.g., "multiple students report subjective grading").

This approach minimizes friction with the professor’s ego while achieving your goal: clarity on the grading basis. It positions you as a proactive, respectful student—which aligns perfectly with scholarship applications. You’ve got this! 🌟
