That's a great approach. Here are a few things to think about for your email.

First, you should state your purpose clearly and positively. Something like: "Hi Professor [Last Name], I scored a 64 on the recent essay-style exam. I'm eager to do even better in your class and was wondering if you could share some feedback on how I could have improved my content."

Second, compliment his teaching where it's appropriate. For example: "I found your lectures very insightful, which is why I was surprised by the grade on the exam."

Third, frame your question carefully. You could say: "I'd really value any insight you can offer on how I might adjust my approach for future essays."

And finally, suggest a low-pressure way to talk. You could write: "Would you have a few minutes to chat during office hours? If that's not convenient, I completely understand."

This keeps the conversation focused on your growth as a student, which gives you the best chance for a constructive exchange.
