For modern-day art museum security, the **unambiguous first line of defense remains controlling all physical and virtual entry points**. This foundational layer prioritizes **access management** above all else, combining visible human and automated systems to prevent unauthorized entry before threats escalate. Here’s why it’s the primary priority, even after considering famous heists and vulnerabilities:

### Key Components of the First Line:
1. **Perimeter Screening**  
   - Mandatory bag checks, X-ray, and RFID screening at all public entrances to restrict weapons, tools, or explosives.  
   - Entry logs tracking visitors, staff, and vendors.  

2. **Access Control Systems**  
   - Biometric or keycard/PIN systems for non-public areas (e.g., storage, maintenance) to limit internal threats.  

3. **Visible Security Presence**  
   - Guards or officers at key points to deter attacks and intervene immediately.  

4. Isolated from *internal* systems (cameras, alarms) because these only respond *after* a breach occurs.  

### Why Gardner Heist Highlights Human Error (But Doesn’t Change the "First Line"):  
The 1990 Gardner heist exploited human trust (guards letting imposters in), but it underscores that **perimeter control is non-negotiable**. Modern museums now mitigate this with:  
- **Guard training protocols** (e.g., no entry for off-hours "police").  
- **Automated barriers** (e.g., turnstiles) to reduce human judgment calls.  
- **AI-enhanced surveillance** to detect suspicious behavior at entry points.  

### Modern Evolution:  
While secure displays (reinforced cases, anti-tamper mounts) protect art *inside* the museum, they fail if entry is compromised first. Similarly, motion detectors and glass-break sensors are critical but are **secondary layers**—they activate only *after* access is breached.  

### Paper Implication:  
Argue that **modern security integrates technology but never sacrifices access control**. The first line must be a "moat" that stops threats at the perimeter, as all internal systems depend on this barrier.
