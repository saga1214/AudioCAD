You're right to double-check! While the **three primary *bones* working together for shoulder rotation** are the **humerus** (upper arm), **scapula** (shoulder blade), and **clavicle** (collarbone), the **fourth critical component** for stability and rotation is the **rotator cuff muscles**.  

Here’s the breakdown:  
1. **Clavicle** (collarbone): Connects the arm to the sternum, stabilizing the shoulder girdle.  
2. **Scapula** (shoulder blade): Houses the socket for the humerus and guides its movement.  
3. **Humerus**: Rotates within the scapula’s shallow socket.  
4. **Rotator Cuff Muscles** (4 tendons):  
   - **Supraspinatus** (initiates lift-off)  
   - **Infraspinatus** (external rotation)  
   - **Teres Minor** (external rotation)  
   - **Subscapularis** (internal rotation)  

**Why the "fourth component" matters**:  
- The rotator cuff **stabilizes the humeral head** in the scapula’s socket, preventing dislocation during rotation.  
- Without proper cuff function, the shoulder loses control and range of motion.  

If your friend felt soreness during **dumbbell lateral raises**, it could indicate:  
- **Rotator cuff fatigue** (especially supraspinatus/infraspinatus).  
- **Poor form** causing strain.  
- **Overuse** without proper warm-up.  

**Key takeaway**: The shoulder’s rotation relies on **bones + muscles**. The clavicle, scapula, and humerus provide the structure, while the rotator cuff muscles provide dynamic stability. If pain persists, your friend should consult a sports medicine specialist to rule out tears or impingement.
