Got it. Here’s a key difference between the Laws of the Game and some college certification interpretations, especially regarding throw-ins.

Let's imagine Red Team versus Blue Team. During a throw-in, the ball is on the sideline and ready to be thrown by a Red Team player.

Now, here's the important distinction. According to the Laws of the Game, a player is not in an offside position when receiving the ball directly from a throw-in. However, college referee certification courses often put a very strong emphasis on a special exception for角 kick (corner kicks).

Because of this focus, some instructors might imply a similar leniency for throw-ins during the exam, even though the rulebook text specifically singles out corner kicks. The safest and most defensible call, based strictly on the written law, is that the throw-in is a direct restart just like a goal kick, and the offside rule does not apply in that specific moment. So, in your certification studies, it's best to treat throw-ins as direct restarts where offside is not an offense for the player receiving the throw.
