Of course. Here is a clear, step-by-step breakdown of a traditional and effective resume format that should be easy for you to create.

First is your Header. Center your large 14-point name. To its right, aligned underneath your name, place your contact info vertically. I'd still avoid icons just to be safe. Start with email in a 9 or 10-point font, then your phone number on the next line, and finally your city in another line. Adding state and zip code here would also be helpful.

Second, and right below your name, is a brief Professional Summary. This is just two or three sentences highlighting your most relevant skills and goals for the new field you're targeting.

Third, you'll have your Experience section. This is where you list your jobs. For each role, include the company name, your job title, the start and end dates, and then one or two bullet points summarizing your key achievements. For roles not directly related to your target field, you can keep the bullet points very brief.

Fourth is your Education section. List your degree, your school, and the year you graduated. You can add extra details like your GPA or honors right under the degree where you earned them.

Fifth is a Skills section. This is very important. List your key skills, like technical abilities or soft skills, and try to match them to the job description. You can even add a small note next to each skill to show how you've used it.

Sixth, you can have an Additional Information section. This is optional but useful for things like relevant certifications, professional memberships, or languages you speak.

Finally, a quick note on formatting: use a clean, simple font like Arial or Calibri, and stick to one or two pages. Using bold for section headers and keeping bullet points short will make it easy to read. This structure should work well for most applications.
