That's a profound question. While all those aspects are vital, the most fundamental aspect of preserving hotspots is preventing the irreversible loss of unique evolutionary history and genetic information.

See, Norman Myers coined his term back in 1988 to create a focused, urgent conservation framework. But the deeper, underlying principle that makes it all necessary is that extinction is permanent. When a species in a hotspot goes extinct, that entire evolutionary story, all the genetic diversity it held, and its unique role in its ecosystem—disappears forever. Protecting hotspots is really about preserving the irreplaceable past and securing a more resilient future for all of us.
