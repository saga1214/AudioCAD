1.  **Pre-1453 Drivers (Existing Momentum):**
    *   **Technological Readiness:** Advances in shipbuilding (caravel, lateen sail) and navigation (astrolabe, compass, portolan charts) *already* made long ocean voyages feasible.
    *   **Portuguese Exploration:** Portuguese explorers like Henry the Navigator and Bartolomeu Dias were already pushing south along the African coast. Dias proving the sea route around Africa (1488) demonstrated the potential.
    *   **Venetian & Genoese Competition:** These Italian trading powers were heavily invested in the spice trade but were squeezed by Ottoman power and high middleman costs.
    *   **Desire for Glory/Spread of Christianity:** European monarchs (especially Portugal and Spain) actively sought prestige and global influence.

2.  **Constantinople's Fall (1453): The Critical Catalyst:**
    *   **Blocked Gateway:** While spice trade *had* flowed through Constantinople/Alexandria, Ottoman control made this route increasingly risky, unstable, and expensive. It wasn't "immediately closed," but the *threat* was undeniable and growing.
    *   **Intensified Urgency:** The fall was a massive shock. It proved the Ottomans were a formidable, expanding power controlling the traditional land routes. Finding a *new*, independent sea route became an urgent matter of national security and economic survival for rivals like Portugal and Spain.
    *   **Focus Shift:** It massively redirected European attention and resources *towards* finding an all-sea route to Asia, validating the explorers already at work and galvanizing monarchs to fund their efforts more aggressively.

3.  **The "Age of Discovery" as a Confluence:**
    *   The Age of Discovery (roughly late 15th to early 16th century) wasn't caused by one event, but by the *interaction* of these forces.
    *   The **technology** and **pre-existing exploration** (especially Portugal's) created the *potential*.
    *   The **Ottoman threat** (symbolized by Constantinople) created the *urgency* and *direction*.
    *   **National competition** (Spain, Portugal, later others) provided the *funding* and *momentum*.

The fall of Constantinople didn't *start* the Age of Discovery, but it acted as a powerful catalyst. It transformed a promising but risky endeavor into an urgent, large-scale, state-sponsored effort with immense potential rewards.
