From the Pilgri's Pilgrims' perspective in 1621, the successful survival of their colony after nearly all of them dying the previous winter made the feast a very happy and hopeful occasion.

However, from the Wampanoag people's perspective, the relationship with the English settlers was very different. The feast was part of a strategic alliance against other tribes, and they were already aware that the English were settlers who would claim land. So, for the Wampanoag, it was likely a complex event, not a purely joyful one.
