Those are two critically important issues.

While the connection you've built is invaluable, working within that connection, your single most important starting point has two parts.

First, focus on predictable routines and sensory preparation. Music is great for this. Have a clear, consistent way you start that he expects.

Second, and this is equally critical, you need to address the environmental safety.

The dogs running around are a major safety and reinforcement issue. Aggression often spikes around pets for many kids. You need to arrange for someone to control the dogs during sessions or at least give them a secure, quiet space away from you two.

And the clutter itself can be overstimulating and contribute to elopement and aggression. Request that the house be cleared of session-specific clutter before you arrive. Working in a chaotic space is incredibly difficult and unsafe.

Addressing these environmental factors will create the stable foundation you need to do effective teaching once you're alone with him.
