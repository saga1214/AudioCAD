Let me clear that up for you. Let's start with the Boulevardier. There are two correct and classic versions. The original ratio uses one and a quarter ounces of rye or bourbon, one ounce of sweet vermouth, and one ounce of Campari. The equally popular ratio uses equal parts of each—that is, one ounce of rye or bourbon, one ounce of sweet vermouth, and one ounce of Campari. So your friend wasn't wrong, he was just making the equal-parts version.

Now, for the cocktail you mentioned, 'Last Chance'. That sounds like a mix-up with the 'Last Word'. The Last Word is an equal-parts cocktail made with three-quarters of an ounce of gin, three-quarters of an ounce of green Chartreuse, three-quarters of an ounce of maraschino liqueur, and three-quarters of an ounce of fresh lime juice. Stir and strain over ice, garnished with a lime wedge. It's a popular choice for a refreshing drink. Hope that helps you decide!
