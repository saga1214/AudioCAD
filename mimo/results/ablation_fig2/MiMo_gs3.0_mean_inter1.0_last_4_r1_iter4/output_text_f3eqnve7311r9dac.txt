Yes, investing in rare vintage comics from the Golden Age of comics can be very promising. It's a market where scarcity and historical value play a big role.

Here are ten key comics from that era that are more accessible than the six-million-dollar Superman books but still have strong investment potential.

Since you like the Defenders and Power Man/Luke Cage, you could look for:
First, *Warrior Angels* number one, from 1952. This was the first comic to feature Angel, who was a key member of the early Defenders.
Second, *The Defenders* number one, from 1967. This is where the team was officially formed, and it's a foundational key issue.
Third, *Marvel Super-Heroes* number one, from 1964. It was the first comic to feature the new lineup, which included Power Man and the Defenders.

For other valuable titles from that era:
* *Tales to Astonish* number twenty-five, from 1963, is the first appearance of the Ant-Man.
* *Tales of Suspense* number fifty-nine, from 1964, is Luke Cage's first cover appearance.
* *Fantastic Four* number forty-eight, from 1965, is the first appearance of Galactus and the Silver Surfer.
* *Fantastic Four* number one hundred, from 1966, is the first full-color cover for the Silver Surfer.
* *Amazing Adventures of the Fantastic Four* number one, from 1968, is the first appearance of the Inhumans.
* *The Amazing Spider-Man* number fifty, from 1965, is the first comic with a black cover.
* And *The Amazing Spider-Man* number seventy-five, from 1968, is the first appearance of the symbiote suit, which later became Venom.

Just a few important things to keep in mind. First, condition is everything. Older comics are fragile, so only invest in copies that have been professionally graded, for example by CGC, to understand their true value. Second, for these vintage books, be patient. They are a long-term play. And finally, make sure you understand the market. It's a good idea to learn the basics of comic grading and what drives value before you spend your five hundred dollars.
