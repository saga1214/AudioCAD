Well, the most important aspect really comes down to preventing the irreversible loss of unique life and the critical services that ecosystems provide. Norman Myers himself said that hotspots are like the 'biodiversity barsometers' of the Earth—they're the places where we can best see the effects of human activity on biodiversity.

So, by protecting them, we're preserving irreplaceable genetic resources, we're safeguarding the foundation for future scientific discoveries, especially in medicine, and we're maintaining the ecological functions that so many people depend on, like clean water and climate regulation. Ultimately, it's about ensuring that these unique, irreplaceable places continue to function and thrive for future generations.
