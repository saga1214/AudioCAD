It sounds like you've put a lot of pressure on yourself to teach your son soon. Here is a weekly schedule designed to reduce stress by mixing up your practice and focusing on steady progress instead of rushing.

On Monday, you could start with about 10 minutes of two-ball one-hand drills to keep that skill sharp. Then, spend 15 minutes transitioning between two and three beanbags. Finally, use the last 10 minutes with scarves to work on slow-motion patterns.

Tuesday could be lighter, with about 10 minutes of very slow three-ball scarves just to focus on rhythm.

For Wednesday, you could try using tennis balls for 15 minutes. Their different feel will help you improve your catches.

Thursday, you could mix things up. Do 10 minutes of random tricks with scarves, and then spend 15 minutes going back to your first three-throw sequence practice.

Friday would be a good day for a race against the clock, trying to juggle three beanbags for just one minute. Don't worry about catching them, just focus on the rhythm.

Saturday is for longer, focused sessions. You could dedicate 20 minutes to mastering a specific pattern with the beanbags, and another 20 minutes to learning a new pattern with the scarves.

Finally, Sunday is for rest. You can either take a complete break or do a very short, 10-minute fun session with whatever you like best.

Remember, it's crucial to listen to your stress levels. If a day feels frustrating, it's okay to skip it or switch to scarves. The goal is to enjoy the process, promise?
