Great choice on the brooder plate! For bedding with 5-week-old chicks on a concrete floor, here are your best safe and effective options:

1.  **Orchard Grass Hay (Top Recommendation):**
    *   **Why:** Fluffy, insulating, natural, and chicks love it! It helps retain warmth and decomposes quickly.
    *   **How:** Spread a 1.5-2 inch layer. Replace daily to keep it fresh and prevent mold. Ensure it's clean, dust-free hay (not hay dust). Chicks can nibble it safely.

2.  **Kiln-Dried Wood Shavings or Pine Shavings (Fine Flake):**
    *   **Why:** Good insulation, readily available, breaks down quickly. *Crucially, ensure they are kiln-dried and dust-free.* Avoid "sawdust" – it's too fine and dusty.
    *   **How:** Use a thin layer (about 1 inch). Scoop out soiled areas daily and replace fresh shavings frequently. *Not ideal for rodents long-term like sand, but better managed than loose straw/shavings if you need more.*

3.  **Newspaper or Paper Towels (Temporary/Backup):**
    *   **Why:** Ultra-safe, cheap, easy to change. Great for initial setup or if shavings/hay are unavailable.
    *   **How:** Layer several sheets thick (3-4 inches). Replace completely every 1-2 days. Can get soggy quickly if chicks get wet.

**What to AVOID:**

*   **Straw/Hay:** Too bulky for rodents, but still attracts them if left long-term. Plus, concrete cools shreds quickly.
*   **Pine/Sawdust (Unkiln-Dried):** Highly dusty and irritating to chicks' respiratory systems. Can mask urine odors, leading to wet feet and chilling.
*   **Coco Coir:** Can hold moisture and get cold on concrete. Requires more frequent soaking/drying.

**Key Tips for Chicks on Concrete:**

1.  **Insulate the Bottom:** Your rubber mats are key! Ensure they are large enough to cover the plate and extend well beyond the plate edges to create a raised, insulated floor. You can even layer thick rubber mats if needed.
2.  **Moisture Control:** Concrete doesn't insulate well against dampness. Sand bedding helps absorb moisture. Ensure the rubber mats are *not* touching the concrete directly – leave a small gap or use a piece of wood between them if necessary. Change wet areas/sand immediately.
3.  **Warmth Zone:** Chicks will instinctively huddle under the brooder plate. The rubber mats + hay/sand create a cozy "nesting" area around the plate.
4.  Brooder Plate Safety: Keep it at least 6-12 inches off the ground/bedding. Ensure no plastic touching the plate (can melt). Never leave unattended. Check for singed edges.

**Summary:** Start with **orchard grass hay** for the best bedding combo (warmth, safety, chicks love it). Use your **rubber mats** to insulate the concrete floor and create a raised platform. Change hay daily. If hay isn't available, use clean **kiln-dried wood shavings** or **newspaper** until you can get hay. Monitor moisture and chick behavior closely! Good luck with the little ones!
