First, about the left-brain versus right-brain idea. It’s mostly a myth. While some brain areas specialize in certain tasks—like the left side often being linked to language and logic and the right side to spatial reasoning—the brain works as a whole. It’s not like one side is purely creative and the other is purely logical. For example, creativity often involves problem-solving, which is logical, and logical tasks can be creative in their approach. The brain is interconnected, so it’s more about how different areas work together than which side is dominant.

Now, for your career. You want something creative, objective, and with lasting value, but not a desk job. Here are some ideas: 
First, consider product design or industrial design. It’s creative and involves building things, but it also requires understanding how things work and how to make them functional. It’s objective because it’s based on solving real problems, and the designs can have a lasting impact. 
Another option is architecture. It’s creative, involves building, and is very objective because it’s based on physics, math, and regulations. Buildings can last for centuries, so they have a long-term impact. 
You could also look into scientific illustration. It’s creative and objective because it requires understanding science and translating that into visuals. The work can be very impactful, like educating people or preserving scientific knowledge. 
Lastly, consider a career in education. If you like structuring things, teaching can be very structured. It’s creative in how you explain things and objective because it’s based on facts. It can have a lasting impact on students' lives. 

These careers all involve building and creating, are objective, and can have a lasting impact, while also being more hands-on than a typical desk job.
