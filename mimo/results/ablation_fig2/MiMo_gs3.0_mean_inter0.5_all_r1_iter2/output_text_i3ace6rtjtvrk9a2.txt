Now, to your question: would it be correct to say the eye sees continuously? 

Technically, no. The eye doesn't see continuously in the way a camera records frames. It's more like a sensor that's constantly reacting to changes. But the brain's processing is what creates the illusion of continuous vision.

Here’s how it works: The eye is always moving—saccades, tracking, or just drifting. But the brain is smart enough to suppress the blur from these movements. So what you perceive as a continuous, stable image is actually a brain-processed version of the eye's quick, jerky movements.

So, in film terms, traditional 24 frames per second works so well because it's close enough to how the brain blends visual information. Video often runs higher because it's aiming for a more real-time, broadcast feel. The difference is in the storytelling intent, not in what the eye can truly see.
