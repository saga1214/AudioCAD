Okay, you want a simple, step-by-step guide to build your resume. I'll lay it out for you. Just follow these sections in order.

First, at the very top, is your name in about a fourteen-point font.

Right below your name is the contact header. Instead of icons side-by-side with your name, create its own section below it. It should include your professional email, phone number with country and area code, your current city, and state. Icons are best avoided, and also add a link to your LinkedIn profile if you have one.

Next comes the Professional Summary. This is a short, powerful paragraph of about three to five lines that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals, especially for the new roles you're targeting.

After the summary, you'll have your Experience section. The most relevant jobs, even if they're older, go here first. For each role, list your job title, the company name, the location, and the dates you worked there. You can add a very brief, one-line description if you want, but it's optional.

Then comes the Additional Work History section, where you list other jobs, maybe using just the title and company name to keep it short.

Following that is the Education section. Here, you'll list each degree you've earned, including the school name, the expected graduation date, and any honors you received, like 'Graduated with Highest Honors'.

After education, you can have a Skills section. List your key skills, maybe grouping them into categories like Technical Skills or Languages. Be sure to tailor these to the job descriptions you're applying for.

If you have significant volunteer work or projects, you can add a Volunteering or Projects section.

Finally, at the very end, you can list any other relevant information like certifications, publications, or professional affiliations.

Just remember to keep the formatting clean and simple, and always tailor your resume for each specific job.
