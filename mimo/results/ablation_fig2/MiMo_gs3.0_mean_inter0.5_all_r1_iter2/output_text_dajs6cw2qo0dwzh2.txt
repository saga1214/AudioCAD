2.  **Shift in Focus:** The Portuguese route wasn't just a parallel effort; it became the *primary alternative* to bypassing the Ottoman-controlled Black Sea and Mediterranean routes. Finding a way *around* them, not just finding a new overland path, became paramount.
3.  **Monetary Incentive:** The high cost and risk associated with trading *through* Ottoman territories (even via Constantinople/Alexandria) made the prospect of a direct sea route to India *far more lucrative* for monarchs and investors. The fall made the potential profits of a direct route seem even more achievable and necessary.
4.  **Geopolitical Competition:** Kings like Henry of Portugal and later monarchs saw funding these voyages as a way to gain wealth, spread Christianity independently (without Ottoman interference), and establish dynastic prestige on a global scale. The fall of Constantinople intensified this competition.
