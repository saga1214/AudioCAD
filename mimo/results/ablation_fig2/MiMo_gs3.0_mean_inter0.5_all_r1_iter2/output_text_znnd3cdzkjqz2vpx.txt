Okay, you've got the basics, but there are a few more things that will make the whole process much smoother and safer.

You'll definitely need a cordless drill with some good Phillips-head bits. It's much faster and easier for screwing into the studs. Shims are still crucial for getting the base cabinets level underneath.

Make sure to have a sturdy work table or a large flat surface in your kitchen where you can lay out all the cabinets and organize them before you start hanging. This will save a ton of time.

Also, grab some painter's tape. You can use it to mark where the doors and drawers will go, which helps with alignment. And some painter's tape can be used to protect the wall from scratches during installation.

A flashlight will be handy for seeing in corners. And a small utility knife is great for trimming any cardboard backing on the cabinets before you screw them in.

And a super important tip: make sure you have a trash bag handy for all the old hardware and packaging from Habitat cabinets. They reuse parts, so there can be old screws and hinges in there that you need to sort out and replace with new ones.

You're all set! With friends helping and the right tools, you can definitely transform those beautiful savings into a fully installed kitchen.
