You are absolutely right to consider these bigger-picture issues.

While building your connection with him remains vital every day you see him, in this specific difficult case, ensuring client and technician safety must be your number one priority within those first 20 to 30 minutes.

This means two things. First, you need to address the environmental factors. The dogs running around and a cluttered house are major distractions and safety hazards. You should strongly recommend to your supervisor or BCBA that sessions be moved to a clearer space, if possible, or that the dogs be managed—perhaps by being kept outside or leashed during your time there. Your safety is part of creating a safe session for him, too.

Second, safety planning right at the start is critical. Have a clear, simple plan for elopement and aggression. Maybe you have a calm 'safe spot' you can go to together, or specific gentle phrases you use to de-escalate. Starting with music is great, but frame it as a safety tool—using it to calm down before trials begin. Implementing these safety strategies from the very beginning will create a much more effective and sustainable session plan overall.
