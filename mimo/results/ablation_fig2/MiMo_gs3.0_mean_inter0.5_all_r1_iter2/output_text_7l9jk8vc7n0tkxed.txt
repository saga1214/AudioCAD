1.  **Safety First:** Put on your gloves and eye protection. Dealing with blockages often involves dirty water and sharp tools.
2.  **Plunger (You've tried):** Did you use a flange plunger (the double-headed one)? It creates more pressure against the drain opening than the cup plunger. Give it several vigorous, full-up-and-down plunges to create a tight seal.
3.  **Basic Hand Tools (Your Toolbox Strength):**
    *   **Closet Auger (Drain Snake):** This is your BEST bet with a plumber's simple toolbox. It's essential.
        *   Insert the auger head firmly into the drain opening.
        *   Rotate the handle clockwise as you push it in. Keep pushing until you feel resistance – that's likely the blockage.
        *   **Crucial:** Keep rotating! Don't just push straight in. The rotation helps the spring coil hook the debris.
        *   Once you feel it hook, keep pushing and rotating until the debris comes out the other end. You might need to pull the auger back slightly and try again.
        *   When you've cleared it, pull the auger out completely and flush the drain with a bucket of hot water.
    *   **Zip-It Tool (if you have one):** Another simple tool often in basic kits. Works similarly to an auger but is smaller. Insert, push, rotate, and pull back with the debris.
    *   **Hand Auger (if you have a larger one):** If the blockage is deeper in a larger pipe (like a main line), a hand auger might be needed. Use the same principle: push, rotate, hook, pull.
4.  **Manual Clearing (Last Resort):**
    *   **Plumber's Putty/Grease:** If the blockage is slimy organic matter (hair, grease), you might be able to work a generous amount of plumber's putty or grease *down the drain* after using the auger. Let it sit for a while to break things down, then flush it out with hot water.
    *   **Bucket & Wet/Dry Vac:** If you have a basic wet/dry vac, you *might* be able to carefully suck debris out of the drain opening *while* using the auger to keep it from falling back in. This is tricky but possible with a simple box.
    *   **Manual Clearing:** If you can safely see part of the pipe (like a P-trap), you *might* be able to disassemble it with your wrench and remove the clog by hand. Only do this if you're sure you can put it back together correctly without causing leaks.
5.  **Know When to Call for Help:** If you've used the closet auger thoroughly, multiple times, and it *still* won't clear the blockage, it's likely a deep or stubborn clog (like tree roots or severe pipe damage). That's when you'd need specialized tools like a machine auger or a hydro jetter, which aren't in a "simple toolbox." It's okay to call a colleague or a larger service if you hit that point.
