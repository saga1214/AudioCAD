Here's your **clothing gear packing list broken into 20-mile daytime/early evening segment** and **final 12-mile nighttime segment**:

### **20-Mile Segment (Daytime/Early Evening)**
Bring **layers you can add/remove easily**:
1. **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking top (long-sleeve for warmth) and leggings (avoid cotton).  
2. **Mid-Layer**: Fleece vest, light fleece jacket, or puffer vest (adjustable warmth).  
3. **Outer Layer**: Wind/water-resistant shell (lightweight jacket or windbreaker).  
4. **Accessories**: Gloves (optional if it's not too chilly yet), cap/hat (sun protection).  
5. **Footwear**: Trail shoes with grip + extra socks (start with dry pairs at aid stations).  
6. **Extras**: Headlamp/handheld light (just in case), buff/neck gaiter (extra warmth if needed).  

### **Final 12-Mile Segment (Nighttime)**
Prioritize **warmth and visibility**:
1. **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking long-sleeve top + leggings (no cotton).  
2. **Mid-Layer**: Fleece jacket or insulated puffy (20-40°F rated).  
3. buff/neck gaiter (wrap hands/head for warmth).  
4. **Outer Layer**: Windproof/waterproof shell (critical for hilly wind exposure).  
5. **Accessories**: Gloves (insulated or fleece), warm hat (wool or fleece-lined), sunglasses (moonlight can be bright).  
6. **Footwear**: Trail shoes + dry socks (night chills hit extremities fast).  
7. **Extras**: **Strong headlamp + extra batteries** (night vision is non-negotiable), handheld light (backup), zip-off pants (if outer layer gets too warm).  

### **Key Tips**
- **Test your gear beforehand**—try layers at home to avoid adjustments mid-pace.  
- **Use aid stations wisely**: Replenish socks, dry clothes, and warm layers as needed.  
- **Bring a pack**: Carry layers without slowing down.  
- **Battery backup**: Night segments rely heavily on lights.
