That's a great question to clarify! The **fourth component working together in the shoulder for rotation is the clavicle**, your collarbone.

Here's a quick recap of their roles:

1.  **Clavicle (Collarbone):** Connects the arm (humerus) to the main axial skeleton (scapula and sternum). It provides stability to the shoulder joint complex and helps distribute forces during arm movements, including rotation. It acts as an anchor point.
2.  **Humerus (Upper Arm Bone):** The actual bone whose head rotates within the socket of the scapula. It performs the primary rotational movement.
3.  **Scapula (Shoulder Blade):** Houses the glenoid socket where the humeral head sits. Its movement (scapular motion) is crucial for achieving the full range of arm rotation. The shallow socket allows movement but relies on the rotator cuff for stability.
4.  **Rotator Cuff Muscles (Tendons):** These are the active stabilizers. The four muscles (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) work together to hold the humeral head firmly in the socket *during* rotation, preventing it from dislocating and enabling smooth movement. They are essential for *controlling* the rotation initiated by the humerus.

**So, together they do:**
*   The **Clavicle** provides stability and connection.
*   The **Humerus** performs the actual rotational movement.
*   The **Scapula** provides the socket and facilitates its own movement for optimal arm positioning.
*   The **Rotator Cuff** actively stabilizes the joint and controls the movement of the humerus within the scapula.

This interplay between the bony structures and the soft tissues allows for the shoulder's remarkable range of rotation.
