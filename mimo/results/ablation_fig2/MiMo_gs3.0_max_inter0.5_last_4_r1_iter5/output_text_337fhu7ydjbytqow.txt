Here’s a **segmented closing gear packing list** tailored to your pacing duties—20 miles (overnight start) and final 12 miles. Prioritize layers, warmth, and night essentials since temps will drop fast after dusk.

### **1. 20-Mile Segment (Daytime/Early Night)**
Bring lightweight, adaptable gear since aid stations will help with heavy nutrition/water. Focus on staying cool/dry initially, then warm as night hits.  
- **Moisture-wicking base layer** (short-sleeve or sleeveless).  
- **Breathable mid-layer** (light fleece or windbreaker).  
- **Wind-resistant outer layer** (light jacket or vest).  
- **Gloves** (waterproof/synthetic for cold hands).  
- **Lightweight hat** (for head warmth).  
- **Buff or gaiter** (neck protection/wind resistance).  
- **Trail shoes** (grip for hills, quick-drying).  
- **Small handheld light** (handheld headlamp + extra batteries—*critical*).  
- **Lightweight pack or vest** (to carry layers if needed).  

Aid stations likely offer sweatbands, hat, and gloves—so pack extras if you think you’ll need them (e.g., hat for your husband if his gets soaked).  

---

### **2. Final 12-Miles (Fully Nighttime)**
Shift focus to warmth and reliability. Racers here are exhausted—they’ll need your gear if theirs fails.  
- **Moisture-wicking base layer** (as above).  
- **Insulated mid-layer** (fleece or puffy vest/jacket).  
- **Waterproof/windproof outer layer** (light shell for rain/wind).  
- **Warm hat** (fleece-lined or balaclava for frozen ears).  
- **Warm gloves** (fingerless or full-finger—waterproof).  
- **Headlamp or handheld light** (with **extra batteries**—*essential*).  
- **Buff or gaiter** (for wind protection or as an arm/neck buff).  
- **Trail shoes** (same as above).  
- **Small handheld light** (backup in case batteries die).  

---

### **Key Extras for Both Segments**
- **Extra batteries** (headlamps die fast on cold nights).  
- **Lightweight pack** (to stash layers as needed).  
- **Spare socks** (if you sweat through shoes/hands).  
- **Zip ties** (quick-fix for gear tears).  

### **Why This Works**
- **Layers > Heat Retention:** Mid-30s feels colder at night with wind + sweat. Insulation is key.  
- **Lights/Batteries:** Night pacing means low visibility—don’t rely on racers’ lights.  
- **Aid Station Synergy:** Three stations mean you won’t need *everything* (e.g., hat/gloves), but pack extras for others.
