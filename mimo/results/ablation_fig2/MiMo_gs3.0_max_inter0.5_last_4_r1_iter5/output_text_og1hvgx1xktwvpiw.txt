You're right, I see where the confusion came from. Let me clarify. The most historically accurate original Boulevardier often uses an imbalance of one and a quarter to one to one. However, the equal-parts Boulevardier, just like your friend makes, is equally classic and very common. 

Now, for a Boulevardier made with equal parts, you'll need three-quarters of an ounce of bourbon or rye, three-quarters of an ounce of sweet vermouth, three-quarters of an ounce of Campari, and half an ounce of fresh lime juice. For garnish, use a lime or orange twist. Stir it all over new ice until very cold and clear, then pour into a glass.

And for a Last Word, which is also made with equal parts, you'll need half an ounce of fresh lime juice, three-quarters of an ounce of sweet vermouth, three-quarters of an ounce of gin, and three-quarters of an ounce of green Chartreuse. You'll shake all that with ice, then double-strain it into a cocktail glass, and garnish with a maraschino cherry. Let me know if you need anything else!
