You mentioned certification studies. College and youth/school rules differ on one key point: how a defender's deliberate touch resets offside. In college, a controlled play by a defender resets immediately. In youth/school, there's an additional test: the player must not benefit from that touch to have it count. Let me explain using a scenario.

First, imagine a situation where resetting a defender's touch is successful under both rules. The Red team is defending their goal. Blue team forward number 9 is in an offside position when a teammate plays the ball. But, the second-to-last defender makes a deliberate, controlled play on the ball. Since number 9 doesn't get the ball directly from that defender, and the situation is reset before they touch it, player number 9 is no longer considered offside. The play would continue without a call.

Now, consider a situation where it would NOT reset player number 9 under the youth/school rules. Again, the Red team is defending. Blue team forward number 9 is in an offside position. This time, the second-to-last defender makes a deliberate play, but the ball simply deflects off their foot and then Blue forward number 9 collects it and scores. Under the youth/school rules, since the attacker benefited from the rebound, it's still an offside offense. But under the college rules, because it was a deliberate, controlled play, it would be a legal play and no offense.

So the key difference is: College rules reset offside anytime a defender makes a deliberate, controlled play. Youth/school rules reset it only if the player does not benefit from the touch.
