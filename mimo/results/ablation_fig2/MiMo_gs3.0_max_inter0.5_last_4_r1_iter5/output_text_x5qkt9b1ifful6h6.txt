Based on current scientific consensus and large-scale health studies, **ultra-processed foods** are widely considered the one type of food **worst for your health if consumed in excess**. Here’s why:

### Key Reasons:
1. **Nutrient Deficiency**:  
   They’re stripped of fiber, vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats while being packed with **added sugars, refined carbs, unhealthy fats (like trans fats), and excessive sodium**.

2. **Harmful Additives**:  
   Contain industrial ingredients (e.g., artificial colors, preservatives, emulsifiers) linked to inflammation, gut microbiome disruption, and metabolic dysfunction.

3. **Concentrated Harm**:  
   Often high in calories but low in satiety (e.g., sugary drinks, chips, instant noodles), leading to overconsumption and obesity.

4. **Disease Links**:  
   Strongly associated with **type 2 diabetes, heart disease, hypertension, and early mortality**. A landmark study in *The BMJ* (2023) found ultra-processed food intake increased all-cause mortality by 14%.

### Examples:
- Sugary cereals, cookies, and pastries  
- Packaged snacks (chips, frozen meals)  
- Sugary sodas and energy drinks  
- Fast food (burgers, fries)  

### Why Not Other "Worst" Candidates?
While **sugar**, **trans fats**, and **excess sodium** are highly damaging individually, they are *components* of ultra-processed foods. These foods combine multiple risks in one package, making their overall impact more severe.

### Expert Consensus:
Organizations like the **World Health Organization (WHO)** and the **American Heart Association** warn that ultra-processed foods make up ~50% of daily calories in many diets—far exceeding safe limits for additives and empty calories.

### Practical Takeaway for Your Class:
Balance whole, minimally processed foods (fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, whole grains) with strict limits on ultra-processed items. Even small swaps (e.g., soda → water, chips → nuts) yield significant health benefits.
