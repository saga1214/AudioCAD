Yes, you're right. Older comics are generally much rarer because they were printed in much smaller quantities and haven't survived in good condition. Investing in pre-1970s comics can be very rewarding.

However, these are very expensive investments, often costing tens or even hundreds of thousands of dollars. For example, as we mentioned, a record-selling Action Comics number one is six million dollars. 

Similarly, *Superman's Club Society* number one sold in 2024 for two and a half million. *The Human Torch* number one, his first appearance, sold in 2022 for just over two million. And a copy of the first appearance of Captain Marvel, *Whiz Comics* number one, sold in 2021 for one point seven million. So, buying these directly is not a realistic investment path for someone just starting out.

Instead, I can suggest ten valuable books from the 1940s and 50s that you could find for under five hundred dollars. These are great entry points.

Since Luke Cage and The Defenders are your favorites, start with *Incredible Hulk* number 181, the first full appearance of Wolverine. It's a cornerstone book and should be in your price range.

Also, look for *Tales to Astonish* number 45, the first appearance of the Hulk's friend, the Abomination. The first issue of *The Defenders* is, of course, essential. You could also grab *Fantastic Four* number 15, the first full appearance of Galactus, or *Thor* number 129, which introduces Beta Ray Bill.

For your Luke Cage interests, *Fantastic Four* number 122 is a great one; it's the first appearance of Power Man and Iron Fist. You can also look for early key issues for Spider-Man, like *The Amazing Spider-Man* number 34, which is his first appearance in the Silver Age, or *Amazing Spider-Man* number 49, which features the first appearance of the Vulture.

These are all excellent, historically significant books that have proven to be great investments.
