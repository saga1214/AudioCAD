### Why this is the refined answer & the human error challenge

1.  **Core Purpose:** Still controlling who and what enters the building (the point of initial contact).
2.  **Modern Enhancements for Human Error:**
    *   **Technology Backup:** Bag checks aren't just manual; they use X-ray machines to reduce human oversight. Electronic access cards are programmed with strict policies and logged automatically.
    *   **Real-Time Monitoring:** Guards or centralized security centers monitor access points via cameras and intrusion detection systems, providing oversight even if a guard is momentarily distracted.
    *   **Deterrence:** High visibility creates a psychological barrier.
3.  **Why Gardner Happened & How Modern Security Adapts:**
    *   The Gardner heist exploited the *trust* placed in the guards' judgment. Modern access control systems minimize this reliance on individual vigilance.     *   While secure displays are the *final* layer protecting the art *inside*, the *first* line's job is to make sure the art isn't *taken out* of the building in the first place. Modern displays are designed to be extremely difficult to remove *even if* an intruder bypasses the first line.
4.  **Internal Sensors/Alarms:** These are the *third* line. They trigger *after* entry is made. The first line aims to prevent that entry.

**In essence:** Modern security acknowledges that relying solely on human guards at the door is risky.
