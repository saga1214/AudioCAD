Of course. Here is a complete breakdown of a standard, effective resume format that you can use.

First, at the very top, you have your name, written in about a 14-point font. To the right, or on a separate line below your name, you list your contact info. Start with your professional email address in quotes. On the next line, your phone number, followed by 'Phone'. Then on the next line, 'City, State, and ZIP Code'. Using icons is optional and, as we discussed, can sometimes cause ATS issues. After your contact info, you'll have a short professional summary, which is usually three to five sentences highlighting your key skills and career goals. Below that is your relevant experience, where you list your most important jobs first, including the company name, your job title, the dates you worked there, and a few bullet points for each role. After that, you can have a smaller section for additional work history. Then comes your education, where you list your degrees, the school name, your expected graduation date, and any honors or awards you received. Finally, you should have a skills section. It's best to list your skills under a heading that says 'Skills' and then break them down into categories like 'Technical Skills' or 'Languages' to make them easy to scan. This format is professional, clear, and covers all the essential parts of a resume.
