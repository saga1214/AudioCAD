Based on your criteria (rescue consideration, spouse prefers purebred "known traits," under landlord's size/noise rules when alone, aversion to heavy shedding, apartment living, hybrid work schedule, spouse's preferences for trainability, dog-friendliness, child-friendliness, non-chasing, and "chill" nature), here are potential dog breeds and hybrid options to explore:

**Top Breed Recommendations (Purebreds):**

1.  **Basset Hound:**
    *   **Why:** Small to medium, gentle giants. Known for being incredibly dog-friendly, calm, affectionate, and good with kids. Very low-shedding coat (hence aversion to Coon shedding). Can be vocal ("bassets bark") when left alone, so training is key. Generally easy to train with patience.
    *   **Considerations:** Can be stubborn, food-motivated, and prone to chasing squirrels (though usually not aggressive). Needs moderate exercise.

2.  **Bulldog (English Bulldog):**
    *   **Why:** Compact, muscular but not overly large. Calm, courageous, affectionate, and famously good with children and other dogs. Very low-shedding coat. Generally easy to train.
    *   **Considerations:** Short snout can cause overheating in hot weather (avoid high-rise heat). Can be stubborn and sometimes prone to separation anxiety/barking. Can be sensitive to pain.

3.  **Pug:**
    *   **Why:** Small, compact, incredibly affectionate, playful, and good-natured. Very low-shedding coat. Generally good with kids and other dogs. Easy to train.
    *   **Considerations:** Prone to snoring, overheating, and can be stubborn. May have separation anxiety/barking tendencies. Can be prone to certain health issues (brachycephalic syndrome).

4.  **Shih Tzu:**
    *   **Why:** Small, compact, affectionate, alert, and generally good-natured. Very low-shedding coat (long, but fine). Good with kids and other dogs. Can be alert barkers when alone, so training is needed. Moderate trainability.
    *   **Considerations:** Prone to eye issues and dental problems. Can be alert and sometimes bossy. Needs regular grooming (brushing).

5.  **Cavalier King Charles Spaniel:**
    *   **Why:** Small, gentle, affectionate, and incredibly loyal. Excellent with children and other dogs. Generally good trainability. Low-shedding coat.
    *   **Considerations:** Prone to chasing squirrels (though usually not aggressive). Can be prone to certain health issues (heart problems, syringomyelia). Needs moderate exercise.

**Excellent Hybrid Options (Rescues):**

1.  **Basset Bulldog (Basset Hound x Bulldog):**
    *   **Why:** Combines the low-shedding coat of both parents. Inherits the Bulldog's calm, courageous, and affectionate nature. Generally good with kids and dogs. Moderate trainability. Often described as "chill."
    *   **Considerations:** May inherit some health predispositions from either breed. Size/weight can vary.

2.  **Cavalier x Pug / Cavalier x Bulldog:**
    *   **Why:** Combines the Cavalier's gentle nature with the Pug/Bulldog's low-shedding coat. Cavalier traits like being affectionate and good with kids are likely. Low-shedding.
    *   **Considerations:** Cavalier traits might be present (chasing squirrels). Health risks could be a factor depending on the specific mixes.

3.  **Shih Tzu x Bichon Frise:**
    *   **Why:** Both breeds are small, affectionate, and generally good-natured. Inheriting the low-shedding coat from both. Good with kids and dogs. Moderate trainability.
    *   **Considerations:** Size/temperament can vary. Bichons can be more vocal.

4.  **Basset Frise (Basset Hound x Bichon Frise):**
    *   **Why:** Combines the Basset Hound's gentle, calm, and dog-friendly nature with the Bichon's low-shedding coat and cheerful personality. Generally good with kids and dogs. Low-shedding.
    *   **Considerations:** Size/weight can vary. Might inherit some Basset Hound stubbornness.

**Steps Locating Candidates:**

1.  **Clarify "Big" with Landlord:** Ask specifically what size limit they mean (height? weight? breed examples they dislike?).
2.  **Define "Too Noisy":** Ask for examples (barking frequency? type of noise?).
3.  **Research Breed-Specific Rescues/Purebred Breeders:**
    *   **Rescues:** Search for shelters/rescues specializing in the breeds you like (e.g., "Basset Hound Rescue," "Bulldog Rescue," "Cavalier Rescue," "Mixed Breed Rescue"). Focus on those with adult dogs or dogs with known histories (easier to predict temperament).
    *   **Purebred Breeders:** Use the AKC website's breeder directory. Filter by breed. Look for breeders who:
        *   Prioritize health testing (hips, eyes, etc.).
        *   Have a written health guarantee.
        *   Are transparent about their dogs' temperament and socialization.
        *   Are willing to provide references.
        *   Avoid puppy mills (research breeder reputation carefully).
4.  **Visit & Meet:**
    *   **Shelters/Rescues:** Visit in person. Observe how dogs interact with you, other dogs, and staff. Ask about temperament, behavior when left alone (if known), and any known noise issues.
    *   **Breeders:** Visit the home (not just a facility). See the environment. Observe parent dogs' temperament. Ask questions about the puppy's history, socialization, and early training.
5.  **Prioritize Adult Dogs (Especially from Rescues):** Adult dogs (1-3 years) often have a more established temperament and personality, making them easier to predict and potentially less prone to separation anxiety than puppies.
6.  **Utilize the Dog Park:** Take potential candidates to the nearby dog park. Observe:
    *   How they interact with other dogs (playful? avoidant? aggressive?).
    *   How they handle the environment (calm? anxious?).
    *   How they behave when left alone briefly in the park (if you can observe).
7.  **Discuss Training Needs:** Talk to shelters/rescues/breeders about any known training issues. Ask about crate training, basic obedience, and noise management strategies.
8.  **Consider Grooming:** While low-shedding, all dogs need brushing (even short-haired breeds need weekly brushing). Factor in time/cost.
9.  **Talk to Potential Neighbors:** If possible, discreetly ask about noise levels from other dogs in the building.
10. **Make a Final Decision Together:** Revisit your list, weigh pros/cons, and choose the dog that best fits *both* of your preferences and lifestyles.

**Key Considerations for Your Situation:**

*   **Noise When Alone:** This is critical. Prioritize breeds known for being independent/calm or adult dogs who may be more settled. Be prepared for training if barking is an issue.
*   **Spouse's Preference:** Frame mixed-breed rescues as a potential middle ground – they offer the predictability of known lines (purebreds) but the heart and mission of a rescue. Adult dogs from breed-specific rescues are also a great option.

*   **Grooming:** Focus on the low-shedding breeds listed. Remember, even low-shedding breeds need regular brushing to prevent mats.
