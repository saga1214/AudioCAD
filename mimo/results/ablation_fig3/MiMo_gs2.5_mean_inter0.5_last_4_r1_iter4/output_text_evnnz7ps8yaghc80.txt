That's a fantastic plan. Consistency is key. Here’s a balanced weekly schedule designed to help you practice effectively without burning out.

On Monday, start with a five-minute warm-up, just throwing two beanbags. Then, spend about 20 minutes adding the third ball from your dominant hand. Finally, take five minutes to try juggling three beanbags with both hands.

Tuesday is a lighter day. Warm up for five minutes with two beanbags, then spend 15 minutes gently practicing with the scarves, focusing on slow, controlled throws.

Wednesday is a rest day. No juggling at all. Just take a break and do something relaxing.

Thursday, warm up again with five minutes of two beanbags. Then, focus on using music for 20 minutes of beanbag practice. Finally, spend five minutes trying to transition again.

Friday is for the scarves. Warm up with five minutes of beanbags, then spend 25 minutes practicing with scarves, focusing on your rhythm. End with five minutes of two beanbags in one hand.

Saturday is a big session. Warm up with five minutes of one-handed two-bag juggling. Then, spend 25 minutes on your transition to three balls, and another 25 minutes on full three-ball practice. If you get really good, you can try some scarves at the end.

Finally, Sunday is another rest day. No juggling, just recovery.

A few key tips for success: Remember to focus on having fun, not on perfection. Keep your sessions short and consistent, and celebrate the small wins to manage stress. This schedule should help you build your skills in a relaxed way. You can do this, and teaching your son will be worth all the practice.
