Based on your situation, I'd recommend using a **low-temperature glue stick** (like the kind used for paper crafts) or a **fabric-safe glue** (such as E6000 or a spray adhesive made for textiles). These are safer for synthetic fabrics common in winter jackets/fleeces and won't melt the lining like a hot glue gun or iron could.

### Here’s how to do it safely:
1. **Prep the fabric**: Clean the sticky area with a damp cloth and let it dry completely. Press the sticky surface lightly with a cloth to remove any debris.
2. **Apply glue**:  
   - For a glue stick: Rub it directly onto the sticky spot where you want to stick the item.  
   - For spray glue: Hold the can 6–8 inches away and apply light, even sprays.  
   *Avoid using strong adhesives (e.g., super glue or hot glue), as they can weaken fibers or melt synthetic linings.*
3. **Press firmly**: Place the item you need to stick (e.g., a hood, pocket, or patch) onto the glued area. Press down with a cloth or your fingers for 30 seconds to secure it.
4. **Let it dry**: Wait 1–2 hours before handling the garment. For extra hold, use a clothespin or weight overnight.

### Why this works:
- **Glue sticks** are non-permanent and easy to remove with a damp cloth if needed.
- **Fabric sprays** (like Aleene’s Tacky Glue) bond well to synthetic materials without residue.
- *Never use a hairdryer, iron, or heat gun*—these can warp or melt polyester, nylon, or down-filled linings.
