I know that feeling when you're ready to dive in and the tool just won't cooperate. It's so frustrating when a glue gun feels hot but won't actually melt the glue! That's actually a pretty common issue, and there are a few likely reasons why it's happening.

Based on my experience, here are the most common culprits and how to fix them:

### Most Likely Causes & Fixes:

1.  **The Nozzle is Stuck:** This is the number one reason. The heating element inside the glue chamber gets stuck and can't transfer the heat effectively through the nozzle to melt the glue stick. The gun feels hot because the element is running, but the glue isn't getting hot enough.
    *   **The Classic Fix (Soaking):** Unplug the glue gun and let it cool for a few minutes. Then, fill the nozzle with warm (not boiling) water and let it soak for about 10-15 minutes. This often softens the residue and gets the element unstuck. Dry it off with a paper towel, plug it back in, and try again!

2.  **Using the Wrong Glue:** While less common, sometimes the glue stick isn't compatible with the gun. It should be labeled for "hot melt" or "glue gun" use.
    *   **Quick Test:** Try a different brand or type of glue stick. If it works with a different stick, you just need to find a glue you like for your project.

3.  **The Heating Element is Faulty:** If the gun has been left on for a very long time or it's an older model, the element itself might be damaged. It will feel hot but won't get hot enough to melt the glue.

4.  **It Just Needs a Break:** Sometimes, especially with older guns, the element can get temporarily overwhelmed. Unplug it, let it sit for a full hour or so, and then try again.
