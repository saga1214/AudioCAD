Okay, let's break this down. Chronic facial redness can come from many sources. Rosacea is a big one, sun damage over your lifetime adds up, genetics plays a part, and allergies can trigger it too.

Now, for vasculitis. Think of it this way: your blood vessels control how much blood flows to the skin's surface, which is what sets your skin tone. Vasculitis is when your immune system mistakenly attacks these vessels. This attack causes inflammation, which makes the blood vessels widen and become leaky. More blood shows up at the surface, and that's why the area looks red.

This condition affects blood vessels of all sizes, from large arteries and veins to the tiny capillaries. Because the inflammation can happen anywhere, the symptoms vary widely. Common ones are fever, fatigue, and aches. Skin issues are also very common, like rashes, red spots, or painful sores.

Diagnosing it usually starts with a doctor's exam and some blood work. To be certain, a biopsy is often needed. Vasculitis can affect people at any age.

And yes, hair and nail weakness can definitely be a symptom because poor circulation and inflammation affect all the tissues growing from your body. Treatment focuses on calming the immune response, and ibuprofen alone isn't a long-term solution; a doctor can help you create the right plan.
