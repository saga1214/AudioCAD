Based on everything we've discussed—the loss of irreplaceable, unique species, the provision of critical ecosystem services, and the potential for scientific discoveries—the most fundamental and overarching reason for preserving biodiversity hotspots is to prevent the irreversible loss of Earth's biological heritage.

It's about safeguarding the planet's genetic library and its capacity to support life in all its forms. Protecting hotspots ensures we don't lose a generation of knowledge and function that could have future benefits for humanity, all while maintaining the health of the natural systems on which all life, including ours, ultimately depends.
