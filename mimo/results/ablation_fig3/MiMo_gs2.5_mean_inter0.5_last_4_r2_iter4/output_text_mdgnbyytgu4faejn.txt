Of course, I can give you a clear, step-by-step breakdown of a standard resume format. You can just follow along and fill in your information.

First, at the very top, you'll have your name in about a 14 or larger point font. To its right, on a new line for each piece of information, you'll put your primary email, then your phone number, and finally your city. It's best to use icons only if they look very professional and simple. And as we discussed, it's safer to remove email icons.

Next, right under your name, you'll have a professional summary. This is a short paragraph, about three to five sentences, that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals. This is key for showing that you have the right experience for the new field you're targeting.

After the summary, you'll have your main Experience section. This is where you list your most relevant jobs first. For each job, include the company name, your job title, the start and end dates, and then just one or two brief bullet points that highlight your most impressive achievements.

Then, you can have your Relevant Experience section. This is where you put jobs that are a great match for the new roles you're applying to, even if they were a long time ago. Again, just list the company, title, dates, and a couple of key achievements for each.

Following that is your Education section. Here you'll list your degrees, the school's name, your expected graduation year, and any awards right underneath, likeHighest Honors or specific scholarships.

After that, you'll have a Skills section. It's a good idea to tailor this to the job description. You can list your hard skills, like software or technical abilities, and your soft skills, like communication.

Finally, you can add a short Volunteering section if it's relevant to the jobs you're applying for. It's optional, but can be a nice touch.

So, to recap the key points: order contact info below your name, group similar older work together, keep awards with their education, and always tailor your skills section. This structure should give you a clear and effective resume.
