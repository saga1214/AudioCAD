Of course. Chronic redness can definitely have many causes besides vasculitis. These include rosacea, sun damage, contact dermatitis from irritants, genetics, or even underlying medical conditions.

Vasculitis, on the other hand, is inflammation of the blood vessels. It can affect vessels of any size—large, medium, or small—which is why its symptoms can be so varied. Common signs are fever, fatigue, and skin rashes, including things like purple spots or ulcers. The cause is typically an autoimmune problem where the immune system mistakenly attacks the blood vessels.

Diagnosis usually starts with a doctor's exam and blood tests, but a skin biopsy is often needed to confirm it. Symptoms can show up at any age. The main risk of vasculitis is that the inflammation can damage the organs by restricting their blood supply.
