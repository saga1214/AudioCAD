Based on current nutritional science and public health research, **ultra-processed foods (UPFs) are widely considered the *one type of food category* that would be the *worst for your health if eaten in excess*.** Here’s why:

### Key Reasons:
1. **Nutrient Deficiency & Calorie Density**:  
   UPFs are often stripped of fiber, vitamins, and minerals while being packed with refined sugars, unhealthy fats, and excessive salt. This leads to "empty calories" that fill you up without providing essential nutrients.

2. **Metabolic Harm**:  
   Excessive sugar (especially high-fructose corn syrup) and unhealthy fats trigger inflammation, insulin resistance, and dyslipidemia. This directly increases risks for:
   - Type 2 diabetes
   - Fatty liver disease
   - Heart disease
   - Obesity

3. **Habit Formation**:  
   UPFs are engineered with high levels of salt, sugar, and fat to override natural satiety signals, leading to compulsive overconsumption.

4. **Synergistic Damage**:  
   Unlike a single ingredient (e.g., sugar or trans fats), UPFs combine multiple harmful components (e.g., fried chicken nuggets contain trans fats, sodium, and sugar). This multiplies their negative effects.

### Examples of UPFs:
- Sugary cereals, pastries, and desserts  
- Frozen meals (e.g., pizza, chicken nuggets)  
- Packaged snacks (chips, candy bars)  
- Sugary sodas and energy drinks  
- Many processed meats (sausages, hot dogs)  

### Why It Edges Out Other Contenders:
- **Artificial Trans Fats**: Though uniquely harmful, they’ve been largely phased out in many countries due to regulations.  
- **Excess Sugar/Salt**: Harmful individually but less pervasive than UPFs, which dominate modern diets.  
- **Processed Cheeses**: High in sodium but lack the metabolic disruptors of UPFs.  

### The Bottom Line:  
While no single food is harmless in excess, **ultra-processed foods represent a systemic threat** due to their prevalence, nutritional void, and role in driving chronic diseases. Limiting UPFs to occasional treats—while prioritizing whole, minimally processed foods—supports sustainable long-term health. Your biology class is exploring critical topics! 🥦🍎
