### **20-Mile Daytime/Hilly Segment (Daylight to Early Evening)**
Bring **versatile layers** since temps will drop as the sun sets, but three aid stations mean you can swap gear easily.  
- **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking shirt/shorts (avoid cotton).  
- **Mid-Layer**: Fleece jacket or light puffy vest (for hilly wind/coolness).  
- **Outer Layer**: Wind-resistant/waterproof shell (lightweight, packable).  
- **Accessories**: Gloves, lightweight beanie, sunglasses (if sunny).  
- **Extras**: Small pack/vest (to carry layers if needed), knee sleeves (optional for hills).  

### **Final 12-Mile Night Segment (2 AM or later)**  
Prioritize **warmth and reliability**—you’ll be running in darkness for hours.  
- **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking shirt/shorts (stay dry).  
- **Mid-Layer**: Fleece or insulated puffy jacket (critical for warmth).  
- **Outer Layer**: **Same wind shell as daytime** (if dry), or a dedicated insulated rain jacket if rain’s possible.  
- **Accessories**: Gloves (thicker than daytime), warm beanie, **headlamp/handheld light + extra batteries** (non-negotiable).  
- **Extras**: Knee sleeves (hills), buff/gaiter (face/wrists), lightweight pack.  

### **Key Things for Both Segments**  
1. **Layer System**: Wear base + mid-layer under outer layer. Add/remove as needed.  
2. **Extremities**: Hat/gloves prevent cold shock—keep them handy.  
3. **Lighting**: One headlamp + one handheld light with spare batteries.  
4. packability: Use a vest or pack to avoid fumbling with straps.  

### **Pro Tips**  
- **Check the forecast**: If wind increases, add a buff or neck gaiter.  
- **Tough terrain?** Wear supportive trail pants/leggings (not shorts) to protect knees.  
- **Test gear**: Try everything before the race!
