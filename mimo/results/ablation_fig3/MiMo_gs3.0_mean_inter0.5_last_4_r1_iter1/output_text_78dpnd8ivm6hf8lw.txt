Here's a quick summary of the human circulatory system using your child-friendly car analogy:

| **Body Part** | **Analogy Role** | **Function** |
|---------------|------------------|--------------|
| **Heart** | Gas Station & Engine | Pumps blood ("cars") all around the body. Acts as both the engine (power source) and gas station (fuel source). |
| **Lungs** | Fresh Fuel Station | Blood cars stop here to pick up oxygen ("fresh fuel"). |
| **Arteries** | Big Highways | Wide, fast roads carrying blood **away** from the heart to deliver oxygen/energy. |
| **Capillaries** | Tiny Neighborhood Streets | Very small roads where blood cars stop to **deliver oxygen fuel** to muscles/organs. |
| **Veins** | Return Roads | Roads the blood cars take **back** to the heart/lungs to refuel. |
| **Kidneys** | Car Wash & Junk Disposal | Blood cars pass through here to clean out waste ("junk") and extra water. Clean blood continues its journey; waste becomes urine. |
| **Liver** | Service Station | Processes nutrients from food, breaks down harmful stuff, stores important things for later. Keeps the fuel system balanced. |

**The Journey in a Nutshell:**  
Little blood cars start at the **heart** (engine/gas station). They drive down big **arteries** (highways) to pick up fresh **oxygen** (fuel) at the **lungs**. They travel on tiny **capillaries** (streets) to deliver that oxygen fuel to your muscles and organs for energy. Once empty, they return via **veins** (back roads) to the heart/lungs to refuel. Meanwhile, the **kidneys** act as a car wash, cleaning out waste, and the **liver** is a service station, processing fuel and waste. That's how your blood system keeps everything powered up and clean!
