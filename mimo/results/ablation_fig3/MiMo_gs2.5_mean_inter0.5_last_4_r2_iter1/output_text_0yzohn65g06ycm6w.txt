Here’s a concise summary of all advice provided:

1. **Feeding the Fussy vs Greedy Cats**:  
   Use chip-activated or timed feeders to control portions and ensure all cats eat. Feed fussy cats separately in a closed room before leaving home.

2. **Stretching Pet Food Budget**:  
   - Buy quality food in bulk for lower "cost per serving."  
   - Mix small amounts of premium food with cheap fillers (e.g., plain cooked rice) under vet guidance to avoid malnutrition.  
   - Network for pet food donations via community groups/shelters.

3. **Preventative Self-Care (Zero/Money Budget)**:  
   - Maintain hygiene: Wash bowls daily and keep spaces clean.  
   - Prevent obesity: Encourage play with homemade toys (e.g., strings).  
   - DIY basic care: Brush cats to reduce hairballs; offer safe scraps (e.g., plain cooked chicken) for nutrients.  
   - Monitor closely: Track early illness signs (lethargy, appetite loss) to address issues at home before they escalate.

4. **Prioritize Critical Vet Care**:  
   Focus on low-cost essentials: spaying/neutering and vaccinations. Explain your situation to clinics for potential payment plans or advice.

**Key Principle**: Balance greed control with fussy cat inclusion via feeding mechanics. On food scarcity, prevention and community support are critical for health without vet dependency. Do the best you can with what’s available, and always consult a vet before mixing food fillers.
