First, the high-end Pinot Noir. It should be stored at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. It should be served slightly below room temp, so between 55 and 60 Fahrenheit, or 13 to 16 Celsius.

Next, Japanese Hon-Mirin. It should be stored in a cool place at about 60 to 68 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you're tasting it, room temperature is fine, around 68 to 75 Fahrenheit, or 20 to 20 Celsius.

For Italian Chianti, it should be stored at 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius, and served at 60 to 65 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 18 Celsius.

For the Pinot Grigio, you store it at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, or 10 to 13 Celsius, and serve it chilled at about 45 to 50 Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius.

Champagne should be stored at 45 to 50 Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius, and also served at that same temperature.

Sauvignon Blanc is similar: store at about 50 Fahrenheit and serve chilled at 45 to 50.

Mulled wine gets warmed hot to between 140 and 160 Fahrenheit, or 60 to 70 Celsius, but the base red wine is stored at normal cellar temps of 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius.

Hon-Mirin is not really sipped straight, it's for cooking. Room temp is fine if you taste it.

And Champagne is fantastic served cold.
