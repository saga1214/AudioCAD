Okay, let's break this down. Chronic facial redness has a lot of potential causes, so we need to be thorough.

First, let's talk about what vasculitis is. It's basically inflammation of your blood vessels, usually triggered by an autoimmune response where your immune system mistakenly attacks them. This inflammation can affect blood vessels of any size—large, medium, or small—which is why the symptoms can be so varied.

Common symptoms you might see include rashes or blisters, fever, fatigue, and hair and nail issues, likely from poor circulation to the outer tissues. Complications can be serious, like organ damage if blood flow is restricted.

Now, beyond vasculitis, there are many other common causes for chronic redness. Rosacea is a big one, causing visible redness and sometimes bumps. Severe sun damage over a lifetime can also lead to persistent redness. Other possibilities include contact dermatitis from irritants, allergies, or even underlying conditions like autoimmune disorders.

For hair and nails, strengthening besides Biotin, you could look to collagen peptides, keratin, iron, and zinc.

For inflammation management, ibuprofen can provide temporary relief, but it's absolutely crucial you discuss a regular regimen with your doctor, especially if vasculitis is a possibility.

Ultimately, to get to the bottom of your specific symptoms, a dermatologist is essential. They can diagnose the root cause and recommend the right treatment plan for your complexion, hair, and nails.
