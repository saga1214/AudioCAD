No problem! Let's recap everything clearly, categorized for you:

### **I. Basic Measurements (Take at EVERY Fitting)**
1.  **Bust** (under the arm, fullest part)
2.  **Natural Waist** (the narrowest part of the torso, where your waistband sits naturally)
3.  **Hips** (widest part of the hips, usually 8-10 inches below the waist)

### **II. Essential Measurements (Take Depending on the Garment)**
*   **Sleeve Length:** From shoulder point to wrist.
*   **Inseam:** From the crotch down the inner thigh to the ankle (for pants/shorts).
*   **Shoulder Width:** Across the widest part of the shoulder (crucial for great fits on jackets/shirts).

### **III. Key Measurements for Fitted Garments (Add Precision)**
*   **Thigh:** From the top of the thigh down to the widest part (critical for tailored pants/skirts).
*   **Neck:** For accurate collar placement on collared items.
*   **Bicep:** From shoulder point down the arm to the fullest part.
*   **Upper Back (Slope/Width):** From shoulder point down the back to the waist, helps prevent pulling.

### **IV. Specific Tips for Your Client's Pants**
1.  **Back Rise:** Crucial for comfort! Measure from the crotch up to the waist measurement. For someone 6'4" wanting to sit comfortably, **aim for 15-18 inches** (significantly longer than the 12" front rise you noted).
2.  **Hem Length:** Have him try the *finished* pants on **with the shoes he plans to wear**. Pin the hem where he wants it to touch the tops of the shoes. This pinned measurement is your inseam.
3.  **Ease:** Add extra ease (fabric) to the **Hip** and **Thigh** measurements for comfort when sitting down.

**Key Takeaway:** Always try garments on with the *actual shoes* and the *actual fabric* to get the perfect fit, especially for inseam and back rise. Good luck with your side business!
