That's a great question! Here's a breakdown:

1.  **Treat Recommendations:**
    *   **Start Simple:** Choose small, soft, smelly, and easy-to-chew treats your puppy loves (e.g., small bits of cooked chicken, turkey, cheese, hot dog, peanut butter, or store-bought puppy treats).
    *   **Consistency is Key:** Use the *same* kind of treat for a specific command or training session so he knows exactly what to expect.
    *   **Size Matters:** Break treats into tiny pieces so he can eat them while focusing on you. You only need to give one or two per successful action.
    *   **High Value:** For harder-to-teach commands or during initial training, use the tastiest treats you have.
    *   **Timing:** Give the treat *immediately* (within a second or two) after he does the right thing.

2.  **Clicker Training Explained (How it Works):**
    *   **The Clicker:** It's a simple handheld device that makes a sharp, clear "click" sound when you press the button. It's not a musical instrument!
    *   **The Sound:** The click itself means absolutely nothing to the dog initially.
    *   **The Process:**
        1.  **Introduce the Clicker:** Start with simple tricks he already knows well (like taking a treat from your hand). Click *as soon as* he does the trick perfectly, *then* give him the treat. Repeat this 10-15 times. He starts associating the click with a reward.
        2.  **Capture Behavior:** Instead of telling him what to do, watch for a behavior you *like* (e.g., looking at you, sitting, looking at the treat bag). The *instant* he shows that behavior, click *and* give him a treat.
        3.  **Shaping:** This is where clicker training shines. You can gradually build behaviors step-by-step. For example:
            *   *Step 1:* Click when he looks at your hand (which usually has a treat).
            *   *Step 2:* Click when he puts his nose near your hand.
            *   *Step 3:* Click when he puts his nose on your hand and *takes* the treat.
            *   *Step 4:* Click when he sits *before* putting his nose on your hand.
            *   *Step 5:* Click when he sits *and* puts his nose on your hand to get the treat. You're building "sit for a treat" from tiny pieces of behavior.
        4.  **Fade the Clicker:** Once he knows the behavior well, you can start adding a verbal cue ("Sit!") *just before* you click. Then, you can phase out the clicker entirely, relying only on your verbal cue and the treat (or eventually just praise/touch).

    *   **Why it's Effective:** The click acts as a precise marker, telling your dog *exactly* which tiny split-second of his behavior earned the reward. This makes training much faster and more precise than just waiting for a full behavior and then giving a reward.
