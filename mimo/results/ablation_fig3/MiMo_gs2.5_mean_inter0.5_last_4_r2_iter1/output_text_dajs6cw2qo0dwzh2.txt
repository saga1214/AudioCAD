Yes, that's a very reasonable argument. The underlying technologies and ambitions were already in motion. Fall of Constantinople was less of a direct cause and more of a powerful catalyst that sped things up.

But, I would add that the event did have a huge symbolic impact. It proved the Ottomans were a real, expanding threat, making independent routes seem even more urgent and necessary. It also gave a huge boost to the prestige and momentum of Portuguese exploration, making them the undisputed leaders of the race. So, while it wasn't the root cause, it was a major accelerator and a key event that shaped the early phase of the Age of Discovery.
