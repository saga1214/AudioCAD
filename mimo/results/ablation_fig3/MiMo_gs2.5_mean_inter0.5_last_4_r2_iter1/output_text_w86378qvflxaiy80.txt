That's a profound question. While all the reasons we've discussed are vital, the single most critical aspect of preserving hotspots is likely its role as a "genetic library for the future."

Think of it this way: every species in a hotspot holds a unique set of genes. Some of those genes might hold the key to discovering new medicines, developing more resilient crops to fight climate change, or understanding fundamental biological processes.

By losing a species from a hotspot, we're not just losing that animal or plant; we're losing a potential source of future scientific discovery and innovation that could one day benefit all of humanity. So, in a way, we are safeguarding the raw material for our own future scientific progress and problem-solving.
