Okay, let's tackle this step-by-plumber. Here's a logical approach with your simple toolbox:

1.  **Safety First (Crucial!):**
    *   **Shut Off the Water:** Find the main shut-off valve for that fixture (likely a toilet or washing machine). Turn it OFF completely. This prevents flooding if you break through the blockage.
    *   **Protect Yourself:** Wear sturdy gloves (if you have them in your toolbox) and eye protection. Deep clogs often involve debris, grease, or sharp objects.

2.  **Assess & Scope (The "Look Before You Dig"):**
    *   **Is it a Toilet?** If so, start there. Pour a bucket of water into the bowl and wait 15-20 minutes. If it drains slowly, you've confirmed the blockage. If it doesn't drain at all, it's severe.
    *   **If NOT a Toilet:** Check if the water level is significantly lower than other drains (like a sink or shower). This helps pinpoint the source blockage.
    *   **Get a "Scope":** Grab your **wire coat hanger**. Straighten it as much as possible. Bend a small hook at the end. Carefully insert the hook into the drain opening (toilet bowl or sink drain) and gently probe around the pipe. Try to feel for the exact location and type of blockage (is it soft sludge? hard grease? a solid object?). *This is your first diagnostic step.*

3.  **First Tool Attempt: Hand Auger (If available):**
    *   **Check Your Toolbox:** A simple toolbox *might* contain a basic hand auger (the kind with the crank handle and cable). This is your best bet.
    *   **Insert & Crank:** Insert the auger cable into the drain opening. Crank the handle clockwise, pushing the cable forward and turning it as you go. You'll feel resistance when the cable hits the blockage.
    *   **Break it Up & Retrieve:** Once you feel the resistance, keep cranking to break apart the blockage. When you feel it loosen or dislodge, crank the handle to retract the cable. Pull it out and inspect it. You might need to do this several times. Wipe the cable clean after each pull.

4.  **Second Attempt: Manual Removal (If no auger, or auger fails):**
    *   **Grab Your Wrench:** Use a basic wrench (like a adjustable wrench or a pipe wrench) if you have one.
    *   **Locate the Drain Plug:** Find the drain plug under the sink or the P-trap (the U-shaped pipe) on a toilet/sink.
    *   **Disconnect the Trap:** Carefully loosen the nuts connecting the P-trap to the drainpipe and to the sink/toilet bowl. Remove the P-trap assembly. You'll likely find a lot of the blockage here! Clean this out thoroughly. This is often the source.

5.  **Third Attempt: Deep Dive & Mechanical Removal (If P-trap is clear):**
    *   **Check the Drainpipe:** If the P-trap is clear but the blockage persists, the problem is further down the drainpipe.
    *   **Access the Pipe:** You might need to cut into the accessible drainpipe (like a closet bolt in a basement) or unscrew a fitting to access the pipe directly. Use your wrench and possibly a hacksaw (if it's in your toolbox) to cut access if necessary.
    *   **Manual Scooping:** Once you have access to the pipe, carefully use your hands, a stick, or the hook from your coat hanger to try and pull out any remaining debris directly from the pipe. This is messy but effective for localized blockages.

6.  Super Important: **DO NOT USE CHEMICAL DRINKERS!**
    *   With only your simple toolbox, you likely don't have strong drain cleaners. Chemicals can often make deep organic blockages (like grease or hair) worse, create dangerous fumes, and damage pipes. Stick to mechanical methods.

7.  **If it's a Toilet Blockage:**
    *   **Check the Bowl:** If the blockage is deep in the toilet, disconnect the overflow tube (the vertical pipe that goes into the tank) and check the bowl for sludge.
    *   **Mechanical Removal:** You might need to cut open the toilet drain flange (the pipe coming out of the floor) and manually clear the blockage from the pipe itself. This requires more tools, but your basic pliers and wrench could help.

**Remember:**

*   **Patience & Persistence:** Deep blockages take time. Don't get discouraged if the first method doesn't work immediately.
*   **Clean as You Go:** Keep wiping down the drain opening and tools to prevent debris from falling back in.
*   **One Problem at a Time:** Focus on clearing the blockage in the drain pipe itself. If the P-trap is the culprit, clear that first.
*   **Know When to Call for Help:** If the pipe is completely shot (cracked) or the blockage is too deep for your tools, it's time to call a colleague with more equipment. That's not stress, it's smart problem-solving.
