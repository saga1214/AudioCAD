Let's talk about indirect free kicks versus direct free kicks. In college high school soccer, both use the same US Soccer Federation rules. So, a key difference isn't in the offside rules themselves, but in how they interpret situations like an offside offense.

For example, in your study scenario where the blue team forward was offside and scored, college soccer treats it as an indirect free kick at the spot of the offense. This means no one can touch the ball before the kick is taken.

This is different from some interpretations in professional soccer. In the pro game, if an offside player touches the ball before the goal, they can be penalized with a direct free kick, which could lead to a penalty kick if it happens inside the penalty area.

So, for you as a referee preparing for college games, the most important thing to remember is that college soccer almost always defaults to an indirect free kick for an offside goal, unless the offside player actively touches the ball first, which might then lead to a direct free kick if it's in the penalty area.
