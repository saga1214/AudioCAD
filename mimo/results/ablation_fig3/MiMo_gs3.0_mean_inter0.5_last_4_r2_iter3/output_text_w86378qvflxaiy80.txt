After weighing the unique species, the potential for new medicines, and especially the vital role in providing ecosystem services, I would say the most critical aspect of preserving biodiversity hotspots is that they act as the planet's "irreplaceable gene bank."

Think of it this way: each unique plant and animal is a living piece of genetic information that has evolved over millennia. We have no idea what potential these genes hold, whether for new medicines, for climate-resilient crops, or for other future scientific breakthroughs.

Once a species goes extinct because its habitat was destroyed, that entire genetic library is lost forever. Preserving hotspots is, therefore, a form of long-term insurance for humanity. We are safeguarding a vast, unknown potential for scientific and medical discovery, ensuring that we don't lose the keys to solutions we haven't even imagined yet.
