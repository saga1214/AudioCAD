That's a great question. Was it a happy occasion for both Pilgrims and Wampanoag families on the exact same level? Almost certainly not.

For the Pilgrims, it was a genuine celebration. They survived their first winter, secured a political ally, and were deeply grateful.

For the Wampano-og, it was likely a more complex diplomatic celebration. They were securing an alliance to protect themselves from rival tribes and gain resources. While there might have been a sense of gratitude for the harvest and new potential, the feeling was probably mixed with the awareness of the European settlers and the devastating epidemics that had recently swept through their communities.

So, while both sides were celebrating harvest and cooperation, the depth and nature of that happiness were likely very different for each group.
