First up is the Ford Mustang, especially the first-generation ones from 1964 to 1966. They’re iconic, easy to find, and appreciate steadily. A good one can easily double in value in a few years.

Then there's the Chevrolet Corvette from the C1 or C2 models, which is from 1953 to 1967. They're American muscle cars that are highly sought after and their value has just kept climbing over time.

Number three is the Porsche 911, specifically the early air-cooled models from the 1960s and early 70s. These are highly collectible and their value can skyrocket if it's in good condition.

Fourth, you could look at a Jaguar E-Type. It's a British classic with a really sleek design. It's become a cult favorite, and its value has gone up a lot in the last decade.

And fifth, the Ferrari 250 GTO. This one is more for the truly serious collector, but it's one of the most valuable cars in the world. A single one can sell for over $70 million, so it's more of a long-term investment.

If he’s into American cars, the Mustang or Corvette are probably the most accessible. If he’s open to something European, the Porsche or Ferrari are great, but they might need more maintenance. 

The key is framing it as a fun way to preserve and grow wealth, not just leaving cash to earn one or two percent. It’s a hobby that can also be a really smart investment.
