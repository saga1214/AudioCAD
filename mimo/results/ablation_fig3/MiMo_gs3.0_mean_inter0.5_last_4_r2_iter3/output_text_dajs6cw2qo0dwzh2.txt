Okay, you've really nailed a key point here. You're right, the fall of Constantinople didn't magically cause it.

While Portuguese advances were already critical, the shock of 1453 acted as a powerful catalyst. It massively funded Portuguese ambitions, exposed the strategic risks of the existing routes, and terrified rivals like Spain and England. It transformed the race from a gradual development into an intense, high-stakes competition for global dominance. So, while the foundation was already being laid, 1453 provided the massive impetus that really launched the Age of Discovery as we know it.
