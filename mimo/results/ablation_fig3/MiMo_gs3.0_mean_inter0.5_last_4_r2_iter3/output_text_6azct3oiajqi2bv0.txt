Using Velcro (or hook-and-loop fastener) directly on winter clothing can be tricky because winter fabrics (like fleece or nylon) are often thicker, softer, or more delicate than synthetic activewear. Here’s how to do it safely without damaging the lining:

### Best Methods to Stick Velcro Securely:
1. **Use a Dedicated Velcro Adhesive Strip**  
   → Purchase a **wide, soft-backed Velcro strip** (e.g., "Velcro® Brand" or "Hook & Loop Tape" on Amazon/eBay).  
   → **Width matters:** Use a 1.5" or wider strip to distribute pressure evenly.  
   → **Apply to a fabric backing:** Cut the strip to size, but **add a piece of thin cardboard or non-stick backing** (like a foam sheet) *under* the hook side. This prevents direct contact with the jacket’s lining.  
   → **Press firmly** for 30 seconds after each layer.  

2. **Use Spray-On Adhesive (Temporary Option)**  
   → Apply a **light coat** of spray adhesive (like 3M Super 77) to the Velcro’s loop side.  
   → Hold it in place for 20 seconds, then press the hook side onto the jacket.  
   → **Test first:** Spray onto a hidden seam or hem to avoid stains.  

3. **Lapel Pins or Clips (Low-Risk Alternative)**  
   → If the jacket has a lapel or collar, use **decorative safety pins** or **fabric clips**.  
   → These are reusable, leave no residue, and won’t damage fabric.  

4. **Avoid Direct Contact (Critical for Winter Clothing)**  
   → **NEVER** press Velcro directly onto fleece, corduroy, or thick nylon. It will likely snag or pull threads.  
   → **Test in a hidden area** (like an inside seam) first.
