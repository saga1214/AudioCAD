Investing in original Golden Age comics from the 1940s and 1950s is extremely high-risk and generally not recommended for most collectors.

Here’s why:

First, their condition is very fragile. Paper degrades over 80 years, and a high-grade copy is often worth tens of thousands of dollars. Most affordable copies are in poor shape.

Second, authenticity is a huge problem. Forgeries and reprints are rampant, and you need an expert to verify them.

Third, the market is niche and volatile. Most older books have plummeted in value compared to the 1990s boom. Only the absolute biggest keys, sold by top auction houses, retain high value.

For example, some iconic books under five hundred dollars include *Whiz Comics* number one from 1951, which is Captain America's first cover appearance. You could also get *Flash Comics* number one from 1939 for Green Lantern's first cover. Other smart buys under that price are *Tales to Astonish* number one from 1955, *Timely Comics* number one from 1939, *Strange Tales* number one, *The Avengers* number one, and *The Fantastic Four* number one. You might even find a modern Luke-centric book like *Ultimate All-New All-Different Defenders* number one under that budget.

For a safer entry point, I'd suggest modern or Silver Age books under five hundred dollars, like Luke-related ones I mentioned earlier, or other strong keys such as *Amazing Spider-Man* three hundred (Venom), *Savage She-Hok* one, and Miles Morales books. These are more durable, easier to authenticate, and have better long-term value potential.
