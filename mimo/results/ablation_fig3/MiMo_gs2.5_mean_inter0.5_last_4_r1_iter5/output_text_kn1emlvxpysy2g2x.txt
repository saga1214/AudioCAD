Of course, let's review the wines we've discussed and their temperatures. I'll also add a quick comparison for hot versus cold weather.

First, the high-end Pinot Noir: you store it at cellar temp, around 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius, and serve it slightly below room temp, at about 55 to 60 Fahrenheit, which is 13 to 16 Celsius. It's best for cool to moderate weather, but if it gets too warm, it can lose those delicate aromas.

Next, Japanese Hon-Mirin: store it at a cool place, about 60 to 68 Fahrenheit or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you're sipping it directly, room temp works, at about 68 to 75 Fahrenheit or 20 to 24 Celsius. It's not typically sipped alone, but if you do, it's best at room temperature.

Then there's Chianti, the Italian red wine: store it at about 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius, and serve it a bit below room temp, at 60 to 65 Fahrenheit or 16 to 18 Celsius. It's best for moderate weather, similar to Pinot Noir.

For Pinot Grigio, the Italian white wine: you store it at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, or 10 to 13 Celsius, and serve it chilled at about 45 to 50 Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius. It's fantastic for hot weather because it's so crisp and refreshing.

And finally, the hot wine, which is usually mulled red wine. You store the base wine at cellar temp, about 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius, but you warm it up for serving to 140 to 160 Fahrenheit, or 60 to 70 Celsius. This is the perfect choice for cold weather.

So, to quickly compare: for hot weather, Pinot Grigio is the best cold choice. For cold weather, mulled red wine is the best warm choice. Pinot Noir and Chianti are both great for moderate or cool weather.
