Okay, let's break this down. Chronic skin redness, or erythema, has many potential causes. Common ones include rosacea, long-term sun damage, genetics, allergic reactions, and contact dermatitis. Other possibilities are infections, like from staph or fungal issues, or heat. Systemic conditions like vasculitis or fibrous dysplasia can also be a cause, although the link between fibrous dysplasia and both vasculitis and chronic redness is rare.

Now, about vasculitis. It's an inflammatory condition where your immune system mistakenly attacks your blood vessels. This inflammation can happen in large, medium, or small blood vessels. Because blood vessels are everywhere, vasculitis can affect any part of your body. Common symptoms include a fever, feeling unwell, fatigue, and rashes, which can be red, purple, or even weepy. Pain or tenderness in your limbs or joints can also be a sign. The cause isn't fully understood, but it's often autoimmune-related. Treatment focuses on suppressing the immune response, usually with medications like steroids. The main risk is that the inflammation can lead to serious issues like reduced blood flow, which can damage organs.

Ibuprofen is an anti-inflammatory, but it's not a cure for the underlying vasculitis. It might help with temporary inflammation, but it's not a long-term solution. You should always consult a doctor before starting any new medication, especially for a complex condition like this. A proper diagnosis is the most important first step.
