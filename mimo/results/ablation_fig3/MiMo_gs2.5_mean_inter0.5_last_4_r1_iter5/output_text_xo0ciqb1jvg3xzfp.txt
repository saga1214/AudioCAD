The traditional story of the "first Thanksgiving" being a single, happy event for everyone involved is a significant oversimplification. 

For the Pilgrims, it was a joyful celebration of their survival and a successful harvest. For the Wampanoag, it was a strategic event to solidify an alliance.

But for the Wampanoag people, especially those who had lost family to the diseases brought by the settlers, it was not a purely happy occasion. For them, the arrival of the English marked the beginning of immense hardship and the loss of their way of life.

So, it's more accurate to say it was a complex event, not a simple, happy moment for everyone. The traditional story often leaves out the Wampanoag's perspective, which is why David Silverman's book is so important. It helps to show the full, and much more complicated, picture.
