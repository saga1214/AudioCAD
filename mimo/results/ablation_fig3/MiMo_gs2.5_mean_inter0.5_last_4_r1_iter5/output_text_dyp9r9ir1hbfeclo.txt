That's a great question to clarify! While the **three primary bones** (humerus, scapula, clavicle) form the *skeletal* structure for shoulder rotation, the **scapula itself acts as the fourth critical component** enabling that rotation. Here's why:

1.  **Humerus & Scapula (Glenohumeral Joint):** The head of the humerus fits into the shallow socket (glenoid cavity) on the *anterior* surface of the scapula. This forms the main ball-and-socket joint responsible for most of the shoulder's circular rotation (internal/external rotation). The scapula's position and movement are crucial for this.
2.  **Clavicle (Sternoclavicular Joint):** The clavicle connects the scapula to the sternum (breastbone). It provides a stable base for the scapula and allows it to move *on* the ribcage. The clavicle also helps stabilize the shoulder joint during movement.
3.  **The Scapula's Role:** This is the key fourth element. The scapula doesn't just sit there; it **moves**:
    *   **Protraction (Forward):** When lifting the arm forward.
    *   **Retraction (Backward):** When bringing the arm back.
    *   **Elevation (Upward):** When lifting the arm overhead.
    *   **Depression (Downward):** When lowering the arm below shoulder height.
    *   **Rotation (Medial/Lateral):** When turning the shoulder blade inward or outward.

**How the Scapula Enables Rotation:**

*   As the humerus rotates internally or externally (e.g., turning your palm up/down), the scapula must simultaneously **elevate or depress** to create space for the humerus to move freely within the shallow socket. This is called **scapulohumeral rhythm**.
*   The movement of the scapula on the clavicle and ribcage provides the necessary platform and dynamic adjustment for the humerus to rotate smoothly.

**In Summary:**

*   **Bones:** Humerus, Scapula, Clavicle.
*   **Joints:** Glenohumeral (shoulder) joint, Sternoclavicular joint.
*   **Fourth Critical Component:** The **Scapula**. Its dynamic movement (protraction, retraction, elevation, depression, rotation) is absolutely essential for the humerus to perform its circular rotation effectively. Without scapular movement, the shoulder joint would be restricted and inefficient.

So, while the humerus *rotates* within the socket on the scapula, the scapula itself is the dynamic fourth bone that makes that rotation possible and functional.
