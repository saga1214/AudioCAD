That's a great question, and it really gets to the heart of what drove the Age of Discovery. You're right that the Age of Discovery was a complex phenomenon that didn't just start with one event.

The Portuguese were absolutely on a path to establishing sea routes to Asia. The fall of Constantinople in 1453 was a huge catalyst that sped things up. It created a sense of urgency and competition, especially for other powerful kingdoms like Spain and Portugal, who were eager to get a piece of the action and avoid being dependent on others. So, while the Age of Discovery would have happened eventually, the speed and intensity of it were massively accelerated by that event.
