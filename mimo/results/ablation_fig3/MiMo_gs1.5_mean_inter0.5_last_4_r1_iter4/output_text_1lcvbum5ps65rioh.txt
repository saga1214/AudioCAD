Here's a breakdown of U-turn rules in British Columbia and the ingredients you'll need for spaghetti carbonara:

**U-Turn Rules in British Columbia (BC):**

1.  **General Rule:** U-turns are generally permitted on straight, clear roads.
2.  **Key Restrictions:**
    *   **Not at Intersections:** You **cannot** make a U-turn at an intersection with a traffic control signal (stop sign, yield sign, traffic light, stop bar) unless a sign specifically authorizes it.
    *   **Not Near Obstructions:** You cannot make a U-turn if it would interfere with pedestrians, cyclists, or other vehicles (e.g., near driveways, bus stops, intersections without signals, curves, hills, tunnels, or where visibility is obstructed).
    *   **Visibility:** You must be able to see a clear and safe path for a distance of at least **150 meters (approx. 490 feet) in both directions** before making the turn.
    *   **Not in Bus/Tram Lanes:** U-turns are prohibited in bus lanes, lanes marked with arrows, or in lanes reserved for buses or trams.
3.  **Penalties:** Making an illegal U-turn is an **infraction**. If caught by a police officer, you can expect a **ticket**. The fine can range from **$100 to $160 plus a 1% surcharge** (as of late 2023/early 2024), depending on the specific violation and your driving history. It's also considered unsafe driving.
4.  **Best Practice:** Always **check signs** for "U-Turn" or "No U-Turn" symbols. If in doubt, wait at an intersection with a signal or find a designated U-turn lane.

**Spaghetti Carbonara Ingredients (Authentic Version):**

Here's the classic ingredient list you need to buy:

1.  **Pasta:** Spaghetti is traditional, but other long pasta like Bucatini or Rigatoni also work well.
2.  **Eggs:** **4 large whole eggs + 4 large egg yolks** (This creates the rich, creamy sauce without cream).
3.  **Cheese:** **1 cup (approx. 100g) grated Pecorino Romano cheese** (Salty and sharp). Parmigiano-Reggiano can be used partially instead if preferred, but Pecorino is key for authenticity. Mix well with the eggs.
4.  **Pancetta or Guanciale:** **150-200g (5-7 oz) diced pancetta** (Italian cured pork belly) or **guanciale** (cured pork cheek - even better if you can find it). *Substitute:* Diced, cooked bacon (smoked bacon is less traditional but works).
5.  **Garlic:** **1-2 cloves**, finely minced or grated (optional, but common).
6.  **Black Pepper:** Freshly ground black pepper to taste (essential!).
7.  **Salt:** For cooking pasta.
8.  **Pasta Water:** Reserved from the pasta cooking water (very important for adjusting sauce consistency).

**Key Notes for Carbonara:**

*   **NO CREAM:** Authentic carbonara gets its creaminess from the emulsion of eggs, cheese, and pasta water. Adding cream makes it a different dish.
*   **Cheese:** Pecorino Romano is salty and sharp. If using Parmigiano-Reggiano, you might need slightly less salt. Mixing Pecorino and Parmigiano is common.
*   **Pancetta/Guanciale:** Must be cooked until crisp before adding to the pasta. This renders out delicious fat.
*   **Egg Mixture:** Whisk the eggs, grated cheese, and a generous amount of black pepper together thoroughly in a bowl.
*   **Timing is Crucial:** Cook the pasta *al dente*. Reserve at least 1 cup of hot pasta water before draining. Immediately combine the hot pasta with the crispy pancetta/guanciale in the pan. Remove from heat. Quickly toss in the egg/cheese mixture. The residual heat will cook the eggs gently without scrambling. Add hot pasta water *a little at a time* to create a silky, creamy sauce that clings to the pasta. Serve immediately, topped with extra grated Pecorino and black pepper.
