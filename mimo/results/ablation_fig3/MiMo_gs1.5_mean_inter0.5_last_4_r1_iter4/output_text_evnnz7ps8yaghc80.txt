That's fantastic you're planning ahead! Scheduling makes all the difference. Here’s a balanced weekly practice plan incorporating beanbags, scarves, music, and both transition strategies. Aim for **15-25 minutes per session**, 5 days a week. Consistency is more important than marathon sessions.

**Weekly Practice Schedule:**

1.  **Monday: Foundation & Rhythm (Beanbags)**
    *   **Focus:** One-Handed Two-Ball Mastery & Music.
    *   **Activity:** Practice juggling two beanbags *only with one hand*. Toss and catch them smoothly. Focus on the distinct rhythm ("throw, catch, throw, catch, throw, catch"). Put on a song with a clear beat. Time your throws to the beat, not just your own rhythm. Don't worry about catching yet, just get the throws smooth and rhythmic. *Goal: Feel the rhythm deeply with one hand.*

2.  **Tuesday: Transition Strategy 1 (First Three Throws)**
    *   **Focus:** Adding the Third Ball - First Three Throws.
    *   **Activity:** Hold two beanbags in your dominant hand and one in the other. Practice the sequence: Toss Beanbag 1 from Dom. As Beanbag 1 peaks, toss Beanbag 2 from Other. As Beanbag 2 peaks, toss Beanbag 3 from Other. Focus *only* on this sequence. Catch is optional at first. Do this for 5-10 minutes. *Goal: Get the sequence pattern ingrained.*

3.  **Wednesday: Transition Strategy 2 (One-Handed Mastery) & Scarves**
    *   **Focus:** Reinforce One-Handed Two-Ball & Introduce Scarves.
    *   **Activity:** Spend 10 minutes reinforcing one-handed two-ball rhythm with beanbags (using music if helpful). Then, spend 10-15 minutes practicing with scarves. Juggle two scarves with one hand. Notice how slow they fall. Focus on smooth, controlled throws and catches. *Goal: Smooth one-handed throws & slow-motion pattern awareness with scarves.*

4.  **Thursday: Hybrid Practice (Beanbags & Scarves)**
    *   **Focus:** Combine Techniques & Catch Focus.
    *   **Activity:** Alternate 5 minutes of practicing the "First Three Throws" sequence with beanbags (focus on catching if possible), then 5 minutes of juggling scarves (focus on smooth catches). Then, spend 10 minutes specifically practicing catching beanbags after the third throw sequence. *Goal: Integrate techniques & build catching confidence.*

5.  **Friday: Pattern Exploration & Fun**
    *   **Focus:** Pattern Practice & Enjoyment.
    *   **Activity:** Use beanbags. Try juggling simple patterns like the "Granny" (just switching hands: right, left, right, left...) or the "Criss-Cross" (right, left, right, left... but swap which hand throws which bag). Use scarves if you want a slower pattern practice. Keep it light and fun! *Goal: Enjoyment, pattern recognition, low-stress practice.*

6.  **Saturday/Sunday: Optional Short Sessions or Rest**
    *   **Focus:** Catch-Up, Review, or Rest.
    *   **Activity:** Do a very short (10-15 min) session focusing on a specific weak point (e.g., just the sequence on Tuesday, or just catches on Thursday), or simply rest. If you feel good, review a technique. *Goal: Maintain momentum or allow recovery.*

**Key Tips for Success:**

*   **Warm-up:** Do 2-3 minutes of wrist circles, shoulder rolls, and light tossing between hands before starting.
*   **Mindset:** **Focus on progress, not perfection.** Celebrate getting one extra throw in a row! Acknowledge the small wins. Remind yourself why you started (your son!).
*   **Stress Management:** If you feel panic rising, STOP. Take 3 deep breaths. Do 10 minutes of *only* one-handed two-ball throws or scarf juggling. Reset. Stress *will* block learning. It's okay to have off days.
*   **Tool Switching:** Use beanbags for most practice (they're forgiving). Use scarves when you feel overwhelmed or want slow-motion focus. Use tennis balls if you want a firmer feel for catching.
*   **Music:** Keep a playlist handy! It sets the perfect pace.
*   **Be Kind:** Juggling is hard! Everyone struggles with the two-to-three transition. It's normal to feel stuck for a while. Trust the process and the schedule.

**Remember:** This schedule provides structure, but listen to your body and mind. If you're exhausted one day, skip it. If you're feeling good, add a few extra minutes. The most important thing is showing up consistently and being patient with yourself. You've got this! Your son will be thrilled to see you practice and learn.
