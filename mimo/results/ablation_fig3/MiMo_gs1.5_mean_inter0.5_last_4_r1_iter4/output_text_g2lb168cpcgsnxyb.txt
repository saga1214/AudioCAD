You've hit on two critical, non-negotiable issues.

Connecting with the client is the heart of good therapy, and your rapport is a strength. However, the environment is a major safety and ethical concern that must be addressed first.

The absolute top priority is to inform the BCBA about the lack of parental supervision and the cluttered, dog-filled environment. This is because:

First, there are significant safety risks. The elopement makes him prone to leaving, and the cluttered space, combined with active dogs, is a major hazard for both him and you.

Second, there's a big ethical concern. Parental or caregiver supervision is standard practice for liability and safety. Without it, you're operating outside of protocol.

Third, the environment itself is a major barrier to learning. Clutter is overstimulating and distracting, which likely contributes to his aggression and desire to leave. The dogs running around also add to the chaos.

So, my recommendation is to pause your current sessions until this is resolved. You need to document everything: the lack of supervision, the clutter, the dogs, and any specific safety incidents.

Then, you must present these facts to your BCBA immediately. Work together to develop a safety plan. This could involve things like creating a designated, less-cluttered session area, managing the dogs during your time there, or even exploring telehealth if in-person isn't safe.

Once a safe environment is established, you can then focus on building on that strong connection you have to structure engaging, reinforcing activities that lead into his learning goals. Safety has to come first.
