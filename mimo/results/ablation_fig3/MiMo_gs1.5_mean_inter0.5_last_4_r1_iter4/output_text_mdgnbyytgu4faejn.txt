Of course. Here is a complete breakdown of a standard resume format that you can follow. It's designed to be clear and professional.

First, at the very top, is your name, in about a 14 to 16-point font. Right below that is your contact information, all in the same section. This should include your professional email address, your phone number, and your city, state. You can also add a link to your LinkedIn profile and your personal website or portfolio if you have them. A good tip is to put icons next to your email and phone, but use the standard ones, not custom ones.

Next comes your professional summary. This is a short, two-to-three sentence paragraph where you highlight your most relevant skills and career goals.

After that is your work experience section. Here, you'll list your jobs in reverse chronological order, meaning the most recent one first. For each job, include the company name, your title, the start and end dates, and your location. Then, under each job, use bullet points to describe your key responsibilities and achievements. Try to use action verbs like 'managed', 'developed', or 'analyzed', and whenever possible, include numbers to show your impact.

Then, you'll have a skills section. You can list your technical skills, like software or programming languages, and your soft skills, like communication or leadership. It's a good idea to tailor this list to the job you're applying for.

If you have any, you can include a section for your education. Here, you'll list your degree, the university, your graduation date, and any honors you received, like 'Summa Cum Laude'.

If you have a lot of relevant volunteer work, you can add a section for that too.

Finally, at the very bottom, you can have an optional section for certifications or licenses.

Just remember to keep the formatting clean and simple, and to proofread it carefully before sending it out. You can always ask me to help you refine any specific part of this format.
