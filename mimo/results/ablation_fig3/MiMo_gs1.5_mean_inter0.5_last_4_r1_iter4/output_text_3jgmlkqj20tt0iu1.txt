Okay, so you're asking about the best way to initiate a raise conversation and how to manage your nerves. Let's break this down.

First, regarding the best way to start the conversation, the most common advice is to request a dedicated meeting. You should schedule a specific time to talk with your boss, making it clear that you want to discuss your career progression and compensation. This shows you're professional and prepared. It's important to frame it as a collaborative discussion, not a demand. You could say something like, "I'd like to schedule some time to discuss my role and contributions and see how we might align my compensation with my performance." This approach gives you control over the timing and sets a positive tone.

Now, for managing your anxiety, there are a couple of effective strategies. One is to practice visualization. Before the meeting, take some time to mentally rehearse the conversation. Imagine yourself speaking calmly and clearly, and picture your boss responding positively to your contributions. This can really help reduce feelings of anxiety by making the situation feel more familiar and manageable. Another strategy is to focus on the facts. Anxiety often comes from worrying about the outcome, so grounding yourself in the facts of your performance can be very helpful. You could prepare a list of your key achievements and quantify them where possible. This shifts your focus from your fears to the evidence you have to support your case, which helps keep you calm and confident.
