Based on your paper's focus on modern-day security and acknowledging the critical point about human error, **controlling access at every entry point** remains the definitive **first line of defense** for an art museum.  

Here's why, incorporating your insights:  

1.  **Prevention Over Detection:** The primary goal of the first line is to *prevent* unauthorized access *before* it happens. Human error (as seen in the Gardner heist) can be exploited, but robust physical barriers and screening *reduce* that vulnerability.  
2.  **Physical Barriers:** This includes:  
    *   **Visible Security Presence:** Guards deter casual threats and respond immediately to breaches.  
    *   **Access Control Systems:** Keycard/PIN entry for staff areas, visitor registration/bag checks, and turnstiles physically restrict entry.  
    *   **Perimeter Security:** Fencing, bollards, and controlled entrances create the initial physical barrier.  
3.  **Mitigating Human Error:** While guards are crucial, they are part of the access control layer. This layer *complements* them by enforcing rules (like no outside bags) and providing automated checks (X-ray scanners) that don't rely solely on guard vigilance.  
4.  **Layered Security (Defense-in-Depth):** The first line (access control) is designed to fail *safely* and trigger the next layers:  
    *   **Layer 2: Surveillance & Alarms:** High-tech cameras, motion detectors, and glass-break sensors *detect* breaches that might slip past the initial access control (e.g., a guard distraction).  
    *   **Layer 3: Secure Displays:** Reinforced cases, anti-dig foundations, and secure mounting *delay* and *deter* theft even if an intruder breaches the building.  

**Why the Gardner Heist is Relevant:**  
The 1990 Gardner heist starkly illustrates the vulnerability of relying *solely* on human vigilance (guards falling for a police ruse). The thieves exploited a *failure* in the first line (access control). The museum's art was then vulnerable because the *next* layers (internal surveillance, alarms, secure displays on those specific works) were bypassed.  

**Conclusion for Your Paper:**  
While technology like cameras, alarms, and secure displays are vital *secondary* layers, the **first line of defense** must focus on **preventing unauthorized entry** through a combination of **physical barriers, access control systems, and trained security personnel**. This layered approach mitigates the inherent risk of human error in the initial screening and entry process, making it the foundational step in protecting irreplaceable cultural assets.
