Okay, so you're going to tackle stir-fry. Don't worry, I can help you avoid the smoke alarm this time. The key to good stir-fry noodles is all about the water.

First, make sure you're using a metal pot, not wood, so no more smoke alarms. Bring a good amount of water to a rolling boil. A good rule of thumb is about four to six cups of water for one pound of noodles. Then, add a generous pinch of salt to the water. This helps season the noodles from the inside.

The most important step is to add the noodles right when the water returns to a full, rolling boil. As soon as you drop them in, stir them gently. This helps prevent them from sticking together. Cook them just until they are al dente, which means they are tender but still have a little bite. Stirring them frequently is key to making sure they cook evenly.

Once they're done, drain them immediately in a colander. And here's the final trick: rinse the noodles under cold water right after draining. This stops the cooking process and makes them easier to toss with your sauce. Then, just toss them with your stir-fry sauce right away. If you do all that, you should have perfectly cooked noodles. Good luck!
