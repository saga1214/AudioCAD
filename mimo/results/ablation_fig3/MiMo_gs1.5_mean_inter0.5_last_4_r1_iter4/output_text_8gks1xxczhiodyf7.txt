Based on the evidence, **the biggest top driver behind increasing obesity rates is the easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods.** Here's why:

1. **Hyper-Palatability**: Processed foods are engineered to be irresistibly tasty (high in sugar, fat, salt, and refined carbs), triggering dopamine responses in the brain that override natural satiety signals. This makes it easy to overeat.
2. **Nutrient Poverty**: They're low in protein, fiber, vitamins, and minerals, so they don't promote fullness. People eat more to meet nutritional needs, consuming excess calories.
3. **Portion Control**: Pre-packaged foods often come in larger portions than home-cooked meals, encouraging overconsumption.
4. **Affordability & Convenience**: They're cheaper and require less time to prepare, appealing to budget constraints and busy lifestyles.
5. **Aggressive Marketing**: Billions are spent promoting processed foods to target vulnerable groups (e.g., children, low-income communities).

### Why Other Factors Are Important but Secondary:
- **Physical Inactivity**: While crucial for overall health, it doesn't fully explain obesity trends. People with sedentary jobs *still* eat processed foods and can gain weight.
- **Genetics/Hormones**: These influence susceptibility but don't drive population-wide obesity. Processed foods exploit biological vulnerabilities.
- **Stress/Sleep**: These affect metabolism and appetite, but they're often consequences of modern lifestyles—rooted in diet and inactivity.

### Key Insight:
Even if someone exercises (like in your scenario), eating processed foods in large portions can still lead to weight gain because **calorie surplus is the core mechanism of obesity**. Processed foods make achieving that surplus effortless. Conversely, unprocessed calorie-dense foods (e.g., nuts, avocados) are harder to overconsume *if* portions are controlled and exercise is included.

**In short: Processed foods create a perfect storm of overconsumption, nutrient deficiency, and marketing power that overwhelms individual willpower and biological regulation.** Addressing this through policy (e.g., taxes on sugary drinks), education, and food environment reform is critical for tackling obesity at scale.
