Okay, let's tackle this step-by-step. Since the plunger didn't work, it's likely a deeper, more stubborn blockage. Here’s a clear plan using your simple toolbox:

1.  **Gather & Prepare:**
    *   **Tools:** Ensure you have your plunger (still), drain snake (if you have one), a bucket, and a pair of gloves. *If you don't have a drain snake, skip to Step 4.*
    *   **Safety:** Put on your gloves. Protect your hands and clothes.
    *   **Containment:** Have the bucket ready near the drain. Place a towel around the sink basin to catch any overflow if needed.

2.  **Step 2: Use the Drain Snake (Auger)**
    *   **Insert:** Carefully insert the snake into the drain opening. You want it to go as far down as possible.
    *   **Engage:** Start rotating the snake handle **clockwise**. This is the key step. Keep applying steady, moderate pressure.
    *   **Feel & Pull:** You should feel resistance as the spiral head gets under the clog. Once you feel it bite, start pulling the snake back out *slowly* and steadily, still rotating clockwise. You might feel it catch or move the clog.
    *   **Repeat:** If it doesn't clear on the first try, try again. Sometimes it takes a few passes. Be patient – don't force it hard, or you could damage the pipe or push the clog further down.

3.  **Step 3: Try a Bucket & Wet/Dry Vacuum (If Available)**
    *   **Create a Plug:** Use the plunger to plug the drain opening tightly.
    *   **Vacuum:** Place the wet/dry vacuum nozzle firmly over the plunger's rubber seal. Turn the vacuum on. The suction should help dislodge the clog. Keep the vacuum on for a minute or two, then turn it off and remove the nozzle. Check if it cleared.
    *   **Alternative:** If you don't have a vacuum, carefully plug the drain with your hand or a folded cloth, then pour a significant amount of hot water down the drain in several stages (e.g., 1 cup, wait 30 sec, another cup). This can sometimes soften the clog enough for the snake to work better next.

4.  **Step 4: Consider a Pipe Wrench (Last Resort - Use Caution)**
    *   **Only if Necessary:** This is aggressive and risks damaging pipes. Only try this if the snake and other methods fail.
    *   **Prepare:** Have towels ready to catch water and debris. Wear thick gloves.
    *   **Locate & Mark:** Find the access panel (P-trap) under the sink. Mark the exact spot on the pipe where you need to work.
    *   **Support:** Place a sturdy box wrench or adjustable wrench *underneath* the pipe, directly supporting it where you marked it. This is CRUCIAL to prevent breaking the pipe.
    *   **Remove:** Place the pipe wrench *on top* of the pipe, squarely on the mark. Apply firm, steady pressure, rotating slightly if needed, to break the joint. It might be a slip nut or compression fitting. Be prepared for some mess and water spillage. Have the bucket ready to catch anything that drains out.

5.  **Step 5: Chemical Drain Cleaner (Use with Extreme Caution)**
    *   **Warning:** This is a last resort. It's harsh, can damage pipes, create fumes, and isn't always effective on tough organic clogs. Only use if you have no other options and it's the *only* tool in your toolbox besides basic hand tools.
    *   **Follow Instructions:** Read the product label *carefully* and follow all instructions precisely. Wear gloves and eye protection.
    *   **Pour:** Pour the correct amount *slowly* down the drain. Do not pour other chemicals down the drain simultaneously.
    *   **Wait:** Let it sit for the full contact time specified on the label (usually several hours, sometimes overnight).
    *   **Flush:** After the contact time, flush the drain thoroughly with very hot water.
