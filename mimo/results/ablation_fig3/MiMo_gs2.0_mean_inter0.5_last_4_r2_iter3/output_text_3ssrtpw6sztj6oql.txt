You've hit on a critical point: **human error is a universal vulnerability**, so the *most robust* "first line of defense" must be a **layered system where human judgment is minimized and technology automates control**. Here’s the refined answer for your paper:

### The First Line of Defense: **Controlled Access & Automated Barriers**
1. **Physical Screening & Presence**: Visible security personnel at all public entry points enforce screening (e.g., bag checks, ID verification). This deters intruders and allows immediate intervention.  
2. **Automated Access Control**: Use of **keycard/PIN/biometric systems** for all staff and contractors entering restricted areas (e.g., storage, offices). This ensures only authorized personnel bypass manual checks.  
3. **Reinforced Entry Points**: Automated doors, anti-slip mats, and intrusion alarms on all entrances to prevent forced entry.  

### Why This is the "First Line"  
- **Proactive Deterrence**: Screening and guards prevent threats before they escalate.  
- **Time-Buying**: If breached, reinforced cases and mounting systems (Layer 3) force intruders to defeat multiple barriers, triggering alarms and allowing response.  
- **Human-Technology Balance**: While humans are fallible, combining their judgment with automated barriers (keycards, sensors) reduces reliance on error-prone decisions.  

### Contrast with the Gardner Heist  
The 1990 Gardner heist succeeded because the thieves exploited **human trust** (posing as police). The museum’s first line failed here—screening was bypassed. Modern museums now prioritize:  
- **Protocol training** for staff (e.g., "never open for anyone without verification").  
- **Decoy alarms** to mislead intruders.  
- **AI-powered surveillance** to detect anomalies (e.g., loitering).  

### Key Takeaway for Your Paper  
Modern security prioritizes **prevention over reaction**. The first line isn’t just a gate; it’s a **system** where technology and human processes work in tandem to make unauthorized access nearly impossible.
