Based on the criteria of **energy conversion efficiency, reliability, and infrastructure synergy**, **hydropower** emerges as the single most efficient renewable energy source overall. Here’s why:

1. **Highest Conversion Efficiency**:  
   Hydropower plants convert **~90%** of water's kinetic energy into electricity. This dwarfs solar (~20-25%) and wind (~35-50%) in terms of direct energy conversion efficiency.

2. **Reliability & Dispatchability**:  
   Unlike solar/wind, hydropower is **on-demand**. Turbines adjust instantly to grid needs, eliminating intermittency issues. This makes it a "baseload" source, providing stable power 24/7.

3. **Infrastructure Synergy**:  
   Leverages existing dams, rivers, and canals (e.g., repurposing flood control infrastructure). Retrofitting older dams for hydropower avoids massive new construction costs.

4. **Long-Term Cost-Effectiveness**:  
   Once built, operational costs are minimal. A single hydropower plant can generate **decades of clean energy** for under $0.05/kWh, far below solar/wind’s current averages ($0.03–0.08/kWh).

5. **Scalability & Storage**:  
   Large-scale projects (e.g., Three Gorges Dam) can generate gigawatts of power, while small installations (run-of-river) suit remote areas. Pumped-storage systems use excess hydropower to store energy in reservoirs, acting as a "battery" for the grid.

**Key Considerations**:  
- *Geographic Limitations*: Requires significant water flow or elevation change.  
- *Environmental Impact*: Ecosystem disruption (mitigated by modern fish ladders and low-impact turbines).  
- *Climate Resilience*: Less vulnerable to extreme weather than solar/wind.  

**Conclusion**:  
While solar and wind dominate in accessibility and cost reduction, hydropower’s unmatched efficiency, reliability, and infrastructure adaptability make it the **most efficient renewable source overall**—especially where water resources are available.
