I'm glad you're back and ready to tackle this. Deep blockages can be tough, but a methodical approach works wonders. Here's a step-by-step plan to get you through it:

**Step 1: Confirm the Blockage Type**
*   Grab a wire coat hanger. Straighten it as much as possible.
*   Bend a small hook at the end.
*   Carefully insert the hook end into the drain and gently fish around. Try to feel for the *exact* location of the blockage (a solid object, grease, hair, etc.). This gives you the best clue for your next tool.

**Step 2: Try a Drain Snake (Auger)**
*   If you suspect a solid object or a deep blockage, this is your best bet.
*   Insert the tip of the snake slowly and steadily into the drain.
*   **Crucial:** Once it enters, *don't* yank it back out immediately. Apply steady, firm pressure and rotate the handle clockwise *while* gently pushing it forward.
*   You'll feel resistance as you engage the clog. Keep pushing and rotating until you feel it break through or "give."
*   Slowly and carefully pull the snake back out. You might pull the clog out with it!

**Step 3: Use a Bucket Plunger (If You Don't Have a Snake)**
*   If the blockage is near the surface or the snake didn't work, try a **bucket plunger** (the type with a rubber head shaped like a bucket).
*   Create an excellent seal over the drain opening.
*   Use strong, rhythmic plunges (20-30) with your body weight, not just your arms. The goal is to build up significant water pressure inside the pipe to dislodge the clog.

**Step 4: Try a Chemical Drain Cleaner (Use with Caution)**
*   **Only if you're comfortable with chemicals and the pipes are PVC (not metal or old cast iron).**
*   Pour a small amount (follow the instructions!) into the drain.
*   **Do NOT use boiling water.**
*   Wait the full contact time (usually 15-30 mins).
*   Check if it worked. If not, try again with a different product or method. Chemicals can damage pipes and are hazardous.

**Step 5: Last Resort - Remove the P-Trap**
*   If the clog is stubborn and all else fails, you might need to remove the P-trap under the sink or wherever the blockage is.
*   **This requires tools:** Adjustable wrenches, pliers, a bucket for water, and a towel.
*   **Process:** Disconnect the supply line (if any), loosen the nuts holding the P-trap to the pipe, and carefully lift it off. Check the trap for the clog. You can then flush it out with a hose or use a plunger on the open trap. Reassemble carefully.

**Remember:**
*   **Patience is Key:** Deep blockages take time. Don't rush the snake or the plunger.
*   **Safety First:** Wear gloves and eye protection, especially when using chemicals or working under sinks.
*   **You've Got This:** Plumbing is problem-solving. Each step you take is progress. You're helping someone, and you're skilled at it. Take it one step at a time.
