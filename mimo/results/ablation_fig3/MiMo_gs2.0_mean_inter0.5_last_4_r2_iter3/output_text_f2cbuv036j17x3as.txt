Great question! Pre-trip maintenance is crucial for an old diesel camper van. Here’s a breakdown of key checks, especially for brakes and spark plugs:

### 🔧 **Brake System Inspection**  
**Signs of Wear:**  
- **Squealing/grinding noises** when braking  
- **Soft or spongy pedal feel** (air in the system)  
- **Vibration** when stopping (worn rotors/pads)  
- **Burning smell** or **dark residue** on pads  
- **Reduced stopping power**  

**DIY Checks (Basic):**  
1. **Visual Inspection:**  
   - Look for **cracks/dust** on rotors (drums) and **thinness** of pads (use a caliper gauge if available).  
   - Check **brake fluid level** (in the reservoir – should be between "MIN/MAX").  
   - Inspect **hoses** for cracks/swelling.  
2. **Test Drive:**  
   - Press the brake pedal firmly at low speed. If it sinks slowly, **top up fluid** (only to MAX line). If it still sinks, **get it professionally bled**.  

**⚠️ When to See a Mechanic:**  
- If pads/rotors are worn down (less than 3mm).  
- If fluid is **discolored** (dark brown = contamination).  
- If there’s **squeaking** after new pads (alignment/bedding-in issue).  

---

### ⚙️ **Spark Plug Replacement (Diesel Engine)**  
**Why It Matters:**  
Diesel engines run on compression ignition, so spark plugs (if equipped) help ignite fuel-air mixtures. Old plugs can cause misfires, poor fuel economy, or rough idling.  

**How to Replace (General Steps):**  
1. **Tools Needed:**  
   - New spark plugs (match your van’s specs – consult the manual).  
   - Socket/wrench set (typically **10mm–13mm**).  
   - Dielectric grease (for boot insulation).  
   - Screwdrivers (if coils are held on).  
2. **Steps:**  
   - Disconnect the **battery** (negative terminal first).  
   - Remove **insulated boots/coils** from plugs.  
   - Unscrew plugs (turn counterclockwise).  
   - Install new plugs (hand-tighten first, then snug with a wrench – *don’t over-tighten!*)  
   - Apply **dielectric grease** to boot connections.  
   - Reattach boots and reconnect the battery.  
3. **Pro Tips:**  
   - Check **gap** on new plugs (use a gap tool – specs vary by engine).  
   - Clean **throttle body** (if plugs are fouled, carbon buildup may follow).  
   - Replace **spark plug wires** if cracked/frayed.  

**⚠️ Caution:**  
- Diesel engines often have **multiple coils per cylinder** – replace all at once.  
- If unsure, **hire a mechanic** (especially for coil-on-plug systems).  

---

### 🛠️ **Other Critical Checks for Diesel Vans**  
1. **Oil & Filter:**  
   - Change oil **before the trip** (use **diesel-specific oil**).  
   - Check oil level/condition (should be clean, no metal flakes).  
2. **Coolant:**  
   - Top up and inspect for **rust/sludge**.  
3. **Tires:**  
   - Check pressure, tread depth, and **age** (many tires wear out after 6 years).  
4. **Fluids:**  
   - Brake fluid, transmission fluid, power steering fluid.  
5. **Battery:**  
   - Test charge capacity – clean terminals.  
6. **Exhaust System:**  
   - Check for leaks or rust (common in older diesels).  

---

### 🚨 **When to Skip DIY & See a Mechanic**  
- If your van is **15+ years old** or has **high mileage**.  
- If you hear **unusual noises** (knocking, rattling).  
- If the van **struggles to start** or idles roughly.  

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** Schedule a **pre-trip inspection** at a diesel-specialized shop. They’ll check for hidden issues (e.g., fuel injector clogs, PTO pump wear) that DIY checks might miss.
