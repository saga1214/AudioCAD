That's a great follow-up! While the three **bones** (humerus, scapula, clavicle) form the *skeletal framework* for shoulder rotation, the fourth critical component enabling smooth rotation is the **tendon of the supraspinatus muscle**.  

Here’s the breakdown:  
1. **Humerus**: Your upper arm bone.  
2. **Scapula**: Shoulder blade, providing the socket for the humerus.  
3. **Clavicle**: Stabilizes the scapula and connects it to the sternum.  
4. **Tendons (specifically the supraspinatus tendon)**: This tendon attaches the rotator cuff muscle to the humerus. It helps initiate arm abduction (lifting your arm out to the side) and stabilizes the humerus in the socket during rotation.  

**Why this matters for rotation**:  
- The supraspinatus tendon is part of the **rotator cuff** (along with infraspinatus, teres minor, and subscapularis).  
- It acts like a "lubricant" and stabilizer, ensuring the humerus glides smoothly within the shallow glenoid socket of the scapula.  
- Without healthy rotator cuff tendons, rotation becomes unstable, leading to pain, stiffness, or injury (like rotator cuff tears).  

**Lateral Raises Connection**:  
If your friend felt **soreness** during dumbbell lat raises, it could indeed involve rotator cuff tendons (especially the supraspinatus), which are heavily engaged in this movement. Persistent or sharp pain warrants evaluation.  

**Bonus Insight**: Ligaments (like the glenohumeral ligaments) also stabilize the joint, but tendons are the primary "working parts" for rotation.  

For your med exam, remember:  
> **"Bones form the structure; tendons enable movement."**
