Let's discuss the indirect free kick for dangerous play, which is a key difference from the International Laws. In this scenario, the Red Team's defender, number two, uses an illegal screening action to block the path of the Blue Team forward, number seven. This is considered dangerous play. The referee sees the foul and stops play.

Now, here's the difference: according to the Laws of the Game, the referee would blow the whistle immediately and award an indirect free kick to the Blue Team from the spot of the offense. However, in the US high school rules, the referee will let the play continue for a few seconds and only then blow the whistle to stop it. After stopping play, they will award the indirect free kick to the Blue Team, but from the spot where they first saw the foul occur, not where the play is now. This is a significant difference in how the game is officiated and managed.
