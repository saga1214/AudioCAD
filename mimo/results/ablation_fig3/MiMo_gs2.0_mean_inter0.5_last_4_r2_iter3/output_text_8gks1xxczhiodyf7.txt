If I had to choose **one top driver** for increasing obesity rates, it would be the **easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods** that are low in nutrients. This is because:  

1. **Caloric Surplus**: Processed foods are engineered to be hyper-palatable, leading people to eat more calories without feeling full.  
2. **Affordability & Convenience**: They’re often cheaper, shelf-stable, and marketed aggressively, making them a default choice.  
3. **Nutrient Deficiency**: Low fiber, protein, and vitamins means fewer satiety signals, perpetuating overeating.  
4. **Behavioral Impact**: They displace healthier whole foods and fit into sedentary lifestyles.  

### But the *single most important factor* is still **caloric imbalance**:  
- **Obesity occurs when calorie intake > calorie expenditure**, regardless of food type.  
- Even calorie-dense *unprocessed* foods (e.g., nuts, oils) can cause weight gain without exercise.  
- Conversely, smaller portions of processed foods *can* prevent obesity if calories stay within limits.  

### Why processed foods are still the top driver:  
They make achieving a calorie surplus **easier and more habitual**. While *any* excess calories can lead to obesity, processed foods uniquely exploit human biology (e.g., salt/sugar cravings) and modern environments (fast food culture, food deserts).  

**In short**: The **caloric surplus** is the core issue, but processed foods are the primary *catalyst* making it widespread.
