The most important aspect of preserving biodiversity hotspots is their role as **irreplaceable centers of biodiversity that simultaneously provide critical ecosystem services essential for human survival and resilience**. Here's why this is paramount:

### 1. **Irreplaceability of Life**  
   - Hotspots contain **>5,000 plant species found nowhere else on Earth** (meeting the 1,500-plant threshold).  
   - Example: The **Madagascar hotspot** hosts 10% of all land-based species—90% of its lemurs and 96% of its land birds are endemic.  
   - Extinction in these areas means permanent loss of genetic diversity and ecological functions.

### 2. **Ecosystem Services for Human Survival**  
   As you noted from Conservation International's data:  
   - Though hotspots cover **only 2.5% of Earth’s land surface**, they deliver **35% of ecosystem services** vital for vulnerable populations.  
   - These services include:  
     - **Water purification**: Forests in the **Cerrado (Brazil)** filter water for 300 million people.  
     - **Pollination**: Coral reefs in the **Indo-Malay hotspot** protect fisheries feeding 60 million people.  
     - **Climate regulation**: Grasslands in the **Sahel (Africa)** sequester carbon and buffer against droughts.  

### 3. **Catalyst for Global Conservation**  
   Hotspots act as **strategic leverage points**:  
   - Protecting one hotspot can safeguard interconnected ecosystems (e.g., Himalayan foothills support the Ganges and Brahmaputra river basins).  
   - They focus resources where biodiversity loss is both rapid and concentrated, amplifying conservation impact.  

### 4. **Uncharted Scientific Value**  
   Hotspots are "biological treasure troves":  
   - **Medicinal potential**: The **Tropical Andes hotspot** hosts plants like *Plectranthus tomentosus* (used to treat cancer).  
   - **Climate resilience**: Mangroves in the **Mediterranean hotspot** reduce storm surges and store carbon 4x faster than rainforests.  

### 5. **Ethical Imperative**  
   Myers’ original framework included habitat loss >70%, acknowledging that **human development is the primary threat**. Protecting hotspots is a direct response to our responsibility to prevent preventable extinctions driven by agriculture, urbanization, and resource extraction.  

### In Essence  
Preserving hotspots isn’t just about saving "rare species"—it’s about **safeguarding the infrastructure of life**: the water, food, climate stability, and genetic resources that billions depend on. Their small size belies their outsized role as the planet’s most efficient conservation targets. As Conservation International states: *"Protecting hotspots isn’t just about saving nature; it’s about securing humanity’s future."*
