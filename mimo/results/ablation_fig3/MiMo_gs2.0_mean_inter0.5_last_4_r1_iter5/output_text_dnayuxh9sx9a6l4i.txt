Based on your criteria—easy to train, friendly with kids/dogs, low prey drive, and ideally purebred or mixed breed—here’s a list of excellent options, keeping your landlord’s "not too big" rule and solo-leave suitability in mind:

### Top Breed Recommendations:
1. **Poodle (or Poodle Mixes)**  
   - **Size:** Standard (50+ lbs), Miniature (11-15 lbs), Toy (under 6 lbs).  
   - **Temperament:** Highly intelligent, trainable, gentle, and non-aggressive. Poodles have **zero prey drive** and adore kids/dogs.  
   - **Hybrid Option:** "Bernedoodles" (Bernese + Poodle) or "Cockapoos" (Cocker Spaniel + Poodle) are popular low-shedding mixes.

2. **Cavalier King Charles Spaniel**  
   - **Size:** 30-40 lbs (ideal for apartments).  
   - **Temperament:** Affectionate, calm, and people-oriented. Excellent with kids/dogs. **Low prey drive** and easy to train.  
   - **Note:** Prone to health issues (heart conditions); adopt adult rescues for better temperament.

3. **French Bulldog**  
   - **Size:** 20-28 lbs.  
   - **Temperament:** Playful but low-energy, great apartment dogs. Minimal barking (landlord-friendly!). **Low prey drive** and friendly with all.  
   - **Caution:** Can be stubborn during training; prone to overheating.

4. **Bichon Frise**  
   - **Size:** 12-18 lbs.  
   - **Temperament:** Cheerful, hypoallergenic, and adaptable. Trained early, they’re great solo-leavers. **Zero prey drive** and love socialization.

5. **Golden Retriever (if "not too big" allows 55-75 lbs)**  
   - **Temperament:** Gentle, patient, and eager to please. **Extremely low prey drive**. Ideal for families.  
   - **Solo-Leave Note:** Needs early crate training to prevent destruction.

### Rescue/Adoption Tips:
- **Prioritize Adult Dogs:** Rescues often have vet records and known temperaments. An adult dog is already trained, less likely to chase squirrels, and better suited for solo hours.  
- **Breed-Specific Rescues:** Search for "Poodle Rescue," "Cavalier Rescue," or "Breed-Specific" shelters.  
- **Ask Questions:** At rescues/shelters, ask:  
  *"Has this dog lived with kids/dogs? Is their prey drive low? Do they bark when alone?"*

### Key Considerations:
- **Training:** All breeds listed are trainable, but start socialization/potty training early.  
- **Noise:** Avoid breeds prone to separation anxiety (e.g., Pomeranians). Test dogs at home before adopting.  
- **Hybrid Perks:** Mixed breeds often combine best traits (e.g., a "Pugapoo" = French Bulldog + Poodle = low-shedding + chill).

### Next Steps:
1. **Visit Shelters:** Focus on adult dogs matching these breeds.  
2. **Temporary Trial:** Many rescues offer short-term foster periods.  
3. **Landlord Approval:** Show a vet check and training plan to confirm suitability.  

Rescue dogs like adult Poodles or Bichons often fit your needs perfectly while saving a life. Prioritize temperament over breed purity—many rescues have purebred mixes with stellar records! 🐾
