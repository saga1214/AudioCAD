### 2. **Does the eye see continuously?**  
**No—and yes**, depending on how you define "see":  
- **Physically (eye only)**: The eye does **not** perceive continuous motion.  
- **Cognitively (brain + eye)**: The brain *creates* the perception of continuous motion.  

#### Why?  
- **Saccades**: The eye moves in rapid, jerky jumps (saccades) to track objects. During a saccade, the brain **temporarily shuts off visual processing** (saccadic masking) to avoid motion blur.  
  - *Result*: You’re briefly "blind" during eye movements.  
- **Stable Vision**: Only during brief pauses between saccades does the brain process stable visual input.  

#### How the brain "sees continuously":  
1. **Blends Inputs**: The brain merges visual data from multiple saccades into a seamless experience.  
2. **Predicts Motion**: It anticipates where objects will be based on prior input.  
3. **Fills Gaps**: During saccades, the brain uses context to "fill in" what it missed.  

---

### 3. **Frame Rate Analogy**  
- **Eye (sensor)**: Captures discrete snapshots (like 60 fps max).  
- **Brain (processor)**: Renders these into a 24 fps "cinematic" experience.  
  - *Why 24 fps feels "real"*: Your brain’s motion-processing system evolved to interpret discrete inputs as fluid. Higher frame rates (e.g., 60 fps) simply provide more data for the brain to work with—no new perception is created.  

---

### 4. **Scientific Evidence**  
- Studies show humans can detect flicker up to **~60 Hz** (60 fps). Beyond this, motion appears continuous.  
- Saccadic masking is measurable: During a 200 ms saccade, visual processing drops to near zero.  
- Brain regions like the **superior colliculus** and **visual cortex** actively reconstruct motion during saccades.

### Key Takeaway:  
The eye is a **sensor** with limits (discrete snapshots, saccadic masking). The brain is a **processor** that constructs reality from these inputs. Neither sees "continuously"—they collaborate to create the illusion.
