If forced to choose **one top driver** behind increasing obesity rates, I’d argue it’s **easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods**. Here’s why:  

1. **Affordability & Convenience**: Processed foods (e.g., chips, sugary snacks, frozen meals) are often cheaper, longer shelf-stable, and marketed aggressively—making them the default choice for busy lifestyles.  
2. **Nutrient Deficiency**: They’re typically low in protein, fiber, and nutrients, so people eat more without feeling full, leading to chronic overconsumption.  
3. **Habit Formation**: Frequent exposure through advertising normalizes overeating these foods, disrupting natural hunger cues.  
4. **Metabolic Impact**: Even if portion sizes are controlled, the high sugar/fat content can disrupt insulin sensitivity and fat storage over time.  

### Why Not Other Factors?  
While inactivity, stress, sleep deprivation, and genetics contribute, **diet is the most direct and modifiable lever**. You can’t out-exercise a poor diet long-term, but you *can* shift eating habits. Processed foods make it easier to slip into overeating without realizing it.  

### The Nuance  
This isn’t to dismiss other roles—like sedentary lifestyles or food deserts—but processed foods act as a **catalyst**. They lower the barrier to overconsumption, making obesity more likely even if other factors (like activity levels) are managed.  

**In short: Processed foods make it *easy* to gain weight, while whole foods make it harder—making them the biggest driver.**
