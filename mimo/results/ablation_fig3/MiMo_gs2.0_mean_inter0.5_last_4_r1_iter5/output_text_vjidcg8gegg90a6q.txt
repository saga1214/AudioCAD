For rodent-proof bedding in a chicks' brooder area (especially on concrete with rubber mats), **sand** is your best option. Here's why and how to use it:

### Ideal Bedding for Chicks: Sand
1. **Rodent-Resistant**:  
   Sand is difficult for rodents to dig into or nest in. It’s also abrasive, making it uncomfortable for them.
2. **Insulating**:  
   Sand retains warmth well when combined with the radiant heat from your brooder plate. The rubber mats also provide an insulating barrier against the cold concrete.
3. **Easy to Clean**:  
   Simply scrape and replace the top layer of sand weekly. Compacted sand underneath won’t become a wet, ammonia-filled mess like shavings can.
4. **Dry & Safe**:  
   Sand stays dry, reducing the risk of chilling or respiratory issues. It’s also non-toxic and doesn’t clump, so chicks won’t get stuck.

### How to Set Up:
- **Layer the Rubber Mats**:  
  Secure rubber stall mats tightly to the concrete floor to cover cracks. Overlap them to prevent gaps.
- **Add Sand**:  
  Spread 2–3 inches of sand over the mats. Replace it every 1–2 weeks as it compacts.
- **Seal Cracks**:  
  Use hardware cloth or metal flashing to cover any gaps around the brooder walls or door. Rodents can chew through gaps as small as 1/4 inch.

### Alternative Bedding (if sand isn’t available):
- **Hardware Cloth + Deep Litter**:  
  Cover the rubber mats with hardware cloth, then add pine shavings (use the deep litter method as described earlier). Monitor moisture closely.
- **Wood Chips/Straw (Limited Use)**:  
  *Only* use very small, tightly packed flakes of wood shavings or straw. Avoid loose straw—it’s a major rodent magnet. Replace daily.

### Key Tips:
- **Ventilation**:  
  Ensure airflow above the chicks to prevent condensation and frostbite. Use a low-hanging vent cover.
- **Heat Source**:  
  Place the brooder plate at a consistent 90–95°F. Use a thermometer probe to monitor the center of the brooder area.
- **Regular Maintenance**:  
  Sweep out droppings daily to keep bedding dry. Check for rodent signs (droppings, chew marks) weekly.

Sand bedding is low-cost, durable, and rodent-resistant—perfect for your setup! 🐔❄️
