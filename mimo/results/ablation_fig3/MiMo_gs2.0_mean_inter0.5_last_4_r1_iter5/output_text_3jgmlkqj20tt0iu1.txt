Okay, so you're feeling confident at work but anxious before meetings, and you're thinking about asking for a raise. You've also brought up time management and how to approach that first raise conversation. Let's break this down.

First, about when to ask for a raise, the general rule of thumb is one year. Six months is possible but risky unless you have a huge justification. As for improving your work, time management is key. Techniques like batching similar tasks together and using time blocking on your calendar can really help you stay on top of everything. 

Now, for the main question: how to start that raise conversation. Here are two ideas. First, you could request a formal performance review. This is a structured time where you can discuss your contributions and the raise in a professional setting. It gives you a clear agenda and reduces the pressure of a spontaneous conversation. Second, you could schedule a brief, focused meeting with your boss. Just propose a short chat to discuss your career development and compensation, and keep it specific to avoid any confusion. Both approaches are professional and help you manage the anxiety by setting clear expectations.
