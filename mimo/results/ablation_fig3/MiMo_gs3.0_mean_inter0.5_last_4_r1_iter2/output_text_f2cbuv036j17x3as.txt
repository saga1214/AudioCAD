Okay, diesel camper vans age, and maintenance is crucial before a long trip. First, brakes: look for any uneven wear on the pads or rotors. Also, check if the parking brake engages smoothly—if it feels spongy or doesn’t hold, that’s a problem. And make sure there’s no unusual noise or vibration when you brake. Next, spark plugs: since diesel engines use compression ignition, they don’t have spark plugs. Instead, they have glow plugs, which help the engine start in cold weather. Those do need to be checked and replaced if they’re worn out. You can do that yourself if you’re handy, but it’s not a simple job and requires a special socket. Honestly, the best approach is to take it to a diesel specialist or a trusted mechanic for a pre-trip inspection. They’ll check everything, from the diesel filter to the radiator, and make sure you’re good to go. Better safe than sorry on a family trip in an old diesel, right?
