No problem at all! Let's recap everything clearly and categorized to make it easy to write down. Here's a complete list of all the measurements I recommended, grouped by when and why you'd take them:

### **I. Every Fitting are REQUIRED**
These are taken at *every single fitting* for *any garment*:
1.  **Bust:** Across the fullest part.
2.  **Natural Waist:** The narrowest point of the waist, not the belt line.
3.  **Hips:** At the fullest part, usually 18-24 inches below the waist.

### **II. Essential Measurements (Take once per garment type)**
These are usually taken *once* when you get the initial measurements, but you should check them at key fittings if the garment changes significantly:
4.  **Inseam (for pants):** From the inside of the crotch to the ankle bone. *Crucial* for pant length.
5.  **Sleeve Length (for tops/dresses):** From the shoulder point to the wrist bone.
6.  **Shoulder Width:** Across the shoulders, not including the neck. Vital for good collar/jacket fit.

### **III. Additional Measurements for Fitted Garments (Take once per garment type)**
These provide a much more tailored fit and are usually taken *once* when you get the initial measurements:
7.  **Neck (for collars):** Circumference around the neck.
8.  **Bicep (for sleeves/tops):** Circumference at the fullest part of the bicep.
9.  **S-G (Sleeve Gap) (for sleeves):** The vertical gap between the bicep and the underarm seam on a flat measurement.
10. **Back Rise (for pants):** The vertical measurement from the crotch to the natural waist. *Essential* for comfort in tall clients (like your neighbor!) to prevent pulling down.
11. **Thigh (for fitted pants/skirts):** Circumference around the thigh, usually at the fullest part.
12. **Rise (for pants):** Total vertical measurement from crotch to waistband (includes both front and back rise). Important for fit.

### **Key Reminders for Your Tall Client:**
*   **Back Rise is CRITICAL:** His 12-inch front rise needs to be longer (check the back rise!) to sit comfortably on his long torso.
*   **Inseam for Length:** Have him try the pants on *with his sneakers* to get the exact inseam length you need. His 37-inch inseam is likely too long for the desired ankle-length look – try them on with shoes to pin the hem.
*   **Ease for Comfort:** Add extra ease to the hip and thigh measurements to ensure he has room when sitting.
