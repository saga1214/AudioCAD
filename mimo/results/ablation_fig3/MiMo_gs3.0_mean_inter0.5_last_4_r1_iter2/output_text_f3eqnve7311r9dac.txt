That's a great point. Older comics are often much rarer and can be fantastic investments.

For example, the record six-million-dollar *Action Comics* number one I mentioned earlier is a 1938 comic.

Similarly, *Amazing Fantasy* number fifteen, from 1939, first showed Spider-Man.

*Detective Comics* number twenty-seven, from 1939, is where Batman made his first appearance.

Also, *Whiz Comics* number fifteen from 1950 is the first appearance of the Hulk.

Another one with rising value is *Tales to Astonish* number twenty-two, from 1961, which is the first appearance of the Fantastic Four.

Just remember, for these truly old books, the condition is everything. Because of their age, they are rarely found in pristine, graded condition. A copy in good shape can still be worth a lot of money.
