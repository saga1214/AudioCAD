You've hit on the core debate in sword-making! While all the steps you mentioned are crucial, **the heat treatment (hardening and tempering) remains the single most important step**. Here's why, even considering your excellent points about materials and design:

1.  **Fundamental Property Determination:** Heat treatment transforms the steel's internal structure (its microstructure). This change is what gives the blade its essential properties:
    *   **Hardness:** Needed to hold a sharp edge.
    *   **Toughness:** Needed to resist shattering on impact.
    *   **Flexibility:** Needed to bend without breaking (in many types of swords).
    *   **Grain Structure:** Affects wear resistance and fatigue life.

2.  **Irreversible & Non-Negotiable:** Once the blade is hardened and tempered, its core properties are largely fixed. You can't easily "fix" a blade that's too brittle or too soft through other steps. A flaw in this process means the sword is fundamentally flawed and unusable as a functional weapon (or even a convincing replica).

3.  **Builds on Everything Else:** All other steps *depend* on the blade being properly heat-treated:
    *   **Design & Geometry:** A perfectly balanced blade will still fail catastrophically if it shatters on the first hit because it was under-hardened.
    *   **Materials:** Even the best 1095 steel will be useless if not properly hardened. Conversely, a flawed heat treatment turns good steel into scrap.
    *   **CNC/Forging:** Both methods create the shape, but the heat treatment transforms that shape into a functional piece of metallurgy.

**Why the other steps, while critical, aren't *the single most* important:**

*   **Materials:** Essential as the foundation, but you can often find suitable substitutes or adjust design if the *exact* material isn't available. A *flawed* heat treatment, however, ruins the steel regardless of what you started with.
*   **Design & Intent:** Absolutely vital for functionality and historical accuracy. A poorly designed blade won't perform well, even if heat-treated. However, a *well*-heat-treated blade can still perform acceptably within its design constraints, even if the design isn't perfect. A *poorly* heat-treated blade fails regardless of design.
*   **CNC/Forging:** These are methods to *create* the shape. The choice between them is secondary to the metallurgical process. Both can produce blades that are properly heat-treated.

**Conclusion:**

While understanding design, selecting materials, and choosing the fabrication method are all critical components of making a *good* sword, the heat treatment is the step that fundamentally determines if the blade can *function* as a sword at all. It's the transformative step that gives the steel its life and purpose. Without perfect heat treatment, the sword is merely a dangerous lump of metal. With it, the other steps become the art of refining that potential into a functional and often beautiful object. So, while your passion for materials and design is spot-on, the heat treatment remains the irreplaceable cornerstone.
