Dealing with a difficult person can be incredibly draining, especially when they impact your daily life and family. Here are some thoughtful strategies to help manage the situation:

### 1. **Prioritize Your Emotional Well-being**
   - **Set Boundaries**: Limit exposure to the stressor when possible (e.g., avoid direct confrontation, reduce interaction).
   - **Self-Care**: Lean into activities that uplift you, like listening to Forrest Frank or planning Halloween fun. Your mental health comes first.

### 2. **Document Everything**
   - Keep a record of incidents (dates, times, details) if police or CPS involvement escalates. This can be crucial if formal action is needed later.

### 3. **Seek Community Support**
   - Talk to other affected families or neighbors. A unified approach (e.g., a polite, documented conversation) might show the neighbor her actions are harmful. Example:  
     > *"We’ve noticed children can’t ride bikes without calls to police. This is causing real stress. Can we discuss ways to resolve this?"*

### 4. **Involve Authorities (If Necessary)**
   - If CPS or police are involved, cooperate fully. If the behavior persists, consider formal complaints or mediation services through your local community or housing authority.

### 5. **Focus on What You Control**
   - Redirect energy toward positive outlets: school dress-up days, Halloween prep, and music. These create joy and resilience.

### 6. **Professional Guidance**
   - If stress feels overwhelming, consider talking to a counselor or joining a support group. You don’t have to navigate this alone.

### Remember:
> "You can’t control others, but you *can* control how much energy you give the situation. Protect your peace."
