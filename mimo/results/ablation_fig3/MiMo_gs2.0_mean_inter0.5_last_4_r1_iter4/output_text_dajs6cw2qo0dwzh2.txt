That's a very insightful question, and it gets to the heart of what drove the Age of Discovery. You're right that the search for a direct sea route to Asia was a powerful and independent force. However, the fall of Constantinople in 1453 was a major catalyst that dramatically accelerated the urgency and scale of that search.

It wasn't just about creating a new route; it was about doing so while the Ottoman Empire was actively threatening to monopolize the old ones. This created an immediate, high-stakes race for European powers. So while the push might have happened anyway, the specific timing and intensity were significantly shaped by that event.
