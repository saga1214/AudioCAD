For modern-day art museum security, the **first and most critical line of defense is controlling physical access at every entry point**, combining visible security personnel with screening procedures (e.g., bag checks) to **manage exactly who and what enters the building**.  

This proactive barrier prioritizes prevention over detection, addressing the root vulnerability highlighted in infamous heists like the 1990 Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum theft—where human error (guards tricked by disguised intruders) became the entry point. While layers like motion detectors, reinforced displays, and alarms are essential, they operate as **secondary or tertiary defenses** that respond *after* a breach. The first line must *prevent* the breach at its origin: the point of entry.  

In essence:  
**Physical access control is the foundation.**  
All other security systems (technical, surveillance, environmental) build *upon* this layer to mitigate threats that slip through.
