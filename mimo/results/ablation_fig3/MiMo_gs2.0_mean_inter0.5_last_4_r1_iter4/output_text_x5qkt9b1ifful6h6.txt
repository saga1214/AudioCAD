Based on your biology class exploration, **ultra-processed foods** would likely be considered the *one type of food* that's **worst for your health if eaten in excess** overall. Here's why:

### Key Reasons:
1. **Concentration of Harmful Additives**:  
   Ultra-processed foods are packed with additives like artificial sweeteners, preservatives, emulsifiers, and artificial colors/flavors—none of which have proven health benefits. These often disrupt gut health and trigger inflammation.

2. **Nutrient Deficiency**:  
   They're stripped of fiber, vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats. Instead, they're loaded with refined sugars, unhealthy fats (like trans fats), and excessive salt.

3. **Engineered to Overeat**:  
   The high levels of salt, sugar, and fat are designed to override your body's fullness signals, leading to overconsumption and obesity.

4. **Linked to Chronic Diseases**:  
   Excess ultra-processed food intake is strongly tied to:
   - Type 2 diabetes
   - Heart disease
   - Fatty liver disease
   - Obesity
   - Increased cancer risk

5. **Displacement of Nutritious Foods**:  
   They crowd out whole foods (fruits, vegetables, lean proteins) that actually support health.

### Why Not Other Candidates?
- **Sugar/Soda**: Extremely harmful, but ultra-processed foods *contain* excess sugar *and* other additives.
- **Trans Fats**: Terrible for heart health, but less prevalent now (many countries ban them).
- **Sodium**: Critical in ultra-processed foods, but not the sole issue.

### Bottom Line:
While individual components (like sugar or trans fats) are dangerous, **ultra-processed foods represent the modern dietary trap** because they aggregate multiple risks while delivering minimal nutritional value. A 2019 *BMJ* study found replacing just 10% of ultra-processed foods with whole foods could reduce mortality risk by up to 16%. For your class, this is likely the most comprehensive answer.
