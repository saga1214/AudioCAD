Here's the best way to attach Velcro securely and safely without damaging your jacket lining:

1.  **Choose the Right Adhesive:** **Spray adhesive** is the **best choice** for fabric-to-fabric bonding. Look for a general-purpose fabric spray adhesive (like 3M Super 77 or similar). Double-sided tape *can* work for small pieces, but spray adhesive offers a much stronger, more flexible bond.

2.  **Position & Mark:** Hold the Velcro strip where you want it. Use a pencil to lightly mark the exact spots where the sticky sides of the Velcro will go on the jacket.

3.  **Apply Spray Adhesive (Carefully!):**
    *   Hold the can upright, about 6-8 inches away from the jacket.
    *   Spray a **thin, even coat** of adhesive onto the *back* of the Velcro strip you're attaching. Don't spray heavily or from too close, or it can soak through the fabric.
    *   Immediately press the sticky side of the Velcro firmly onto the jacket at the marked spots. Hold it for a few seconds to ensure good contact.

4.  **Apply Spray Adhesive (To Jacket):**
    *   Spray a **thin, even coat** of adhesive onto the *exact spots* on the jacket where you want the Velcro to stick.
    *   Immediately press the *hook side* of the Velcro firmly onto the jacket. Hold it for a few seconds.

5.  **Peel Back & Align:** Slowly peel back the backing paper from the *hook side* of the Velcro (the rougher side). This allows you to see where the sticky sides are aligned. Press them together firmly and hold for at least 30 seconds.

6.  **Apply Spray Adhesive (To Remaining Velcro):**
    *   Spray a thin coat of adhesive onto the *remaining sticky side* of the Velcro strip (the loop side).
    *   Press the *loop side* firmly onto the jacket at the remaining marked spot. Hold for a few seconds.

7.  **Final Press & Cure:** Press the entire Velcro strip firmly against the jacket again, ensuring all edges are sealed. Hold for a full minute. **Crucially, allow the bond to cure for at least 24 hours before stressing it.** Rushing this is the most common cause of failure.

**Why this works & why it protects the lining:**

*   **Flexible Bond:** Spray adhesive creates a strong bond that moves *with* the fabric, minimizing stress points that could tear the lining.
*   **Hook Side Down:** By attaching the *hook* side to the *inside* of the jacket (facing the lining), the rough, potentially snagging part is hidden. The *loop* side is on the *outside*.
*   **Thin Coats:** Prevents the adhesive from soaking into the lining fabric or causing it to wrinkle.
*   **Curing Time:** Essential for the bond to fully set and become permanent.

**Alternative (Less Ideal but Works for Small Pieces):**

*   **Strong Double-Sided Tape:** Use high-strength, wide, fabric-backed double-sided tape (like Command Strips or similar). Follow the same positioning and pressing steps. Test strength carefully, as it might not be as durable as spray adhesive for frequent use or heavy items.

**Key Tip:** **Always test your adhesive choice and placement on a small, inconspicuous area of the jacket first** (like an inside seam) to ensure it doesn't damage the fabric or lining before committing to the main attachment.
