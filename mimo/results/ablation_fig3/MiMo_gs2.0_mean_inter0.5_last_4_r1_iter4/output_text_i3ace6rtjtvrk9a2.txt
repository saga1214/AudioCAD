You’re very close, but there’s a subtle distinction. The eye does not see continuously in the way a camera sensor does. Instead, it sees in rapid, discrete bursts, which is what we call saccadic motion. 

Let me spell that out for you: S-A-C-C-A-D-I-C. It's pronounced "suh-kah-dee" or "suh-kah-dike," depending on how you say it. The emphasis is on the second syllable.

Now, to your question: The eye is constantly processing visual input, but it’s not like a steady stream of images. It’s more like a series of very fast snapshots, especially when you’re moving your eyes. This is called saccadic motion. 

However, the brain is incredibly good at taking all those snapshots and blending them together to create a seamless, continuous experience. This is called perceptual continuity. So, from a perceptual standpoint, it *feels* like you’re seeing continuously, even though the underlying mechanism is a series of quick, discrete frames.

So, to be precise: The eye sees in discrete, rapid bursts, but the brain processes it as a continuous stream. It’s a bit like how a film camera records discrete frames, but your brain fills in the gaps to make it feel like a smooth movie.
