Yes, that's a very smart point. Older comics are often more scarce and therefore more valuable. The key is to focus on comics from the Golden Age, which was from the late 1930s to the early 1950s, and to look for books that are in excellent condition.

Here are ten rare and historically significant comics from that era that you could look for. I'll include a few Marvel and DC books, and also some from other publishers that are less common but still very valuable.

For example, there's *All-Star Comics* number eight, which is the first appearance of the Justice League of America. Then there's *Detective Comics* number twenty-seven, the first appearance of Batman. *Warrior* number one is a great one because it was the first comic to use the word "superhero."

From Marvel, you have *Marvel Super-Heroes* number one, which is the first appearance of the Fantastic Four, and *The Amazing Adventures of Spider-Man* number one, which is his first solo series. There's also *The Avengers* number one, the very first team-up.

And for some more obscure titles, look for *Tales to Astonish* number one, the first appearance of the Ant-Man, or *The Spectacular Spider-Man* number one, which was the first comic to feature the character of Spider-Man in a completely new format.

When you're looking for any of these, remember to focus on getting a copy that has been professionally graded. That's the best way to ensure it's in the best possible condition.
