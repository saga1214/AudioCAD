Okay, let's break this down. For your contact info, putting it directly below your name is better than to the side. Order it like this: Name in 14-point font, then on the next line, Email, then Phone, then City. Using icons is fine, but nine-point font might be a bit small. Removing them entirely is often safest for automated tracking systems.

For your work experience, creating two sections is the best approach. First, a "Relevant Experience" section where you list your older, most relevant jobs in full, including dates and bullet points. This answers the recruiter's unspoken question of "Why are you qualified?" Then, an optional "Additional Work History" section where you list your more recent jobs with just the title, company, and dates. This shows your full timeline without cluttering the key section.

Regarding the 15-year gap, a strong professional summary at the very top is your best tool. It's a one or two-sentence punch that immediately connects your valuable past experience to your new career goals, explaining the gap.

For education, definitely list your degree in progress with the expected graduation month and year. For multiple degrees from the same school, just list the school once and then each degree you earned there, one after the other.

For skills, a dedicated skills section is important, but keep it concise. List only the hard and soft skills that are explicitly mentioned in the job descriptions you're targeting. Tailor this list for each application.

Finally, awards and honors belong in the education section. List them right after the degree where you earned them, for example, "Highest Honors" or the name of a scholarship you received.

So, the basic structure would be: Professional Summary, Contact Info, Relevant Experience, Education, Skills, and then Additional Work History if needed. This structure is professional, easy to scan, and strategically highlights your most valuable assets.
