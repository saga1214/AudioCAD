Here's a concise summary of the advice for your fussy feline family and financial situation:

**For Feeding & Schedule:**

1.  **Control Access:** Use **microchip-activated feeders** or **automated timed feeders**. This ensures each cat gets *only* their portion at scheduled times, preventing the greedy ones from hoarding and ensuring the fussy ones get fed.
2.  **Feed Separately:** Put the two fussy cats in a separate room to feed them *before* you leave home.

**For Managing on a Tight Budget:**

1.  **Seek Free/Low-Cost Help:** Actively look for **local pet food banks, shelters, or charities** offering free or discounted cat food.
2.  **Buy Smart:**
    *   Purchase **higher-quality food in bulk** (check unit price).
    *   **Mix quality food with safe fillers** like plain cooked rice or oats to stretch portions.
3.  **Community Resources:** Check **online platforms or local community groups** for pet supply donations.
4.  **Prioritize Essential Vet Care:** Focus on securing **low-cost spay/neuter and core vaccinations**. Explain your situation to clinics for potential payment plans or sliding scales.

**For Home Health Care (No Money):**

1.  **Prevent Illness:** Maintain **strict hygiene** (clean litter boxes, food/water bowls daily).
2.  **Basic Nutrition:** Provide **fresh water daily**. Supplement infrequent, safe table scraps (plain cooked chicken/fish, no onions/garlic/salt) if possible.
3.  **Weight Management:** **Monitor weight closely** to prevent obesity (a major cause of expensive health issues). Encourage play with homemade toys (string, crumpled paper).
4.  **Be Vigilant:** **Regularly check** your cats for any signs of illness (lethargy, changes in appetite/bowels, lumps, breathing issues). Catching problems early, even at home, can prevent costly vet bills later.

**Key Takeaway:** Use technology (chip/timed feeders), community resources, smart purchasing, and proactive home care to ensure your cats are fed appropriately and kept healthy despite financial challenges.
