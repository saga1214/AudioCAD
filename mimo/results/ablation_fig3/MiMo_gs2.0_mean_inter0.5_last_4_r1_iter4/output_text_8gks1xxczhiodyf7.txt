If I had to choose **one top driver** behind rising obesity rates, it would be **the easy access and overconsumption of highly processed, calorie-dense foods**. Here’s why:  

### Key Reasons:  
1. **Nutritional Deficiency**:  
   - Processed foods are often stripped of fiber, vitamins, and minerals, leaving them low in satiety. This leads to overeating because they don’t trigger fullness signals as effectively as whole foods.  
   - Example: A soda or fast-food meal delivers excess sugar/fat without nutritional value, making it easy to consume excess calories.  

2. **Affordability and Accessibility**:  
   - These foods are cheaper per calorie, especially in food deserts or low-income areas. They’re also engineered to be hyper-palatable (addictive), encouraging mindless consumption.  

3. **Systemic Factors**:  
   - Heavy marketing and ubiquitous availability (e.g., convenience stores, ads) make unhealthy choices the default, not the exception.  
   - They displace nutrient-rich whole foods (e.g., fresh produce, lean proteins) in daily diets.  

### Why Not Other Factors?  
- **Physical Inactivity**: While crucial, it’s often a *symptom* (e.g., desk jobs) or *consequence* (e.g., fatigue from overeating) rather than the root cause.  
- **Stress/Sleep**: These influence *hormonal regulation* (e.g., cortisol, ghrelin) but don’t directly drive overconsumption like processed foods do.  
- **Genetics**: May predispose individuals but doesn’t explain population-wide trends.  

### The Bigger Picture:  
Processed foods create a **caloric surplus trap**. Even with moderate activity, the sheer density of calories in these foods makes it easy to exceed daily needs. Pair this with modern sedentary lifestyles, and obesity becomes a systemic issue.  

**In short: Processed foods lower the barrier to overconsumption, making it the most pervasive and scalable driver.** Addressing this through policy (e.g., sugar taxes), education, and food industry reform is critical for progress.
