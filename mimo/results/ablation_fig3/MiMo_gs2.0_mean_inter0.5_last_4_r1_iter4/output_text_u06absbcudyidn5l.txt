Okay, let's break this down. Vasculitis is basically when your immune system mistakenly attacks your blood vessels. This inflammation can happen in small, medium, or large vessels, and depending on the type, it can cause a whole range of symptoms, from fatigue and rashes to more serious issues like organ damage.

As for the other potential causes of chronic redness, there are quite a few. Sun damage is a big one, it can break down collagen and lead to redness over time. Rosacea is another common one that causes persistent redness, especially on the face. Genetics can also play a part, sometimes running in families. There are also contact dermatitis, which is a reaction to something you're touching, and even fungal infections can cause a red, itchy rash.

So yes, the size of the blood vessels involved does matter. Small vessel inflammation usually affects the skin and can cause rashes or ulcers. Medium vessel issues are more likely to affect organs like the kidneys or brain. And large vessel vasculitis can cause issues in major organs like the heart or lungs. It's a complex condition, and a specialist can help determine what might be going on with you.
