Here's a list of breeds/hybrids known for being calm, easygoing, and family-friendly, while meeting your size/noise criteria and fitting well with kids/dogs and *not* chasing squirrels/cats:

### **Purebreds (or Hybrids with Known Lineage)**  
1. **Poodle (Miniature or Toy)**  
   - **Why:** Highly trainable, hypoallergenic, low-shedding, great with kids/dogs. Minimal barking when alone.  
   - **Hybrid Option:** Poodle mixes (e.g., "Poodle Pup" rescues) retain these traits.  

2. **Bichon Frise**  
   - **Why:** Cheerful, hypoallergenic, adaptable to apartments. Minimal barking, gentle with everyone.  

3. **Cavalier King Charles Spaniel**  
   - **Why:** Affectionate, low-energy, great with kids/dogs. Minimal prey drive. *Note:* Prone to separation anxiety—avoid if leaving for >6 hours.  

4. **Miniature Schnauzer**  
   - **Why:** Alert but not yappy, loyal, good with kids. Low shedding, trainable.  

5. **French Bulldog**  
   - **Why:** Low-energy, cuddly, minimal barking when alone. Avoid if you have cats (some chase them).  

6. **Labrador Retriever (Small/Lab Mix)**  
   - **Why:** Friendly, trainable, great with kids/dogs. *Caution:* Labs *can* chase squirrels—opt for adult Labs or mixes with smaller breeds.  

7. **Golden Retriever (Small/Golden Mix)**  
   - **Why:** Patient, sociable, great with all ages/animals. *Caution:* Same squirrel-chasing tendency—adults or mixed breeds are better.  

8. **Border Collie (Small/Border Collie Mix)**  
   - **Why:** Highly trainable, intelligent. *Caution:* Needs *daily* mental/physical exercise—adults or senior mixes are calmer.  

---

### **Hybrid/Rescue Recommendations**  
- **Poodle Mixes:** Often retain trainability, low-shedding traits.  
- **Border Collie/Small Breed Mixes:** Great trainability, but *only* adult dogs (high energy in puppies).  
- **Cavalier/Schnauzer Mixes:** Calm, adaptable, low-shedding.  
- **French Bulldog/Chihuahua Mixes:** Low-energy, affectionate, apartment-friendly.  

---

### **Key Steps to Find Candidates**  
1. **Research Rescue Groups:**  
   - Focus on shelters/rescues specializing in **small adult dogs** (e.g., "Poodle Rescue," "Cavalier Rescue," "Senior Dog Rescues").  
   - Use sites like [Petfinder](https://www.petfinder.com/) or [Adopt-a-Pet](https://www.adoptapet.com/) and filter by "small," "adult," and "low-shedding."  

2. **Ask Rescues Directly:**  
   - "Do you have adult dogs under 25 lbs, calm, no prey drive, and proven good with kids/dogs?"  
   - Request videos or meet-and-greets to assess temperament.  

3. **Breed-Specific Puppies (if spouse insists):**  
   - Adopt from breeders who **screen temperament** (avoid "fluff mills").  
   - Prioritize **puppies from adult parents** (easier to gauge personality).  

4. **Trial & Observe:**  
   - Bring home a dog bed, toys, and treats for a short visit to see how they handle your home/work routine.  
   - Ask foster families about behavior when alone.  

5. **Training & Adjustment:**  
   - Start with **basic obedience** (e.g., "leave it" for squirrels).  
   - Use a **calming mat** or puzzle toys for alone time.  

---

### **Final Tips**  
- **Avoid High-Energy Breeds:** Like young Poodles, Bichons, or Chihuahuas—unless you can provide daily walks/play.  
- **Shedding:** If Mainecoon shedding bothers you, prioritize hypoallergenic breeds (Poodle, Bichon, Schnauzer, Maltese).  
- **Cat Safety:** Avoid terrier mixes or high-strung breeds if cats are present.  

Start with **adult small breeds from shelters/rescues**—they’re often calmer, already trained, and fit all your criteria! 🐾
