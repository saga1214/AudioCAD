That's a profound question. While the potential for new medicines is incredibly valuable, the most fundamental aspect of preserving hotspots is ensuring the long-term health and stability of the planet's ecosystems.

This is because healthy ecosystems provide the essential services that support all life, including us. Things like clean air and water, stable climate regulation, and the pollination of our food crops are all critical. Losing biodiversity means losing the resilience of these systems, which could have catastrophic consequences for humanity. So, ultimately, protecting hotspots is really about protecting the very foundation of our own survival.
