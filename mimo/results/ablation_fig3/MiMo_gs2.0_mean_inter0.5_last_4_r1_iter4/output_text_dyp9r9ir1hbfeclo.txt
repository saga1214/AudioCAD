You're right to ask for clarification - I initially mentioned three bones but actually the shoulder involves **four primary components** working together for rotation:

1.  **Clavicle (Collarbone):** Stabilizes the scapula and facilitates arm movement.
2.  **Scapula (Shoulder Blade):** Houses the glenoid socket and stabilizes the shoulder joint.
3.  **Humerus (Upper Arm Bone):** The head rotates within the scapula's socket.
4.  **Rotator Cuff Muscles (Tendons):** While tendons are part of the fourth component, the *rotator cuff muscles themselves* (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) are the fourth key structural group enabling controlled rotation.

**Their combined function for rotation:**

*   The **clavicle** and **scapula** form the shoulder girdle, positioning the arm.
*   The **humerus** head moves within the shallow **glenoid socket** on the scapula.
*   The **rotator cuff muscles** dynamically stabilize the humeral head within this socket and actively control the rotation range of motion (both internal and external rotation).

**Regarding your friend's soreness:**

Yes, soreness during or after exercises like lateral raises can definitely be related to the rotator cuff, especially if proper form isn't used or the exercise is too heavy. Common causes include:

1.  **Rotator Cuff Strain/Irritation:** Overuse or improper loading can inflame these small muscles/tendons.
2.  **Muscle Fatigue:** The deltoids and rotator cuff work hard during lateral raises.
3.  **Poor Form:** Leaning back excessively or using too much weight puts extra stress on the shoulder joint and cuff.
4.  **Weakness:** Underdeveloped rotator cuff muscles can't handle the load effectively.

**Key Takeaway:** While normal muscle soreness (DOMS) is expected, **sharp pain, pain at the shoulder joint itself, or pain that limits movement** warrants caution. Your friend should ensure proper form, start with lighter weights, and if the soreness is persistent or painful, they should consult a healthcare professional (doctor or physical therapist) to rule out an injury.
