That's a great question—and you're touching on a nuance in ice physics! At **-10°C**, the coefficient of friction between skate blades and ice can indeed be **lower than at warmer rink temperatures**, but it's not as extreme as you might expect. Here's the breakdown:

### Expected Coefficient at -10°C:
- **Typical range**: **~0.005 to 0.01**  
  *(Compared to ~0.01–0.02 at -5°C)*  
- **Why?**  
  - **Ice hardness increases**: At very low temperatures, ice becomes harder and less deformable. This reduces the microscopic "welding" effect where pressure melts a thin layer of ice (the *quasi-liquid layer*), which is crucial for smooth gliding.  
  - **Reduced water layer**: The pressure-induced melting that creates a lubricating film diminishes, increasing friction slightly.  
  - **Surface texture matters**: If the ice is artificially frozen (like in rinks), it may still be smooth. Natural ice at -10°C often has more imperfections, increasing friction further.

### Key Factors Influencing Friction:
1. **Pressure**:  
   - Skaters apply ~500–1,000 kg/cm² at the blade tip. Higher pressure *melts more ice*, temporarily reducing friction—but only if the temperature allows.  
2. **Blade sharpness**:  
   - Sharper edges concentrate pressure, enhancing the melting effect and reducing friction.  
3. **Temperature extremes**:  
   - Below **-20°C**, friction rises sharply as the ice becomes too rigid for pressure melting.  
   - Above **-5°C**, friction increases due to a thicker quasi-liquid layer (less gliding efficiency).

### Comparison to Natural Ice:
- **Outdoor lake ice** at -10°C:  
  - Friction coefficient: **~0.03–0.10** (due to irregular surface, air pockets, and variable thickness).  
  - Gliding is much harder; blades "dig in" rather than glide.

### Practical Implication:
- **Competition rinks** prefer temperatures near **-5°C**—cold enough for smooth gliding but warm enough for pressure melting to work optimally.  
- At **-10°C**, while friction is lower than at -2°C, it’s not as low as at -5°C. Skaters might need to adjust their technique for sharper turns.

In short: **-10°C reduces friction vs. warmer temperatures, but the effect is modest.** The real magic happens near **-5°C**, where pressure melting is maximized for effortless gliding. 🧊⛸️
