That's a great approach. Here's how you could structure your email, focusing on respect while also being direct.

First, start with a clear subject line, something like: "Meeting to Discuss Recent Exam Feedback."

In the email, you can start with a polite greeting and a brief mention of your purpose. For example: "Hi Professor [and his last name], I hope you're well. I scored a 64 on the recent exam and was hoping to discuss your feedback to help me improve on essays for future tests."

Then, you can add a sentence that acknowledges the subjective nature of grading, but frames it as a desire to understand. You could say: "I understand that grading for an essay can be subjective, and I'd really appreciate understanding what factors led to my score, as I'm eager to do better."

Finally, propose a next step. You could write: "Would you have 10 to 15 minutes available during your office hours sometime this week? Alternatively, if another time is more convenient, I'm happy to work around your schedule."

This way, you're professional, you express a genuine desire to learn, and you give him a clear, easy way to respond.
