Let's clarify this distinction using precise terminology:

**1. Eye as a Biological Sensor:**
*   The eye detects visual stimuli via photoreceptors (rods for low light/dark motion, cones for color/contrast).
*   It responds to rapid changes in light and motion patterns up to the **flicker fusion threshold** (typically **60-75 Hz** for many observers in bright light, but potentially higher in darkness).
*   **Crucially, the eye does NOT see a continuous stream of high-resolution images.** It relies on sampling visual information at discrete points (focal fixation) and during rapid saccades (jumps between points).

**2. Brain as a Processing & Perception Unit:**
*   The optic nerve transmits signals from the eye to the visual cortex.
*   The brain performs several critical functions:
    *   **Saccadic Masking:** During saccades (rapid eye movements), the brain actively **suppresses the visual input** experienced during the brief period of blur caused by the eye's movement. This creates a stable perceptual field.
    *   **Temporal Integration:** The brain integrates visual signals arriving over time to create the *illusion* of continuous motion and scene stability. It fills in gaps and smooths out discontinuities.
    *   **Perception:** Conscious visual experience is the *result* of this processed information, not the raw data from the eye.

**Pronunciation:** Saccade is pronounced **suh-KAH-dee**.

**Answering Your Core Question:**

*   **Does the eye see continuously?** **No, not literally.** The eye itself senses visual information in discrete bursts – during fixation at a point and during saccades where masking occurs. It does not generate a continuous, high-resolution video signal.
*   **Does the brain perceive continuously?** **Yes, in a perceptual sense.** The brain's processing, especially saccadic masking and temporal integration, actively constructs the *experience* of continuous visual perception. It masks the eye's sampling behavior and the resulting blur, creating a smooth, stable, and continuous visual field.

**Connection to Frames Per Second (FPS):**

*   The **60-75 Hz flicker fusion threshold** explains why motion above this rate feels smooth and lifelike to most people. If a display shows flashes faster than this threshold, the brain integrates them into a continuous perception, much like it integrates the eye's own sampling during saccades.
*   **Saccadic masking is analogous to a high frame rate:** It demonstrates that the brain can "average out" or "smooth" rapidly changing visual information (like the eye's own sampling) to create the perception of continuity. A high frame rate provides more data points for the brain to integrate seamlessly.

**In Summary:**

*   **Eye:** Acts as a biological sensor sampling visual information rapidly but discontinuously (during fixation and saccades).
*   **Brain:** Actively processes this sampling, masks motion blur (saccadic masking), integrates information over time, and constructs the *perception* of continuous visual reality.
*   **Result:** We *perceive* continuous motion and a stable visual field, even though the eye's underlying sampling is not inherently continuous. The brain's processing, including saccadic masking, is essential for this continuous perceptual experience.
