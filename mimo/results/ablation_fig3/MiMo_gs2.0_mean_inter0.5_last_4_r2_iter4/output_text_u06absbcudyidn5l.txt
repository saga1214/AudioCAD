Okay, let's break down chronic skin redness and vasculitis.

There are many potential causes for lifelong skin redness. Rosacea is a very common one, and genetics is often a major factor. Long-term sun damage is another big cause, as UV rays can make the skin more sensitive and reactive. Environmental triggers, like certain foods or drinks, can also cause it.

Now, about vasculitis. It's a condition where your body's immune system mistakenly attacks its own blood vessels. This inflammation can affect different sizes of vessels. Large vessels might be affected in conditions like giant cell arteritis, which can cause headaches and jaw pain. Medium-sized vessels are affected in conditions like polyarteritis nodosa, which can impact organs like the kidneys. And it's the small and medium vessels that are most often affected in something like Henoch-Schönlein purpura, which can cause rashes on the skin, in the gut, and kidneys.

The symptoms really depend on which vessels are involved and where. They can range from a persistent facial rash, fever, and fatigue to more serious issues like organ damage. A proper diagnosis usually involves a physical exam, blood tests, and often a biopsy to confirm it.

As for hair and nails, poor circulation, nutritional deficiencies like iron and biotin, or systemic inflammation can all contribute to weakness or slow growth. Supplements can help support health, but again, a doctor can guide you safely. For inflammation, ibuprofen provides temporary relief but isn't a long-term solution for an underlying condition, especially without knowing the cause. A doctor can determine the best approach for you.
