Okay, since Northern California cold is making you want to swim and escape, Big Sur is perfect! It has warm weather potential, swimming, and is just spectacular.

Here’s a detailed itinerary for four to five days, which should fit right after your Alcatraz celebration.

For Day One, you'll arrive and settle in. I'd suggest staying in Carmel-by-the-Sea. It's charming and central for your first day's exploring. A place like the Carmel River Inn is a great, reliable choice.

In the afternoon, you could start with a short walk on the beach at Carmel Beach, and then explore downtown Carmel, checking out the shops and art galleries.

For dinner, you could go to Nepenthe in Carmel River. It's a bit pricey, but it has stunning ocean views, and they can handle gluten-free needs really well. It feels very special.

For travel, it's best to rent a large SUV or a minivan, which should only be about 200 to 300 dollars a week. It's a must for the roads in Big Sur.

On Day Two, you can drive along the iconic Big Sur coast. You'll see some amazing stops like the Bixby Bridge, McWay Falls—which is where that famous waterfall picture comes from—and Pfeiffer Beach with its purple sand. You could also visit the Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park to swim and hike.

For lunch, you could grab a quick bite from the Big Sur Bakery. For dinner, a great spot is the cliffside restaurant, The Post. They have an ocean-view terrace, and they are very good about gluten-free dining. Just be sure to make a reservation.

Day Three, you could visit Pigeon Point Lighthouse and then drive up to the charming town of Santa Barbara. You could rent a car for the day if you haven't already, but you'll need one to explore the area.

In Santa Barbara, you can walk along Stearns Wharf, which has great shops and restaurants. For dinner, The Black Sheep Bistro is a fun, trendy spot with a creative menu that includes lots of gluten-free options.

Day Four, you could head to Malibu. You can drive along the Pacific Coast Highway to see the famous Surfrider Beach and the Getty Villa. For dinner, Nobu Malibu is a fantastic choice for a high-end experience with amazing sushi and gluten-free options.

On your last day, Day Five, you could visit the Santa Ynez Valley for some wine tasting. You can find wineries with dedicated gluten-free dining areas. For your final dinner, you could return to Carmel for a classic seafood experience at the Old Fisherman's Grotto.

Just a couple of final tips: definitely pack reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses. And for her birthday cruise, just remember to call Fog Harbor Fish House ahead of time to confirm they can handle her Celiac needs. I hope you have an incredible trip!
