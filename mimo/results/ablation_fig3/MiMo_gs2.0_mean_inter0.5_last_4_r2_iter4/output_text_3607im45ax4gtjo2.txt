Dealing with a difficult person like this can feel overwhelming, but here are some practical strategies to help manage the stress and protect your family:

### 1. **Document Everything**
   - **Record incidents**: Keep a detailed log (date, time, reason for police/CPS call, witnesses, etc.). This creates evidence if legal action becomes necessary.
   - **Save communications**: Keep texts, voicemails, or emails from the neighbor.

### 2. **Set Clear Boundaries**
   - **Public behavior**: If safe, calmly assert boundaries in public (e.g., "Our children are playing in their own yard. Please respect that."). Avoid escalating.
   - **Private space**: Keep interactions minimal. If approached, stay calm and disengage.

### 3. **Involve Authorities (If Needed)**
   - **Police**: If calls are baseless, ask officers to document the false reports and note any impact on your family.
   - **CPS**: If involved, cooperate fully. Request case numbers and follow up regularly. If you suspect CPS is acting in good faith, support them with documentation.

### 4. **Seek Community Support**
   - **Neighbors**: If others share your concern, form a unified front. Consider contacting a neighborhood association or local council.
   - **Local groups**: Organizations like **Neighborhood Watch** or **Community Safety Coalitions** can help address recurring issues.

### 5. **Prioritize Your Kids’ Wellbeing**
   - **Reassure them**: Explain that the situation isn’t their fault. Reinforce safety rules (e.g., "Stay near the house if you feel uneasy").
   - **Monitor their stress**: If calls persist, temporarily restrict outdoor play until the situation improves.

### 6. **Legal Options**
   - **Trespassing orders**: If the neighbor harasses you on your property, consult a lawyer about a restraining order.
   - **Civil action**: False reports to CPS/police can have legal consequences. A lawyer can advise on this.

### 7. **Protect Your Mental Health**
   - **Vent supportively**: Talk to trusted friends, family, or a therapist to avoid bottling up stress.
   - **Limit exposure**: Avoid social media debates with the neighbor; it rarely helps.

### 8. **Contact Professionals**
   - **CPS advocate**: Request an advocate if involved.
   - **Social worker**: Local agencies can mediate or offer resources.
   - **Therapist**: Especially for kids if trauma arises.

### 9. **Consider Relocation**
   - If the situation escalates despite efforts, moving might be the healthiest long-term option.
