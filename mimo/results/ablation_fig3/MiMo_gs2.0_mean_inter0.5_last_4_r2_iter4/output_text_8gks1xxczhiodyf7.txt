Based on the evidence, **easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods remains one of the biggest top drivers behind increasing obesity rates**—even after exploring other factors like activity levels and food types. Here’s why:

### Key Reasoning:
1. **Caloric Efficiency vs. Fullness**:  
   Processed foods are engineered to be hyper-palatable (easy to overeat) while being low in protein, fiber, and water. This leads to higher calorie intake without satiety, making it easier to consume excess calories without realizing it.

2. **Accessibility and Affordability**:  
   These foods are cheaper, shelf-stable, and heavily marketed—especially in low-income areas. This creates a systemic barrier to healthier choices, regardless of individual effort.

3. **The "Processed vs. Calorie-Dense" Distinction**:  
   While calorie-dense *unprocessed* foods (e.g., nuts, avocados) can contribute to weight gain if overeaten, they lack the *additive* factors of processed foods:  
   - Added sugars, fats, and salt that disrupt metabolism.  
   - Lack of nutrients, leading to overeating to meet basic needs.  
   - Marketing and packaging designed to encourage overconsumption.

4. **Activity Can’t Fully Offset Poor Diet**:  
   Even with exercise, a diet high in processed foods can lead to long-term health issues (e.g., insulin resistance). Portion control helps, but the *quality* of food impacts metabolic health beyond just calories.

### Other Factors (Supporting Roles):
- **Physical Inactivity**: A major contributor, but often *linked* to sedentary lifestyles tied to processed food consumption.  
- **Stress/Sleep**: Can dysregulate appetite hormones, but processed foods exacerbate this via blood sugar spikes.  
- **Genetics/Hormones**: Influence individual susceptibility, but diet and environment are the strongest modifiable drivers.

### Bottom Line:
While **total calorie balance** is fundamental to weight gain, the *systematic increase in processed food availability* is the most significant *social and environmental driver* of rising obesity rates globally. It creates a perfect storm: cheap, addictive, and nutritionally empty calories that overwhelm individual behavior.
