Let’s pronounce it first. Saccade is said like “sah-kah-dee” or “suh-kah-dee,” with the stress on the second syllable.

Now, to your main question: with saccadic masking in mind, would it be correct to say the eye sees continuously?

It really depends on how you define “sees.”

If “sees” means the raw, unfiltered sensory input from the retina: then yes, in a way, the eye perceives visual changes very rapidly up to that flicker fusion threshold we just discussed. Rods and cones react to changes in light patterns quickly.

But if “sees” means what you become consciously aware of after the brain’s smoothing and stabilization: then no, the eye does not deliver continuous perception. Saccadic masking and other higher-level visual processing edit the raw data sent from the eyes. So what you perceive is fluid and continuous, even though your eyes might be jumping around.

So a more precise way to put it is: the eye senses rapidly changing visual stimuli, while the brain processes and presents that information in a continuous, stable way. 

Saccadic masking is the brain’s way of minimizing blur during rapid eye or head movements. Without it, what you consciously saw during a quick jump of your eye would feel much more jumpy or stuttery. The brain ensures motion looks smooth.

Does that distinction make sense? Eye senses, brain interprets at a higher level.
