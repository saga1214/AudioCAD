That's a great way to approach it. You want to be professional and clear.

Here are a few pointers for your email.

First, start with a clear subject line, like 'Meeting Request to Improve for Future Exams'.
Then, in the body, state your purpose clearly and positively. You could write: "I scored a 64 on the recent essay-style exam and wanted to schedule a brief meeting to understand how I could have improved and done better on the next test."

Second, keep the focus on your future success. Avoid phrases that question the past grade, like "I don't understand how you could give me this score."

Third, make it easy for him to agree to the meeting. You could add, "Office hours seem to conflict with my schedule. Would another time be convenient for you?"

Fourth, keep it brief and to the point. Professors are often very busy, so respect his time.

This approach frames the conversation as a constructive step you're taking to be a better student, which should bypass his ego and make him more receptive. Good luck!
