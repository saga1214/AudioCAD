Here’s your final, segment-specific packing list to keep you prepared without overpacking:

### **For the Day/Night (20 miles): Minimal & Modular**
1. **Moisture-wicking base layer** (top + bottom)  
2. **Light fleece or puffy vest** (mid-layer for warmth)  
3. **Wind/water-resistant jacket** (outer layer)  
4. **Gloves** (for grip/hills)  
5. **Warm hat + buff/gaiter** (head coverage)  
6. **Trail shoes with grip**  
7. **Compact handheld light/headlamp** (with **extra batteries**)  
8. flip-flops or lightweight shoes (to swap at aid stations)  
9. *Optional*: Small pack to carry layers as body temp changes.  

### **For the Final 12 Miles (Overnight): Warmth & Redundancy**
1. **Moisture-wicking base layer**  
2. **Light fleece jacket** (mid-layer)  
3. **Water-resistant insulated jacket** (e.g., 50-70°F range)  
4. **Windproof/waterproof shell jacket** (if rain is possible)  
5. Gloves + **extra-fuzzy hat** or beanie  
6. **Buff/gaiter** (for neck/wrists)  
7. Headlamp/headlamp + **spare batteries or backup light**  
8. Trail shoes with grip  
9. **Small running vest** (to stash/remove layers easily)  
10. Flip-flops (to protect feet from wet grass)  

### **Universal Extras (Both Segments)**  
- **Trekking poles** (optional, but great for hills)  
- **Light rain poncho** (just in case)  
- **Small towel** (quick dry-off)  
- **Zip ties** (to secure loose gear)  

### **Why This Works**  
- Aid stations cover nutrition, so focus **only** on clothing.  
- Mid-30s temps drop significantly at night—prioritize insulation for the final miles.  
- Wind/water resistance keeps you dry without bulk.  
- Layers let you adapt instantly (e.g., remove base layer when warming up).
