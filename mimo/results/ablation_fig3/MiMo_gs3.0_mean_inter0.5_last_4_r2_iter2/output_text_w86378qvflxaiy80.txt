After looking at all the different reasons—the unique species, the potential for future medicine, and especially the critical role in providing "ecosystem services"—I would say the single most important aspect is safeguarding the life-support systems for vulnerable human populations.

Protecting hotspots means ensuring the communities who depend on them for their most basic needs, like water, food, and safety, can continue to thrive. In that sense, it's a fundamental act of protecting human well-being on a global scale.
