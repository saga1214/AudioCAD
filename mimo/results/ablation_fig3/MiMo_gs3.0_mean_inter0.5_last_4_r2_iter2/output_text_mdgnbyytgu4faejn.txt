Of course, I can give you a clear breakdown of a standard professional resume format. Just think of it as a recipe.

First, at the very top, you have your name in about a 14-point font. To its right—though I still wouldn't use icons because of automated systems—you list your email, phone number with the city, expected graduation date if you're a student, and any other relevant contact info in nine or ten-point fonts.

Right below your name, you'll have a short professional summary of about two to four sentences. This is where you highlight your most relevant experience and goals to frame your entire resume.

Next is your experience section. As we discussed, you can group similar roles here. For each employer, list the company name, your title, and the specific years you worked there. If there are bullet points, keep them very short, focusing on key achievements.

After that comes your education section. Here you'll list your degree, the school, graduation year, and academic honors right where you earned them, like Highest Honards.

Then, you'll have a dedicated skills section. This is very important. List your hard skills, like software proficiency or technical abilities, and your soft skills, like communication, in a clean, easy-to-scan list.

You can also add optional sections like Certifications, Awards (listed separately from education awards), Languages, or Volunteer Work, depending on the job you're applying for.

Finally, at the very bottom, you can include a line for your LinkedIn profile or a link to your personal website or portfolio.

This structure should give you a clear, professional, and effective resume that covers all the key parts without being overwhelming.
