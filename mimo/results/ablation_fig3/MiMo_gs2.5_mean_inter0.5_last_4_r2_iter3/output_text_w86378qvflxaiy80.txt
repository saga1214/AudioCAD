After considering all the factors—the unique species, the untapped scientific potential, and especially the vital ecosystem services—it really comes down to a combination of things.

But if I had to rank them, I'd say the most critical aspect is preventing irreplaceable extinctions. Each hotspot is holding onto plant species found nowhere else on Earth. Losing even one of those would be a permanent loss to the entire planet's genetic library.

However, the second-highest priority is absolutely safeguarding those ecosystem services. As Conservation International points out, these services are fundamental for vulnerable human populations. Protecting a hotspot is, in effect, protecting their water, their food, and their safety.

Ultimately, the best approach is to see them as interconnected. Protecting the unique species also protects the ecosystem that provides essential services. It's a holistic way to do conservation that benefits both wildlife and people.
