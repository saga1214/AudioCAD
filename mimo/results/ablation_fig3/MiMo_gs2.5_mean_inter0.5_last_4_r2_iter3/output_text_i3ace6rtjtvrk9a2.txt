That's an excellent question—and you're right to separate the roles. Let's clarify using precise terminology:

### 1. **Eye as a Sensor (Detection)**
   - The eye detects visual stimuli via photoreceptors (rods for low light/dark motion; cones for color/detail).
   - It responds to rapid changes in light/motion, with a **flicker fusion threshold** of ~60–75 Hz for most people. Beyond this, discrete flashes "fuse" into perceived continuity.
   - During **saccades** (rapid eye jumps between focal points), the eye gathers visual data at irregular intervals. This creates "jumps" in input.

### 2. **Brain as a Processor (Perception)**
   - The visual cortex interprets signals from the eye, smoothing discontinuities and suppressing blur during saccades (**saccadic masking**).
   - It constructs a **perceptual stream** of continuous motion by:
     - Filling gaps between saccadic jumps.
     - Prioritizing stable visual elements over transient blur.
   - This is why you perceive motion as fluid, even though the eye's input is intermittent.

### 3. **Saccadic Masking's Role**
   - Saccadic masking actively *edits* eye input: during rapid eye movement between points, the brain suppresses blur to prevent dizziness or "jitter" perception.
   - **Key implication**: The eye *senses* rapid details (e.g., >60 Hz flicker), but the brain *processes* them into a continuous experience. The two are not the same.

### Scientific Terminology Recap:
- **Saccade**: Rapid, ballistic eye movement between fixation points.  
  *Pronunciation: "suh-kahd"* (like "suck-uhd").
- **Saccadic Masking**: Neural suppression of visual blur during saccades.
- **Flicker Fusion Threshold**: Frequency (Hz) above which discrete flashes appear continuous.

### Conclusion:
**No, the human eye does not "see" continuously in a raw, unedited sense.** It acts as a biological sensor capturing discrete data bursts during saccades. The brain then *processes* this input into a continuous perceptual experience via saccadic masking and motion stabilization.  

For filmmaking:  
- **35mm at 24 fps** leverages this brain processing to create a "cinematic" feel—fluid yet stylized.  
- **Video at 30/25 fps** mimics this but feels more "real-time" due to higher rates smoothing everyday motion.  
- **Ultra-high fps (e.g., 60+ fps)** reveals details your brain normally masks (e.g., motion blur), useful for slow-motion but less "filmic."
