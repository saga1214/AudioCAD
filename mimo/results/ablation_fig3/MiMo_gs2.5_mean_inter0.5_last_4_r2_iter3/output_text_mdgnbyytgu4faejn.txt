Okay, I can definitely give you a clear breakdown of the standard format. Here's a simple, step-by-step guide to build your resume efficiently.

First, at the very top, center your full name in 14-point font. Right directly underneath your name, on the same line, list your email address, phone number, city, state, and optionally your LinkedIn profile URL. Icons aren't recommended, and the dates can be omitted. Using nine-point font for this contact info section works well.

Next is your professional summary, which should be just two to four sentences highlighting your most relevant experience and career goals.

After that, list your relevant experience. This is your main section where you detail your most relevant jobs, including the company, your title, and the dates. For similar roles across different employers, list each one separately. Keep the descriptions brief with just a few bullet points highlighting key achievements.

Then comes your education section, listed in reverse chronological order. For each degree, include the school name, graduation date, and any academic honors or scholarships you received directly there.

If you have a skills section, it's best to place it after your experience and education. Tailor it to the specific job description, listing out your relevant hard and soft skills.

You can also have an optional 'Additional Experience' section for jobs that aren't as relevant, where you just list the company, title, and dates. Your 'Awards and Certifications' can be combined into one section at the end, listing any awards or important professional certifications.

Finally, you can add optional sections like 'Projects' or 'Volunteer Work' if they're relevant. Just remember to keep everything concise and tailored to the job you're applying for. This structure will give you a clear and professional resume that's easy to scan.
