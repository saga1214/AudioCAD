Based on your criteria (easy to train, good with kids/dogs, non-chasing, small/moderate size, manageable alone time, purebred or mixed options), here are excellent breed suggestions:

### Purebred Options:
1. **Cavalier King Charles Spaniel**  
   - *Why*: Gentle, affectionate, low-shedding, great with kids/dogs. Small (under 20 lbs), quiet, and highly trainable.  
   - *Note*: Prone to health issues (heart conditions), so choose a reputable breeder.

2. **Poodle (Toy/Miniature)**  
   - *Why*: Superb trainability, hypoallergenic, excellent with families. Small sizes fit "not too big." Highly socialized and low-key alone.  
   - *Hybrid Option*: Poodle mixes (e.g., Cocker Spaniel, Schnauzer) retain these traits.

3. **French Bulldog**  
   - *Why*: Playful, adaptable, and low-energy. Small (under 25 lbs), quiet (barking is rare), and great with kids. Minimal exercise needs.  
   - *Note*: Can be stubborn; monitor alone time due to separation anxiety.

4. **Bichon Frise**  
   - *Why*: Cheerful, hypoallergenic, and adaptable. Small (under 20 lbs), low-shedding, and thrives in apartments. Very social and easygoing.

### Mixed Breed Rescues (Likely Fit Your Criteria):
Look for breeds commonly in rescues with these traits:
- **Cavalier + [Mix]**: Cavalier rescues often have temperament-tested dogs.
- **Poodle + [Mix]**: "Cockapoo," "Pooodle," or "Schnappoo" mixes retain intelligence and trainability.
- **French Bulldog + [Mix]**: French Bulldog rescues screen for prey drive and socialization.
- **Pug + [Mix]**: Low-energy, friendly, and low-shedding (e.g., "Pugapoo").

### Key Steps to Find Candidates:
1. **Research Local Shelters/Rescues**:  
   - Use sites like [Petfinder](https://www.petfinder.com) or [Adopt-a-Pet](https://www.adoptapet.com) to filter by size, breed mix, and temperament.  
   - Contact breed-specific rescues (e.g., Cavalier Rescue, French Bulldog Rescue).

2. **Visit & Meet Potential Dogs**:  
   - Bring both of you to meet dogs. Observe how they interact with you, kids (if any), and other dogs.  
   - Ask about the dog’s history: Was it socialized? How do they handle alone time? Any prey drive?

3. **Assess Energy/Trainability**:  
   - Ask for training records. Look for dogs with positive reinforcement experience.  
   - Test their calmness during brief alone periods (e.g., 30-minute errands).

4. **Rule Out Prey Drive**:  
   - Ask rescuers/breeders if the dog has chased squirrels/cats in the past.  
   - Watch how they react to small movements (e.g., a ball rolling).

5. **Finalize Logistics**:  
   - Ensure the dog’s size fits your high-rise (e.g., elevator access).  
   - Verify proximity to a dog park for exercise.  
   - Budget for pet insurance (e.g., Lemonade, Spot) and supplies.

### Pro Tip:  
Prioritize **temperament testing**—especially for rescue dogs. Many shelters screen for aggression or anxiety. If your spouse prefers purebreds, start with adult dogs from breed rescues; they often have known temperaments and are easier to place.
