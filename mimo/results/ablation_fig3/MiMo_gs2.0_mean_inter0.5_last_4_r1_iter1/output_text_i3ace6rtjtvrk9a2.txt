Now, to your main question: would it be correct to say the eye sees continuously? Well, it’s a bit of a mix.

The eye doesn’t perceive motion in discrete, frame-by-frame jumps like a camera. It’s more like a continuous stream of data. But the brain processes that data in a way that gives us a fluid perception of movement. So, it’s more accurate to say that the eye captures visual information rapidly and continuously, but the brain synthesizes that into a smooth, coherent experience.

During a saccade, your vision is momentarily suppressed. So, technically, the eye sees continuously, but the brain doesn’t consciously process everything it sees all at once. It’s like a camera with a very fast shutter speed—your eye can capture rapid movement, but the brain chooses what to focus on and what to ignore to create a stable, understandable picture.

So, in short: the eye sees continuously, but the brain’s processing isn’t always continuous. It’s selective and adaptive, which is why we perceive the world as fluid rather than a series of snapshots.
