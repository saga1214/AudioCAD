Here's a clear packing list split into two segments, prioritizing warmth and function for trail running:

**For the 20-Mile Day Segment (Midday to Night):**
1.  **Base Layer:** Moisture-wicking top (short or long sleeve) & bottoms (shorts or tights). *Lightweight is fine for mid-30s.*
2.  **Mid-Layer:** Fleece jacket or vest. *Essential for hilly terrain and variable temps.*
3.  **Outer Layer:** Light wind/rain shell jacket. *Water-resistant is ideal.*
4.  **Hands:** Trail running gloves.
5.  **Head:** Warm hat (like a baseball cap or fleece beanie) + Sunglasses (if sunny).
6.  **Footwear:** Lightweight trail running shoes with good grip.
7.  **Visibility & Light:** Headlamp with extra batteries + handheld light (or spare headlamp).
8.  **Pack:** Lightweight waist pack, vest, or handheld bag.
9.  **Extras:** Buff or neck gaiter (versatile for warmth, sweat, sun), lightweight rain poncho.

**For the 12-Mile Night Segment (Late Night):**
1.  **Base Layer:** Moisture-wicking top & bottoms. *(Same as day segment)*
2.  **Mid-Layer:** Fleece jacket or vest. *(Same as day segment)*
3.  **Outer Layer:** Insulated jacket (fleece, puffy, or hybrid) or lightweight puffer jacket. ***CRITICAL for mid-30s at night.***
4.  **Hands:** Trail running gloves.
5.  **Head:** Warm hat (fleece beanie is ideal).
6.  Footwear: Lightweight trail running shoes with good grip. *(Same as day segment)*
7.  **Visibility & Light:** Headlamp with extra batteries + handheld light. *(Ensure headlamp brightness is sufficient for night)*
8.  **Pack:** Lightweight waist pack, vest, or handheld bag. *(Same as day segment)*
9.  **Extras:** Buff or neck gaiter (useful for extra warmth or sweat), extra pair of socks (if needed), arm sleeves (optional extra layer).

**Key Differences & Why:**

*   **Insulated Jacket:** The biggest upgrade for the night segment. You'll need this warmth to combat the chill of the mid-30s after dark.
*   **Beanie:** A fleece beanie provides significant warmth for your head and neck, which loses heat quickly.
*   **Battery Priority:** Extra batteries are even more crucial for the night segment where you'll rely heavily on your lights.
*   **Visibility:** Headlamp brightness needs to be sufficient for the later hours.
*   **Aid Stations:** Remember you'll have access to water, nutrition, and potentially hand warmers at these stations, so you don't need to carry *everything*.

**Final Tip:** Check the actual forecast closer to the race. "Mid 30s" can range from cool (mid-40s°F) to chilly (mid-30s°F), and wind chill at night can make it feel much colder.
