Okay, you've got macramé as your passion craft, cookies as a go-to, and a big family to involve. The pine cone idea is a great starting point, but you’re looking for something even simpler, more universal, and still meaningful. Let’s break it down by age and effort.

First, for the little ones, the two-year-old, five-year-old... something tactile and visual. You could do paper snowflakes or origami ornaments. They can color and cut simple shapes, and you can fold the more complex parts. They’re festive, not messy, and they last forever.

For the older kids, the macramé plant hangers could actually work really well as gifts if you customize them. You could make a mini version for a teacher’s desk or a keychain hanger. That way, you’re using your favorite craft, but it’s still manageable. Or you could do simple woven bands, like a friendship bracelet, but with a holiday twist—maybe red and green thread or a little pom-pom charm.

The cookie idea is a good one too. If you want minimal rolling, no cutters, maybe try thumbprint cookies. Just scoop dough, add a little jam or chocolate in the middle, and bake. They look cute and are easy to decorate with sprinkles. Or even just no-bake energy bites—mix oats, nut butter, and honey, roll them into balls, and dip in melted chocolate. Super quick and customizable.

And for something totally different, like that pine cone idea but simpler, maybe decorated rocks? You can paint rocks with holiday designs, or even write names or wishes on them. They’re cheap, customizable, and the little ones can do it too. Or you could do mini wreaths using things you already have around the house—like pinecones, dried orange slices, and twine.

The main thing is to match the activity to the age. The little ones get to do the decorating part, the older ones can handle the crafting. That way, everyone feels involved without it being overwhelming. Does that sound like a good place to start?
