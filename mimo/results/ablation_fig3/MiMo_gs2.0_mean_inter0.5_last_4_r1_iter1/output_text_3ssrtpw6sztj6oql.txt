Based on modern security principles and the vulnerability exposed by the Gardner heist, the **first line of defense for an art museum must be controlling access at every public entry point**, but it must be implemented with layered redundancy to counter human error:

1.  **Visible Security Presence:** Guards or uniformed officers deter potential threats and provide immediate response capability. Their presence alone can deter casual attempts.
2.  **Rigorous Screening Procedures:** Mandatory bag checks (using X-ray machines and trained personnel), ID verification (for special access), and potentially metal detectors manage who and what physically enters the museum. This is the primary barrier against smuggling weapons or tools.
3.  **Fail-Safe & Fail-Secure Design:** The access control system must be designed so that if human error occurs (e.g., guards fall asleep, screening is bypassed), the *default state* is security. For example:
    *   **Procedural Safeguards:** Strict protocols for handling access requests, regular guard patrols, and independent verification of access logs.
    *   **Physical Redundancy:** Reinforced doors, panic bars requiring multiple keys, and intrusion detection systems (like motion sensors or glass break alarms) triggered *inside* the building act as a secondary, automatic response if the first line (guards/screening) fails. They alert security and potentially deter or delay entry.
    *   **Technology Integration:** While cameras and alarms are critical *layers*, they rely on human monitoring and response. The *first line* (access control) provides the initial point of control before the internal layers activate.

**Why this is the core "first line":**

*   **Proactive Prevention:** Its goal is to prevent entry of unauthorized individuals or contraband *before* they can reach the galleries or artworks.
*   **Manageable Risk:** Public entry points are the highest-traffic, highest-risk locations for external threats. Controlling them first reduces the attack surface.
*   **Foundation for Other Layers:** Effective access control allows security to focus resources (guards, cameras, sensors) on managing threats *inside* the building. A breach at the entrance drastically increases the risk to the entire collection.
*   **Addressing Human Error:** By combining presence (deterrence) with screening (control), it creates multiple points of failure. If one element fails (e.g., a guard is distracted), the next (bag check) can still act as the barrier. Technology like sensors provides automatic response if both fail.

While secure displays, alarms, and advanced surveillance are vital *secondary* and *tertiary* layers, the **first line of defense remains the managed entry point itself.** Modern security acknowledges human fallibility, so this first line must be designed to be robust and fail securely, even when human error occurs. The Gardner heist underscores that deception targeting the human element at the gate remains the most dangerous vulnerability, making rigorous access control the critical initial defense.
