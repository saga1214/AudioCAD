That’s a really good question! At minus ten degrees Celsius, the coefficient of friction is likely to be even lower than the typical competition range of 0.01 to 0.02. The ice is colder and harder, so the blade can cut into it more easily, which actually reduces friction. The friction is lowest when the blade is sharp, and the ice is just right—not too hard, not too soft. At minus ten degrees, the ice is likely harder and drier, so the coefficient could drop to around 0.005 to 0.01. That’s even lower than the ideal competition range. It’s really fascinating how all those tiny changes in temperature and pressure affect the physics of it.
