You've hit on the most critical insight from your studies. **Yes, ultra-processed foods (UPFs) are widely considered the *one type of food that would be the worst for your health if eaten in excess*.** Here’s why they stand out as the top concern:

### Why Ultra-Processed Foods Are the Biggest Threat:
1. **Nutrient Depletion**:  
   UPFs are stripped of fiber, vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats while being loaded with **added sugars, unhealthy fats (like trans fats), refined carbs, and excessive sodium**. This creates a "nutrient-negative" diet.

2. **Metabolic Harm**:  
   Excess sugar and refined carbs cause **insulin resistance**, directly increasing risks for type 2 diabetes, obesity, and cardiovascular disease.

3. **"Empty Calories"**:  
   They deliver high calories with minimal nutritional value (e.g., soda, candy, packaged pastries), making it easy to overconsume without feeling full.

4. **Hidden Additives**:  
   Preservatives, emulsifiers, and artificial flavors disrupt gut health, trigger inflammation, and may alter metabolism long-term.

5. **Combined Impact**:  
   Unlike a single food like excess salt or sugar, UPFs often combine these harms (e.g., a frozen pizza has refined flour, cheese, processed meat, and added sugar/sodium).

### Other Contenders & Why They’re Less "Worst":
- **Artificial Trans Fats**: Uniquely harmful to cholesterol but less common in modern diets (banned in many countries).  
- **Excess Sugar**: Major driver of diabetes/obesity but easier to avoid than UPFs.  
- **High-Sodium Foods**: Critical for blood pressure but UPFs often contain *both* sodium *and* sugar/fats.  

### The Verdict:
While all these foods are harmful in excess, **ultra-processed foods represent a systemic threat** because they’re engineered to override natural satiety signals and accumulate multiple health risks simultaneously. Your class is learning a vital lesson: reducing UPFs (even by 10-20% per day) is one of the most impactful changes for long-term health.
