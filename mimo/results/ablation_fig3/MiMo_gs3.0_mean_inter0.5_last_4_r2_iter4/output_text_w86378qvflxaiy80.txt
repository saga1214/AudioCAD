That's a profound question.

While all the reasons are important, you could argue that the most critical aspect is preserving irreplaceable evolutionary heritage and maintaining the planet's resilience.

Think of it this way: each unique plant and animal is the product of millions of years of evolution. When it goes extinct, that entire evolutionary story is lost forever. We often discover the value of this "heritage" only after we lose it. For instance, after a bee species went extinct, scientists discovered it was pollinating a crop that farmers had completely forgotten about.

Also, a healthy, diverse hotspot ecosystem is more resilient to change. A complex web of life can better absorb shocks like climate change or disease. In essence, we are preserving the genetic library and the safety net for our own future, both for ecological health and for our own survival.
