If I had to choose one driver, I’d still say **total calorie balance** is the biggest top driver for increasing obesity rates. The key factor is consistently consuming more calories than your body burns over time.  

That said, other factors—like processed foods, physical inactivity, or poor food quality—make it *easier* to go into a calorie surplus. For example:  
- **Processed foods** are often hyper-palatable and calorie-dense, making it easy to overeat.  
- **Lack of exercise** reduces calorie burn, tipping the balance.  
- **Nutrient-deficient diets** can lead to hunger and overeating.  

But at the end of the day, even someone eating small portions of processed foods might gain weight if they consistently eat more than they need. Likewise, someone eating calorie-dense non-processed foods (like nuts or oils) without exercising is more likely to gain weight *if* they consistently overconsume. The single most important factor remains the **calorie surplus**.
