Okay, Belize sounds like your home turf for amazing dives. Building on that, Costa Rica also has tropical waters but in a different environment. Let's map out a dream Costa Rica trip tailored to scuba divers like you.

**Step 1: Pinpoint Costa Rica's Scuba Strongholds**

*   **Cocos Island:** This is the undisputed star for deepwater, technical, and cavern diving. Famous for its dramatic drop-offs, caves, wrecks (like the *García*), and immense biodiversity (sharks, mantas, hammerheads, deep-sea creatures). Requires advanced certification. *Advantage:* Unmatched deepwater adventure & marine life.
*   **Puntarenas / Playa Conchal (Aguas Claras):** Home to **Cabo Blanco Marine Park**. Offers beautiful, sheltered reefs teeming with fish, turtles, rays, and sometimes dolphins. Great for beginners, intermediate divers, and visibility of sea lions. *Advantage:* Easy access, beautiful coral reefs, excellent for beginners/intermediates, high chance of sea lions.
*   **Manzanillo (Aguas Zarcas):** Features the **Guanacaste Marine Park**. Known for its beautiful, clear, sandy bottoms, good visibility, and strong currents. Famous for its **"Dive Train"** (a wreck train). Excellent for macro photography, drift diving, and seeing pelagics like tuna and sharks. *Advantage:* Strong currents, clear water, wreck diving, pelagics, macro life.
*   **Tortuguero:** Primarily known for surface adventures (canals, sea turtles), but offers accessible shore dives on the coast. Focuses on turtle encounters (especially nesting season) and reef life. *Advantage:* Unique turtle access, relaxed pace, beautiful coast.

**Step 2: Tailor Fin Choice to Locations & Your Debate**

*   **For Punta Arenas / Cabo Blanco:**
    *   *Short Fins:* Excellent maneuverability in the park's channels, travel ease wins. Great if most dives are calm.
    *   *Long Fins:* Still fine for efficiency in slightly stronger currents than Cabo Blanco.
*   **For Manzanillo / Guanacaste:**
    *   *Long Fins:* Highly recommended! The strong currents and sandy bottoms benefit greatly from the extra thrust and efficiency long fins provide. Makes the drift dives easier.
    *   *Short Fins:* Possible, but tiring and less efficient in those currents. Not ideal.
*   **For Cocos Island:**
    *   *Long Fins:* Strongly preferred for efficiency over the long descents and in strong currents/caverns. Save leg energy.

*   **For Tortuguero:**
    *   *Short Fins:* Often the best choice for the relatively calm shore dives and the canals (if you do snorkeling). Compact and easy.

**Step 3: Build the Ultimate Itinerary Mix**

*   **Days 1-3: Punta Arenas / Cabo Blanco Marine Park:** Focus on getting comfortable, seeing turtles (shoreside), exploring the beautiful sheltered reefs with lots of fish. Use short fins here. This gives you a fantastic warm-up and a taste of classic Paddy湾 reefs.
*   **Days 4-6: Manzanillo / Guanacaste Marine Park:** Dive the "Dive Train" wreck, experience the strong currents and clear water, spot pelagics. Use long fins here for efficiency and comfort. This delivers the powerful drift diving experience.
*   **Days 7-9: Cocos Island (Advanced):** If you're up for it, this is the deep adventure. Requires at least Advanced Scuba certification. Dive the Bluester, the caverns, the wrecks. Use long fins. This is the ultimate deepwater spectacle.
*   **Days 10: Tortuguero (Optional):** Spend the last day relaxing, exploring the canals (snorkel or kayak), and potentially seeing nesting sea turtles up close. Short fins ideal here. A perfect, serene end.

**Key Considerations for Your Costa Rica Dream Trip:**

1.  **Certification:** Ensure you both have at least **Advanced Scuba Diving** certification. Cocos Island requires it, and Guanacaste currents demand it. Rescue Diving is a bonus.
2.  **Live-Aboard vs. Resort:** Liveaboards (especially for Cocos/Manzanillo) offer the best experience for remote locations and wrecks. Resorts are great for Cabo Blanco/Tortuguero. Factor in travel time.
3.  **Travel with Gear:** Decide *now* on fins. If short, they are compact and travel fine. If long, factor in the extra weight and cost of shipping them separately to Cocos if needed. Cocos is remote.
4.  **Best Time to Dive Costa Rica:** Year-round, but the best conditions (warmest water, highest visibility) are generally **December to April**. May to November can have more rain and slightly cooler, murkier water (though still great diving!).
5.  **Safety:** Choose reputable dive operators with good safety records. Inform them of your debate about fins so they can offer advice.

**Conclusion:**

For Costa Rica, long fins offer clear advantages for the strong currents and deep wrecks of Guanacaste and Cocos Island, while short fins are fantastic for the sheltered, beautiful reefs of Punta Arenas and the relaxed pace of Tortuguero. The best trip combines all these experiences. Prioritize Punta Arenas (short fins), Guanacaste (long fins), and then choose between the technical adventure of Cocos (long fins) and the turtle serenity of Tortuguero (short fins) based on your energy levels. Get those Advanced Certs and enjoy planning your dream trip!
