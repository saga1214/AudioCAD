3. **Step 1: Plunger (Different Technique):** Try a **double-bubble plunge**. Use *two* plungers together over the drain. Push them down firmly, seal completely, then pull up sharply. Do this 10-15 times with strong, steady pressure. Sometimes this dislodges deep clogs.

4. **Step 2: Drain Snake (Key Tool):** If plunging fails, grab your drain snake (the flexible, springy tool). Insert it slowly into the drain. Don't force it. Feel for resistance or a "gripping" sensation. Once you feel it, stop, rotate the snake slightly (like turning a key), and pull it out slowly. You should pull out a solid mass of debris.

5. **Step 3: Wet/Dry Vac (If Available):** If the line is flooded or you suspect a clog deeper down, position your small wet/dry vac right over the drain. Turn it on and suck out the water and debris. Sometimes this clears the path for a snake.

6. **Step 4: Zip-It Tool (If You Have One):** If you have a small "Zip-It" drain opener (a stiff wire tool), insert it and try to break up the clog by rotating it forcefully.
