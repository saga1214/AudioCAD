Of course, I can give you a complete breakdown of a standard professional resume structure. Just follow these sections in order.

First, at the very top, you have your name in about a fourteen-point font. To the right, or below it if you prefer, you'll list your primary email and phone number, followed by your expected graduation date if you're a student, or your city in parentheses. Icons avoid ATS issues best left alone. A hidden hyperlink to your LinkedIn can be a nice touch.

Next, right under your name, is your professional summary. This is a short, two-to-three sentence paragraph that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals.

After that is the experience section. This is where you list each employer separately, along with your title and the dates you worked there. Keep it concise.

Then comes your education. Here you'll list your degree, the university, and any awards or honors you mentioned, like Highest Honers or specific scholarships.

Following that, you'll have a skills section. This is where you list your most relevant hard and soft skills based on the job descriptions you're looking at. You can even use bullet points to make it easy to scan.

If you have any, you can add a section for certifications, volunteer work, or languages you speak.

As for the order of contact info, vertical alignment—meaning it goes below your name—is definitely preferred. And again, remove the icons to be ATS-friendly. Grouping similar roles without employers isn't ideal because it can obscure your timeline.

So, to summarize: list employers individually even for similar roles, integrate academic awards within the education section, place contact info vertically below your name, and remove icons.
