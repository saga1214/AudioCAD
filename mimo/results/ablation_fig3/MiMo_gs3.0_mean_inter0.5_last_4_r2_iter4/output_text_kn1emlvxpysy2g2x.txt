Of yours, high-end Pinot Noir is best stored at cellar temp, around 55 Fahrenheit, and served slightly below room temp, at about 55 to 60 Fahrenheit. Italian Chianti is very similar—store it at 55 and serve it at 60 to 65 Fahrenheit. Pinot Grigio, on the other hand, is best served cold. You'll store that one chilled at around 50 Fahrenheit and serve it chilled, between 45 and 50. Italian Sangiovese, which makes a robust red, is also best served slightly below room temp, stored at 55 and served at 60 to 65 Fahrenheit.

We also talked about Japanese Hon-Mirin, which isn't really wine, but if sipped straight, room temp is fine, served at about 68 to 75 Fahrenheit after being stored cool. Champagne should be stored and served around 45 to 50 degrees Fahrenheit. Mulled red wine is served hot, warmed to about 140 to 160 after being initially stored at normal cellar temps of 55 Fahrenheit.

So, for hot weather, I'd say Sangiovese is a great choice, served warm. And for cold weather, Champagne is crisp and refreshing. Hope that helps!
