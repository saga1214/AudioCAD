*   **Loss of the Water Film:** At temperatures just above absolute zero, ice is harder and more crystalline. The thin layer of liquid water that lubricates the blade-to-ice interface at slightly warmer temperatures becomes much thinner or even disappears entirely.

*   **Pressure Melting Effect:** While pressure does slightly melt the ice directly under the blade (creating a tiny water layer), this effect becomes less pronounced at very low temperatures because the ice itself is harder and more rigid.
