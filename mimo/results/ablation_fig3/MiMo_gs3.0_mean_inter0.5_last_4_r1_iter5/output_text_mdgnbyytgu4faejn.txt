Of course. Here is a clear, traditional, and recruiter-friendly format you can follow. Just replace the placeholder information with your own.

First, at the very top, centered and using a fourteen-point font, put your full name.

Right below that, use smaller nine-point text to create your contact information, either left-aligned or using icons without them. You should have your email, phone number, city, and optionally your LinkedIn URL. Adding a link here is safe again, since we removed the icons first.

Next, under your name, have a short, powerful Professional Summary. This is a one or two-sentence paragraph that highlights your most relevant experience and career goals.

After the summary, create a section called "Relevant Experience." Here, you'll list your most important jobs, including the company name, your title, the start and end dates, and a few key bullet points that focus on achievements relevant to the jobs you're applying for now.

Then, you can have a smaller section called "Additional Work History." This is for jobs that are less relevant but still part of your career. Just list the job title, company, and dates with minimal details.

Next is the "Education" section. List your highest degree first, followed by your school name. Under each degree, you can add details like your graduation date, your honors, and any awards you received, such as a scholarship.

After that, create a "Skills" section. It's best to list your skills in two categories: "Technical Skills," for things like software or programming languages, and "Soft Skills," for things like communication and teamwork.

Finally, you can add an optional "Certifications" section for any relevant professional certifications.
