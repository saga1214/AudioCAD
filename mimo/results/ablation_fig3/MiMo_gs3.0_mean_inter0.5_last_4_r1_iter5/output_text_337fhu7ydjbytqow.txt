Here's your optimized closing gear packing list split between the 20-mile overnight segment and the final 12-mile daybreak segment, prioritizing warmth, adaptability, and safety:

### **20-Mile Overnight Segment (Night Pace Crew)**
1. **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking running shirt (short or long-sleeve) and leggings  
2. **Mid-Layer**: Fleece jacket or lightweight puffy (down/synthetic)  
3. **Outer Layer**: Wind/water-resistant shell (e.g., lightweight rain jacket)  
4. **Headwear**:  
   - Gloves (fingerless for warmth, full-finger for grip)  
   - Warm hat (beanie or balaclava if temps drop below 40°F)  
   - Buff/neck gaiter (adjustable neck/wrist cover)  
5. **Footwear**:  
   - Trail shoes with aggressive tread (e.g., Vibram soles)  
   - Extra socks (lightweight wool blend)  
6. **Visibility**: Headlamp/handheld light + **spare batteries**  

### **Final 12-Mile Segment (Dawn Pace Crew)**  
1. **Base Layer**: Moisture-wicking shirt/leggings (same as overnight)  
2. **Mid-Layer**: Fleece jacket (adjustable for changing light)  
3. **Outer Layer**: **Lightweight wind shell only** (no heavy rain/wind layers needed)  
4. **Headwear**: Hat (beanie for dawn chill, cap if sunny)  
5. **Footwear**: Same trail shoes + socks (focus on grip for potential trail changes)  
6. **Optional Extras**:  
   - Small pack/vest (to carry layers between aid stations)  
   - Light gloves (if dawn is chilly)  

### **Key Adjustments**  
- **Overnight**: Prioritize insulation (add hat/gloves if temps stay low).  
- **Final Miles**: Simplify layers; remove wind shell if daylight warms you.  
- **Both Segments**:  
  - Use aid stations to shed/dry layers.  
  - Avoid cotton (retains moisture).  
  - Test gear during training if possible.  

This setup keeps you warm and visible overnight, then transitions to lightweight layers for daylight. Skip nutrition advice, but bring a handheld light—nights get darker than you expect! Good luck pacing! 🏃‍♀️💨
