It was a very different experience for the Pilgrims and the Wampanoag people.

For the Wampanoag, the feast was likely a strategic event to strengthen their alliance with the English, but it was also a time of uncertainty. They knew that the settlers would eventually bring disease and conflict.

For the Pilgrims, it was a celebration of their survival and a new harvest, but it was also a reminder of how much they still didn't know about the land and the people.

So, it was a moment of cooperation, but not a universally "happy" event.
