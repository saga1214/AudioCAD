You're very close, but there's a subtle distinction. The word is "saccadic" or "saccade." Let’s break it down. Saccade is pronounced like “sah-kahd.” It’s a quick movement of the eye.

Here’s the key point: The eye doesn’t see continuously in the way a film sensor does. Instead, it sees in rapid, discrete snapshots. Saccadic masking is the brain’s way of blending these snapshots so that you perceive continuous motion. 

So, if you were to say it out loud, it would be: “The eye sees in rapid, discrete snapshots, but the brain blends these with saccadic masking to create a perception of continuous motion.”
