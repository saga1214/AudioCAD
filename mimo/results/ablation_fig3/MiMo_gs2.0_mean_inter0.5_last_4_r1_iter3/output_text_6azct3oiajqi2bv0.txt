Here's a safe and effective way to add Velcro to your jacket lining without damaging it, using common household items:

**Best Method: Iron-On Adhesive (with Protection)**

1.  **Choose Heat-Safe Velcro:** **Crucial!** Only use Velcro strips specifically labeled as "Iron-On" or "Heat-Sensitive." Regular Velcro will melt the lining.
2.  **Prep the Fabric (Optional but Recommended):** Lightly iron the inside of the jacket *without* the Velcro backing applied. This helps the fabric fibers swell slightly, improving adhesion.
3.  **Position the Velcro:**
    *   Cut the Velcro strip to the desired length (e.g., 2-3 inches).
    *   Lay the jacket lining flat. Position the Velcro tab (the sticky part) where you want it attached. The hook side (the raised part) will be the part you sew or glue to the outer fabric later.
4.  **Apply Heat with a Barrier:**
    *   **Get a thin piece of cloth:** A cotton handkerchief, thin cotton napkin, or even a small piece of felt works best. It needs to be thin enough not to block heat but thick enough to protect the lining.
    *   **Iron the Cloth:** Place the cloth over the Velcro tab.
    *   **Iron Over the Cloth:** Using medium heat (not high/cotton setting), press the iron firmly over the cloth for 15-30 seconds. The heat activates the adhesive.
    *   **Check Adhesion:** Carefully peel the Velcro backing away. It should stay firmly attached to the lining. If it lifts easily, apply more heat for a few more seconds and re-peel.
5.  **Repeat:** Attach the other Velcro tab (the hook side) to the *outer* fabric of the jacket using the same method.
6.  **Secure the Hook Side:** Now you need to attach the hook side (the part you just ironed onto the lining) permanently to the jacket. You have two options:
    *   **Hand-Sew:** Use a needle and thread to sew the hook side tab securely onto the jacket's outer fabric. Knot the thread well.
    *   **Strong Fabric Glue (Optional):** If you can't sew, use a strong fabric glue (like E6000 or a heavy-duty textile glue) applied *only* to the *back* of the Velcro hook tab. Press firmly and hold for a minute. Let it cure fully according to the instructions (usually 24 hours).
7.  **Test:** Gently tug on the Velcro to ensure it's secure before wearing.

**Alternative Method: Sewing**

*   If you have access to a sewing machine or needle and thread, this is the most permanent and reliable method for *both* tabs.
*   **Thread the Needle:** Use a strong thread (like polyester or nylon) and a sturdy needle.
*   **Sew the Tab (or Use Fabric Glue):** You can either:
    *   Sew the entire Velcro tab securely to the lining.
    *   **OR** Glue the Velcro tab to the lining using strong fabric glue (like E6000) and then sew over the glued area for extra security.
*   **Sew the Hook Side:** Sew the hook side tab securely to the outer fabric.

**Key Tips to Prevent Damage:**

*   **Heat-Safe Only:** **NEVER** use regular Velcro or hot glue on the lining.
*   **Protective Barrier:** The cloth barrier is essential when ironing.
*   **Test First:** Always test the iron-on method on a small, inconspicuous area of the lining first.
*   **Avoid High Heat:** Medium heat is usually sufficient. High heat will definitely melt synthetic linings.
*   **Press, Don't Slide:** When ironing, press firmly and hold for the full 15-30 seconds. Sliding the iron can damage the lining.
*   **Secure the Hook Side:** Whether sewing or gluing, ensure the hook side is attached *very* securely to the outer fabric.

This method gives you a functional, temporary (or semi-permanent) Velcro closure while minimizing the risk of damaging your jacket's lining. The iron-on method is the most accessible for most people.
