If I had to choose **one top driver** behind increasing obesity rates, it would be **chronic energy imbalance (consistently consuming more calories than expended)**. 

While highly processed foods are a major *con contributor* (due to their calorie density, lack of satiety, and easy availability), obesity fundamentally stems from a prolonged state of **energy surplus**. This imbalance can occur through:
- **Diet:** Overconsumption of calories, even from unprocessed sources (e.g., nuts, oils, sugary drinks).
- **Activity Level:** Reduced physical exertion (sedentary behavior) failing to burn enough calories.
- **Metabolic Factors:** Genetics, hormones, and sleep affecting metabolism and hunger signals.

**Why processed foods are a critical *subdriver*:**
They are engineered to override natural satiety cues, making overconsumption easier. Their low fiber and nutrient density reduce fullness, while hyper-palatability (salty, sweet, fatty combinations) encourages prolonged eating. This makes it *easier* to achieve an energy surplus, but the surplus itself is the root cause.

**In short:** Processed foods are a powerful *catalyst* for obesity, but the core driver remains the sustained calorie surplus. Addressing both dietary quality *and* energy expenditure is essential for prevention.
