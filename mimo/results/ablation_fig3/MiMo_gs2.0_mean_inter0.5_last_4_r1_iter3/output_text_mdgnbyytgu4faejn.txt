Of course. Here is a step-by-step breakdown of a standard resume format that you can follow. I'll describe the different sections and what information to include in each.

First, at the very top, you have your name in about a 14-point font. To its right, on a new line for each piece of information, you'll list your contact details: your email, phone number, city, and a link to your LinkedIn profile. Using nine-point font for these details is fine, but standard practice is often to use the same 14-point font for everything in the header for consistency. Icons are best avoided, as I mentioned.

Next, right below your name, is your professional summary. This is a short, two-to-three sentence paragraph that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals, especially for the new direction you're taking.

After that, you'll have your experience section. This should be your main section and should be organized chronologically, from most recent to oldest. For each job, list the company name, your job title, and the dates you worked there. Then, use two to four bullet points to describe your key responsibilities and achievements in that role.

Then comes your skills section. This should be near the top, after your experience. You can list your hard skills, like technical abilities or software you're proficient in, and your soft skills, like communication or leadership.

After that is your education section. Here, you'll list your degrees, the school you attended, and the dates you graduated. You can also add details like your GPA if it's high, or any awards or honors you received, like a scholarship or graduating with highest honors. If you have multiple degrees from the same school, just list them all under the school's name.

If you have significant volunteer work, a hobbies section, or other relevant projects, you can include those after your education section.

Finally, at the very bottom, you can add a references section, which usually just says "References available upon request." You don't need to list them out unless you're required to.

This structure should give you a clear roadmap to create a professional and effective resume. Good luck!
