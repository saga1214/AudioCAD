Dealing with a difficult person like this is incredibly draining, especially when it impacts your children's safety and peace. Here's some advice that might help:

1.  **Prioritize Your Own Well-being & Boundaries:**
    *   **Acknowledge the Stress:** You're right, it's "very, very stressful." Don't minimize that feeling.
    *   **Protect Your Kids' Space:** Be clear with your children about the situation (age-appropriately) and set firm boundaries on *your* property. "We know she might call, but we won't let her control our lives. Ride safely within our yard if you feel nervous."
    *   **Control Your Reactions:** Avoid engaging directly with her. Responding with anger or arguments often fuels the situation. Stay calm and disengaged.

2.  **Document Everything:**
    *   **Keep a Log:** Write down every incident: dates, times, what happened, who was involved, what was called (police, CPS), and any witnesses. This is crucial evidence if you need to involve authorities later.
    *   **Save Communications:** Keep copies of any letters, emails, or texts related to the incidents.

3.  **Know Your Rights & Report If Necessary:**
    *   **Research Local Laws:** Understand what constitutes harassment, nuisance calls, or false reporting in your area. Laws vary significantly.
    *   **Report False Reporting:** If she's making false reports to police or CPS, that is a serious offense. You can report her to the police department or CPS agency.
    *   **Consider Nuisance Laws:** If her behavior is a persistent nuisance, you can file a complaint with your local city/county government (code enforcement, nuisance abatement).
    *   **Consult an Attorney:** If the harassment escalates significantly or causes major harm, talking to a lawyer specializing in landlord-tenant or personal injury (if applicable) can be wise.

4.  **Seek Support & Community:**
    *   **Talk to Others Affected:** See if other neighbors are also dealing with this. You're not alone. Consider forming a group to document and potentially report collectively.
    *   **Talk to School Staff:** Since dress-up days are happening at school and Halloween is an upcoming event, she *might* try to make reports there too. Inform the school administration proactively about the situation and your efforts to manage it. This gives them context if they receive any reports.
    *   **Lean on Your Support System:** Talk to trusted friends, family, or a therapist. Processing this stress with others is vital.

5.  Forrest Frank is a great choice for coping! Keeping the music and school fun as anchors is smart. Lean into those positive moments.

6.  **When to Escalate:**
    *   If she continues false reports, contact the police or CPS directly to report her behavior.
    *   If harassment becomes physical, threatening, or involves threats, contact the police immediately.
    *   If legal action seems necessary, consult an attorney.

**Remember:** You can't control her actions, but you *can* control your response, protect your family, document the behavior, seek support, and utilize the systems designed to handle harassment and false reporting. Focus on building resilience within your own family unit and finding joy in the things you *can* control, like the school events and Halloween preparations.
