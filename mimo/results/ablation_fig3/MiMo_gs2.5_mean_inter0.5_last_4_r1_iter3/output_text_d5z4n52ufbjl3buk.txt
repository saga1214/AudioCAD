Here are some key pointers for your email that balance respect with clarity:

1. **Subject Line:** Keep it neutral and professional. Something like:  
   *"Meeting Request: Exam Feedback for [Course Name]"*

2. **Opening:**  
   > *"Dear Professor [Name],*  
   > *I hope you’re doing well. I scored a 64 on the recent exam for [Course Name] and wanted to respectfully request feedback on my performance to help me improve in future assignments."*

3. **Focus on Learning, Not Accusation:**  
   > *"Subjectively graded exams can be challenging to understand, but I’d value any specific points or areas you’d recommend I strengthen. My goal is to align better with your expectations for this course."*

4. **Be Specific (But Tactful):** Essay exams often lack clear rubrics. If you know any grading criteria, mention them. Example:  
   > *"If you have a rubric or key criteria for essay-based exams, I’d appreciate any guidance on how I might have fallen short."*

5. **Suggest Low-Pressure Options:**  
   > *"Would you be available for a brief 10–15 minute chat during your office hours, or at another convenient time? If your schedule is tight, I’m happy to ask questions via email."*

6. **Close Professionally:**  
   > *"Thank you for your time and guidance. I look forward to your insights."*  
   > *Sincerely, [Your Name]*

---

### Key Strategies:
- **Avoid Emotional Language:** No phrases like "unfairly" or "huge ego." Stay neutral.
- **Offer an Exit:** If he says no, pivot to:  
  *"No problem at all! If you have any written feedback on the exam, I’d be grateful to review it."*
- **Document Everything:** Save all emails. If he’s unresponsive, escalate to the department chair later (but only if needed).
