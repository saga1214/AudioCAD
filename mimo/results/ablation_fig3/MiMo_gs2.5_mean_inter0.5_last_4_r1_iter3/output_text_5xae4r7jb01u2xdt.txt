Of course. Let's look at a scenario involving a corner kick. Imagine this: The Red team is in an offside position when the Blue team takes a short corner kick. The Blue team defender number two deliberately plays the bouncing ball off of Red team defender number one. Defender number one controls the ball and then passes it to midfielder number seven, who then shoots and scores.

Now, here's the difference depending on which rulebook you're using. According to the Laws of the Game from the International Football Association Board, or IFAB, this reset happens. Because defender one made a controlled play on the deflected ball, Forward number nine is no longer considered offside. The goal would stand.

However, in the U.S. high school soccer rules, the reset only applies if the defender's touch was deliberate and controlled. A deflection, as in this case, might not be enough to reset the situation. In that case, Forward number nine would still be considered offside, and the goal would be disallowed.
