That's fantastic you're creating a plan! Structure is key for learning, especially when stress is a factor. Here’s a balanced weekly schedule designed for steady progress from 2 to 3 beanbags, incorporating scarves and built-in stress management:

**Weekly Practice Schedule (Choose 5-6 days):**

*   **Monday:** **Beanbag Focus (Transition Day)**
    *   Warm-up: 5 mins of gentle wrist/shoulder circles.
    *   **Practice:** 15-20 mins focused *only* on the **first three throws** (Dominant Hand -> Peak -> Other Hand -> Peak -> Last Ball). Catch or drop after each sequence. Don't worry about consistency.
    *   Cool-down: 5 mins of slow, deliberate beanbag catches (1-2 at a time).
    *   *Optional:* 5 mins with tennis balls (same throwing sequence) for different texture.

*   **Tuesday:** **Rest or Active Recovery**
    *   *No juggling.* Do light stretching, walk, or practice deep breathing/meditation if feeling stressed. Crucial for preventing burnout.

*   **Wednesday:** **Beanbag Focus (Building Consistency)**
    *   Warm-up: 5 mins.
    *   **Practice:** 15-20 mins focusing on getting *one full cycle* of throws with all three beanbags. Aim for smoothness and rhythm over catching.
    *   Cool-down: 5 mins of slow beanbag catches.

*   **Thursday:** **Scarf Focus (Rhythm & Pattern)**
    *   Warm-up: 5 mins.
    *   **Practice:** 15-20 mins practicing your beanbag rhythm with **scarves**. Focus on the *timing* of your throws and catches. Let the scarves fall slowly. This builds muscle memory without the pressure of drops.
    *   Cool-down: 5 mins of slow beanbag catches.

*   **Friday:** **Beanbag Focus (Catching Focus)**
    *   Warm-up: 5 mins.
    *   **Practice:** 15-20 mins focusing *only* on catching the balls. Toss them gently and focus on landing them in your palms. Use beanbags or scarves.
    *   Cool-down: 5 mins of slow beanbag catches.

*   **Saturday:** **Beanbag Focus (Full Practice - Catching Focus)**
    *   Warm-up: 5 mins.
    *   **Practice:** 15-20 mins: Aim for 1-2 successful cycles where you catch all three beanbags. Celebrate small wins!
    *   Cool-down: 5 mins of slow beanbag catches.

*   **Sunday:** **Rest & Enjoyment**
    *   *No structured practice.* Spend time with your son! Watch a juggling video together, play catch, or simply relax. Recharge.

**Key Principles for Success:**

1.  **Short & Focused:** 20-25 mins is plenty. Quality focus is better than long, frustrating sessions. Stop *before* you feel panicked.
2.  **Specific Goals:** Each session has a clear focus (throws, rhythm, catching). This prevents overwhelm.
3.  **Embrace the "Drop":** Dropped balls are *data*, not failure. Analyze *why* it dropped (too high? too low? wrong hand?) and adjust next time. Don't get discouraged.
4.  **Listen to Your Stress:** If you feel panic rising mid-session, STOP. Take 3 deep breaths. Do a cool-down. Come back later. Your mental state is crucial.
5.  **Celebrate Micro-Wins:** "Got all three up once!" "Had a cleaner throw!" "Felt the rhythm better!" Acknowledge these.
6.  **Consistency Over Intensity:** Practicing 5 times a week for 20 mins is far more effective than 2 hours once a month.
7.  **Use Music:** Put on a beat-driven song during beanbag sessions to help internalize the rhythm.
8.  **Be Patient:** Transitioning from 2 to 3 takes time and repetition. It might take weeks. Trust the process.

**Remember:** The scarves are your best friend for building that internal rhythm and reducing the frantic feeling of the beanbags. The rest days are essential to prevent burnout and manage stress. You *can* do this, and you're setting an amazing example for your son by learning something new and overcoming challenges! Good luck!
