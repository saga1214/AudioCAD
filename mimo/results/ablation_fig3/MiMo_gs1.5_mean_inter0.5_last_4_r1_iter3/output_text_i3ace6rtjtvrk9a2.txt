The eye itself doesn’t perceive continuous motion in the way a camera does. Instead, it captures information in these very brief, rapid bursts, which are the saccades. During these saccades, your visual field is actually blurred for a fraction of a second. That’s saccadic masking in action.

However, your brain is incredibly good at processing and filling in the gaps between these visual jumps. It integrates the information from each point to create the illusion of a smooth, continuous visual experience. That’s why you don’t notice the blurring—it’s just happening so fast that your brain fills in the gaps seamlessly.

So, while the eye’s actual capture of visual information isn’t continuous, the brain’s processing makes it feel like you’re seeing a continuous, fluid world. It’s a bit like how a film camera takes discrete frames but the brain blends them to create the illusion of motion.
