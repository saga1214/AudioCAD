To attach something to your jacket lining without damaging it, here are the safest methods:

### Best Options:
1. **Double-Sided Fabric Tape (Reversible & Low Damage):**
   - *How:* Clean the fabric surface, peel backing, press firmly for 30 seconds.
   - *Why:* No residue, removable if needed. Ideal for small patches or tags.

2. **Fabric Glue (Permanent but Test First):**
   - *How:* Apply a thin line of glue (e.g., Aleene’s Fabric Fusion), press patch for 5 minutes.
   - *Tip:* Test on an inner seam first—some glues warp synthetic fabrics.

3. **Velcro (Removable & Adjustable):**
   - *How:* Use soft-loop Velcro (e.g., 1" x 1") pressed between lining and shell.
   - *Bonus:* Reusable and won’t peel if used sparingly.

### Methods to Avoid:
- **Iron-On Patches:** Heat melts synthetic linings.
- **Strong Adhesives (E6000, etc.):** Can seep into seams and degrade materials over time.

### Pro Tips:
- **Test First:** Try on a hidden seam or hemline.
- **Less is More:** Use small patches or clips instead of large adhesive.
- **Work Fast:** Press firmly for 10-30 seconds to ensure adhesion without shifting.

**Example:** For a small tag, use double-sided tape pressed between the shell and lining. For a patch, glue Velcro to the shell and attach the patch to the lining—this minimizes stress on the fabric. Always prioritize reversible options for future removal! 🧵
