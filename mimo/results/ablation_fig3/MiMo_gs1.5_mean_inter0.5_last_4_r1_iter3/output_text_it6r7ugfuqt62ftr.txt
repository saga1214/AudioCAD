Okay, based on your request for a warm-weather California vacation for four to five days, I'd really recommend the Big Sur coast. It's perfect for swimming and has a more secluded, dramatic feel compared to the beaches in Northern California.

Here’s a sample itinerary for you. Let's say you're traveling from San Francisco, and your budget is flexible.

For Day One, you could drive to Big Sur, which is about a three-and-a-half-hour trip. You can stop at Pigeon Point Lighthouse and then check into a nice spot like Nepenthe Resort for dinner. For places to eat along the way, you could stop at the Point Lobos Lodge for lunch.

On Day Two, you could do a full day of exploring Big Sur. You could swim at Natural Bridges Beach, which is a great spot for a family. For lunch, there's a lovely spot called the Big Sur Lodge. In the afternoon, you could hike to McWay Falls, and then have a picnic at the Pfeiffer Beach with the famous purple sand. For dinner, Nepenthe is a great choice again.

For Day Three, you could drive up to the Monterey Peninsula, which is about an hour and a half away. You could visit the Monterey Bay Aquarium, which is a must-see. For lunch, you could try a place called Fisherman's Grotto. In the afternoon, you could explore Cannery Row and even go on a whale watching tour. For dinner, I'd recommend The Sardine Factory for a special seafood meal.

On Day Four, you could take a day trip to Carmel-by-the-Sea. It's a charming little town with great shopping and art galleries. You could have lunch at a place called The White Horse, which has a great patio. In the afternoon, you could walk around the village and visit the fairgrounds to see the famous free-form boulders. For dinner, you could go to Carmel Valley Ranch, which is known for its beautiful setting and accommodating food.

For Day Five, which would be your last day, you could drive back towards San Francisco. You could stop at the Santa Cruz Beach and Boardwalk for a final swim. There's a great place called the Cowell's Beach Seafood Shack for lunch. In the afternoon, you could visit the Santa Cruz Wharf for some last-minute souvenirs. For a final dinner, you could go to the Michelin-starred restaurant in Santa Cruz called The Spinnaker.

For accommodations, besides Nepenthe Resort in Big Sur, you could also look into the Big Sur Lodge or the Ventana Big Sur. If you prefer something more like a hotel, the Carmel Valley Ranch is fantastic.

For your travel needs, I'd recommend renting a large SUV or an SUV with a roof rack to carry all your gear. For packing, bring swimsuits, reef-safe sunscreen, hats, sunglasses, and some light layers. It's also a good idea to pack a small first-aid kit and any necessary gluten-free snacks.

And just a couple of final tips: It's a good idea to book your accommodations and popular restaurants in advance, especially during the holiday season. You can also get a California Adventure Pass to save on some of the attractions. And if you're up for a longer drive, you could even consider adding a trip to Joshua Tree National Park, which is about a four-hour drive from Big Sur. 

I hope this helps you plan an amazing trip!
