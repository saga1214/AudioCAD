Okay, let me clarify the Boulevardier confusion for you. The one and a quarter to one to one ratio is the original, classic recipe. Your friend's equal-parts version is a popular and valid variation, but not the standard. So, if you want to stick to the classic, use one and a quarter ounces of whiskey, one ounce of vermouth, and one ounce of Campari. 

Since you asked about the Last Word, that's a completely different drink. It's equal parts gin, green Chartreuse, maraschino liqueur, and fresh lime juice, each at three-quarters of an ounce. It's more herbal and tart than the Boulevardier. 

So, to recap: Boulevardier is classic with whiskey dominant, while Last Word is herbal and tart. You can't really mix them. Just pick one! 

Oh, and a quick tip: if you're making a Boulevardier, use fresh citrus in your vermouth. It's a game-changer. For the Last Word, make sure to squeeze the lime juice fresh. It's important. Enjoy your party! 

By the way, if you're still unsure about the Boulevardier, just remember: whiskey is the star. If you're making the Last Word, the Chartreuse and maraschino are key. Hope that helps clear things up!
