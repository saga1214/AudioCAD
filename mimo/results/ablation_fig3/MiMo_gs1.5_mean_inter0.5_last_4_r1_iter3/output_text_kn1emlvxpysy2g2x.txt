Okay, let's review everything we've discussed. For high-end Pinot Noir, you store it at cellar temperature, which is about 55 degrees Fahrenheit, and serve it slightly below room temp, around 55 to 60 degrees. For Japanese Hon-Mirin, you store it cool, at about 60 to 68 degrees Fahrenheit, and if you're sipping it, room temp is fine, which is about 68 to 75 degrees.

For Italian wines, we looked at a Chianti, which is similar to the Pinot Noir: store at 55 Fahrenheit, serve at 60 to 65. And for a Pinot Grigio, you store it cooler, at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, and serve it chilled, around 45 to 50.

For the best wine in hot weather, I'd recommend the Pinot Grigio or Sauvignon Blanc. Both are served chilled, around 45 to 50 degrees, which helps keep things refreshing. For cold weather, the Pinot Noir or Chianti are great choices, served slightly below room temp, between 55 and 60 degrees. The Hon-Mirin is more versatile, as it can be served at room temp, which works well in moderate weather.

So to quickly compare them all: Pinot Noir is for cool weather, served a little cool. Hon-Mirin is versatile, served at room temp. Pinot Grigio and Sauvignon Blanc are for hot weather, served very cold. And the Chianti is similar to the Pinot Noir. That should give you a good idea of what to serve when.
