Dealing with a difficult person—especially one causing stress and disruption in your community—can feel overwhelming. Here’s practical advice to help you navigate this situation:

### 1. **Prioritize Your Well-being & Boundaries**
   - **Limit Exposure**: Avoid direct confrontation if possible. If you must interact, keep it brief, neutral, and factual.
   - **Protect Your Kids**: Reinforce rules about safety (e.g., "Don’t go near her property"). Monitor interactions if she approaches children.
   - **Vent Strategically**: Share with trusted friends/family or a therapist to release stress. Avoid gossip, which can escalate tension.

### 2. **Document Everything**
   - **Keep Records**: Log dates, times, and details of incidents (e.g., "Called CPS on [date] for [reason]"). Save texts, voicemails, or photos if applicable.
   - **Witnesses**: Note if others saw the behavior. This could support community action later.

### 3. **Address Systematically**
   - **School/Trusted Adults**: Since CPS is involved, communicate with school staff or a counselor. They can advocate for the children and provide support.
   - **Community Engagement**: If the neighbor’s actions target others, consider discreetly gathering similar documentation from affected neighbors. A unified approach may encourage action.
   - **Legal Recourse**: If threats, harassment, or safety concerns arise, consult a lawyer. Retaliation or intimidation could have legal consequences.

### 4. **Safety First**
   - **CPS Follow-Up**: If your child is involved, contact CPS directly to understand the process and ensure their safety.
   - **Home Security**: Consider cameras, lights, or security systems to deter unwanted attention.
   - **Know Your Rights**: Research local laws on harassment, nuisance calls, or false reports.

### 5. **Focus on Positivity**
   - **Embrace the Fun**: Use school events and Halloween as anchors—these moments remind you of what matters most.
   - **Music as Therapy**: Lean into Forrest Frank’s vibe! "Lemonade" is a great pick-me-up—its upbeat energy can counter stress.

### 6. **When to Escalate**
   - If threats persist, contact local authorities or a community mediation service. In extreme cases, legal action may be necessary.

**Remember**: You’re not alone—many neighborhoods face similar challenges. Prioritize your family’s peace while taking steady, measured steps. If the stress feels unmanageable, speaking to a counselor or support group can provide clarity and strength. 💙
