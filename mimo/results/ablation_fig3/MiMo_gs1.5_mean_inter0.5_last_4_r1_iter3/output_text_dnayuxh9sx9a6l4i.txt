### 🔍 Key Considerations for Your Search
- **Rescue Focus**: Look for shelters/shelters with dogs labeled "family-friendly," "low prey drive," or "calm." Ask about breed mix (if unknown) and past behavior.  
- **Temperament Testing**: Visit rescues/shelters to observe how the dog interacts with kids (if you have them) or other dogs.  
- **Breed-Specific Traits**:  
  - **Low Prey Drive**: Avoid terriers, beagles, and sighthounds.  
  - **Manageable Alone Time**: French Bulldogs, Bassets, and Cavaliers adapt better than high-energy breeds.  
- **Training**: All breeds listed are trainable, but Poodle mixes (Cockapoo/Goldendoodle) and Labradors excel. Use positive reinforcement methods.  
- **Shedding**: Poodle mixes, Bulldogs, and Cavaliers shed minimally.  

### 🏠 Apartment-Friendly Tips
- **Exercise**: Daily walks + indoor play (toys, puzzle feeders) to prevent boredom.  
- **Barking**: Train "quiet" commands early. Use white noise machines to mask outdoor sounds.  
- **Crate Training**: Essential for overnight alone time (supervised initially).  

### Next Steps
1. **Identify Your "Dealbreakers"**: Max size, noise tolerance, shedding, or energy level.  
2. **Contact Rescues**: Reach out to local shelters/purebred clubs. Ask:  
   - *"Do you have adult dogs under 20 lbs that are calm, dog-friendly, and low-maintenance?"*  
   - *"Can we meet a few candidates for temperament testing?"*  
3. **Trial Period**: If possible, foster a dog for a week to test compatibility.  

**Final Tip**: Prioritize adult dogs (1-5 years) from rescues—many are already well-socialized, less destructive, and more predictable than puppies. 🐶❤️
