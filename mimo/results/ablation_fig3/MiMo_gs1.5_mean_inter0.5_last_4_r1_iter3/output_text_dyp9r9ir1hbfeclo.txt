You've hit on a key point! Let's clarify the components involved in shoulder rotation:

1.  **The Three Bones (Articulating Joints):**
    *   **Humerus (Upper Arm Bone):** Head articulates with the glenoid cavity of the scapula.
    *   **Scapula (Shoulder Blade):** Glenoid cavity (shallow socket) articulates with the humeral head.
    *   **Clavicle (Collarbone):** Forms the sternoclavicular joint with the sternum and the acromioclavicular joint with the acromion of the scapula, connecting the arm to the axial skeleton.

2.  **The Fourth Component: The Rotator Cuff Muscles**
    *   While the humerus, scapula, and clavicle form the *bony framework* for movement, the **four muscles of the rotator cuff** are the critical *soft tissue components* enabling stable and controlled rotation.
    *   **Function:** They surround the shoulder joint, pulling on the humeral head to:
        *   **Stabilize:** Keep the humeral head centered in the shallow glenoid socket during movement.
        *   **Guide Rotation:** Act as a "pulley" system, controlling the humeral head's motion during various arm movements (especially abduction, rotation, and overhead motions).
        *   **Enable Rotation:** Provide the muscle power for internal and external rotation movements.

**In Summary:**

*   **Bones:** Humerus, Scapula, Clavicle form the *skeletal structure* for rotation.
*   **Muscles:** The four rotator cuff muscles (Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres Minor, Subscapularis) provide the *stability and power* for controlled rotation.

The rotator cuff muscles are absolutely essential for functional rotation, preventing the humeral head from dislocating and allowing the bones to move smoothly. Dysfunction or injury to these muscles is a common cause of shoulder pain and instability, like your friend might be experiencing.
