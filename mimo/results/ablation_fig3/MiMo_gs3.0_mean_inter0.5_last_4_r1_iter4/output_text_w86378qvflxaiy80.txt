That's a really complex question, and it's true, the most important aspect really depends on who you ask. Norman Myers himself probably argued it's about scientific knowledge and understanding the web of life. Conservation groups like the WWF might say the most important thing is simply preventing species from going extinct. But if you look at Norman Myers' later work, he actually shifted his focus.

He started to argue that the most critical aspect is the livelihoods of the people who live in these areas. He believed that empowering local communities to take charge of their own conservation efforts was the key to long-term success.
