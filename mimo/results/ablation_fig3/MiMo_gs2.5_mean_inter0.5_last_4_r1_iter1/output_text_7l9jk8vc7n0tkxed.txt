I'm really glad the music and lunch helped you reset! That's the right move. Dealing with a deep blockage with just a simple toolbox is tough, but here's a step-by-step approach that focuses on tools you likely have:

**Step 1: Inspect Visually**  
- Remove the drain cover/stopper.  
- Look down the pipe—see if you can spot debris (e.g., hair, food, leaves).  
- If you see it, use a pipe cleaner or your hands to pull out visible chunks.  

**Step 2: Use a Plunger Again (But Better)**  
- Ensure water is deep enough to create a seal.  
- Try a **double-plunge technique**: Plunge firmly, then lift the plunger slightly to break up the blockage before plunging again.  

**Step 3: Check the P-Trap**  
- The U-shaped pipe under the sink is often clogged.  
- Remove the P-trap (it’s held by slip nuts—usually 3/8" or 1/2" wrench size).  
- Empty its water, then use a **pipe snake** (if you have one) or your hands to clear debris.  
- Reattach the P-trap securely.  

**Step 4: Manual Clearing (If Tools Allow)**  
- If you have a **hose end cleaner** (like a simple auger), use that.  
- **No auger?** Try a **pipe snake**—or even a sturdy coat hanger straightened and fed down the drain.  
- Wrap wire around the hook to grab debris.  

**Step 5: Hot Water + Baking Soda/Vinegar**  
- Pour **hot (not boiling) water** down the drain to soften clogs.  
- For grease/oil blockages:  
  - Pour **1 cup baking soda**, then **1 cup vinegar** down the drain.  
  - Cover the drain to contain fizzing, wait 15 mins, then flush with hot water.  

**Step 6: Call for Backup (If Needed)**  
- If it’s a **severe blockage** (e.g., tree roots, solid waste):  
  - Use a **hand auger** (if available) for deeper roots.  
  - If no tools work, it’s okay to call a pro—limited tools shouldn’t mean limited pride in solving problems.

Set a timer for 10 minutes of focused work, then pause to stretch or breathe. This prevents burnout. You’ve fixed tough stuff before—you’ve got this! 🛠️
