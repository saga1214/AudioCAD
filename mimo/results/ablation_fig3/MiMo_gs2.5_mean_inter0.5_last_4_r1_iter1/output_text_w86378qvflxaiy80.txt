That's a great question, and the answer really depends on who you ask and what their priorities are. For Norman Myers, the term's inventor, the focus was on the irreplaceable nature of unique species. For Conservation International, it's the tangible ecosystem services they provide to people. For others, it's the sheer beauty and intrinsic value of nature itself.

However, if you have to pick the single most important aspect, it's probably the irreplaceable loss of unique species. Because once a species goes extinct, it's gone forever. You can't get it back. It represents a unique piece of the planet's history and evolutionary story that is erased. While ecosystem services and medicine are critically important, a species that is already gone can't be studied or used for anything. That permanent, irreversible loss is the core tragedy of a hotspot disappearing.
