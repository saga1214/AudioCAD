First, get permission. Double-check with the facility beforehand. Even though it’s assisted living, you still need to make sure they’re cool with you recording. 

Second, gear. You don’t need fancy stuff. A tripod helps. Your phone’s built-in camera is fine, but you can also use a lavalier mic if you have one, or even a cheap clip-on mic. Just make sure the lighting’s decent, and maybe do a quick test run before you start. 

Third, structure. Keep it short. Five to ten minutes is perfect. Focus on your best characters—Melvin’s hilarious failures, Sloppy Snake’s charm. Maybe even add a little call-to-action at the end, like, “Hey kids, like this show? Check out my YouTube!” 

Fourth, editing. If you can, cut out any awkward pauses or mistakes. You don’t need to be a pro—just make it look polished. 

Fifth, post it. Upload to YouTube or TikTok. Tag it as kid-friendly. Share the link with friends or even that influencer family who’s been supportive. 

And last, have fun. This is volunteer work, remember? Don’t stress too much. Just perform for the kids and let the camera do the rest.
