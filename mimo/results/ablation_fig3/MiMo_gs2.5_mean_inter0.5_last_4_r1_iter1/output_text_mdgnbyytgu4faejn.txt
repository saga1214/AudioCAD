Okay, I can definitely help you with a complete, step-by-step format for your resume. Here's a breakdown of the standard structure that's both professional and easy to fill out.

First, at the very top, you'll have your name in about a fourteen-point font. To its right, in a nine-point font, you'll list your contact info in this order: email, phone number, city. Icons should be avoided to prevent issues with tracking software. It's also highly recommended to include a link to your LinkedIn profile here. Below that contact section, place a short two-to-three sentence professional summary that highlights your most relevant experience and career goals.

Next is the Education section. Here you'll list your highest degree first, followed by your college name and graduation year. You can add details like your honors or any scholarships you received right under that degree entry.

After that, you'll have your Professional Experience. The best practice is to list each job separately, even if the role was similar across different companies. For each position, include the company name, your job title, the dates you worked there, and then a couple of key bullet points highlighting your main accomplishments.

Then, you'll have a Skills section. This is very important. List your most relevant technical and soft skills. It's a good idea to organize them into categories, like technical skills, soft skills, and languages.

You can also add an optional section for Awards and Certifications, where you list any notable achievements or professional certifications.

Finally, at the very bottom, you can add a line for your additional work history, which is perfect for listing roles that are less relevant but still good to show a complete timeline.

This structure is clean, traditional, and covers all the key information recruiters are looking for.
