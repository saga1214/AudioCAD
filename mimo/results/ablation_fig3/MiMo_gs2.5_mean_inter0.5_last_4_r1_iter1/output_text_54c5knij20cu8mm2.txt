Okay, let’s break this down.

First, the left-brain versus right-brain idea. It’s mostly a myth. The brain doesn’t really split functions that neatly. Both hemispheres work together, and creativity and logic happen in overlapping networks. But the idea is useful as a metaphor. For example, the left side often handles language and logic, while the right handles spatial and musical skills. So, it’s not a myth in the sense that it’s completely wrong, but it’s over-simplified.

Now, for your career. It sounds like you want something with structure, but also creativity and a measurable impact. Here are some ideas:

One is architecture. It’s very structured, with math and physics, but also creative. It’s about building things that last. 

Another is data visualization. It’s creative, like painting with data, but also very objective and based on analysis. 

There’s also scientific illustration or technical writing. It’s about taking complex information and making it beautiful and clear. 

And finally, product design. It’s about creating things that solve real problems, so it’s creative and measurable. 

So, in short, architecture, data visualization, scientific illustration, or product design. All of them are structured, creative, and have lasting value.
