But in the second scenario, the Blue Team scores after controlling the rebound of a corner kick. This situation highlights a key difference in how the Laws are interpreted between the International Laws and some versions of the U.S. Laws.

According to the International Laws, a defender's deliberate touch resets offside. So if the Blue defender made a controlled play on the ball, scoring after that would not be an offense.

However, in some U.S. Laws interpretations, a reset only applies if the defender intentionally plays the ball forward. If they just deflect it, it's still an offense. This means that in the second scenario I described, a referee certified under U.S. Laws might call an offside offense, whereas an official following the International Laws might not.
