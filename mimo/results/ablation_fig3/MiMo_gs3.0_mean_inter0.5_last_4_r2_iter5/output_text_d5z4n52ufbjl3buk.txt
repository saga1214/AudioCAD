That's a great goal—to be clear and respectful. You can strike that balance with a carefully worded email.

Here are a few pointers for what you could include. Start with a subject line like, "Meeting Request to Improve for Future Exams."

In the body, you could say something like:
"I scored a 64 on the recent essay-style exam and wanted to schedule some time to discuss what I could improve on for the next test. My other grades have been excellent in your class, and I'm committed to doing better."

Then, you could add, "Subjectively understanding where I fell short based on the content is a bit difficult for me. Could you share the key areas you focused on when grading?"

You can also mention scheduling by writing, "Your office hours seem difficult to attend, but I'm happy to meet at any time that works for you."

And if you feel comfortable, you could add a brief, non-emotional reason for your confidence concern, something like, "As a student concerned about scholarship applications, constructive feedback would be very valuable to me."

Finally, close with, "Thank you for your time and guidance."

This approach frames the whole thing as a learning opportunity, which should lower the chance of him getting egotistical about it.
