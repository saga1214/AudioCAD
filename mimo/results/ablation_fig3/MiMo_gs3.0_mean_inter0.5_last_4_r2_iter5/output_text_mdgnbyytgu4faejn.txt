Of course, I can give you a straightforward, traditional format that recruiters almost always prefer. Just picture it like this:

First, center your last name at the top. To the right, aligned with it, is your 14-point name. Just below that, on smaller 9-point text, is a clear heading like "Contact Information". Under that heading, list your email on its own line, then your phone number on the next, and finally your city on the line after that. Adding icons is optional and as we discussed, might cause automated issues. And like I suggested, remove them for maximum compatibility.

Right below that contact section, create a short "Professional Summary". This is a powerful paragraph that briefly highlights your most relevant skills and career goals.

After the summary, comes the main "Work Experience" section. Here, you'll list each employer separately, with your job title, company name, and the start and end dates for that specific role.

Next comes your "Education" section. List your degrees from most recent to oldest, including the school name and your honors or scholarship right there under the corresponding degree.

Then, you have a concise "Skills" section. List out your most relevant skills.

Finally, you can have an optional "Additional Information" section. This is where you can put that consolidated timeline for experience you asked about, awards separate from academics, or even your LinkedIn profile link. This part can be kept very brief.

This order is clean, logical, and makes it super easy for a human and an automated system to find what they need.
