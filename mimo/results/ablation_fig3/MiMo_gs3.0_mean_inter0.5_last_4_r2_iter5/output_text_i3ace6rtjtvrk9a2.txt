Now, the core question: With saccadic masking in mind, can we say the human eye sees continuously?

The short answer is no. The eye does not see continuously in the way we normally perceive things. It operates in discrete sampling moments. 

Here's the breakdown using proper terminology:

1.  **Eye as Biological Sensor (Sampling):**
    *   Photoreceptors (rods for low light/dark motion, cones for color/细节) respond primarily to *changes* in light intensity and luminance contrast.
    *   Signals travel via the optic nerve.
    *   While capable of rapid responses (potentially up to flicker fusions threshold ~60Hz+), vision from the eye is fundamentally based on discrete moments of sampling during fixation and rapid jumps (saccades).

2.  **Saccadic Masking (Brain Editing):**
    *   During a saccade, the brain actively suppresses the visual information it receives *during* the eye movement itself. This creates a brief period of "visual silence" or masking.
    *   Your conscious visual awareness is primarily tied to the brief moments *after* a saccade when the eyes arrive at a new focal point.

3.  **Brain as Processor & Interpreter:**
    *   The visual cortex takes the signals from the eye.
    *   It performs critical smoothing, temporal interpolation (filling gaps between saccadic points), and spatial interpolation (filling in details during saccades).
    *   It integrates motion and stabilizes perception, making the visual world appear continuous and stable despite the eye's sampling pattern.

**Conclusion on Continuous Seeing:**

*   The **eye** does **not** see continuously. It samples visual information in discrete moments, primarily during fixation and between saccades. Saccadic masking proves that the eye's visual input during rapid movement is actively suppressed.
*   The **brain** processes this information *as if* it were continuous. Through temporal and spatial interpolation, it constructs a seamless, stable perception of the visual world from the eye's discrete samples.
*   Therefore, your **conscious perception** of continuous vision is a product of **brain processing** acting on the **eye's discrete sampling**.

**Analogy:** Think of the eye as a camera with a mechanical shutter that only captures an image during a brief exposure (sampling moment). The brain is like sophisticated software that takes those few images, stitches them together, and adds motion blur and stabilization to create a smooth video stream. The camera (eye) captures discrete frames; the software (brain) creates the continuous video feed (perception).

So, saccadic masking is key evidence: it shows the eye *does not* provide continuous data during saccades, and the brain must actively mask blur and interpolate to create the continuous perception we experience. The eye senses discretely; the brain processes continuously (for perception).
