Based on everything we've discussed, it seems there isn't just one single "most important" reason. The real strength of the concept is how all the criteria and motivations are interconnected.

Norman Myers designed the term this way on purpose. Meeting both conditions—the high unique plant diversity and the 70% habitat loss—is what makes a hotspot so urgent. And the value extends far beyond just scientific interest.

The most compelling argument is probably that combination you found from Conservation International: hotspots occupy a tiny fraction of the Earth's geography but deliver a massive share of the ecosystem services vulnerable communities depend on. Protecting them is really about protecting fundamental human well-being on a global scale.
