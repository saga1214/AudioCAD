   At -10°C, ice becomes more rigid. The surface premelting (a thin quasi-liquid layer) that lubricates gliding at warmer temperatures (e.g., -5°C) diminishes or disappears. Without this layer, the blade "grips" the ice more, increasing friction.

2. **Pressure Effects**:  
   Skate blades exert immense pressure during pushes. At -10°C, the lack of a water layer can cause localized melting under pressure, but this is less efficient than at -5°C. The blade may not "hydroplane" smoothly, leading to higher resistance.

3. **Surface Roughness**:  
   Extremely cold ice can develop micro-cracks or uneven surfaces due to thermal stress, increasing frictional contact points.

- **Typical Range**: 0.01–0.03 (vs. 0.005–0.015 at -5°C).
