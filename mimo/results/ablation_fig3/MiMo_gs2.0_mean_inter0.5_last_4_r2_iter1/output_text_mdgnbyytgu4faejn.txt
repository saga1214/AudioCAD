Of course, I can give you a clear breakdown of the standard resume format. Just follow these sections and you'll be all set.

First, at the very top, is your contact information. It should be a single line with your name in a 14-point font. Below that, on the next line, put your email, then your phone number, and finally your city. It's best to remove icons to ensure compatibility with automated tracking systems.

Next, you'll have a professional summary. This is a short paragraph, about three to five lines, that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals. This is a great way to address any gaps in your work history.

Then comes your experience section. This should be split into two parts. Your most relevant experience goes into a "Relevant Experience" section, where you list each job with the company name, your title, and the dates. For the less relevant work, you can use an "Additional Work History" section for the same thing. For any experience跨越 different employers, list each one separately.

After that is your education section. Here, you'll list each degree you earned, the school it's from, and any awards you received with that degree, like Highest Honors or specific scholarships.

Then, you'll have a skills section. This is very important. List out your key skills, focusing only on those that are relevant to the jobs you're applying for. You can even group them into categories like Technical Skills or Soft Skills if that helps.

If you have them, you can also add an optional certifications section, and a volunteer work section if it's relevant to your field.

And that's pretty much the whole structure. Following this format will give you a professional, clean, and effective resume.
