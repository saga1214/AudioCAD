You know, I've been thinking. While Ancient Egypt scores slightly higher in our little exercise, I've had a change of heart. I'd actually recommend Mesopotamia.

Here's why: an Egypt exhibit is great, but it's a common choice and often just focuses on burial customs. A Mesopotamian exhibit, however, tells the foundational story of human progress—the invention of writing, the first cities, and the birth of law. That's a more profound and universal narrative.

Artifacts like cuneiform tablets aren't just objects; they are the world's first historical records. Their availability has actually improved with modern archaeological discoveries, too. You could create a really dynamic exhibit with things like a reconstructed ziggurat, a replica of a school for scribes, and even a timeline showing the spread of writing.

So, my final answer is Mesopotamia. It offers a deeper educational impact and a truly unique cultural perspective that would be incredibly valuable for a museum.
