Of course, I can summarize everything we've discussed. Here are the temperature guidelines for each wine.

First, the high-end Pinot Noir: you store it at cellar temp, around 55 degrees Fahrenheit, and serve it slightly below room temp, also around 55 to 60 Fahrenheit.

Next, Japanese Hon-Mirin: you store it cool, at about 60 to 68 degrees Fahrenheit, and if you sip it, room temp is best, around 68 to 75 Fahrenheit.

For Italian Chianti: you store it around 55 Fahrenheit and serve it between 60 and 65.

The Pinot Grigio: you store it cool, at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, and serve it chilled, around 45 to 50.

For mulled red wine: you store the wine normally at 55 Fahrenheit first, then serve it hot, warming it up to between 140 and 160 degrees Fahrenheit.

So, to compare them: for hot weather, the Pinot Grigio is the best choice because it’s light and crisp. For cold weather, the Pinot Noir is ideal because its structure is great for cooler temperatures. Hon-Mirin is a bit more neutral, so it's a good all-rounder. Hope that helps!
