Ironing winter jackets without damaging the lining requires careful technique. Here's a step-by-step guide:

1. **Check the Care Label**:  
   Look for symbols like "Iron" (with a dot under it) or "No Iron." If it says "Dry Clean Only," skip ironing.

2. **Prepare Your Workspace**:  
   - Use a **pressing cloth** (thin cotton or muslin) over the jacket. This shields the lining from direct heat.  
   - Set your iron to the **lowest heat setting** (synthetic or wool setting). Avoid steam unless the care label allows it.  
   - Unfasten all zippers and buttons to prevent snagging.

3. **Ironing Technique**:  
   - Place the jacket **inside out** on a flat, smooth surface.  
   - Use **short, quick strokes** (don’t glide the iron back and forth).  
   - For wrinkles on the outside, press lightly. For inside wrinkles, lift the jacket slightly and press down gently.  
   - If using steam, hold the iron 1–2 inches above the fabric and use brief bursts.

4. **For Stiff Linings (e.g., fleece or thermal fabric)**:  
   - Use a **fabric shaver** or a **dryer on low heat** (with a towel barrier) for 10–15 seconds to relax wrinkles without melting.

5. **Avoid Common Mistakes**:  
   - Never iron **waterproof or treated linings** (like Gore-Tex) – heat damages the coating.  
   - Don’t iron while the jacket is warm or damp.  
   - For heavy-duty wrinkles, let the jacket hang in a steamy bathroom for 15–20 minutes first.

**Alternative Solutions**:  
- **Steam Carefully**: Hang the jacket and use a handheld steamer 6–8 inches away, moving quickly.  
- **Dry Cleaning**: If unsure, take it to a reputable dry cleaner and ask for "light pressing."

**Pro Tip**: If the lining is already frayed or damaged, consider replacing it yourself with a fabric glue (like E6000) or visit a tailor for a professional fix.

Prioritize safety over ironing – if in doubt, leave it unpressed!
