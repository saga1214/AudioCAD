Before hitting the road, a thorough pre-trip inspection is crucial—especially for a diesel camper van. Here's how to check brakes and spark plugs, plus other key maintenance:

### 🔧 **Brake Inspection**
1. **Visual Check**:  
   - Look for worn brake pads/shoes (thin material or metal showing).  
   - Check for unevenly worn rotors or drums (grinding noise = wear).  
   - Inspect brake fluid level (should be between "MIN" and "MAX" on the reservoir).  
   - Look for leaks (dark fluid under the van).  

2. **Signs of Wear**:  
   - Squealing/grinding noises during braking.  
   - Soft, pulsating, or inconsistent pedal feel.  
   - Reduced stopping power.  

3. **Action**:  
   - Replace pads/shoes if worn.  
   - Have rotors/drums resurfaced or replaced if necessary.  

### ⚙️ **Spark Plug Replacement (Diesel-Specific)**  
Diesel engines use robust plugs (e.g., Bosch or Motorcraft), but here’s how to replace them:  
1. **Tools Needed**:  
   - Spark plug socket (13/16" or 19mm).  
   - Torque wrench.  
   - Gap tool.  
   - New plugs (check manual for correct type).  

2. **Steps**:  
   - Disconnect the battery.  
   - Remove the coil pack (or distributor cap, depending on van).  
   - Unscrew the old plug (don’t yank—use penetrating oil if stuck).  
   - Check plug gap (should match manual, typically 0.030–0.034").  
   - Tighten new plug to spec (usually 25–30 ft-lbs for diesel).  
   - Reassemble coils/distributor.  

3. **Tip**:  
   - Clean the area with compressed air.  
   - Use anti-seize compound on threads (prevents future corrosion).  

### 🛠️ **Other Critical Checks**  
- **Tires**: Inspect tread depth (use the penny test) and pressure.  
- **Fluids**: Oil, coolant, and transmission fluid levels.  
- **Diesel-Specific**:  
  - Check the **diesel particulate filter (DPF)** for clogging (cold starts may be needed).  
  - Ensure the **glow plugs** are functional in colder climates (test with a multimeter).  
- **Camper Systems**:  
  - Test water pump, fridge, and solar power.  
  - Check gas/propane tanks for leaks.  

### 🚨 **When in Doubt, Consult a Pro**  
Diesel engines are tough but complex. If you’re unsure about brakes, spark plugs, or unfamiliar noises, visit a trusted mechanic before departure.
