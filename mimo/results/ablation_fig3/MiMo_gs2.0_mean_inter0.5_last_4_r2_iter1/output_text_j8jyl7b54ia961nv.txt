Yes, giving public acceptance extra weight definitely changes the calculus. The sugar campaign scores lower overall, around 27. But the vaccination campaign, now heavily penalized by its polarized acceptance score, ends up with a slightly higher total of 31. Public resistance really does make it the tougher campaign to execute.

But beyond the numbers, there are other things to consider. A vaccination campaign carries immense ethical risks. Any negative outcome could have severe political consequences for the department. A sugar reduction campaign, while also ethically tricky, is framed differently as encouraging wellness rather than enforcing it. So, even if the vaccination campaign might be the medically wiser choice, the national health department must also weigh the political and ethical risks against the public health benefits. It's a very complex decision.
