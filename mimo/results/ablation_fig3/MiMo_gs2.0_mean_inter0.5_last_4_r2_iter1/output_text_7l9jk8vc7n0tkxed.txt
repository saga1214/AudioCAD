That's completely understandable. Deep clogs are tough, but you can definitely tackle this with patience and the right approach. Here's a step-by-step plan focusing on tools likely in your simple toolbox:

1.  **Confirm & Prepare:** Double-check it's a deep clog (water doesn't drain at all after plunging). Place a bucket strategically to catch any water that might come out if you dislodge some gunk. Have a flashlight ready.

2.  **Clear the Trap (P-Trap):** Get a bucket, a rag, and your hands. **Locate the P-trap** (the U-shaped pipe under the sink or tub). Carefully remove the slip nuts (usually two) and the plastic/tri-metal trap. **Scoop out all the debris** from inside the trap with your hands, a rag, or pipe cleaners. Rinse the trap and nuts in the sink. Reattach the trap securely. *This is often the biggest source of clogs.*

3.  **Check Visible Sections:** If the clog is further down, use a **flashlight** to inspect the visible drain pipe (like behind a toilet or under a sink). Look for any visible blockages or debris you can manually remove with a rag or small tool.

4.  **Use a Drain Snake (Auger):** This is your best bet for a simple toolbox. Grab your **drain snake** (manual or spring-type). Feed the tip slowly and carefully down the drain. Twist the handle gently as you go. When you feel resistance (the snake head hitting the clog), **stop twisting and gently pull back**. Keep doing this, pulling back slowly and twisting occasionally, until you either clear the clog or the snake head comes out. *Be patient and don't force it.* You might need to do this several times.

5.  **Try a Manual Auger (if available):** If you have a manual auger (the kind with the long cable and handle), it's even better for deep clogs. Feed it down slowly, feeling for the clog. Once you hit it, use the handle to **twist and push** the auger head through the clog. You might need to pull it back a bit to clear the debris around the head, then push again. It requires more force but is very effective.

6.  **Last Resort - Chemical Drain Cleaner (Use with Caution):** If everything else fails and your toolbox *does* have a basic chemical drain cleaner:
    *   **READ THE LABEL CAREFULLY.** Follow instructions precisely.
    *   Pour a small amount down the drain.
    *   Let it sit for the recommended time (usually 15-30 mins).
    *   **Flush thoroughly** with a large amount of hot water. *This is a last resort due to potential pipe damage and environmental concerns.*
