That's a really profound question. It's difficult to pick just one aspect, as they are all interconnected.

However, if I had to choose, I would say the most critical aspect is preserving the irreplaceable uniqueness of life on Earth and maintaining the foundation of our planet's ecological security.

Think of it this way: unique species are like a library of genetic information and potential solutions to future problems, like new medicines or climate-resilient crops. Their extinction is irreversible. At the same time, by protecting this unique genetic library, we are also safeguarding the ecosystems that provide essential services like clean water and pollination for the billions of people who depend on them. So, it's really about preserving both the incredible variety of life and the life-support systems that allow all of us to survive.
