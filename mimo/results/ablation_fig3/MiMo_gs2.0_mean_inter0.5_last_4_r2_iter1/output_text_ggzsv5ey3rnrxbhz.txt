I understand your concern—especially with little ones at home. While there's no "detox" method to remove PFAS from your body, you *can* significantly reduce ongoing exposure. Here's a clear, actionable approach:

### Key Actions to Reduce Exposure:
1. **Switch cookware**: Replace non-stick pans with stainless steel, cast iron, or ceramic.
2. **Avoid packaging**: Skip microwave popcorn bags, fast-food wrappers, and grease-proof pizza boxes.
3. **Choose fabrics wisely**: Opt for natural fabrics (cotton, wool) over synthetic "stain-resistant" coatings.
4. **Use certified water filters**: Activated carbon filters on faucets/pitchers remove PFAS while preserving essential minerals. Avoid bottled water.
5. **Check personal care products**: Look for "PFAS-free" labels on shampoos, lotions, and sunscreens.

### Electronics & Other Concerns:
- **Electronics**: Direct exposure from cell phones or TVs is minimal. Manufacturing residues are low risk.
- **Fish**: Limit high-accumulation fish like salmon or tuna if concerned.
- **Minerals in water**: Activated carbon filters retain minerals—reverse osmosis is the only system that removes them.

### What to Avoid (Top 5 Products):
1. **Non-stick cookware** (PFOA/PFAS-coated pans)  
2. **Fast-food wrappers/packaging** (grease-resistant materials)  
3. **Waterproof/stain-resistant clothing** (carpets, furniture, outdoor gear)  
4. **Certain cosmetics/dental floss** (check ingredient lists for "PFOA/PFOS")  
5. **Untreated tap/bottled water** (use NSF-certified filters instead)  

### Important Notes:
- **PFAS vs. PFA**: You’re correct—PFAS is the accurate term (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances).  
- **Juice cleanses**: Won’t remove PFAS; focus on reducing intake.  
- **Dust/air**: Use HEPA filters and avoid synthetic stain treatments.  

Prioritize avoiding the top 5 products and using certified filters. While PFAS persist in the environment, these steps drastically lower your exposure. If anxiety persists, consult a doctor about testing levels or managing symptoms.
