After weighing the unique species, the untapped potential for medicine, and especially their critical role in providing essential "ecosystem services," I would say the single most important aspect of preserving biodiversity hotspots is their role as irreplaceable hubs for ecosystem services.

These small, fragile regions are performing thirty-five percent of the critical functions vulnerable communities around the world depend on for clean water, food pollination, and climate stability. Losing these hotspots would mean rapidly degrading the very life-support systems for hundreds of millions of people, making their protection an urgent, non-negotiable priority for global human security.
