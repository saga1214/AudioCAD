Got it. Here’s a high school versus college scenario.

Player number seven from the Red team is in an offside position as Player number ten from the Blue team takes a goal kick. Player number seven gets a touch on the ball after it bounces off a defender, resetting them from offside, and then passes it to Player number nine who scores. No whistle is blown.

In the high school rules, this is still an offside offense. Even though the defender's initial touch reset things, the player was still in an offside position when they received the goal kick. The deliberate play by the defender did reset the situation, but the offside position itself was still an offense.

Under the college rules, however, it's not an offense at all. The exception for restarts applies directly to a goal kick, throw-in, and corner kick. Therefore, in this situation, Player number seven cannot be called offside when receiving the ball directly from that goal kick.
