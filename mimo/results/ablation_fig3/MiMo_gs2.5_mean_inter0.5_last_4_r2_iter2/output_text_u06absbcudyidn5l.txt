Okay, let's break down chronic skin redness and vasculitis.

Beyond rosacea and sun damage, other common causes for long-term redness include genetics, contact dermatitis from irritants, or conditions like lupus. It's best to get a professional diagnosis to rule things out.

Vasculitis is, at its core, an autoimmune condition where your immune system mistakenly attacks your blood vessels. The inflammation can be categorized by the size of the vessel it's affecting. For example, large vessel vasculitis, like giant cell arteritis, mainly affects larger arteries and can cause joint pain and fatigue. Medium vessel vasculitis, like the one that causes GPA, can affect arteries and veins in the skin and lungs, leading to ulcers and shortness of breath. And small vessel vasculitis, like Henoch-Schönlein purpura, mainly affects tiny capillaries and can cause widespread redness and joint pain.

The symptoms you see depend entirely on where the inflammation is.
