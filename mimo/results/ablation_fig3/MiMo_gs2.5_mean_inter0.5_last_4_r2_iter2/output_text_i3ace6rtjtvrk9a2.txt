Now, the core question: Does the eye see continuously?

Not exactly. The eye senses changes in light and movement very quickly, up to around 60 hertz or more. Rods and cones react to rapid flashes of light, but perception isn't direct—it goes through the brain.

Here's the crucial difference: during saccades, the eyes make rapid jumps between focal points. Saccadic masking means the brain actively suppresses blur during these movements. So even if the eye senses a lot of motion or rapid details, the brain edits that information before you’re consciously aware of it. 

So, in essence: The eye senses, the brain interprets at a higher level.

Without this processing, you'd notice jitter or blur during fast head or eye movements. The brain essentially acts like a stabilizer, making sense of visual information so you perceive things as a continuous flow instead of discrete flashes.
