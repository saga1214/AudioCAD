If I had to choose **one top driver behind increasing obesity rates**, I’d still say it’s the **easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods**. Here’s why it remains the biggest factor—especially when comparing scenarios like yours:

### Key Reasons:
1. **Overconsumption**: Processed foods (e.g., chips, sugary snacks, fast food) are engineered to be hyper-palatable, making it easy to overeat without feeling full. Even unprocessed calorie-dense foods (e.g., nuts, oils) become risky without portion control.  
2. **Affordability & Accessibility**: These foods are often cheaper and more widely available than nutrient-dense alternatives, especially in low-income areas.  
3. **Nutrient Deficiency**: They’re typically low in protein, fiber, and vitamins, so people eat more to feel satisfied, leading to chronic overconsumption.  

### Your Scenario Breakdown:
- **Calorie-dense unprocessed + no exercise**: High risk of weight gain/obesity because total calories exceed energy expenditure.  
- **Small portions of processed foods**: Lower risk for obesity if calories stay within needs, but long-term health suffers due to nutrient deficiencies and metabolic effects (e.g., blood sugar spikes).  

### Why Calories Aren’t the *Only* Factor:
While **caloric balance** is crucial for weight gain, processed foods uniquely drive **unintentional overconsumption**. For example:  
- A person eating 2,000 calories of nuts (unprocessed) might stay full longer than someone eating 2,000 calories of processed junk (which could be 3,000+ calories due to hidden sugars/oils).  
- Processed foods also disrupt hormones like leptin (satiety) and ghrelin (hunger), making it harder to self-regulate intake.  

### Final Verdict:
**Processed, calorie-dense foods remain the top driver** because they create a perfect storm: cheap, addictive, and easy to overconsume. However, **total calorie intake vs. expenditure is the ultimate determinant of obesity**. For maximum health, combining balanced calories with nutrient-rich foods (even if unprocessed calorie-dense options like nuts) and activity is ideal.
