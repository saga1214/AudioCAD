Of course. Here is a clear, standard resume format that should be efficient and effective. Just follow these sections in order:

First, at the very top, is your name, using a 14-point font.

Right below that is your main contact information. Instead of icons side-by-side, it flows down under your name. You could have a small line break, and then list your email on the first line, followed by your phone number on the next, and finally your city on the third line. Using 9-point font is fine, but using a standard, readable font like Arial or Calibri is even better.

Next up is a brief, powerful Professional Summary. This is just two or three sentences that highlight your most relevant skills and career goals, which is perfect for bridging the gap in your work history for the new field you're targeting.

After the summary, you'll have your main Experience section. This is where you list your most relevant jobs, with each entry including your job title, the company, the dates, and just one or two brief bullet points highlighting a key achievement.

Then, create a section called 'Relevant Experience' for the older jobs that are directly related to the new career path you're pursuing.

After that comes your Education section. Here you'll list each degree you earned, the school, your graduation year, and any academic achievements right there, like highest honors.

Next is a Skills section. List your key skills, perhaps categorized into groups like 'Technical Skills' and 'Soft Skills'. Tailor this list to match the job description.

An Awards and Certifications section is also a good idea. You can list any scholarships here separately from the education section.

Finally, you can add an optional Additional Information section for things like professional memberships or languages you speak.

This structure is clean, professional, and makes it easy for a recruiter to find what they need. You can easily copy and paste this into a text editor to get started.
