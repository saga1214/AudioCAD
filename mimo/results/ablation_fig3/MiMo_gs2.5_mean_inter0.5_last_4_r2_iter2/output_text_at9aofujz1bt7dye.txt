Based on what we've discussed, the coefficient of friction at rink temperatures usually falls in that 0.01 to 0.1 range. At warmer, ideal competition temperatures like minus five degrees, it’s often around 0.01 to 0.02. When the rink gets extra cold to minus ten degrees Celsius, friction does tend to increase slightly without that helpful water layer, but it's still one of the lowest coefficients out there. So putting some numbers on it, a reasonable estimate for minus ten degrees might be around 0.005 to 0.015, leaning towards the higher end, so maybe closer to 0.01. It’s a tricky balance! Skaters and rink staff have to manage it carefully to get that perfect glide for the best performance.
