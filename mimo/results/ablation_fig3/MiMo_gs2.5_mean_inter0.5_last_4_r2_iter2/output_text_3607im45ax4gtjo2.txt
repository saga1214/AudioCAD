Dealing with a difficult person can be emotionally draining, but these strategies might help you cope and protect your peace:

### 1. **Document Everything**
   - Keep a detailed log of incidents (dates, times, details of calls to police/CPS, witnesses). This builds a case if legal action becomes necessary.

### 2. **Set Boundaries (Without Engaging)**
   - Avoid direct confrontation. If you encounter her, keep interactions brief and neutral (e.g., "Have a nice day" and walk away).
   - If she approaches you or your kids, calmly state, "We’re not discussing this," then disengage.

### 3. **Focus on Community Support**
   - Talk to other affected families. Share experiences, document incidents together, and consider a polite, unified request to the neighborhood board (e.g., "Our children feel unsafe骑自行车出去玩或玩耍，警察被频繁呼叫。我们是否可以一起讨论如何让我们的社区对孩子们更安全？").
   - If others agree, consider a formal request to the HOA or local authorities for mediation.

### 4. **Prioritize Your Kids’ Safety**
   - Reinforce that calling police/CPS is unacceptable. If CPS is involved, follow their guidance. If you feel unsafe, contact your local Child Protective Services (CPS) directly to understand their process.

### 5. **Emotional Self-Care**
   - **Limit Exposure:** Avoid social media or neighborhood groups where she might post.
   - **Mindfulness:** Try deep breathing or meditation apps (like Calm or Insight Timer) to manage stress.
   - **Seek Support:** Talk to friends/family or a therapist. Stress can feel overwhelming—professional help is valid.

### 6. **Legal Awareness**
   - Research local laws: Harassment or false reporting to authorities (CPS/police) may have legal consequences. Save evidence in case you need to report her behavior.
   - If CPS involvement was unwarranted, contact their office to clarify their findings and next steps.

### 7. **Redirect Focus to Joy**
   - Lean into the positive: Halloween preparations, school dress-up days (Jersey Day, Neon Day!), and your family’s music ritual. Small joys build resilience.

### 8. **Professional Intervention**
   - If tensions escalate, consider mediation through a community leader, counselor, or legal advocate.

> 💡 **Remember**: You can’t control others, but you *can* control your response. Protecting your kids’ peace and your own mental health is paramount. If CPS involvement feels unresolved, reaching out to a family law attorney for guidance might help. You’re doing a great job balancing stress with positivity for your family.
