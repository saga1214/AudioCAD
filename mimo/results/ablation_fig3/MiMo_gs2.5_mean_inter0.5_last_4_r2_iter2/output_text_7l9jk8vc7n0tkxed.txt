I hear you. A deep blockage can be tough, and when the plunger doesn't work, it's definitely frustrating. But don't panic. Here’s a clear, step-by-step plan to tackle it with your simple toolbox:

1.  **Assess & Empty:** First, make sure the water is off at the main stop valve for that fixture. Then, empty the bowl completely into a bucket. This gives you more room to work and prevents overflow.

2.  **Use a Drain Snake (Auger):** This is your best bet for a deep clog. Insert the snake head as far as it will go. Once you feel resistance, start cranking the handle slowly. Don't force it, let the spiral part do the work. You're trying to hook or break up the debris. If you feel something solid, gently twist the snake to grab it.

3.  **Try a Two-Plunger Method (If the first didn't work):** Fill the bowl with as much water as possible. Then, place a standard plunger *and* a bucket over the drain. Pump the plunger vigorously while simultaneously using the bucket to catch any water that might start to siphon out. This creates more pressure.

4.  **Use a Wet/Dry Vacuum (If you have one):** If the clog is severe, a wet/dry vacuum is your next tool. Set it to the "Wet" mode. Place the hose nozzle directly over the drain and create a tight seal. Turn the vacuum on and keep it firmly in place. The suction can often pull the blockage through.

5.  **Last Resort - Manual Removal:** If nothing else works, you might need to remove the P-trap (the U-shaped pipe under the sink). It's a bit more work, but it's the most effective way to get to the clog. Use pliers to loosen the slip nuts, carefully remove the trap, and you'll likely see the blockage. You can then flush it out or use a pipe wrench to break it up.

Start with the drain snake – it's the most effective tool for deep clogs. You've got the skills to do this. Just take it one step at a time, and you'll get it sorted out.
