Okay, after thinking it through, I completely agree with your point. The Age of Discovery was definitely a complex process.

First, let's look at the timeline you brought up. You're right that the Portuguese were already actively mapping the African coast before 1453. They weren't starting from zero; they were building on existing knowledge. The fall of Constantinople didn't start the race, it lit a huge fire under it by proving the threat of long-term Ottoman control real and immediate.

Second, the main driver was the technology and ambition. Advances in shipbuilding and navigation, plus the competitive spirit among European kingdoms, were the fundamental reasons. These things were happening anyway.

So, third, to your main question: yes, Constantinople's fall was a huge catalyst. It gave powerful monarchs the justification and the urgency to pour resources into risky overseas voyages. It turned a long-term goal into an immediate priority. Without that event, it might have taken much longer for Europe to commit on such a massive scale.

But it wasn't the only cause. The underlying economic competition, the religious drive to spread Christianity, and the technological readiness were just as important. So, to sum it up: the Age of Discovery was a complex process driven by many things, but the Ottoman capture of Constantinople was a major catalyst that dramatically sped up the race for independent sea routes led by the already-active Portuguese.
