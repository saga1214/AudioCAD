Alright, here are the classic recipes with precise ingredients and measurements for your Art Deco airport reception. I've also added finger food ideas to tie everything together.

First, for the Old Fashioned: You'll need two ounces of bourbon, like a Maker’s Mark or Buffalo Trace. Add one sugar cube, or if you prefer, half an ounce of simple syrup. Then, two to three dashes of Angostura bitters, a lemon twist for garnish, and a cherry if you want. To make it, just muddle the sugar cube with bitters and simple syrup in a shaker, add the bourbon, stir it with ice, and then strain it into a chilled rocks glass over fresh ice.

Next is the Paper Plane. For this, you'll need two ounces of bourbon, an ounce of Aperol, half an ounce of Amaro Nonino, and three-quarters of an ounce of fresh lemon juice. Shake all that with ice and strain it into a coupe glass. The garnish is optional, but an orange twist or a lemon peel would look nice.

Now for the Whiskey Sour. If you're including the egg white, you'll need two ounces of bourbon, three-quarters of an ounce of fresh lemon juice, and half an ounce of simple syrup. Plus, one egg white. If you're skipping the egg white, just leave that part out. The steps are: dry shake everything in a shaker without ice to get it frothy, then add ice and shake again. Strain it into a rocks glass over ice, and garnish with a cherry or an orange slice.

For the Aviation, the ingredients are: two ounces of gin, half an ounce of maraschino liqueur, a quarter-ounce of crème de violette, and half an ounce of fresh lemon juice. Shake it with ice, strain it into a cocktail glass, and garnish with a cherry. A maraschino cherry is a nice touch for that vintage look.

And for that third gin cocktail, I recommend the French 75. You'll need an ounce of gin, three-quarters of an ounce of lemon juice, half an ounce of simple syrup, and two ounces of Champagne or Prosecco. You can shake the gin, lemon, and syrup first, then strain that into a champagne flute and top it with the bubbly. Garnish with an edible flower or a lemon twist for that Art Deco elegance.

As for finger foods, to match Kentucky fall, you could do some spiced mini pork sliders with apple slaw, or bourbon-brown sugar glazed shrimp with caramelized onions. For something sweet, bourbon pecan rolls or maple-glazed donuts would be great. And for a savory snack, you could have some smoked gouda and fig skewers with a balsamic glaze.
