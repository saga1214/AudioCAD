To attach something to your jacket without damaging the lining, here are the best approaches, starting with the safest:

1.  **Fabric Glue or Fabric-Specific Tacky Glue:**
    *   **Best for:** Small patches, appliqués, or non-structural items.
    *   **How:** Apply a small amount of glue to the back of the item you want to attach, then press it firmly onto the jacket. Hold for several minutes (or as per glue instructions) to ensure adhesion. Avoid getting glue on the lining.
    *   **Pros:** Quick, easy, no sewing needed, good for small items.
    *   **Cons:** Not ideal for heavy items or areas with high friction (like elbows/knees). Can be visible. Test on a scrap first!

2.  **Iron-On Adhesive (Fabric-Specific):**
    *   **Best for:** Small patches, appliqués, or lightweight items.
    *   **How:** Follow the instructions on the adhesive patch. Usually involves pressing the patch onto the jacket (backside down), ironing it (often for a short time without steam or with low heat), and then pressing firmly for several minutes after removing the iron.
    *   **Pros:** Strong bond for small items, relatively quick.
    *   **Cons:** Heat risk (test on scrap!), can be visible, not for heavy items. Quality varies.

3.  **Sewing (Hand or Machine):**
    *   **Best for:** *The most durable and recommended method for anything that needs to stay securely attached*, especially larger items or areas under stress (elbows, shoulders).
    *   **How:** Use a simple running stitch or a small blind stitch. If sewing by hand, use a needle and thread appropriate for the fabric weight. If machine sewing, use a very small, tight stitch length and a fine needle. **Crucially:** Take the stitches *through the outer fabric only* and bring the needle up and down *through the outer fabric only*. Never stitch through the lining. You can anchor the stitches on the inside if needed for security, but the visible stitches should only go through the outer layer.
    *   **Pros:** Extremely secure, invisible on the inside, long-lasting.
    *   **Cons:** Requires a needle and thread (or access to a sewing machine), takes more time.

4.  **Tailor (Professional Service):**
    *   **Best for:** Items that need to be attached securely and invisibly, or if you're unsure how to do it yourself.
    *   **How:** Bring your jacket and the item you want attached to a tailor. They can hand-stitch it through the outer fabric only, making it very secure and invisible from the outside.
    *   **Pros:** Highest quality, invisible result, expert technique.
    *   **Cons:** Cost (though usually reasonable for a small job), takes time to book and get it done.

**Key Considerations:**

*   **Test First:** Always test any adhesive or sewing technique on a scrap piece of similar fabric *before* working on your jacket.
*   **Avoid the Lining:** This is paramount. Stitching through the lining is the main way to ruin a jacket.
*   **Weight & Location:** Heavy items or areas with a lot of movement (elbows, knees) need the strongest attachment method (sewing or professional tailoring).
*   **Visibility:** If the attachment needs to be invisible from the outside, sewing (hand or machine) is the only truly invisible option. Adhesives can be visible.

**Recommendation:** For anything beyond a tiny, non-structural item, **sewing (hand or machine) through the outer fabric only** is the safest and most reliable method. If you're uncomfortable sewing, **consulting a tailor** is the best investment in your jacket's longevity. Avoid stitching through the lining at all costs!
