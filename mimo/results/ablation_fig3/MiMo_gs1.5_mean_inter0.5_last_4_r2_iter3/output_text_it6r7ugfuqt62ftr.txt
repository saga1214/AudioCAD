Absolutely! For a family vacation with a large vehicle, I highly recommend Big Sur on the Central Coast. It's a stunning area with warm weather, swimming spots, and plenty to do. Here’s a detailed plan for a four to five-day trip.

First, a couple of key things to know. Since you'll need a large vehicle, you'll want to book a good size SUV or a small motorhome. You'll also definitely want to reserve accommodations and popular tours well in advance, especially since the weather is nice.

For your trip, I'd suggest four days in Big Sur, and a fifth day in Monterey if you'd like a bit more city time.

Let's start with Day One, which is your travel day. You'll want to leave Northern California early, maybe around 6 AM. The drive is about five and a half hours, so you'll arrive in Big Sur in the early afternoon. I'd recommend stopping for lunch in San Simeon. You can pack a picnic for the drive. For your first night, a great place to stay is the Ventana Big Sur Resort. It's luxurious, has stunning ocean views, and is very accommodating for gluten-free diets.

On Day Two, you can explore the heart of Big Sur. In the morning, I suggest a guided kayaking tour at Pfeiffer Beach. You'll paddle through the famous sea caves, and it's a great way to see wildlife. In the afternoon, you can hike to McWay Falls. It's a short walk down to a beautiful waterfall that flows right into the ocean. For a special dinner, the Ventana Resort's restaurant, Ventana Cafe, is excellent for gluten-free food and has amazing views.

For Day Three, you could take a day trip to Monterey. It's about a one-and-a-half-hour drive. You can visit the historic Cannery Row and the world-famous Monterey Bay Aquarium. For lunch, The Sardine Factory is a great spot. Afterwards, you can drive up to Point Lobos State Natural Reserve for some incredible coastal hiking. You'll definitely want to pack a picnic for that. For dinner, I'd suggest The Fisherman's Grotto in Pacific Grove; they are very experienced with gluten-free needs.

Now, for Day Four, you can head south towards Pfeiffer Beach and Big Creek. In the morning, you could hike the Pfeiffer Falls Trail, which is a beautiful, easy walk through a redwood forest to a waterfall. In the afternoon, you can relax at Pfeiffer Beach, which is famous for its purple sand. You can even swim there if the tide is right. For your last night, you could try a different accommodation, like the very luxurious Nepenthe Resort, which is known for its incredible cliffside dining.

On your final day, Day Five, if you're feeling adventurous, you could take a detour to Hearst Castle. It's about an hour's drive from Big Sur and is a must-see. You'll want to book tickets for that in advance. For lunch, you can eat at the beautiful restaurant at Hearst Castle. Afterwards, you can either drive back towards San Francisco or head to Carmel-by-the-Sea for a last stop.

Just a few final tips: Big Sur is beautiful but can be winding, so drive slowly. Always double-check that restaurants are comfortable with gluten-free needs. And definitely pack for variable weather – layers are key! This itinerary should give you a wonderful, relaxing, and adventurous family vacation. Have a fantastic trip!
