Based on the evidence, **easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods** stands out as one of the biggest top drivers behind increasing obesity rates. Here's why:

1.  **Increased Calorie Intake:** These foods are often engineered to be hyper-palatable (tasty and hard to stop eating), high in calories, sugar, unhealthy fats, and salt, while being low in protein, fiber, and other nutrients that promote satiety. This makes it very easy to consume large amounts of calories without feeling full.
2.  **Reduced Satiety:** Their low nutritional density means they don't fill you up effectively, leading to overconsumption throughout the day.
3.  **Ubiquity and Affordability:** They are heavily marketed, widely available (especially cheaply), and often marketed as convenient, making them a default choice for many, especially those with limited time or resources.
4.  **Impact on Diet Quality:** They displace consumption of whole, nutrient-dense foods like fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins, which are associated with better health outcomes and potentially lower obesity risk.
