Yes, it's important to understand that the feast itself was likely a genuinely positive event for both sides at that specific time. The Pilgrims were grateful for their survival and the help they received, and the Wampanoag leader, Massasoit, likely saw it as a strategic alliance to protect his people.

However, and this is the critical point, that single moment of shared gratitude doesn't erase the fact that for the Wampanoag, it marked the beginning of a devastating history of disease, conflict, and the loss of their homeland.

So, the event was happy for both groups, but only for a very brief period and from a very limited perspective. The long-term consequences for the Wampanoag were overwhelmingly tragic.
