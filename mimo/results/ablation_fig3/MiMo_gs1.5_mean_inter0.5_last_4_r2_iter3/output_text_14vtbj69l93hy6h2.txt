That's a common hurdle! If cheese isn't doing it, let's explore other enticing treat options and training strategies:

**Try These Treat Alternatives:**

1.  **Size Matters:** Ensure your treats are *very* small (pea-sized or smaller for a Maltese). A whole piece might be too big.
2.  **Strong Scent is Key:**
    *   **Cheese:** Try a small piece of mild, smelly cheese like cheddar or mozzarella. Avoid anything too hard.
    *   **Freeze-Dried Liver:** Look for "original liver" or "beef liver" variety packs. Sometimes chicken or salmon flavors are even more enticing than plain beef liver.
    *   **Other Scenty Options:** Small bits of cooked, plain chicken (skinless, boneless), scrambled eggs (cooked very soft), or even a tiny piece of smoky bacon (in moderation, check vet if unsure about bacon).
    *   **Commercial Training Treats:** Many brands make tiny, smelly training treats specifically designed to be motivating. Look for ones with high-value ingredients like liver, chicken, or cheese.
3.  **Texture:** Some dogs prefer soft treats (like cheese, egg, cooked chicken) over hard ones (like kibble or some liver treats).
4.  **Warmth:** A *slightly* warmed-up cooked chicken or egg might smell even more enticing!

**Training Strategies to Boost Engagement:**

1.  **Start INSIDE, Quietly:** Begin training in a calm, distraction-free environment with the door closed. This lets Maisy focus solely on you and the reward.
2.  **Short & Sweet:** Keep initial sessions *very* short (1-3 minutes max). End on a success. Short bursts of high-value rewards are often better than long, drawn-out sessions.
3.  **Make it a Game:** Play with the treat! Move it around, behind your leg, in your hand, near her nose. Ask for tiny movements like "look at me," "touch" (nose to hand), or "find it" (toss treat on floor).
4.  **Use "Click" Training (Highly Recommended):** Get a dog training clicker. When Maisy *accidentally* does something you want (like looks at you, nudges your hand), click *immediately*. Then instantly give her the super-treat. The click bridges the gap between her action and the reward, teaching her *exactly* what to repeat. It's incredibly motivating!
5.  **Mix Up Rewards:** Don't just rely on food. Incorporate:
    *   **Verbal Praise:** Enthusiastic "Good girl!" or "Yes!" (match her energy level).
    *   **Physical Praise:** A gentle pet, a head scratch, or a happy wiggle.
    *   **Play:** A short game of tug or fetch (if she likes it).
    *   **Treats:** Rotate between different high-value treats throughout the day.
6.  **Focus on Basics First:** Start with the easiest, most rewarding tricks:
    *   **"Touch":** Hold a treat in your closed hand near your leg. Say "Touch," then open your hand when she nudges it with her nose. Reward.
    *   **"Sit":** Hold a treat over her head (让她抬头看), slowly lower it towards her nose while saying "Sit," and gently guide her bottom down. Reward when her bottom touches the floor.
    *   **"Paw":** Hold a treat in your closed fist near her nose. Say "Paw," then slowly lower your fist towards the floor. When she lifts her paw to take it, close your fist and reward.
7.  **Patience & Consistency:** Training is a process. Maisy might not get it immediately. Be patient and consistent with your commands and rewards. Practice a few times a day for short periods.

**Key Takeaway:** Maisy might be a "treat-smeller" rather than a "treat-eater" initially. Experiment with different *types* and *sizes* of treats, especially strong-smelling ones. Combine them with short, fun sessions in a quiet space and use clicker training if possible. The goal is to make learning a genuinely positive and exciting experience for her! Good luck!
