That sounds like a wonderful real-life romance to plan! Since seasickness is a factor, you'll want to focus on **calm, stationary, or stable water activities** that still feel intimate and adventurous. Here are some creative ideas perfect for ocean lovers and seafood fans:

1.  **A Private Pontoon Boat Lunch & Sunset Cruise:**
    *   **Why it works:** Pontoon boats are incredibly stable and wide, minimizing any rocking motion. They're perfect for stationary anchoring.
    *   **Romance Factor:** Hire a private chef onboard! They can prepare your favorite seafood dishes (grilled fish, lobster, oysters) right there on the boat, fresh from the catch nearby. Enjoy a gourmet meal while drifting peacefully in the sunset, surrounded by calm water. The chef disappears, leaving just the two of you with a bottle of wine and the ocean.

2.  **Glass-Bottom Boat Exploration & Snorkel Stop:**
    *   **Why it works:** Very stable, slow-moving vessels designed for viewing marine life. Minimal motion.
    *   **Romance Factor:** Glide silently over vibrant coral reefs or sandy bottoms. Spot fish, rays, maybe even sea turtles together. The shared wonder of the underwater world is incredibly bonding. If the boat has a small, stable platform or you can anchor calmly, even a short, guided snorkel stop in crystal-clear water feels magical.

3.  **Stable Catamaran Sunset Sail (with a twist):**
    *   **Why it works:** Catamarans have a wide beam and twin hulls, making them significantly more stable and less rolling than monohulled sailboats, even in moderate conditions. Motion is usually very gentle.
    *   **Romance Factor:** Charter a small, private catamaran for an evening sail. The captain can navigate to a protected cove or open water with minimal swell. Enjoy champagne, cocktails, and simple snacks as the sky turns fiery orange and purple. The stability allows for easy conversation and leaning against each other without worry.

4.  **"Floating Market" & Seafood Feast:**
    *   **Why it works:** Many coastal towns have small, calm harbors or lagoons where you can rent a small, stable boat (like a wide dinghy or a small electric boat) for a short trip.
    *   **Romance Factor:** Explore a charming, quiet little harbor with small, traditional boats (like stilt houses or small fishing boats) bobbing peacefully in the water. Visit a tiny local seafood shack or market dockside and pick out the day's catch. Bring it back to your rented boat (or a nearby dock) and have the owner or a chef prepare it simply for your private, moving feast on the water.

5.  **Calm Kayaking & Beach Picnic:**
    *   **Why it works:** While not on the water *per se*, kayaking in protected bays, lagoons, or calm coastal waters is fantastic for intimacy and exploration. It's low motion and very personal.
    *   **Romance Factor:** Paddle together silently through calm waters towards a secluded little beach or cove. Pack a picnic with fresh local fruit, cheese, bread, and maybe a simple seafood salad. Enjoy the quiet, shared effort, the sense of discovery, and the peaceful beach setting together. It's active but gentle.

**Key Considerations for Success:**

*   **Communicate:** Be upfront with the boat company about your partner's seasickness. They'll know the best stable options and conditions.
*   **Focus on Comfort:** Dress in layers, bring sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses. Comfort = better mood!
*   **Keep it Intimate:** Opt for private charters or small group tours whenever possible. Less crowd, more focus on each other.
*   **Combine with Land:** Mix water activities with time on charming seaside towns, beaches, or coastal hikes for a well-rounded romantic getaway.
*   **Embrace the Calm:** The lack of motion isn't a negative; it allows for deep conversation, shared appreciation of the scenery, and a more relaxed, unhurried pace.

These ideas prioritize your shared love of the ocean and seafood while cleverly working *with* your partner's seasickness, ensuring a truly romantic and memorable escape. Enjoy planning your real-life novel!
