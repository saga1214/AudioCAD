Based on the evidence, the most important aspect is their role in providing essential ecosystem services that support vulnerable human populations.

The key reason is that these services—like clean water, pollination, and climate regulation—are not just valuable; for millions of people, they are fundamental to their survival. When a hotspot like the Sundaland landbridge in Southeast Asia is lost, it's not just a unique plant that disappears. It's the system that provides fresh water to millions of people that starts to fail.

While the unique species and potential for new medicines are incredibly important, the immediate, tangible impact on human well-being is arguably the most compelling reason for action. Protecting these hotspots is fundamentally about protecting the very systems that keep people alive and provide for their basic needs.
