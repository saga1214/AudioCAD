Dealing with a difficult person (especially a neighbor) is incredibly stressful, but here are some actionable strategies to help you cope and protect your family:

### 1. **Document Everything**
   - **Record incidents:** Dates, times, witnesses, what happened, and how it impacted you (e.g., "Called CPS on a neighbor’s child on [date]").
   - **Save evidence:** Photos, emails, or recordings (where legal). This builds a case if authorities need it.

### 2. **Involve Authorities (When Necessary)**
   - **CPS/police:** If CPS is involved, cooperate fully. If threats persist, file formal reports with law enforcement. Repeat offenses may lead to restraining orders.
   - **HOA/Community Board:** If applicable, report her behavior to your homeowners' association for mediation or fines.

### 3. **Set Boundaries & Avoid Engagement**
   - **Don’t escalate:** Avoid arguments or retaliation. Stay calm and disengage if she confronts you.
   - **Communicate limits:** If you must interact (e.g., about trash cans), keep it brief, polite, and factual.

### 4. **Seek Support**
   - **Community:** Talk to trusted neighbors. A united front can deter harassment.
   - **Professional Help:** Consider counseling to manage stress. Support groups (online or in-person) can also help.

### 5. **Prioritize Safety & Normalcy**
   - **Protect your kids:** Keep activities supervised initially. Use safe outdoor spaces (parks, bike trails) if your street feels unsafe.
   - **Focus on joy:** Lean into Halloween preparations and school events like Red Ribbon Week—these positive routines counterbalance stress.

### 6. **Self-Care**
   - **Reduce exposure:** If possible, avoid direct contact. Limit time spent in the yard.
   - **Mindfulness:** Apps like Calm or Headspace can help manage anxiety. Physical activity (e.g., walks) also reduces stress hormones.

### 7. **Legal Options**
   - Consult a lawyer about nuisance laws or restraining orders if harassment continues.

> 💡 **Key Reminder:** You can’t control others, but you *can* control your response. By documenting, seeking support, and focusing on what you *can* influence (like your family’s safety and routines), you reduce her power over your stress levels. Stay patient—this process takes time, but it *will* improve.
