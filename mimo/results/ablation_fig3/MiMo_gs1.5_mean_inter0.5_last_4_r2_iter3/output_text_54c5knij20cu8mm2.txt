Okay, let’s break this down.

First, the right-brain versus left-brain idea. It’s mostly a myth. While different parts of the brain have slightly different functions—like language processing being more common on the left side—both hemispheres work together constantly. Creativity and logic aren’t neatly separated; they overlap and interact. For example, a creative person might still need logical thinking to structure their work, and a logical thinker can be highly creative in problem-solving. So, it’s not a strict divide.

Now, for your career, if you want something creative, objective, measurable, and with lasting impact, here are some ideas to consider:

First, computational design or data visualization. You get to be creative with visualizing data, which is objective and measurable. It’s a blend of art and science.

Second, architecture or industrial design. You’re building things, which you like, while also incorporating creative expression and structural logic. It’s very objective and can be very creative.

Third, science communication. You’re taking complex, objective scientific information and making it accessible and engaging. It’s creative and measurable.

Fourth, UX/UI design. You’re structuring user experiences, which involves creativity and logic. It’s measurable through user metrics and feedback.

And fifth, graphic design in fields like data journalism or science visualization. You’re translating facts into creative visuals, which is both objective and artistic.

These fields all allow for that structure you like, while also being creative and having a tangible, lasting impact. They’re not desk-bound, either—many involve physical or digital creation.
