Okay, let's break this down. Vasculitis is inflammation of the blood vessels. It's an autoimmune process where the immune system mistakenly attacks the vessel walls. This inflammation can affect vessels of all sizes, which is why the symptoms can vary so much depending on where the inflammation is. For example, large vessel vasculitis might cause systemic symptoms like fever and fatigue. Medium vessel types can affect the skin, causing rashes or ulcers. Small vessel vasculitis often presents with things like palpable purpura, which are red, raised spots.

The symptoms can range from fever and joint pain to skin rashes, fatigue, and in some cases, more serious complications if blood flow is blocked. The cause isn't fully understood, but it's often believed to be a mix of genetic predisposition and an environmental trigger. A proper diagnosis usually involves a combination of a physical exam, blood work, and often a biopsy.

As for other common causes of chronic redness, rosacea is a big one, especially for the face, and it's often linked to sun exposure, heat, or spicy foods. Rosacea is also associated with chronic inflammation. Other causes could include allergies, contact dermatitis from irritants like cleansers, or even conditions like pityriasis rosea. It's also possible for other medical conditions to be the underlying cause. So, while vasculitis is a possibility, rosacea is a very common cause of facial redness that you might want to consider.

For hair and nail health, besides biotin, things like collagen peptides, keratin supplements, making sure you get enough iron, and zinc can be beneficial.
