Okay, it sounds like you're back on track with the painting. Let's get back to the cabinet prep. Before you apply primer, here are the most critical steps. First, and this is the most important, make sure the sanding dust is completely gone. You should wipe down all the surfaces with a tack cloth or even a slightly damp rag that you then wipe dry. This is key, because any dust left behind will ruin your primer's finish.

Next, give the whole cabinet a final light sanding with fine-grit sandpaper, maybe around 220-grit. This creates a slightly rough texture for the primer to grip onto.

After that, apply the primer. You'll want to use a brush for the edges and corners, and a roller for the larger flat surfaces. And remember what we discussed for a dark color change: you'll absolutely need two full coats of primer. Let the first coat dry completely, which usually takes two to four hours, then apply the second one.

So, the main thing to focus on right now is that thorough cleaning after sanding. If you get that done perfectly, the primer will apply beautifully.
