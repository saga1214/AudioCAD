That's a smart approach—crafting a clear, respectful email gives you control. Here’s a framework with pointers to balance professionalism and your goal of understanding the grade:

### **Key Email Structure & Tips**
1. **Subject Line**:  
   `Request for Feedback on [Course Name] Exam [Date]`  
   *(Neutral and specific.)*

2. **Opening (Respectful & Concise)**:  
   > "Dear Professor [Last Name],  
   > I hope this email finds you well. I’m writing to respectfully request feedback on my recent exam for [Course Name] (exam date: [Date])."

3. **State Your Goal Clearly**:  
   > "As someone committed to improving in this course, I’d value any insights you could share about areas where my response could have better aligned with expectations. Specifically, I’m curious about how you evaluated the content and structure of the essay."  
   *(Focuses on learning, not the grade itself.)*

4. **Be Specific (Without Accusation)**:  
   > "For example, I noticed the rubric emphasized [mention key topic or skill, e.g., 'critical analysis of X'], but I’m unsure how my approach to [specific section] compared to expectations."  
   *(Shows you’ve reflected and are seeking clarity.)*

5. **Offer Flexibility & Close Professionally**:  
   > "If you have time, I’d be grateful for even brief feedback. Alternatively, I’m happy to discuss this further during your office hours or at a convenient time. Thank you for your guidance—it’s greatly appreciated."  
   > "Sincerely, [Your Name]"

---

### **Critical Tips to Avoid Triggering His Ego**
- **Avoid**: "I feel graded unfairly," "Do you agree this was subjective?"  
- **Use Instead**: "I’d like to understand your evaluation criteria better," "How could I improve similar responses in the future?"  
- **Keep it Short**: Professors skim emails. Be concise and solution-focused.

---

### **If He Responds (Or Doesn’t)**
- **If he replies**: Great! Ask follow-ups like, "Could you clarify how I should strengthen [specific point]?"  
- **If he ignores you**: Don’t push. His behavior speaks volumes. Focus on other professors for feedback.  
- **If he’s dismissive**: Document it (e.g., "He dismissed my request without feedback"). This could support future feedback to your department if needed.
