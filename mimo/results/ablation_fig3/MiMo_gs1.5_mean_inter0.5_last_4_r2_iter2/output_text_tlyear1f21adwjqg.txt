### Key Advantages of Hydropower:
1. **Reliability**:  
   Unlike solar or wind, hydropower provides **consistent baseload energy** 24/7. Dams store water and release it on demand, unaffected by weather or time of day.  
   - *Example*: Pumped-storage hydropower can balance grids by storing excess energy during peak production and releasing it during demand spikes.

2. **Infrastructure Efficiency**:  
   Hydropower leverages **existing dams and reservoirs**, drastically reducing deployment costs and time. Upgrading turbines can also unlock energy from aging infrastructure.

3. **High Conversion Efficiency**:  
   Modern hydropower plants convert **90% of water energy into electricity** (vs. ~20–30% for solar/wind). This efficiency holds over decades due to low maintenance and durability.

4. **Scalability and Lifespan**:  
   - **Lifespan**: 50–100+ years (vs. 20–30 years for solar panels).  
   - **Capacity**: Large-scale projects (e.g., Three Gorges Dam) can generate terawatts of power, while small "run-of-river" systems suit local needs.

5. **Ecosystem Synergy**:  
   Dams can generate hydroelectricity while supporting controlled water flow for agriculture, recreation, and flood control.

### Caveats:
- **Geographic Limitation**: Requires significant water flow or elevation change (rivers, lakes).  
- **Environmental Impact**: Disrupts aquatic ecosystems and displaces communities (mitigated by modern designs).  
- **Storage Dependency**: Pumped storage is essential for grid flexibility but adds complexity.
