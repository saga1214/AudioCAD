Okay, kosher dietary laws mean no mixing of meat and dairy, and no pork, shellfish, or insects. So, since you have blood pudding—which is a meat dish—you'll need to keep it separate from any dairy-based items. For example, the hummus you mentioned is dairy. So, one way is to have a separate platter for the meat dishes like the blood pudding and the bacon-wrapped snacks. Then, have a completely separate platter for the fruit and veggie snacks, which are naturally dairy-free. If you serve the dark chocolate, make sure it's kosher-certified and keep it in a separate bowl too. You could also offer a couple of non-alcoholic beverages, like sparkling water or tea, as a safe option. The key is just to keep all the meat and dairy items clearly separated on different tables or platters. That way, Tony can just pick from the meat side, and the rest of the group can have the dairy-free snacks. It keeps things simple and inclusive.
