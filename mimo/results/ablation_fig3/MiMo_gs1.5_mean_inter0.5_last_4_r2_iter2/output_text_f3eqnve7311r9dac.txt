That's a great point. Older books are often rarer and more valuable, but they are also more fragile and expensive to get in good condition. Let's look at ten significant books from that era.

First, there's *The Amazing Fantasy* number fifteen from 1959. It's the first appearance of Spider-Man and the most expensive book ever sold at auction.

Then, *Action Comics* number one, from 1938, is Superman's debut and a cornerstone of the market.

Another one is *Detective Comics* number twenty-seven, from 1939, which is Batman's first appearance.

*Whiz Comics* number fifteen, from 1941, is the first full appearance of the Golden Age Flash.

*The Flash* number one, also from 1956, is the first appearance of the Silver Age Flash.

*The Brave and the Bold* number twenty-eight, from 1960, is the first team-up of Batman and Superman.

*The Spectacular Spider-Man* number twenty-five, from 1967, is the first appearance of the villain Kraven the Hunter.

*Conan the Barbarian* number one, from 1972, is the first appearance of Conan in comics.

*The Incredible Hulk* number one, from 1962, is the first appearance of the Hulk.

And finally, *Thor* number one, from 1962, is the first appearance of Thor and the Asgardians.

Just a quick note: these are all very valuable books, and the prices are mainly for high-grade, professionally graded copies. The condition is the most important factor.
