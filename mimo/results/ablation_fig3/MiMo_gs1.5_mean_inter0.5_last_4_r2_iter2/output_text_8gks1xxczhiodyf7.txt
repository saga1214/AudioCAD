Based on the evidence, the **single most important driver** of increasing obesity rates is **easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods**. Here's why:

### Key Reasons:
1. **Hyper-Palatability**:  
   Processed foods are engineered to override satiety signals. High sugar, fat, salt, and refined carbs trigger dopamine release, making them addictive and harder to stop eating. This disrupts natural hunger cues.

2. **Nutrient Density Gap**:  
   These foods are low in protein, fiber, vitamins, and minerals. They fill stomachs but lack nutritional value, leading to overconsumption without feeling full. For example, a soda or fast-food meal provides calories without nutrients.

3. **Affordability & Accessibility**:  
   Ultra-processed foods are cheaper and more available than fresh, whole foods (especially in low-income areas). This creates a systemic advantage, making poor dietary choices the "default" option.

4. **Impact on Activity Levels**:  
   Even with exercise, overconsumption of processed foods can outpace calorie expenditure. Studies show people eat ~500 extra calories/day from ultra-processed foods alone.

### Why Not Other Factors?
- **Physical Inactivity**: While critical, it’s often a *consequence* of processed food reliance (e.g., sedentary lifestyles fueled by convenience foods).  
- **Genetics/Environment**: These are amplifiers, but processed foods are the primary *behavioral* trigger.  
- **Portion Sizes**: Smaller portions of processed foods still contribute to obesity if calorie-dense (e.g., a small bag of chips = 300+ calories).

### Bottom Line:
Processed foods dominate because they **exploit biological vulnerabilities** (hunger, reward systems) while being **systemically promoted and subsidized**. Reducing intake is the most actionable step for prevention, though addressing food deserts, marketing, and affordability is equally vital for systemic change.
