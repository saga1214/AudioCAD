That's a great question, and the answer is a bit complex.

For the Pilgrims, it was definitely a moment of joy and relief after surviving a tough winter and celebrating a good harvest.

For the Wampanoag, it was likely a strategic alliance, but they had also suffered terribly from European diseases. So, while they were cooperative, it was probably not a purely happy event for them.

The key thing to remember is that the two groups had very different experiences and futures. The traditional Thanksgiving story tends to focus only on the Pilgrims' experience, while the Wampanoag story highlights the painful consequences that followed.

So, it wasn't a single, shared happy occasion. It was a complex moment that was meaningful in different ways for both groups.
