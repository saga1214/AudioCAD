To tune your instrument (likely a guitar or ukulele) while waiting for the dye to process, here’s how to use a **tuner app** or **clip-on tuner**:

### 1. **Tuner App Method**  
   - **Download an app**: Use free apps like *GuitarTuna*, *Fender Tune*, or *Tuner*.  
   - **Position your phone**: Hold it close to the strings (1–2 inches away).  
   - **Start tuning**:  
     - Press a string down (e.g., 6th string = low E).  
     - The app will show a visual guide (like a dial) and audio feedback.  
     - Slide the app’s tuner knob up/down until the string’s pitch hits the target note (e.g., E4 for guitar’s 6th string).  
   - **Repeat**: Do this for all strings (typically from 6th to 1st string on guitar).  

### 2. **Clip-On Tuner**  
   - **Attach it**: Clip the tuner to a headstock (guitar) or neck (ukulele).  
   - **Tune strings**:  
     - Press a string down and play it.  
     - The tuner’s LED light will turn green when the note is perfectly in tune.  
     - Adjust with the tuning pegs until green.  
   - **Repeat**: Tune all strings one by one.  

### Pro Tips:  
- **Tune down first**: If you’re tuning to a lower pitch (like for rose gold tones), tune down gently to avoid damaging strings.  
- **Check frequently**: Dye processing takes time (30–45 mins). Recheck tuning every 10–15 mins.  
- **Tuning order**: Tune from the thickest (lowest) string to the thinnest (highest).  

### Why This Works:  
Apps use your phone’s microphone to detect string vibrations, while clip tuners measure frequency directly. Both methods are **more accurate than ear alone** and help avoid damaging strings.
