Absolutely. For a deep clog, let's break it down methodically. Here’s a step-by-step guide:

### Step 1: **Confirm the Blockage**
   - Pour a small amount of water down the drain. If it doesn’t drain or pools, it’s likely a solid object or severe buildup.
   - If it drains slowly, it’s likely organic matter (hair, grease, etc.).

### Step 2: **Try a Wire Hanger Method**
   - Straighten a wire coat hanger, leaving a small hook at the end.
   - Wrap a rubber band around the hook to prevent scratches.
   - Insert it into the drain and gently hook out any debris you can see.

### Step 3: **Use a Pipe Snake (Drain Snake)**
   - Insert a pipe snake (available at hardware stores) into the drain. Start slowly, then push it in 1–2 inches at a time.
   - Twist the snake as you go to break up the clog.
   - When you feel resistance, pull it out to remove the debris. Repeat until it clears.

### Step 4: **Boiling Water + Dish Soap (If Safe)**
   - For grease clogs, pour 1 cup of dish soap down the drain, then follow with 2–3 cups of boiling water. Let it sit for 10–15 minutes before trying to flush it.

### Step 5: **Chemical Drain Cleaners (Last Resort)**
   - Use a **sodium hydroxide-based** cleaner (e.g., Liquid Plumber) **only if the pipe is PVC or ABS plastic**. Avoid these in older metal pipes.
   - Follow the instructions exactly—never mix chemicals.
   - Wear gloves and eye protection.

### Step 6: **Check for Leaks & Isolate**
   - If water is leaking under the sink, turn off the main water valve and use a towel to dry the area.
   - Try unclogging a nearby drain (e.g., a sink or tub drain) to confirm the clog is isolated.

### Step 7: **Call a Professional if Needed**
   - If the snake won’t budge, or if water is backing up into other drains, it could be a main line issue. Call a plumber.

### Safety Tip:  
Avoid mixing chemicals with bleach or other cleaners—this can create toxic fumes.
