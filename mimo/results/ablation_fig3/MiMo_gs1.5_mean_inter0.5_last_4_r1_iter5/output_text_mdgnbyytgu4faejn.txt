Of course. Here's a clear, step-by-step breakdown of a standard resume format to make it easy to put together.

First, at the very top, you'll have your name, written in a 12 to 14-point font. Below that is your contact information: email, phone number, city, and state. Including icons is optional, and you might want to avoid them to prevent any ATS issues. Your LinkedIn profile URL is a must-have, and you should make sure it's easy to click on.

Next, a short and powerful Professional Summary, which is just a few sentences about your career goals and key qualifications. This is especially important if you're changing careers.

Then, a Skills section. Here, you'll list your hard skills, like software or technical abilities, and your soft skills, like communication. It's best to tailor this list to match the job description you're applying for.

After that comes your Experience section. You'll want to list your most relevant jobs first, with the most recent one at the top. For each job, include the company name, your title, and the start and end dates. Use bullet points to describe your key accomplishments, focusing on your impact with numbers whenever possible, like saying you "increased sales by 15 percent."

If you have a lot of older work experience that isn't as relevant, you can create a separate section called "Additional Work History" to list it concisely.

For Education, list your degree, the school, and your graduation year. You can also add honors like "Summa Cum Laude" or any significant awards you received.

If you have a portfolio, a link to it is great to include. For certifications, just list the name of the certifying organization.

Finally, you can add a "Projects" section to show off your work, especially if you're in a creative or technical field.

And a few quick tips: keep your resume to one or two pages, use a clean font, and always, always proofread it before sending. Let me know if you'd like me to elaborate on any of these sections!
