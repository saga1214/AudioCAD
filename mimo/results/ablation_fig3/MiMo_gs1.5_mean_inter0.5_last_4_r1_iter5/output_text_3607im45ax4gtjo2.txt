Dealing with a difficult person—especially one causing stress in your neighborhood—can be incredibly draining. Here’s practical advice to help you navigate this situation:

### 1. **Prioritize Safety & Documentation**
   - **Document Everything**: Keep a detailed log of incidents (dates, times, what happened, witnesses). Save photos, texts, or recordings (where legal) if possible.
   - **CPS Involvement**: If CPS is involved, cooperate fully but follow their guidance. Report *every* interaction with the neighbor to CPS. If you suspect safety risks, contact authorities immediately.

### 2. **Set Clear Boundaries**
   - **Be Direct but Calm**: If safe, communicate boundaries firmly. Example: *"Our children are not going to be harassed for playing in their own yard."*
   - **Avoid Engagement**: If the neighbor is volatile, limit interactions. Respond neutrally to avoid escalation (e.g., *"I’ve already called CPS about this"* instead of arguing).

### 3. **Seek Mediation or Legal Support**
   - **Mediation**: A neutral third party (like a community mediator) can help resolve conflicts. Check if your city offers free services.
   - **Legal Action**: If threats or harassment persist, consult a lawyer. You may have legal options (e.g., restraining orders, nuisance lawsuits).

### 4. **Protect Your Family’s Well-being**
   - **Safety Plan**: Ensure kids feel secure. Explain boundaries simply (e.g., *"If she calls the police, we’ll handle it—we won’t let her scare you"*).
   - **Emotional Support**: Talk to trusted friends, family, or a therapist. This stress is heavy—don’t isolate yourself.

### 5. **Focus on What You Control**
   - **School Dress-Up Days**: Use these positive moments to distract from stress. Celebrate small joys (like Halloween costumes) to counterbalance the negativity.
   - **Community**: Reach out to other affected families. Collective support can strengthen your resolve.

### 6. **Know When to Escalate**
   - If CPS/police don’t act, file a formal complaint or request a supervisor review. Document every step.

### Remember:
- **You’re Not Alone**: Many neighborhoods face similar issues. Support groups (online or local) can provide insight.
- **Prioritize Peace**: Your family’s safety and mental health come first. If the situation doesn’t improve, consider moving if feasible—your well-being matters.

You’re doing a great job balancing stress with moments of joy for your kids. Lean on your support system, and trust that addressing this systematically will reduce the burden over time. 🌟
