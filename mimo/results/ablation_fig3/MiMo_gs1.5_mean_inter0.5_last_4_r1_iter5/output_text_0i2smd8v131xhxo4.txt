That's a great question about balancing the grind. Here's a breakdown of your points:

1.  **AI Tools for Trimming/Silence & Labeling:**
    *   **Trimming Silence:** Many DAWs (like Audacity, Adobe Audition, Reaper) have basic noise/gap removal. **Descript** is fantastic for this – it shows your waveform visually, and you can simply click and drag to delete silent sections instantly. **Adobe Audition** has powerful "Noise Reduction" and "Voice Isolation" tools (especially for background noise). **iZotope RX** (paid, but industry-standard) is the gold standard for advanced noise removal and repair.
    *   **Labeling:** AI is less mature here. **Descript** is again great for tagging clips within the transcript view. **Audacity** allows manual labeling via the project window. Dedicated AI labeling tools for audio exist but are often niche or require integration. **Trello** or **Asana** are excellent for project/client organization outside the audio editor.

2.  **Switching to Adobe Audition:**
    *   **Recommendations:** Start small! Focus on learning the core workflow: recording, editing (cut, copy, paste, basic tools), and exporting. Use tutorials specifically for voiceover workflows. Don't try to learn *everything* at once. Leverage Audition's "Compare" window to see before/after edits. Its multi-track editing and automation are powerful.
    *   **Learning Curve:** Yes, it's significant compared to Audacity. Expect a few days to a week of focused learning. The payoff in organization, effects, and processing power is usually worth it for serious VO work.

3.  **Balancing Recording, Editing & Marketing (Avoiding Burnout):**
    *   **Time Blocking:** Schedule dedicated blocks for recording, editing, and marketing. Don't let them bleed into each other. Protect your recording time fiercely.
    *   **Batch Processing:** Record multiple takes/scenes in one session. Edit multiple projects in one session. Market multiple projects in one session.
    *   **Set Realistic Goals:** Don't try to do everything perfectly on the first pass. Aim for "good enough" for initial delivery, then refine later if needed.
    *   **The Pomodoro Technique:** Work in focused 25-50 minute sprints followed by short breaks (5-10 mins). Get up, stretch, look away from the screen.
    *   **Schedule Breaks & Meals:** **This is CRITICAL.** Treat meals like important recording sessions. Set alarms if needed. A quick 10-minute break every hour or so is better than pushing through exhaustion.
    *   **Automate & Streamline:** Use templates for projects, consistent file naming, and tools like Descript for fast edits.
    *   **Separate Work & Life:** Have a clear start/end time. Avoid checking emails/social media constantly outside work hours.
