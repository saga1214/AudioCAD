Here's a concise summary of the key advice for feeding and caring for your cats while managing your budget:

1.  **Control Access to Food (For Feeding):**
    *   **Use Microchip Feeders:** Only your specific cats can access their food.
    *   **Use Timed Feeders:** Releases food at scheduled times (e.g., multiple small meals).
    *   **Separate Feeding Spaces:** Feed fussy cats in a different room or location away from greedy ones.
    *   **Feed Just Before You Leave:** Ensures everyone gets a chance *before* you go.

2.  **Manage Food Costs & Quality (For Nutrition):**
    *   **Buy in Bulk:** Look for larger bags of quality food that offer lower cost per serving.
    *   **Mix Fillers (Carefully):** Add small amounts of safe, plain, cooked plain protein (chicken, fish) or unseasoned rice to stretch out the more expensive food.
    *   **Seek Donations:** Check local shelters, rescues, or community groups for pet food donations.
    *   **Prioritize Needs:** Focus on getting the absolute basics (protein, fat, vitamins) even if the food isn't gourmet.

3.  **Prevent Illness at Home (For Health):**
    *   **Keep Environment Clean:** Regularly wash food/water bowls and litter boxes.
    *   **Ensure Fresh Water:** Always have clean water available.
    *   **Brush Regularly:** Helps with hairballs and skin health.
    *   **Supplement Safely:** Use small amounts of *safe* table scraps (plain cooked meat/fish, no onions/garlic/salt).
    *   **Monitor Closely:** Watch for early signs of illness (lethargy, changes in appetite/bowels). Catching problems early is crucial.
    *   **Focus on Prevention:** Good hygiene and basic nutrition are your best defenses.

4.  **Prioritize Essentials:**
    *   **Vet Care:** Use low-cost clinics for *essential* procedures like spaying/neutering and core vaccinations. Save for *urgent* health issues.

**Key Takeaway:** Focus on controlled feeding, stretch your food budget smartly, maintain basic hygiene, monitor closely for problems, and prioritize essential vet care for preventative measures and emergencies. Small, consistent actions make a big difference.
