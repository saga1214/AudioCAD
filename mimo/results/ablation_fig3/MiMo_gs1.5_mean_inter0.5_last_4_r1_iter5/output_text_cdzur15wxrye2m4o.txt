No, **using your friend's car ramps on your own identical vehicle does not guarantee safety**. The critical factor is **not the car, but the surface it's on**. Here's why:

1.  **Surface Stability is Paramount:** Car ramps are designed to work **only on perfectly flat, level ground**. Even a "tiny bit" of slope creates instability. Your car's weight can cause it to roll, especially if it's a heavy vehicle or if you shift your weight while working.
2.  **Your Driveway's Slope is the Problem:** You explicitly stated your driveway is sloped. This makes it inherently unsafe to park on for this task, regardless of which ramps you use. Your friend's ramps won't magically make your sloped driveway flat.
3.  **Ramps Don't "Know" the Car:** The ramps themselves don't care *what* car is on them. They rely solely on the stability of the surface they're placed on. An identical car on a slope is just as dangerous as your car on a slope.

**The critical safety rule remains: You MUST park on a completely flat and level surface.**

*   **Best Solution:** Find a flat spot in your driveway or yard (if possible) and use the ramps there. If your driveway is too sloped, consider using a flat parking lot or a friend's driveway.
*   **Alternative:** If finding a truly flat spot isn't possible, the *only* safe method is to use a **proper floor jack** and **jack stands** on the solid ground of your driveway. The jack lifts the car, and the stands securely hold it in place, preventing any rolling. This requires more effort but is the only way to safely work on a sloped surface.

**Do not rely on the slope being "tiny" or the car being identical to compensate for the unsafe surface.** The risk of a rollover is very real and can lead to serious injury or damage. Always prioritize a flat, level surface for this job.
