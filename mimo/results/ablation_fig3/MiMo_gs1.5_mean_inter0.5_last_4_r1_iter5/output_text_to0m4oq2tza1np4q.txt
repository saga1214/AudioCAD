That's a fantastic way to use them! Pickles are versatile and add great flavor, crunch, and even some probiotics. Here are some tasty ideas using your pickles and pickle juice:

1.  **Classic Pickle Spears:** The simplest! Serve them as a low-calorie, crunchy snack or with cheese, crackers, or chicken for a quick appetizer or light lunch.
2.  **Pickle Juice "Garnish" for Salmon:** Your salmon marinade idea is perfect! The bright acidity and tang of pickle juice pair *amazingly* with rich salmon. A splash in the marinade or a drizzle on top after cooking will be delicious. *(Bonus: It's a great low-sodium alternative to some dressings!)*
3.  **Pickle Juice Pickled Eggs:** Turn your regular hard-boiled eggs into a tangy, briny snack. Slice them in half, fill with pickle juice, and let them sit in the fridge for a few hours or overnight.
4.  **Pickle Juice Vinaigrette:** Dilute pickle juice with olive oil (maybe 1:3 ratio) and a touch of mustard, honey/maple syrup, salt, and pepper. It's a unique, tangy, and refreshing salad dressing.
5.  **Pickle Juice Marinade for Meat/Fish:** Beyond salmon, pickle juice works well as a marinade for chicken, pork chops, or even tofu. The acidity helps tenderize and adds a great flavor punch.
6.  **Pickle Juice "Pickled" Veggies:** While technically already pickled, you can use the juice as the base for quick-pickling other veggies. Thin the juice slightly with water or vinegar and submerge sliced cucumbers, onions, or bell peppers for a quick pickled side.
7.  **Pickle Juice Vodka Pickle Spears:** For a cocktail! Mix pickle juice with vodka, a splash of lemon juice, and a touch of simple syrup (optional). Garnish with a pickle spear. (A unique twist on a classic pickled spear!)
8.  **Pickle Juice Vinaigrette for Grains:** Toss cooked quinoa, farro, or rice with a pickle juice vinaigrette, chopped cucumber, red onion, and fresh herbs for a bright, flavorful grain bowl.
9.  **Pickle Juice "Pickled" Potato Salad:** Replace some of the mayo/vinegar in your potato salad recipe with pickle juice for a tangy twist.
10. **Pickle Juice Vinaigrette for Roasted Veggies:** Toss roasted potatoes, Brussels sprouts, or cauliflower with a pickle juice vinaigrette before serving for a zesty finish.
11. **Pickle Juice Vinaigrette for Pasta:** A lighter alternative to pesto or cream sauces. Toss cooked pasta with a pickle juice vinaigrette and some fresh greens or chopped veggies.
12. **Pickle Juice "Pickled" Everything:** Use the juice as the liquid base for quick-pickling onions, carrots, radishes, or even jalapeños for salads, sandwiches, or tacos.

**Tips for Using Pickles & Juice:**

*   **Low-Sodium Option:** Choose low-sodium or no-salt-added pickles if you're being extra mindful, but remember the juice will still contain sodium.
*   **Flavor Balance:** Pickle juice can be quite strong! Start with less in marinades, vinaigrettes, or dressings and adjust to your taste.
*   **Freshness:** Homemade pickled items (like the eggs or quick-pickled veggies) are best eaten within a few days to a week.
*   **Garden Synergy:** If you're growing herbs like dill, chives, or cilantro, they pair wonderfully with pickles!
