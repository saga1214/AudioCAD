That's a great question. The key is to understand that public resistance is an ethical issue. So, while the vaccination campaign is still the most medically urgent and effective, the high level of resistance creates a major ethical problem. You're essentially trying to enforce a public health measure against a strong public will. This conflict could be very damaging to the government's credibility and could lead to a situation where you're trying to force compliance, which is never ideal.

So, to answer your question, yes, the high ethical weight of public resistance would make me reconsider. The vaccination campaign is more urgent, but the sugar campaign avoids that ethical minefield. It's a proactive health promotion campaign, not a reactive one trying to overcome deep-seated distrust. Therefore, if the government's long-term survival and trust are paramount, the sugar campaign might be the safer, more sustainable choice.
