Based on current scientific understanding, **the single most important factor driving the increase in obesity rates is the easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods.** Here's why:

1.  **Overpowering Hunger Signals:** These foods are engineered to be hyper-palatable – meaning they are excessively salty, sweet, fatty, and often combined in ways that override our natural satiety (fullness) mechanisms. We eat more than we need without feeling satisfied.
2.  **Low Nutrient Density:** They provide a lot of calories but very few nutrients (vitamins, minerals, fiber). This leads to poor nutritional quality even if calorie intake isn't drastically higher than before.
3.  **Constant Availability & Marketing:** They are cheap, readily available in vast quantities (convenience stores, restaurants, fast food), and heavily marketed, making them the default choice for many people.
4.  **Displacement of Healthier Options:** They crowd out the space in our diets for less calorie-dense, more nutrient-rich whole foods (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins).
5.  **Foundation for Other Factors:** While inactivity, stress, sleep, and genetics contribute, the *availability* and *palatability* of these foods make it incredibly difficult to maintain a healthy weight consistently, even for people with good intentions and some activity.

**Why other factors, while important, aren't the *single* biggest driver:**

*   **Physical Inactivity:** Crucial for maintaining weight, but processed foods create the calorie surplus that inactivity struggles to burn off alone.
*   **Stress & Sleep:** Affect hormones regulating appetite and metabolism, but processed foods provide the readily available calories that stress often leads us to crave.
*   **Genetics:** Influences susceptibility, but doesn't cause obesity; environmental factors like food availability are key triggers.
*   **Portion Sizes:** While portion control is vital, the *type* of food consumed within that portion matters immensely (calorie density).

**In essence:** The massive shift towards a diet dominated by ultra-processed foods, engineered to be highly palatable and calorie-dense, creates a fundamental challenge to maintaining a healthy weight. It makes it biologically and behaviorally difficult to consume calories within the limits needed for a healthy body weight, even with some activity. This environmental shift is the core driver of the modern obesity epidemic.
