You're absolutely right. Older comics from the forties and fifties are generally much rarer because of lower printing numbers and the fact that they degrade over time. That makes them a much more stable investment. I can give you ten of the most iconic and valuable ones.

First, the ultimate grail: *Action Comics* number one. It's the first appearance of Superman and the very first comic ever published by DC. A perfect copy recently sold for six million dollars.

Next, *Detective Comics* number twenty-seven, the first appearance of Batman. It's one of the most famous and valuable Batman keys.

Another huge one is *Superman* number one. It's the first Superman comic and is incredibly scarce.

Then there's *All-Star Comics* number eight. This is the first appearance of the Justice League of America.

For Marvel, *Strange Tales* number one is the first appearance of the Human Torch, one of the first Marvel superheroes.

*Whiz Comics* number one is another valuable book from the Golden Age.

For a first appearance of a major villain, *Detective Comics* number thirty-three is the first appearance of the Joker.

If you're a Batman fan, *Batman* number one is a must-have, as it's the first dedicated Batman comic.

*The Flash* number one, from 1940, is the first appearance of the Flash, one of the first DC heroes to get his own solo comic.

And finally, *Aquaman* number one, from 1953, is the first appearance of Aquaman, who was considered a joke character for a long time but has become a major hero.

So, to recap, for Marvel, you should focus on books like *Strange Tales* number one, *Whiz Comics* number one, *Tales of Suspense* number fifty, which is the first appearance of Doctor Strange, and *Tales to Astonish* number twenty-two, the first appearance of Ant-Man. For DC, the absolute holy grails are *Action Comics* number one and *Detective Comics* number twenty-seven.
