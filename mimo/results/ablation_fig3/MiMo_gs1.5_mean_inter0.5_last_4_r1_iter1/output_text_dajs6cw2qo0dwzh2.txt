That's a really interesting counterfactual question. You're right that the Portuguese were already making significant progress. But the fall of Constantinople in 1453 was still a huge catalyst. It removed the overland route and, more importantly, signaled a major shift in power. This made European rulers more desperate and willing to invest in the risky Portuguese sea voyages, and it also intensified the competition among kingdoms like Spain and England. So, while the Age of Discovery might have eventually happened, the timing and the intensity of the effort were likely accelerated and amplified by that specific event.
