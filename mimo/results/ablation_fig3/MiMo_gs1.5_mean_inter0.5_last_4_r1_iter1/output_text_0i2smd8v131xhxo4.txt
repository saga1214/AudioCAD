Here's a breakdown of your questions, along with recommendations:

**Switching to Adobe Audition:**
1.  **Gradual Approach:** Don't try to learn everything at once. Start with the Waveform panel, basic editing (cut, copy, paste), and the Pen Tool for drawing volume adjustments. Master these before moving to complex effects.
2.  **Utilize Resources:** Adobe's official tutorials and forums are excellent. Search YouTube for "Adobe Audition beginner tutorial" or specific tasks ("Adobe Audition noise reduction," "Adobe Audition speed ramp"). Many voiceover communities share tips.
3.  **Focus on Workflow:** Identify your most frequent tasks (noise reduction, leveling, editing mistakes, applying effects). Learn the keyboard shortcuts for these specifically. This will speed things up significantly once you master them.
4.  **Expect Initial Time Investment:** Learning a new DAW takes time. Be patient, but focus on the long-term efficiency gains. The initial slowdown will pay off quickly as you become proficient.

**Reliable AI Tools:**
*   **Trimming Silence:** Adobe Audition's built-in "Noise Reduction" and "De-Esser" are powerful. iZotope RX (especially RX 10/11) is the gold standard for AI-powered noise reduction and repair, far more advanced than basic DAW tools. Audacity also has decent AI-based noise reduction plugins now.
*   **Labeling:** Adobe Audition's "Speech-to-Text" feature (requires subscription) is surprisingly good for basic transcription and labeling. iZotope's RX also has AI-powered speech transcription. Dedicated transcription services (Rev, Otter.ai) often integrate with workflows.
*   **Important Note:** AI tools are aids, not replacements for good recording and editing. Always listen critically and verify AI results. Don't rely solely on them.

**Balancing Recording, Editing, & Marketing:**
*   **Time Blocking:** Schedule dedicated marketing time (social media, website updates, outreach) *and* dedicated recording/editing time. Treat marketing like a recording session block.
*   **Batch Processing:** Group similar tasks. Record several characters or scenes back-to-back. Edit similar projects together.
*   **Automate Marketing:** Use email sequences, social media schedulers (Buffer, Hootsuite), and CRM tools to automate repetitive marketing tasks.
*   **Set Boundaries:** Define work hours. Turn off work notifications during personal time. Schedule breaks and meals *before* they become an afterthought.
*   **Outsource:** Consider outsourcing marketing tasks (graphic design, video editing, social media management) if possible.
*   **Prioritize:** Focus on high-impact marketing activities that directly lead to work (e.g., outreach, targeted ads) over vanity metrics.
