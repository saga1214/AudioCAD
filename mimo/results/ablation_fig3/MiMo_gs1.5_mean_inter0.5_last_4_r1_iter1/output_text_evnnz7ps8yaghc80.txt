Okay, you've got a solid plan. Let's create a weekly schedule to keep you on track. It's important to be consistent but not to overwhelm yourself. Here’s a balanced routine you can try for about four to six weeks.

First, let's set some general goals. The main one is to successfully transition from juggling two beanbags to three. You should also aim to improve your rhythm, reduce stress, and have fun. For your equipment, you'll need your beanbags, a pair of scarves, and a metronome or some music. A timer is also helpful.

Now for the schedule. Let's aim for about five to six days a week, with two days off to avoid burnout. Each session should last about 15 to 30 minutes to keep it manageable.

Here’s a possible weekly plan:
On Monday, you could start with a five-minute warm-up, just tossing the beanbags gently. Then, spend ten minutes practicing the first three throws with two balls in one hand, like we discussed. After that, for five minutes, add the third ball from your other hand and just focus on the rhythm, not catching. Finally, take five minutes to experiment with scarves, practicing slow, controlled throws.

Tuesday could be similar. Warm up for five minutes, then spend ten minutes on the first three throws with your dominant hand. The next ten minutes could be for catching, where you focus on soft, controlled hands. Then, spend five minutes on your weaker hand to build up that side.

Wednesday is a rest day. You could do some light stretching or just reflect on what went well.

On Thursday, warm up for five minutes. Then, spend ten minutes on the first three throws. For the next ten minutes, try adding the third ball and practicing a steady rhythm. Finally, spend five minutes with scarves, focusing on timing with a metronome.

Friday is another rest day. Maybe watch a juggling tutorial for inspiration.

On Saturday, warm up, then spend ten minutes on the first three throws with your dominant hand. For the next ten minutes, try juggling all three beanbags at once, even if it's just for a few seconds before they drop. Then, spend five minutes with scarves, practicing the cascade pattern.

Sunday is a full rest day. You can plan for the next week or just relax.

A few key tips for success: always start with a warm-up to avoid injury. If stress builds up, it's okay to stop and take a break. And remember to track your progress in a journal to see how you're improving.

If you find this routine too much, just scale it back. The goal is consistency, not perfection. You've got this, and your son will be so proud when you master it!
