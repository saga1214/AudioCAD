Of course. Here's a way you could structure your email to strike that balance between respect and being direct. You'll need to fill in the specifics, but this gives you a clear framework.

First, start with a clear subject line. Something like: "Meeting Request: Exam Feedback for [Course Name and Section]".

Then, in the body of the email, you can start with a polite greeting, like "Dear Professor [Last Name],"

After that, state your purpose. You could say something like: "I hope this email finds you well. I'm writing to request a brief meeting to discuss my recent exam score on [Date] for [Course Name]."

Next, state your goal. Explain that your primary goal is to learn how to improve for the next assignment, but also mention you'd like to understand the grading criteria. You could say: "My main goal is to understand how I could have improved on the essay to do better on future exams. I'd also appreciate any insight you could share about the grading criteria for this type of assessment."

It's also a good idea to acknowledge his teaching. You could add a line like: "I know your teaching is rigorous, and I'm committed to meeting your expectations."

Then, you can ask about scheduling. Just ask if he has 10 to 15 minutes available sometime soon, and mention that his office hours might be difficult because of your class schedule.

Finally, close the email politely. Something like: "Thank you for your time and guidance. I look forward to your response."

Then sign off with "Sincerely," and your name.

So, to recap the key points: keep it professional, focus on learning and future improvement, be specific about the exam, and frame it as a request for guidance, not a challenge to his grading.
