You've hit on a fascinating aspect of human vision! Let's clarify this using precise terminology:

### 1. **Saccadic Masking & Eye Physiology**
- **Saccade**: A rapid, ballistic eye movement lasting 100–200 milliseconds (ms) to shift gaze between points. Pronounced: **suh-KAH-dee-k** (emphasis on first syllable).
- **Saccadic Masking**: During saccades, the visual system *temporarily suppresses* incoming visual data (blurring motion) to prevent conflicting signals when the eyes stabilize. This creates a ~20-30ms "gap" in conscious perception.
- **Photoreceptor Sampling**: The eye's retinal cells (rods/cones) detect changes in light. However, they don't capture a "frame" per se—they respond to *temporal changes* in light intensity. The eye is a biological sensor, not a camera.

### 2. **Brain Processing vs. Conscious Perception**
- **Visual Cortex**: Processes visual data hierarchically. Early areas (V1) detect edges/motion; higher areas (V5/MT) integrate motion across time.
- **Temporal Resolution**: The brain's "frame rate" is debated but likely **~60-80Hz** (60–80 ms) for conscious motion perception. Below this, motion appears stuttery (e.g., 24fps feels "cinematic" vs. 10fps).
- **Perception ≠ Reality**: We don't perceive discrete "frames." Instead:
  - The brain *predicts* what we see based on prior input (predictive coding).
  - It *fills gaps* during saccades (e.g., using prior/expected motion).
  - It *smooths motion* by extrapolating between sampled points.

### 3. **"Continuous" Vision?**
- **No**: The eye does **not** see continuously. It samples rapidly but intermittently (via saccades).
- **Yes (in practice)**: The brain constructs a *continuous percept* by:
  - Blending images during saccades.
  - Filling motion gaps (e.g., a bird in flight appears smooth despite eye sampling).
  - Filtering noise (e.g., ignoring irrelevant details).

### 4. **Film vs. Reality**
- **Cinema (24fps)**: Matches the brain's temporal resolution. Motion feels "real" but stylized.
- **Video (60fps)**: Smoother but can feel "real-time." Excessively high frame rates (e.g., 120fps) may appear unnatural if not used artistically.
- **Saccadic Influence**: If filming relies on rapid head movement (e.g., tracking shots), saccadic masking can break continuity. This is why handheld camera work feels "raw" but unstable.

### Key Insight
**The eye captures data discontinuously; the brain constructs continuity.** This is why:
- 24fps feels "cinematic" (matches brain's sampling rate).
- Saccades create perceptual gaps (masked by neural processing).
- High frame rates feel "real" but lack artistic weight unless intentionally used.
