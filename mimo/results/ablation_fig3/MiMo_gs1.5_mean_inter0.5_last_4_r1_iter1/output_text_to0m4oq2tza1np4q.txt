That's a fantastic question! Pickle juice is actually a surprisingly versatile ingredient, especially with its tangy flavor and electrolyte boost (sodium). Here are some delicious and healthy ways to use it, keeping your goals in mind:

1.  **Quick & Simple Marinade:**
    *   **Base:** Use pickle juice straight or mix it with a little olive oil (like in your salmon idea!).
    *   **Add-ins:** Whisk in a splash of apple cider vinegar, a pinch of garlic powder, onion powder, dried dill, or black pepper.
    *   **For:** Chicken breasts, pork chops, shrimp, tofu, or even roasted potatoes. Marinate for 30 mins to a few hours. *Great for adding flavor without needing lots of added salt.*

2.  **Savory Breakfast Boost:**
    *   Stir a small amount (1-2 tsp) into scrambled eggs or tofu scramble just before serving. The tang cuts through the richness beautifully.
    *   Add a teaspoon to a bowl of oatmeal or cream of wheat for a surprising twist.

3.  **Post-Run Recovery Drink (Moderation Key!):**
    *   **Electrolyte Mix:** Dilute pickle juice significantly with water (e.g., 1/4 cup juice to 1 cup water) and add a squeeze of lemon. This provides sodium quickly. *Important:* Since you're mindful of salt, this is a *small* amount compared to sports drinks. Use it sparingly and focus on whole foods for main hydration.

4.  **Savory Snack Dip:**
    *   Mix pickle juice with plain Greek yogurt, sour cream, or cottage cheese. Add a little chopped fresh dill or chives. A healthy, tangy dip for veggies, whole-grain crackers, or even sliced cucumber.

5.  **Flavor Enhancer for Soups & Stews:**
    *   Stir in a teaspoon or two towards the end of cooking a tomato-based soup, lentil stew, or chili. It adds a layer of savory, complex tang without needing extra salt.

6.  **Vinaigrette Booster:**
    *   Replace *some* of the vinegar in your salad dressing recipe with pickle juice. Start with 1:1 ratio and adjust. It brightens flavors and adds a unique tang. Remember to balance with mustard, oil, and herbs.

7.  **Sauces & Relishes:**
    *   Add it to your homemade aioli or tartar sauce for a tangier kick.
    *   Mix it into a simple relish (finely chop cucumber, onion, and mustard seed) for a quick, crunchy pickle relish.

8.  **Cooking Agent:**
    *   Add a splash when sautéing onions, celery, or bell peppers to deglaze the pan and add depth (like a mini brine).
    *   Use it in quick pickling recipes (like cucumbers or carrots) for a faster pickle.

**Important Considerations for Health-Conscious Use:**

*   **Sodium Content:** Pickle juice is *very* salty. **Always use it in small amounts.** Dilute it (mix with water, yogurt, etc.) whenever possible.
*   **Bloating:** Since you mentioned this, be mindful of how salty foods affect you. If pickle juice seems to make bloating worse, reduce the amount or avoid it.
*   **Acidity:** It's quite acidic. Use it in moderation, especially if you have acid reflux.
*   **Portion Control:** Even healthy additions need balance. A little goes a long way!

Enjoy experimenting! Pickle juice can definitely add a unique and sometimes healthy tang to your cooking if used thoughtfully.
