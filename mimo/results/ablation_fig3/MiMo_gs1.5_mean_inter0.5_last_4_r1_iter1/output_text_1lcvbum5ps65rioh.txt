Here's a breakdown of U-turn penalties in British Columbia and a classic Spaghetti Carbonara recipe:

### U-Turn Penalties in British Columbia
*   **Minor Offence:** Making an illegal U-turn (e.g., not at an intersection, not in a designated U-turn lane, not where visibility is obstructed) is typically a minor traffic offence.
*   **Penalty:** You'll receive a traffic ticket. The standard fine is **$116** plus court costs (usually around $15-$30, bringing the total to approximately **$130-$145**).
*   **Severity:** This is a "summary offence" ticket. You can contest it in court, but you'd need evidence (like a dashcam video) to support your case. A conviction adds points to your driver's license.
*   **Important:** Penalties *can* be higher if the illegal U-turn caused an accident or significantly interfered with traffic flow.

### Spaghetti Carbonara Recipe (Serves 4)
**Ingredients:**

*   **Pasta:** 400g (14 oz) Spaghetti (or Rigatoni/Bucatini)
*   **Meat:** 150g (5 oz) Guanciale (Pancetta is a good substitute) - diced
*   **Eggs:** 3 Large Eggs + 1 Extra Large Egg Yolk (4 total)
*   **Cheese:** 100g (about 1 cup) Pecorino Romano Cheese, finely grated (Parmesan cheese can be used if you don't have Pecorino)
*   **Pepper:** Freshly ground black pepper (lots!)
*   **Olive Oil:** 1-2 tbsp Extra Virgin Olive Oil (optional, for cooking)
*   **Salt:** For pasta water

**Instructions:**

1.  **Cook the Guanciale:** In a large skillet over medium heat, cook the diced guanciale in a little olive oil (if using) until crisp and golden. Don't drain the fat - it's essential for flavor. Remove from heat and set aside.
2.  **Cook the Pasta:** Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a rolling boil. Cook the spaghetti until *al dente* (firm to the bite). **Crucially, reserve at least 1 cup of the starchy pasta water before draining.**
3.  **Make the Egg Mixture:** In a large bowl, whisk together the 3 whole eggs, the extra yolk, the grated Pecorino Romano, and a generous amount of freshly ground black pepper until smooth.
4.  **Combine:** Working quickly off the heat (or over *very* low heat), add the drained pasta to the skillet with the guanciale and its fat. Toss well to coat.
5.  **Temper the Eggs:** Remove the skillet from the heat entirely. **This step is critical:** Pour in about 1/2 cup of the reserved hot pasta water while tossing vigorously. This "waters down" the egg mixture and prevents scrambling. Gradually add more hot pasta water (1/4 cup at a time) while tossing constantly, until the sauce reaches a creamy, silky consistency. The residual heat from the pasta should cook the eggs gently.
6.  **Finish:** Immediately add the egg and cheese mixture to the skillet with the pasta and guanciale. Toss continuously and vigorously for about 1 minute until the cheese is melted and the sauce is perfectly creamy and coats every strand of pasta. If it seems too thick, add a splash more reserved pasta water. If too thin, add a bit more grated Pecorino.
7.  **Serve Immediately:** Plate the carbonara right away. Garnish with extra Pecorino and black pepper.

**Key Tips:**
*   **No Cream:** Authentic Carbonara uses no cream. The creaminess comes from the starchy pasta water and the cheese/egg mixture.
*   **Off the Heat:** Never add the egg mixture to hot pasta *on* the stove burner. The residual heat is enough.
*   **Pasta Water:** This is your secret weapon for achieving the perfect creamy texture.
*   **Guanciale:** This cured pork cheek is traditional and adds incredible depth. Pancetta is the next best thing. Avoid bacon.
