Okay, let's put all of this together into a clear, step-by-step format for you.

First, at the very top, you'll have your name in about a 14-point font. Right below that is your contact information, all in a single line. It should include your email, phone number, city, and state, along with icons. It's better to put this directly under your name rather than to the side.

Next up is a professional summary. This is a short paragraph, maybe two or three sentences, that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals. This is especially important if you have a gap in your work history.

After the summary, you'll have a section for your relevant experience. Here, you list the jobs that are most important for the roles you're targeting now, including the company name, your job title, and the dates you worked there. You can use brief bullet points to describe your achievements.

Then comes your education section. You'll list your degrees, the school's name, and your expected graduation date. It's a good idea to add any honors you received, like 'Highest Honors', right under the degree where you earned them. You can also include any scholarships you got in that same section.

If you have an 'Additional Work History' section, you can use it to list your more recent jobs, but keep it very brief, just with the job title, company, and dates.

Now, about the skills section. It's highly recommended. You should create a separate section for it. List your skills, maybe starting with hard skills like software or technical abilities, and then your soft skills like communication or leadership. It's a good idea to tailor this list to the specific job description you're applying for.

Finally, you can add a section for other achievements, like awards or professional certifications, if they're relevant. And if you have a portfolio or a personal website, you can list that at the very bottom.

This structure should give you a clear path to create a professional and effective resume.
