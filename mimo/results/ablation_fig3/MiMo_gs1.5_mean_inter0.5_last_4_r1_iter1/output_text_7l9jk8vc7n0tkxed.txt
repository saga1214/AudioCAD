If you have a bucket and a length of PVC pipe (like a 4" or 6" piece), grab those too.
3.  **Position & Loosen:**
    *   Place the **pipe wrench** securely on the pipe just below where it enters the drain (or further down if you can reach).
    *   Get a firm grip. Position your body so you can apply steady pressure.
    *   Apply slow, steady pressure with the wrench. Don't yank. Use your body weight. You're trying to **break up and dislodge the clog** at the pipe wall, not just force it through. Rotate the wrench slightly if needed.
4.  **Clear with the Snake:**
    *   Once you feel some resistance loosening, carefully insert the **drain snake** into the drain.
    *   Slowly rotate the snake handle while pushing it in. You're trying to hook or break up the debris.
    *   Keep pushing and rotating until you feel the snake hit the drain pipe itself (usually after a foot or two).
    *   Pull the snake back out slowly, carefully removing the debris it picked up.
5.  **Flush & Test:**
    *   Pour a bucket of **hot water** (as hot as your tap will safely get) down the drain. This helps flush out the loosened debris.
    *   Wait a minute or two.
    *   Test the flow by running water again. If it drains slowly or not at all, you might need to repeat steps 3 & 4 on the next section of pipe down. If water runs freely, you're clear!
6.  **If Still Stuck:**
    *   If water still doesn't run freely after flushing, you might need to repeat steps 3 & 4 further down the pipe.
    *   If you don't have a snake, you'll need to use the pipe wrench method again more aggressively on a section you can reach, or see if you can access the drain from underneath (if you have tools for that).
