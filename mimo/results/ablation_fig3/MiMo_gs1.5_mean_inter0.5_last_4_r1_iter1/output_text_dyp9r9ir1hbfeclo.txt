The "fourth component" you're likely referring to isn't a fourth bone, but rather the **critical role of the scapula (shoulder blade) in enabling rotation**. While the humerus (upper arm bone), scapula, and clavicle form the primary bony structure, the scapula's movement is essential for smooth rotation. Here’s how they work together:

### 1. **Humerus (Upper Arm Bone)**  
   - The **head of the humerus** fits into the **glenoid socket** of the scapula.  
   - **Rotation** occurs as the humeral head moves *around* this shallow socket.  

### 2. **Scapula (Shoulder Blade)**  
   - The **"fourth component"** in motion: The scapula doesn’t stay fixed—it **rotates and glides** on the ribcage.  
   - **Key movements**:  
     - **Protraction** (pulling forward) during external rotation.  
     - **Retraction** (pulling back) during internal rotation.  
     - **Elevation/Depression** (lifting or lowering) during overhead movements.  
   - **Why it matters**: Without scapular movement, the humerus would be unstable, and rotation would be severely limited.  

### 3. **Clavicle (Collarbone)**  
   - Acts as a **stabilizer** between the scapula and sternum.  
   - **Protracts** the scapula during arm extension (e.g., reaching backward).  
   - Ensures the shoulder joint stays aligned during movement.  

### How Rotation Works:  
- **Internal Rotation**: Humerus rotates *inward* (toward the body), while the scapula *retracts* (pulls back).  
- **External Rotation**: Humerus rotates *outward* (away from the body), while the scapula *protracts* (pushes forward).  
- **Overhead Lifting**: Requires coordinated scapular elevation + humeral external rotation.  

### Clinical Insight:  
- **Poor scapular control** (e.g., from rotator cuff injuries or muscle imbalances) causes **rotation dysfunction**.  
- Your friend’s soreness during lateral raises could stem from:  
  - Overuse of rotator cuff muscles.  
  - Inadequate scapular stabilization.  
  - Poor mechanics during the lift.  

**In short**: The humerus rotates *on* the scapula, while the clavicle stabilizes the scapula. The scapula’s own movement is the "fourth" essential element enabling fluid rotation. If the scapula is immobile or misaligned, rotation becomes inefficient and painful.
