Okay, let's break this down. Vasculitis is an autoimmune condition where your immune system mistakenly attacks your blood vessels. This inflammation can affect vessels of any size. For instance, large vessel vasculitis might involve carotid arteries, medium vessel types could affect kidneys or nerves, and small vessel types can impact tiny vessels in your skin, which is why you might see redness, rashes, or even small sores.

The symptoms really depend on where the inflammation is. You might feel unwell with fever, fatigue, or joint pain. Skin issues like redness or rashes are common, especially if the small vessels are involved. Hair and nails can also be affected because poor blood flow to the follicles and nail beds can weaken them, leading to issues with strength and growth.

As for supplements, collagen and keratin are good for structure. Iron and zinc are crucial for overall health and preventing deficiency-related issues. For inflammation, things like omega-3s can help, but always check with your doctor first.

And yes, ibuprofen can help with inflammation, but it's not a long-term solution and can have side effects. A proper diagnosis is key. A dermatologist or rheumatologist can run tests like a biopsy or blood work to pinpoint the cause. They can then recommend the right treatment, which might involve immunosuppressants or other medications.

So, while there are many possible causes for your symptoms, vasculitis is one to investigate. A specialist can help figure out what's really going on and give you the best path forward.
