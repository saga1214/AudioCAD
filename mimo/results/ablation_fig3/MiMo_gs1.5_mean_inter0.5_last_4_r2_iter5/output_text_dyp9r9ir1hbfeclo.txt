1.  **The Humerus (Upper Arm Bone):** The ball-shaped head rotates within the socket.
2.  **The Scapula (Shoulder Blade):** Provides the socket (glenoid cavity) and acts as a platform for the rotator cuff muscles.
3.  **The Clavicle (Collarbone):** Connects the arm to the main torso and stabilizes the shoulder joint.
4.  **The Biceps Tendon (Often included in "components"):** While not technically a bone, the biceps tendon (specifically the long head) is a critical *soft tissue* component that attaches the biceps muscle to the top of the humerus (the greater tubercle of the humerus). This attachment point is crucial for certain types of rotation and overall shoulder stability.

**Why the Biceps Tendon is important for rotation:**

*   **Stabilization:** The biceps tendon helps stabilize the humeral head within the glenoid cavity, especially during movements like rotation and lifting.
*   **Contribution to Rotation:** While the rotator cuff muscles primarily control the direction and speed of rotation, the biceps tendon and muscle play a role in initiating and supporting certain rotational movements, particularly when the arm is extended or in a "dead hang" position.
