Based on your paper's focus and the historical example of the Gardner heist, the **first and most critical line of defense for an art museum is robust access control at all entry points**.  

This isn't just about physical guards—it's a **multi-layered human-machine system** designed to prevent unauthorized access before threats even enter the building. Here’s why:  

### Core Components of Access Control:  
1. **Visible Security Presence**:  
   - Guards at entrances deter crime and act as the first point of human verification.  
   - They assess threats, enforce policies (e.g., no unattended bags), and respond to anomalies.  

2. **Screening Protocols**:  
   - Mandatory bag checks using X-ray machines to identify weapons or suspicious items.  
   - Metal detectors to detect concealed objects.  

3. **Technology Integration**:  
   - Electronic access cards for staff-only areas (e.g., storage, offices).  
   - Turnstiles or sensors on public entrances to track visitors.  

### Why It’s the "First Line":  
- **Prevention Over Reaction**: Access control stops threats *outside* the museum, buying time for internal systems (cameras, alarms) to function.  
- **Human Error Mitigation**: As seen in the Gardner heist (where guards broke protocol by letting fake police officers in), access control layers *force* compliance.  
- **Deterrence**: Visible security and screening make theft harder, discouraging criminals.  

### After the First Line Fails:  
- **Second Layer**: Surveillance systems (cameras, motion sensors) monitor internal spaces.  
- **Third Layer**: Reinforced display cases and mounting systems physically resist theft.  
- **Fourth Layer**: Alarms and rapid-response teams for breach containment.  

### Key Insight for Your Paper:  
Modern security prioritizes **access control as the foundation** because it directly addresses the human vulnerabilities exploited in heists. Without this layer, even advanced tech (like biometric sensors) is useless if someone bypasses entry. The Gardner heist’s legacy underscores this: *control access, and you control the threat*.
