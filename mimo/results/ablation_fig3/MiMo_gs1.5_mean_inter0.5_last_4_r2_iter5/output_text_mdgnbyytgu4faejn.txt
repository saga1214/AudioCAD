Of course. Let's break down a standard, effective resume format. First, at the very top, you'll have your name, in a 14 to 16-point font. To its right, you'll list your contact info, like your email and phone number, and maybe a link to your LinkedIn profile, using about a nine to ten-point font. You can also include your city and state, and icons, but icons can sometimes cause issues with automated tracking systems, so you might want to leave them out.

Right below your name is your professional summary. This is a short, two-to-three sentence paragraph that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals.

Next comes your experience section. This is where you list your jobs, starting with the most recent. For each position, include the company name, your job title, the dates you worked there, and then two to four bullet points that describe your key responsibilities and achievements.

After that is your education section. Here you'll list your degrees, starting with the most recent, along with the school name and your graduation date. You can add honors or scholarships directly under the degree where you earned them.

Then comes that skills section you asked about. This is very important. List your skills in a separate section, grouping them into categories like Technical Skills, Software Proficiency, or Languages if they're relevant. Make sure to tailor this list to match the job description.

If you have significant volunteer work or relevant projects, you can add a section for that as well.

Finally, at the very bottom, you can add a link to your online portfolio if you have one. This whole structure keeps your resume clean, professional, and easy for a recruiter to scan.
