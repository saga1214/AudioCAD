Here's a scenario demonstrating a key difference in offside rules between U.S. high school rules and international soccer rules:

**Scenario:**  
The Red Team is defending. The Blue Team's goalkeeper kicks the ball out for a goal kick. The ball travels directly to Blue Team's forward, number 9, who is in an offside position. Player 9 controls the ball, turns, and shoots, scoring.  

**Why It's an Offside Offense (US High School Rules):**  
Player 9 was in an offside position when the ball was played by the goalkeeper. Since they received the ball directly from a goal kick (per exception #1), this is an offense. The goal is disallowed.  

**How It Differs Under International Rules:**  
Under FIFA rules, a player is **not** considered offside when receiving the ball directly from a goal kick. Player 9 would be legal, and the goal stands.  

**Key Difference Explained:**  
The US high school rules (and NCAA rules) include a stricter exception for goal kicks, treating them as a restart where players are *never* offside. International rules allow offside if a player is in an offside position when receiving the ball from a goalkeeper, even though they are directly receiving it.  

This distinction affects officiating: in US high school soccer, a referee would call an offside offense here, but internationally, the goal would be valid.
