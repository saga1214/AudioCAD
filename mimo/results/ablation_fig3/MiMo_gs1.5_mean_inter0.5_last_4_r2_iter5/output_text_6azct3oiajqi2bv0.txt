That's a smart choice! Nordstrom Rack is great for finding quality winter gear at good prices. To stick your jacket lining without tearing it, here are the best adhesive options, ordered from most recommended to least:

1.  **Sewing Glue (The Gold Standard):**
    *   **Why it's best:** Specifically designed for fabrics. It bonds well to different materials (lining, outer fabric, sometimes even some synthetic trims), is relatively flexible, and holds up well to washing. It's water-resistant once cured.
    *   **How to use:** Clean the sticking area thoroughly with rubbing alcohol on a cotton ball. Apply a *thin* line of glue along the seam or edge you need to stick. Press firmly for 10-15 seconds. Let it cure completely (usually 24-48 hours) before testing wash/dry resistance. *Best for most lining repairs.*

2.  **Fabric Glue Pen (Most Accessible & Precise):**
    *   **Why it's good:** Easy to find (craft stores, online), very precise application with a fine tip, dries clear, and holds well for most fabric-to-fabric bonds. Good for smaller repairs or securing loose threads.
    *   **How to use:** Clean the area. Apply glue in short, controlled strokes along the seam or edge. Press firmly for 10-15 seconds. Dries clear within minutes. *Great for smaller repairs or if sewing glue isn't available.*
    *   **Look for:** Brands like Aleene's Fabric Fusion or E6000 Fabric Glue (the clear version) are popular choices.

3.  **E6000 Fabric Glue (Very Strong & Durable):**
    *   **Why it's strong:** An extremely strong, clear, flexible adhesive that bonds well to many fabrics and synthetic trims. It's waterproof and durable once cured.
    *   **How to use:** Clean the area. Apply a *very thin* line of glue. Press firmly for 10-15 seconds. **Crucial:** Let it cure for at least 24 hours (often longer) before testing wash/dry resistance. It can take longer to cure fully. *Best for critical repairs needing maximum strength, but requires patience.*

**Key Tips for Success (Regardless of Adhesive):**

*   **Clean is Crucial:** Always clean the sticking area with rubbing alcohol (isopropyl alcohol) on a cotton ball or swab. This removes oils, dirt, and residues that prevent adhesion.
*   **Less is More:** Apply glue in thin, even lines or dots. Too much glue can seep into the fabric, become sticky, or cause the lining to stretch or sag over time.
*   **Press Firmly:** After applying glue, press the fabric pieces firmly together for at least 10-15 seconds. Use your fingers or a roller.
*   **Test Before Washing:** Let the glue cure completely (follow the product instructions, usually 24-72 hours) before testing the repaired area in water or a dryer. Sometimes a small test wash in a sink is wise.
*   **Patience with Cure Time:** Especially with strong glues like E6000, allow the full recommended cure time before stressing or washing the garment.
*   **Avoid Stretching:** Be gentle when pulling the pieces together to stick them. Avoid stretching the fabric excessively.

**Recommendation:** For a jacket lining, **Sewing Glue** is often the most reliable and versatile choice. If that's not available, a **Fabric Glue Pen** is the next best thing for precision. **E6000** is excellent if you need maximum strength and durability and are willing to wait for the full cure time.
