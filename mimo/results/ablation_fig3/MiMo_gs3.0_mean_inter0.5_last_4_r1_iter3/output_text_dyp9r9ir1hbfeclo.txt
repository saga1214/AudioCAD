The fourth key component working with the shoulder for rotation is the **rotator cuff muscles**. Specifically, these four muscles stabilize and guide the humerus (upper arm bone) within the shallow socket of the scapula (shoulder blade):

1.  **Supraspinatus:** Initiates abduction (lifting the arm out to the side).
2.  **Infraspinatus:** Controls external (outward) rotation and abduction.
3.  **Teres Minor:** Assists the infraspinatus with external rotation.
4.  **Subscapularis:** Controls internal (inward) rotation and adduction (bringing arm in).

**Why they are crucial for rotation:**
*   They work as a dynamic "cuff" surrounding the shoulder joint.
*   They prevent the humeral head from dislocating while allowing precise rotational movements.
*   They coordinate the rotation initiated by the bones (humerus, scapula) and assisted by other muscles.

So, the full system is: **Humerus (Upper Arm Bone) + Scapula (Shoulder Blade) + Clavicle (Collarbone) + the four Rotator Cuff Muscles.** The cuff muscles are essential for controlling and stabilizing the rotation happening between the humerus and scapula.
