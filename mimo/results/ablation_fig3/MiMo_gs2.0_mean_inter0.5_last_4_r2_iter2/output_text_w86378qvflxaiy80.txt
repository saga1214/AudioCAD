That's the ultimate question. While the value of ecosystem services and medical resources are incredibly important, I would argue the most profound reason is the preservation of unique evolutionary history and the irreplaceable loss of knowledge.

Think of it this way: each unique plant and animal represents millions of years of evolution. When it goes extinct, that entire story, that unique genetic code, is lost forever. We will never know what medical breakthroughs might have come from that creature. We will never understand its role in its ecosystem. It's like burning a library of knowledge that we haven't even had a chance to read yet. Protecting hotspots is fundamentally about preventing this permanent, irreversible loss of Earth's biological heritage.
