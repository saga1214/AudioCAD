You're absolutely right to focus on that intersection—**Quincy Jones** is the standout here, but **Sia** and **Gamble & Huff** also excel at this. Here’s why each matters:

### 1. **Quincy Jones**  
   - **Why?** His work with Bruce Swedien (e.g., *Thriller*, *Off the Wall*) transformed songs into *sonic experiences*.  
   - **Key Insight:** He uses **production as storytelling**. For example:  
     - In Michael Jackson’s "Billie Jean," the silence *before* the bass drop creates tension.  
     - The layered vocals and reverb in "Human Nature" evoke nostalgia and intimacy.  
   - **Lesson:** Structure isn’t just about lyrics/melody—it’s how silence, space, and sonic texture shape emotion.  

### 2. **Sia**  
   - **Why?** She crafts anthemic, soulful pop with dramatic dynamic shifts.  
   - **Key Insight:** Her production (e.g., "Chandelier," "Titanium") uses:  
     - **Contrast:** Soaring synths vs. sparse verses to build emotional release.  
     - **Vocal Layers:** Adding depth and vulnerability (e.g., the whispered vocals in "Unstoppable").  
   - **Lesson:** Production choices amplify the *journey* of a song—making climaxes feel earned.  

### 3. **Gamble & Huff**  
   - **Why?** Their **Philly Soul** productions (e.g., "Every Little Bit Hurts" by Harold Melvin & the Blue Notes) prove groove drives emotion.  
   - **Key Insight:** They used:  
     - **Rhythmic Nuance:** Tight basslines and horn stabs to create urgency or tenderness.  
     - **Dynamic Swings:** Soft verses exploding into full-band choruses for catharsis.  
   - **Lesson:** Groove isn’t just "feel good"—it’s a tool for emotional pacing.  

### Bonus: Max Martin  
While known for melody/hook perfection, his production is equally surgical. Study how he uses:  
- **Subtle Auto-Tune** as an emotional texture (e.g., Justin Bieber’s "Boyfriend").  
- **Piano/Pad Swells** to create warmth in minimalist tracks (e.g., Harry Styles’ "Watermelon Sugar").
