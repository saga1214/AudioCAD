Of course, let's go through all the wines we've discussed and I'll give you the storage and serving temperatures for each, plus I'll point out which one is best for hot or cold weather.

First, the high-end Pinot Noir. You should store it at cellar temperature, around 55 degrees Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius. For serving, it's best slightly below room temp, so aim for 55 to 60 degrees Fahrenheit, or about 13 to 16 Celsius. This is great for cooler weather.

Next up is Japanese Hon-Mirin. You should store it at a cool place temperature, around 60 to 68 degrees Fahrenheit, or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you sip it directly, room temperature works well, which is about 68 to 75 degrees Fahrenheit, or 20 to 24 Celsius. It's not really a hot or cold-weather drink.

Then we have Italian wines. For a Chianti red, you should store it at 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius, and serve it just below room temp, around 60 to 65 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 18 Celsius. For a Pinot Grigio, you should store it at 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, or 10 to 13 Celsius, and serve it chilled, around 45 to 50 Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius.

We also talked about mulled wine served hot. Before you warm it up, you store the base red wine at normal cellar temps of about 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius.

So, to sum it all up, for hot weather, the best choice is the Pinot Grigio. For cold weather, the best choice is the Pinot Noir. Hope that helps you plan your next glass!
