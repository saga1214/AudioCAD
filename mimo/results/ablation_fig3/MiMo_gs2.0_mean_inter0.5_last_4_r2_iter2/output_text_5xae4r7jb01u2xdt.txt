Let's talk about a difference in high school versus college soccer. In high school, an indirect free kick is given for a high kick, like when a player kicks number nine of the red team in the head. Number ten then passes the ball to number nine, who scores.

Now, in college soccer, a direct free kick is given for that same high kick. Number ten takes the direct free kick and passes the ball directly to number nine, who scores.

The difference is this: In college soccer, the direct free kick is the restart. The offside rule applies at the moment the ball is kicked. In this case, number nine was not in an offside position when number ten kicked the ball, so it's a valid goal. In high school soccer, the indirect free kick means play must continue until the ball is touched. So, number nine was in an offside position when number ten received the ball and passed it. That makes it an offside offense, and the goal is disallowed.

The key difference is that a direct free kick in college means the offside rule is checked immediately. An indirect free kick in high school means play continues, and the offside rule is only checked when the ball is finally played to an attacking player.
