Based on the evidence, the **single biggest top driver behind increasing obesity rates** is:  
**Easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods that are cheap, convenient, and heavily marketed.**  

### Why this stands out:  
1. **Calorie Density & Portion Control**: Processed foods (like chips, sugary drinks, and fast food) pack excessive calories into small, easy-to-eat portions. This makes it effortless to consume large amounts of calories without feeling full.  
2. **Nutrient Deficiency**: They’re often low in protein, fiber, and nutrients, so people eat more to satisfy hunger without gaining satiety.  
3. **Marketing & Availability**: Aggressive advertising and ubiquity in stores, restaurants, and even schools make these foods the default choice.  
4. **Synergy with Inactivity**: While inactivity contributes, processed foods *accelerate* weight gain even for active people if portions are unchecked.  

### Other significant factors (but secondary to processed food access):  
- **Physical inactivity**: Modern sedentary lifestyles reduce calorie burn.  
- **Stress/sleep disruption**: Affects metabolism and appetite regulation.  
- **Genetics/hormones**: Influence individual susceptibility.  
- **Socioeconomic factors**: Food deserts limit access to fresh, affordable whole foods.  

### Key nuance:  
- **Calorie density matters, but processed foods are unique** because they combine high calories with poor satiety + aggressive marketing.  
- **Unprocessed calorie-dense foods** (e.g., nuts, oils) can still lead to weight gain if overeaten, but they’re harder to overconsume in the same way due to natural satiety signals.  

In short, while diet, activity, and genetics all play roles, the **systemic availability and design of processed foods** create the perfect storm for rising obesity rates.
