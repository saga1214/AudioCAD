Of course. A structured plan is a great idea. Here’s a weekly schedule to help you.

For your first two days, focus on just one hand. Spend about 15 to 20 minutes juggling two beanbags in your dominant hand, really feeling that rhythm. Then, spend another 15 to 20 minutes adding the tennis balls, trying to get clean catches. After that, spend the last 10 minutes gently trying with three beanbags.

For the next two days, you can mix things up. Start with 15 minutes of one-hand beanbag practice. Then, spend 15 minutes with scarves, slowly practicing a basic pattern. Finally, spend 15 minutes trying to juggle the three beanbags again.

On your third day off, take a break from beanbags and scarves. Instead, watch a short video of someone juggling three beanbags and just focus on the pattern. No need to try yourself, just observe.

On your fourth day, try the one-hand beanbag practice again. After that, spend about 20 minutes juggling scarves, really focusing on your timing. Then, try for about 15 minutes to juggle three beanbags.

On your fifth day, just do a quick 10-minute warm-up with your one-hand beanbag skill. Then, try juggling tennis balls for 15 minutes. After that, spend 15 minutes practicing your three beanbag throws, maybe even just focusing on the feeling of catching them.

On your sixth day, you can do a mix. Spend 15 minutes with beanbags, 15 minutes with scarves, and 15 minutes with tennis balls.

Finally, on your seventh day, take a full rest day. You can optionally watch some juggling videos just for fun, but no actual practice.

Remember to be patient with yourself. Juggling is a journey, and the stress you mentioned will fade as you get more comfortable. Celebrate every small win!
