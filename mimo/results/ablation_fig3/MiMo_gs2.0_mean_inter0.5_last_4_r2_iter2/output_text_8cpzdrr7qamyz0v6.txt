Let's break down your scenario using your parameters:  
- **Buy stock at $62.87 per share**  
- **Stop-loss at $90 per share**  
- **Target sell price at $110 per share**  
- **Stop-loss loss limit: 10%**  
- **Target gain: 10%**  

### 1. **If the stock moves to $110 (10% gain):**
   - **Gain per share** = $110 - $62.87 = **$47.13**  
   - **Total gain** = $47.13 × shares owned.  
   - **Result**: You make **at least 10%** on your investment.  

### 2. **If the stock falls to $90 (10% loss):**
   - **Loss per share** = $62.87 - $90 = **-$27.13**  
   - **Total loss** = $27.13 × shares owned.  
   - **Result**: You lose **up to 10%** (but note: this is $27.13 per share, not 10% of $10,000).  

### Key Notes:
- **Leverage**: If you use margin (borrowing to buy stocks), losses/gains are amplified.  
- **Probability**: Hitting $110 in a month is possible but **not guaranteed**. Volatility, news, or market shifts could prevent this.  
- **Stop-loss mechanics**: If the stock hits $90, you’re automatically sold. You **cannot** hold longer to wait for recovery.  

### Example Calculation:
- **Invest $10,000** → Buy **~159 shares** ($62.87 × 159 ≈ $10,000).  
  - **Target gain**: 10% → **$1,000 profit** ($10,000 → $11,000).  
  - **Stop-loss loss**: 10% → **-$1,000 loss** ($10,000 → $9,000).  

### Why Futures Are Different:
If you trade futures (e.g., stock index futures), your loss/gain is **much larger** due to leverage. For example:  
- A $10,000 margin could control $100,000+ in assets.  
- A 10% move would yield **~10% gain/loss** on your *margin*, but the *total loss/gain* could be 10x larger.  

### Bottom Line:
- **Stocks**: You achieve your targets if the price hits $90 or $110.  
- **Real-world risk**: Short-term trading is volatile. Never invest money you can’t afford to lose.  

If you explore futures, start with paper trading (simulators) to understand leverage risks. Always consult a financial advisor before investing.
