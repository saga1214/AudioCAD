Okay, you want a complete breakdown. Let's go through the standard structure, section by section.

First, at the very top, you have your name in about a 14 or 16-point font. To its right, in a smaller 9-point font, you'll have your contact info, like your email, phone number, and city. Icons are best to avoid. You can also add optional links, like your LinkedIn profile. A quick note: some automated systems might have trouble with icons, so it's safer to stick with standard text.

Next is your professional summary. This is a short, one or two-sentence paragraph that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals. This is key for showing you've changed fields.

Then comes your experience. This is the main part. You'll want to list your most relevant jobs first, including the company, title, dates, and a few bullet points highlighting your key achievements. After that, you can have a smaller section for your 'additional work history', where you briefly list other roles, maybe just the company, title, and dates.

For education, you list your degree, school, and dates. Awards and certifications earned with that degree, like highest honors or scholarships, should go right in that same education section.

After that, you'll have your skills section. It's a good idea to tailor this to the job you're applying for, listing relevant technical and soft skills.

Finally, you can include other optional sections like certifications or volunteer work, but only if they're truly relevant to the job.

So, in order, it's: Name, Contact Info, Professional Summary, Relevant Experience, Additional Work History, Education with Awards, Skills, and then any other optional sections. This structure is clean, professional, and makes it easy for a recruiter to find what they need.
