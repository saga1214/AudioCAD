That's a really interesting counterfactual to think about. But the timing of the Constantinople event was still crucial. It acted as a major catalyst that sped things up significantly. Think about it: the Portuguese were already exploring, but the capture of Constantinople in 1453 was the big shock that made monarchs like Henry the First of Portugal and Ferdinand and Isabella of Spain pour money into these expeditions. It shifted the entire focus of Europe and gave the explorers the urgency they needed to push forward and secure their routes before anyone else could.
