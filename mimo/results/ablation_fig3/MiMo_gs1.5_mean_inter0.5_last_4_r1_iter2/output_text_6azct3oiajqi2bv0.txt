Here are several effective ways to attach something to your jacket lining without tearing it, ranging from simple to more secure:

1.  **Sewing (Most Secure & Permanent):**
    *   **Materials:** Needles, thread (similar weight/color to lining), needle threader, scissors.
    *   **Method:** Thread the needle. Make small, controlled stitches (like running stitches or backstitches) directly through the lining fabric *only*. Avoid pulling the thread tight enough to distort the lining. Secure the stitches with a small knot on the inside. This is the most durable and invisible method.

2.  **Fabric Glue or Fabric Tack (Quick & Easy):**
    *   **Materials:** Fabric glue (like E6000 or Aleene's Tacky Glue - test on scrap first!), fabric tack glue, or a strong, clear fabric glue.
    *   **Method:** Apply a small amount of glue to the *back* of the item you want to attach (e.g., a name tag, patch). Press it firmly onto the lining fabric. Hold for 30-60 seconds. Allow ample drying/curing time according to the glue instructions before wearing. *Crucial: Test glue on an inconspicuous area first to ensure it doesn't damage the lining or the jacket material.*

3.  **Iron-On Adhesive (For Patches/Labels):**
    *   **Materials:** Iron-On Patch or Adhesive Label (ensure it's designed for fabric).
    *   **Method:** Place the patch/label with the glue side facing *down* onto the lining fabric. Iron over it with a pressing cloth (like a thin cotton scrap) for the time specified on the package (usually 30-60 seconds). Cool completely before peeling off the backing if present. *Caution: Test on a hidden seam or hem first. Avoid high heat if the lining is very delicate or synthetic.*

4.  **Fabric Safety Pins (Temporary or Semi-Permanent):**
    *   **Materials:** Fabric Safety Pins (the large, flat kind).
    *   **Method:** Push the pins through the lining fabric *only* on the inside, away from the seam. The pin's head will catch the item (like a tag or small item) and the point will catch the lining. This is best for temporary fixes or items you plan to remove. It won't damage the lining, but it's visible from the inside.

**Key Tips for Success:**

*   **Work on the Lining:** Always attach items to the *lining* fabric itself, not the outer shell of the jacket. This keeps the jacket looking good.
*   **Small Stitches/Small Amounts:** When sewing or gluing, use small stitches or glue points. This minimizes stress on the lining fabric.
*   **Test First:** Always test any adhesive (glue, iron-on) on a small, hidden area of the lining (like an inside seam) to check for damage or discoloration.
*   **Avoid Direct Heat:** If using an iron, always use a pressing cloth (thin cotton) between the iron and the lining to prevent scorching or melting, especially on synthetics.
*   **Choose the Right Method:** Sewing is best for permanent, visible items like name tags. Glue or iron-on is great for patches or labels. Pins are best for temporary fixes.

**For your situation:** Since you're shopping online, an iron-on patch or fabric glue applied carefully to the lining is often the easiest solution to implement once you receive the jacket. Sewing is the most reliable long-term fix if you have basic supplies.
