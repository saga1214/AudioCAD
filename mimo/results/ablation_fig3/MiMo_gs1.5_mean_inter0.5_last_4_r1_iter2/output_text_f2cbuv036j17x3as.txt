### 🔧 Brake Inspection (DIY Basics)
1.  **Visual Inspection:**
    *   **Pad Wear Indicators:** Most modern pads have small metal tabs. If you can see them touching the rotor when the brake is applied, pads are worn out (usually 2-3mm).
    *   **Pad/Shoe Thickness:** Use a caliper or micrometer to measure pad thickness. Replace if below the manufacturer's minimum (often 3-4mm). Check rear drum shoes for wear indicators and overall wear.
    *   **Rotor/Disc Condition:** Look for deep grooves, severe warping (bumps), or excessive rust/pitting. Rotors can usually be resurfaced once, but replacements are needed after the second resurfacing or if severely damaged.
    *   **Brake Fluid:** Check level in the master cylinder reservoir (when cold). Top up with the correct DOT-rated fluid (DOT 3, 4, or 5.1 - consult your manual). Look for dark, smelly fluid (indicating contamination).
    *   **Brake Lines/Hoses:** Inspect for cracks, leaks, or bulges. Replace if damaged.

2.  **Function Test:**
    *   **Pedal Feel:** Should be firm and consistent. A spongy pedal (air in system) or one that sinks to the floor (air in ABS module or major leak) needs immediate attention.
    *   **Brake Test Drive:** Test braking lightly in a safe, open area. Listen for grinding, squealing, or knocking noises. Ensure brakes stop the vehicle smoothly without pulling to one side.

**Signs of Significant Wear/Danger:**
*   **Grinding/Squealing:** Harsh noises when braking (metal on metal).
*   **Reduced Stopping Power:** Vehicle feels like it takes much longer to stop.
*   **Pulling to One Side:** Brakes dragging or unevenly applied.
*   **Spongy or Soft Pedal:** Requires constant pressure to hold the brake.
*   **Burning Smell:** Brakes overheating.
*   **Brake Fluid Leak:** Visible fluid under the car or on the ground.

### 🔧 Spark Plug Replacement (Simplified Steps)
**Important:** Consult your van's service manual for the *exact* procedure, torque specs, and gasket/seal requirements. Diesel engines can be complex.

1.  **Tools Needed:**
    *   Correct Spark Plug Socket (usually 13mm, 15mm, or 17mm - check manual)
    *   Spark Plug Wrench (Protects threads)
    *   Ratchet & Extensions
    *   Torque Wrench (CRITICAL!)
    *   New Spark Plugs (Genuine or high-quality aftermarket - match spec)
    *   New Anti-Seize Compound (if specified by manual)
    *   Gasket or Sealant (if applicable - some plugs have built-in seals)
    *   Dielectric Grease (for boot connections)
    *   Insulated Screwdriver (for distributor if applicable - common on older diesels)
    *   Safety Glasses & Gloves

2.  **Procedure:**
    1.  **Park & Cool Down:** Park on level ground, engage parking brake, chock wheels. Let the engine cool *completely* (at least 30 mins).
    2.  **Disconnect Battery:** Prevents accidental starts.
    3.  **Access Plugs:** Remove engine covers or access panels if needed.
    4.  **Remove Old Plugs:**
        *   Loosen plugs *slightly* with the wrench to break corrosion, then fully unscrew.
        *   **Crucial:** Tap the socket handle gently with a hammer *while holding the wrench* to break the seal without damaging threads. *Do not* hammer the wrench directly on the plug.
        *   Carefully pull out the old plug. Inspect threads for damage/corrosion.
    5.  **Prepare New Plug:**
        *   Apply a *small* amount of anti-seize to the plug threads (if specified - *do not* overdo it).
        *   Ensure gasket/sealant is correct and applied.
    6.  **Install New Plug:**
        *   Hand-tighten the plug * snugly* into the hole. Ensure it's straight.
        *   Attach the wrench and socket. **DO NOT** hammer the wrench on.
        *   Tighten *slightly* with the wrench (just enough to seat it firmly).
        *   Use the spark plug wrench to tighten *slightly* more (often 1/8 to 1/4 turn *after* hand-tight).
        *   **Torque:** This is VITAL. Refer to your manual for the *exact* torque value in Newton-meters (Nm) or foot-pounds (ft-lbs). Torque too low = plug falls out. Torque too high = threads stripped/broken.
    7.  **Reconnect:** Apply dielectric grease to the boot connections. Reconnect the ignition coil/wiring harness.
    8.  **Reassemble:** Replace any covers or access panels.
    9.  **Reconnect Battery.**

### ⚠️ Essential Pre-Trip Maintenance (Beyond Brakes & Spark Plugs)
1.  **Tires:**
    *   **Pressure:** Check and adjust to manufacturer specs (cold) using the placard on the door jamb or fuel door.
    *   **Tread Depth:** Use the penny test (head upside down; if Lincoln's nose is covered, tread is good). Aim for 4/32" or more.
    *   **Tires:** Check for uneven wear, bulges, cracks, or damage.
    *   **Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS):** Ensure it's working.
2.  **Fluids:**
    *   **Engine Oil & Filter:** Check level and condition. Ensure correct type and grade. *Change if needed*.
    *   **Coolant:** Check level and condition (color, smell). Top up if necessary.
    *   **Power Steering Fluid:** Check level.
    *   **Brake Fluid:** Check level (as mentioned above).
    *   **Windshield Washer Fluid:** Top up.
    *   **Battery Terminal:** Clean terminals and cables if corroded.
3.  **Battery:** Check terminals, cables, and connections. Ensure no corrosion. Test charge if unsure.
4.  **Belts & Hoses:** Visually inspect serpentine belt(s) for cracks, fraying, or glazing. Check hoses (especially radiator hoses) for cracks, leaks, or bulges.
5.  **Lights & Signals:** Test all exterior lights (headlights, brake lights, turn signals, reverse lights, taillights) and interior lights.
6.  **Wiper Blades:** Replace if streaking or worn.
7.  **Exhaust System:** Look for leaks, excessive noise, or damage.
8.  **Campervan Specifics:** Check fridge/freezer compressor, water pump, generator (if used), solar panels, awning, slide outs, and interior systems (lights, outlets).

### 🛠️ Pro Tip: Get a Professional Pre-Trip Inspection
Despite your DIY checks, **schedule a comprehensive inspection at a trusted mechanic or diesel specialist *before* your trip.** They can:
*   Perform a thorough brake test.
*   Check for leaks (oil, coolant, transmission fluid).
*   Inspect under the hood for issues you might miss.
*   Verify all fluids are correct.
*   Ensure the van is mechanically sound for the long haul.
