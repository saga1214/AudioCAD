Here’s a clear, step-by-step breakdown of a standard, ATS-friendly resume format based on our conversation. I’ll organize it logically for you to type out.

First up is the Contact Information section. It should immediately follow your name. This section should include your name in about a 14 to 16-point font, followed by your email address, phone number, city, state, and zip code. You can also add a link to your LinkedIn profile. It's best to avoid icons to prevent any issues with Applicant Tracking Systems. For example, you could write: John Doe, followed by his email, phone number, city, and a link to his LinkedIn.

Next is the Professional Summary, which is about three to five lines long. Here, you should highlight your most relevant skills and experiences, and state your career goals. For instance: “Seasoned marketing professional with 15 years of experience in digital campaign management and brand development. Seeking to leverage expertise in SEO and content strategy to drive growth at a forward-thinking agency.”

After that comes the Experience section. This should be divided into two parts. The first part is your “Relevant Experience,” which lists your most relevant jobs, including the job title, company, location, and dates. For each job, you'll add a short bullet point for a key achievement. For example: “Senior Marketing Manager at Acme Corp, New York, from January 2010 to December 2015. Led a team that increased web traffic by 40% through targeted social campaigns.” The second part is the “Additional Work History,” which lists your other jobs, again with just the title, company, location, dates, and a very brief description. For example: “Marketing Coordinator at XYZ Inc, Chicago, from June 2000 to May 2005. Assisted with campaign planning and client communications.”

Then, you'll have the Education section. Here, you list your degree, the university, and your graduation date. You can add honors or awards right under the degree line. For example: “Bachelor of Science in Marketing, University of Chicago, May 2000. Graduated with Highest Honors.”

If you have any, you can add a Skills section. This is optional, but it's a good place to list key skills relevant to the job you're applying for, like software proficiency or languages. You can group them if you want.

Finally, you can add a section for Awards and Certifications, listing any notable awards or professional certifications you have.

To put it all together, here’s a quick example of how the sections would flow: First, your name. Then contact info. Followed by the professional summary. Then the Relevant Experience section. Then the Additional Work History section. Then Education, Skills, and any Awards.

Just remember to keep the formatting consistent with your name and contact info. And always proofread it carefully before sending it out. This structure should give you a clear path forward.
