Alright, let's look at a scenario that highlights a key difference in how the offside rule is applied between older interpretations and the current Laws of the Game. I'll use the Red Team and Blue Team as you requested.

The scenario is this: The Red Team is defending their goal. The Blue Team's striker, Player A, is making a very deep run behind the Red Team's last defender, Player D. As Player D is tracking back, the Blue Team's midfielder, Player B, plays a long pass forward to Player A. At the exact moment Player B touches the ball, Player A is level with the defender, Player D. The ball travels forward, but Player A continues running ahead of the ball and then controls it with a touch just before scoring.

Now, here's the critical part: the defender, Player D, makes a controlled play on the ball. They slide in and touch the ball, but the ball rebounds and continues forward towards Player A, who then scores.

So, how does this differ between old and new rules?

First, under the older interpretation, which is common in high school and some college systems, the focus was on the player's position at the moment the ball was played. Since Player A was level with the defender when the pass was made, they were considered to be in an offside position. Because they were in an offside position when the ball was played, and then they got involved by controlling the rebound and scoring, it would be an offside offense. The defender's touch wouldn't reset the situation because the player was already in an offside position *before* the touch.

However, under the current Laws of the Game, the focus is on the defender's *deliberate* play on the ball. Since Player D made a deliberate, controlled play on the ball, it resets the offside situation for Player A. This means that at the moment of the defender's touch, Player A is no longer in an offside position. Therefore, receiving the rebound and scoring is a legitimate goal, and there is no offside offense.

The key difference is the emphasis on whether the defender's touch is deliberate and controlled, which resets the situation for all players involved, versus simply checking if a player was in an offside position at the moment the ball was played. The current rules put more weight on the defender's action.
