If forced to choose **one single top driver**, the **energy balance imbalance** (consuming more calories than you burn) is the most fundamental factor behind increasing obesity rates. Here’s why:

### 1. **Core Principle of Weight Gain**  
   Obesity fundamentally occurs when **caloric intake exceeds daily expenditure** over time. This imbalance leads to fat storage, regardless of the source of calories (processed or unprocessed).

### 2. **Processed Foods Accelerate Imbalance**  
   While processed foods (high in refined carbs, sugar, and unhealthy fats) are a *major contributor* to this imbalance (due to their calorie density, poor satiety, and addictive properties), they aren’t the *only* cause. For example:  
   - Overconsumption of unprocessed calorie-dense foods (e.g., nuts, oils, dried fruits) without exercise can also cause weight gain.  
   - Sedentary lifestyles or reduced physical activity directly lower calorie burn.  

### 3. **Other Contributing Factors**  
   - **Physical inactivity**: Reduces calorie expenditure.  
   - **Diet quality**: Processed foods often lack nutrients, leading to overeating without feeling full.  
   - **Sleep/stress**: Poor sleep lowers metabolism; chronic stress increases cravings.  
   - **Genetics/environment**: Some individuals are more susceptible, but environment (e.g., food availability, urban design) drives behavior.  

### Why Energy Balance is the "Top Driver":  
Even if someone eats *small portions* of processed foods or *unprocessed calorie-dense foods* (like nuts), weight gain only occurs if their **total calories consumed > total calories burned**. Conversely, a highly active person eating moderate portions of calorie-dense foods is unlikely to become obese.  

### Key Nuance:  
Processed foods are a **primary vector** for energy imbalance because they are engineered to override natural satiety signals. But the *underlying mechanism* remains **caloric surplus**.  

**In short:** Energy balance is the root cause. Processed foods, inactivity, and stress are powerful *amplifiers* of this imbalance.
