If the coefficient of friction increases when the ice gets colder, then at minus ten degrees Celsius, it would likely be in the range of 0.005 to 0.008, or maybe even a little higher, like 0.01. The exact number really depends on the quality of the ice and how well the blade is sharpened, but it's definitely lower than what you'd find in warmer conditions. The key thing is that the super low friction at around minus five degrees is what makes it possible for skaters to glide so smoothly.
