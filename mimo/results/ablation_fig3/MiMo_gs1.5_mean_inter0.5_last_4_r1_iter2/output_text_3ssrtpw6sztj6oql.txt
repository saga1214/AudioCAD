You've hit on a critical point—human error *is* a major vulnerability. Therefore, the **most reliable "first line of defense" is a layered system focused on controlling human access at every entry point**, combining these elements:

1.  **Visible Security Presence (Guards):** Creates deterrence and provides immediate response capability.
2.  **Access Control & Screening (Bag Checks, ID Verification):** Actively manages *who* enters and *what* they bring in, minimizing threats before they enter the museum space.
3.  **Technology Integration (Cameras, Sensors):** Monitors the interior as a secondary layer, but its effectiveness relies on preventing entry in the first place.

**Why this is the "first line":**

*   **Proactive Containment:** It prevents unauthorized individuals or threats from *gaining entry* into the secured environment where the art is housed.
*   **Foundation for Other Layers:** All interior security systems (cameras, motion detectors, alarms on display cases) become exponentially more effective if they only have to monitor a *small, controlled* number of authorized individuals entering the space. Without this initial control, those systems become overwhelmed and less effective.
*   **Directly Addresses the Human Factor:** While technology and physical barriers are essential, the entrance point is the *only* location where you can directly control the *human element* entering the space. It's the primary point of contact with the public.

**In essence:** The "first line" isn't just a single tool; it's the **strategic process of managing human entry** through guards, access checks, and verification. This layer aims to *prevent* a breach from happening at the outset, making the subsequent layers of technology and physical security far more manageable and effective.
