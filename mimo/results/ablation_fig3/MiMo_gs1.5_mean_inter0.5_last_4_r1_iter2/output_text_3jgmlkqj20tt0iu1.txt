Based on your situation, here are two distinct approaches to initiating the first raise conversation, designed to help manage anxiety while being professional and effective:

### Approach 1: The Formal Performance Review Path (Recommended)
This leverages your anxiety by creating a structured, expected opportunity.  
**How to do it:**  
1. **Wait for a natural trigger:** Aim for the annual performance review cycle (typically 6–12 months post-hire). If none exists, proactively request one:  
   > *"I’d appreciate feedback on my performance since starting. Could we schedule a 30-minute review?"*  
2. **Prepare quantifiable achievements:** Document 3–5 specific wins (e.g., *"Reduced report turnaround time by 20%"* or *"Secured client renewal after X initiative"*).  
3. **Frame it as growth:** During the review, say:  
   > *"I’ve taken on [specific responsibility] and delivered [result]. Based on this, I’m exploring compensation alignment for my role and value to the team."*  
**Why it reduces anxiety:**  
- You’re using a company-sanctioned process.  
- Focuses on facts, not emotion.  
- Sets clear expectations for future reviews.  

### Approach 2: The Proactive Career Conversation
This builds confidence by positioning the raise as part of your professional development.  
**How to do it:**  
1. **Schedule a neutral meeting:** Request 15–20 minutes to discuss "career growth."  
2. **Use the "3 C's" framework:**  
   - **Context:** *"I’ve been handling [new responsibility] since [date] and feel confident in my contributions."*  
   - **Competition:** *"Market data shows the role’s salary range is $X–$Y. My current rate is $Z."*  
   - **Commitment:** *"I’m committed to [specific future goal] and want to align my compensation with my impact."*  
3. **End with a question:**  
   > *"What would it take for my compensation to reflect my current responsibilities?"*  
**Why it reduces anxiety:**  
- Shorter time commitment (15–20 mins).  
- Focuses on collaboration, not confrontation.  
- Provides actionable feedback for improvement.  

### Key Tips to Manage Anxiety
- **Reframe the conversation:** You’re discussing *future opportunities*, not demanding money.  
- **Practice aloud:** Rehearse with a trusted colleague to build familiarity.  
- **Anchor in facts:** If nervous, keep a note of 1–2 key achievements on your desk as a reminder.  
- **Set boundaries:** If your boss avoids the topic, say:  
  > *"I understand we need to align on timelines. Could we revisit this in [specific timeframe, e.g., Q1]?"*  

> 💡 **Rule of Thumb**: Most companies expect raises to be discussed after **6–12 months** of performance. If your work exceeds expectations, you can initiate earlier—but use one of these frameworks to stay grounded.
