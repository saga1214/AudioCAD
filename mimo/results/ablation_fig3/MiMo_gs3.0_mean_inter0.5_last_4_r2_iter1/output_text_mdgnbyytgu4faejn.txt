Of course. Let's go through a standard professional resume structure from top to bottom.

First, you'll have your name in about a 14 or larger point font.
To the right or below it, but at a nine-point or smaller font, you'll have your contact info in this order: email, icons or not icons followed by phone number, comma, comma, city. You can also add a link to your LinkedIn right around this section.

Next, right under your name, is a short one or two-sentence professional summary that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals.

Then, you'll have a section for your Relevant Experience. This is for the key roles that match the job you're applying for, including the company, title, dates, and just a brief description.

After that comes your Experience History section, for any other jobs, especially older ones that are less relevant. Here you just list the company, title, and dates, very concisely.

Then comes your Education. You'll list your degree, where you got it, and your honors or scholarship right there under that specific degree.

Following that is a Skills section. This is where you list the key technical and soft skills tailored to the job description you're targeting.

You can also have an Additional section for things like Awards and Certificates. Scholarships definitely belong here or with your education. Highest honors also fits perfectly here.

Finally, you can add other optional sections like Volunteer work, Projects you've done, or Languages you speak.

Just remember to keep it to one page if you can, and always tailor the content to each specific job you apply for. Does that structure work for you?
