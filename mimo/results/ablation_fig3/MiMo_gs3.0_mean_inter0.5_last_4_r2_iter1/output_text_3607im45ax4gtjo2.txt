Dealing with a difficult person can feel overwhelming, but here are some practical strategies to help manage stress and protect your well-being:

### 1. **Prioritize Safety & Boundaries**  
   - **Document everything**: Keep records (dates, times, and consequences) of incidents like calls to CPS/police. This could be crucial if legal steps are needed later.  
   - **Protect your kids**: Supervise outdoor activities closely initially, and reinforce rules (e.g., bike rides only when she seems less vigilant).  
   Report threats or harassment to authorities if they escalate.

### 2. **Limit Engagement**  
   - **Avoid arguments**: Respond neutrally if confronted ("I’ll look into that" or "I’ve heard your concerns"). Don’t validate their behavior.  
   - **Block communication**: Use apps to filter harassment, or ask your HOA/neighborhood board to mediate if applicable.

### 3. **Focus on What You Control**  
   - **Shift your narrative**: Instead of "She’s ruining our neighborhood," think, "I can’t control her, but I can control how we respond."  
   - **Build community**: Connect with other affected families for mutual support (without organizing confrontations).

### 4. **Practice Self-Care**  
   - **Mindfulness/grounding**: When stressed, pause and breathe deeply (e.g., 4-7-8 technique). Apps like Headspace can help.  
   - **Celebrate small wins**: Like your kids enjoying dress-up or Halloween prep—these moments anchor positivity amid stress.  
   - **Limit exposure**: Reduce news/social media about the situation to avoid retraumatization.

### 5. **Seek External Support**  
   - **Talk to a counselor**: Stress like this can impact mental health—therapy can provide coping tools.  
   - **Legal resources**: If CPS involvement occurs, contact a family advocate or lawyer (many offer free consultations).

### 6. **Reframe the Challenge**  
   View this as a temporary situation. Focus on the resilience you and your kids are building—like mastering Forrest Frank’s dances or planning Halloween costumes. Small joys ("Lemonade day" or Neon week) create pockets of light.
