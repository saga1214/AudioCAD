Now, to your question: in light of saccadic masking, is it correct to say the eye sees continuously? 

Well, it’s a bit of a yes and no situation. The eye itself doesn’t see in a continuous, unbroken stream. It’s more like a series of snapshots. But the brain doesn’t just accept those snapshots—it processes them into a continuous flow. So, while the eye is capturing data in jumps, your brain is doing the heavy lifting to create the illusion of continuous vision. 

Think of it like a camera with a very fast shutter speed. The eye takes quick pictures, and the brain's motion smoothing is like a post-production filter that makes the footage look fluid. Without that filtering, you'd see the jumpy motion of saccades. 

So, in a way, you can say the eye sees continuously, but it’s really the brain that’s doing the continuous processing of those snapshots. It’s a partnership between the eye and brain.
