That end-of-year celebration is a perfect opportunity to celebrate the girls' achievements and build that troop bond! Here's a breakdown of your options and suggestions:

**For the End-of-Year Event:**

1.  **The Park BBQ & Games (Highly Recommended):**
    *   **Pros:** This is classic Brownie fun! It's outdoors (easier logistics), allows for active games, is generally less expensive than a large indoor venue, and lets the girls burn off energy. It fosters teamwork and fun.
    *   **Cons:** Weather dependent (have a rain date!), requires setup/cleanup, potential noise or parking issues.
    *   **How to Make it Work:**
        *   **Food:** A simple BBQ setup is great. You can provide a main (like hot dogs, burgers, or veggie burgers) and chips/salads. Let **parents bring sides** (potato salad, coleslaw, pasta salad, fruit platter, rolls). This spreads the work and cost.
        *   **Activities:** Focus on 1-2 big, classic Brownie games:
            *   **Three-Legged Race:** Always a hit! Pair girls up carefully.
            *   **Wheelbarrow Race:** Needs a sturdy wheelbarrow.
            *   **Tug-of-War:** Easy and fun.
            *   **Sack Race:** Simple and silly.
            *   **Pin the Tail on the Donkey:** A classic party game.
        *   **Ice Cream Truck:** This is a *fantastic* addition! It provides a special, universally loved treat and is easy to manage. Schedule it for an hour or so during the event.
        *   **Sundae Bar:** If pizza or ice cream is the main meal, setting up a simple sundae bar (chocolate chips, sprinkles, nuts, whipped cream) is a fun, customizable dessert.
        *   **Keep it Relaxed:** Don't try to do everything. Focus on the food, the games, and letting the girls socialize. Have a simple trophy or certificate for the winners of the games.

2.  **The Cafeteria Meeting (Simple & Low-Key):**
    *   **Pros:** Very low cost, low logistics, easy cleanup, no weather worries, perfect for a rainy day or tight schedule.
    *   **Cons:** Less "special event" feel, limited space for active games, can feel cramped if too many people are there.
    *   **How to Make it Work:**
        *   **Food:** Keep it simple – pizza parties are classic and easy! Or, set up a table with cake, cupcakes, and snacks.
        *   **Activities:** Focus on a fun craft, a simple science experiment, or a storytelling session. Maybe a group dance party or just letting them chat and celebrate.

**Recommendation:** **The Park BBQ with an Ice Cream Truck is the winner!** It captures the Brownie spirit, creates lasting memories, is relatively manageable, and feels like a proper celebration. Emphasize the fun games and the special ice cream truck.

**Logistics Recap (Based on Your Questions):**

*   **Sashes:** **Parents purchase these.** Clearly communicate the cost and timeline early on (e.g., "Sashes will need to be ordered by X date to arrive before the end of the year").
*   **Snacks:**
    *   **Meetings:** You can provide a simple snack (like chips and dip - avoiding dairy if needed!), but rotating a **"Snack Buddy"** (a parent/guardian) each week can help share the load.
    *   **Events (BBQ, Field Trips):** **Parents bring sides** as mentioned above. You provide the main item (BBQ meat, pizza) or coordinate the ice cream truck.
*   **Dues:** A small, clear fee (e.g., $10-$25) is common and can cover:
    *   Basic craft supplies for badge activities.
    *   Simple field trip costs (like a local zoo visit).
    *   Small event items (like a few bags of chips/dip for a meeting).
    *   **Crucially:** Dues should *not* cover major events (BBQ, camping), troop sashes, or the troop leader's expenses. Clearly state what the dues cover and what parents are responsible for separately (like sashes, major event costs, extra snack supplies).
*   **Patches/Badges:** Focus on the badges outlined in the Brownie troop book. Starting with the Friendship Badge (building community) or a simple Nature Badge is a great way to begin. Your plan to follow the book is spot on!

**Key to Success:** **Communicate clearly and early!** Send out a welcome packet including the troop book, dues information, sash details, a tentative meeting schedule, and a "Snack Buddy" signup sheet. Emphasize the fun and the importance of parent support, especially while you're pregnant. You've got this – your enthusiasm for your daughters and the troop will shine through!
