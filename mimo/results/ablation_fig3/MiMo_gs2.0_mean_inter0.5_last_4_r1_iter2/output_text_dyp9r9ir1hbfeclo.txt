You've caught a great detail! While the three bones—**humerus (upper arm), scapula (shoulder blade), and clavicle (collarbone)**—work together for *primary rotation*, the clavicle's role as a **fourth component** is crucial for stability and coordinated movement. Here’s the breakdown:

1.  **Humerus:** Rotates *within* the shallow socket of the scapula.
2.  **Scapula:** Provides the socket and glides to accommodate humeral movement.
3.  **Clavicle:** Forms the shoulder girdle's connection to the axial skeleton (spine). It **stabilizes the scapula** against the ribcage and **guides the humerus' path** during arm elevation and rotation. Think of it as the "rider" on the "horse" (scapula/humerus) that keeps everything aligned.

**How the Clavicle Contributes to Rotation:**
*   **Stability:** By anchoring the scapula, it prevents excessive tilting or flapping during arm movements.
*   **Guidance:** During arm elevation (abduction), the clavicle itself lifts slightly, helping to position the humerus correctly.
*   **Range of Motion:** Its position influences the overall range of motion the shoulder can achieve.

**Regarding Your Friend's Dumbbell Lateral Raises:**

*   **Rotator Cuff Risk:** Poor form (e.g., shrugging, excessive range, momentum) or overloading can strain the rotator cuff tendons.
*   **Signs to Watch For:**
    *   **Mild Soreness:** Normal muscle fatigue.
    *   **Sharp Pain:** Especially in the front or side of the shoulder.
    *   **Pain During/After:** Movement or daily activities.
    *   **Weakness:** Difficulty lifting or rotating the arm.
    *   **Swelling:** In the shoulder joint.
*   **Advice:** He should focus on **proper form** (keep shoulders down, move slowly with control, don't raise beyond shoulder height), **reduce weight**, and **rest**. **Key Takeaway:** While the humerus, scapula, and clavicle form the basic structure for rotation, the **clavicle's stabilizing role is essential** for smooth, controlled movement. Proper form during exercises like lateral raises helps prevent rotator cuff strain.
