That's a great question. While the traditional Thanksgiving story focuses on a shared celebration, the Wampanoag perspective reveals a more complex reality. For the Pilgrims, it was a moment of relief and gratitude after surviving their first winter with the help of the Wampanoag. For the Wampanoag, it was a strategic alliance against their enemies. However, for the Wampanoag people, the arrival of the English settlers led to devastating losses from disease and conflict. So, while the 1621 feast itself may have been a moment of cooperation, the broader context of colonization is crucial to understanding the event's full history.
