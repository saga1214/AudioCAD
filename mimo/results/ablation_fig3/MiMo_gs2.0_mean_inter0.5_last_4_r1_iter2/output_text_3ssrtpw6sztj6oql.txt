You've hit on a crucial point: **human error is the weakest link** in art museum security. So while physical barriers matter, the **most critical "first line" is actually *managed access control***—a combination of:  

1. **Visible Security Presence**: Guards at entrances to deter intruders and observe behavior.  
2. **Bag Checks & Screening**: Limiting what enters (e.g., X-rays for bags, metal detectors).  
3. **Access Control Systems**: **Keycards, biometrics, or PINs** for restricted areas (staff-only zones, storage, server rooms).  
4. **Staff Training**: Training guards to recognize threats and enforce protocols (like Gardner’s heist, where guards ignored police-disguised intruders).  

### Why This is the "First Line":
- **Prevention**: It stops threats *before* they enter the building.  
- **Deterrence**: Visible security discourages casual theft.  
- **Human Oversight**: Counters automated systems (e.g., sensors can be bypassed; guards can respond).  

### Modern Enhancements:
- **AI-Powered Surveillance**: Cameras with facial recognition or behavior analysis.  
- **Intrusion Detection Systems**: Glass-break sensors, motion detectors, and panic buttons.  
- **Secure Display Cases**: Reinforced cases with anti-tamper alarms (e.g., Vermeer’s stolen painting was in a display case).  

### Key Takeaway for Your Paper:
**Modern security layers *around* human fallibility—not just technology.** The first line must balance *access control*, *deterrence*, and *response protocols* to mitigate risks like insider threats or social engineering.
