If I had to choose **one single top driver** behind increasing obesity rates, it would be **easy and consistent access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods**. Here’s why:

### Key Reasons:
1. **Hyper-Palatable Design**: Processed foods are engineered to be excessively tasty and satisfying, overriding natural satiety signals (e.g., high sugar/fat combinations disrupt hormones like ghrelin and leptin).  
2. **Nutrient Deficiency**: They’re often low in fiber, protein, and micronutrients, leading to overeating without feeling full.  
3. **Affordability and Convenience**: Cheap, shelf-stable, and marketed aggressively, making them the default choice for busy lifestyles.  
4. **Systemic Influence**: Industrialized food systems prioritize profit over health, embedding processed foods into daily routines (e.g., fast food, packaged snacks, sugary drinks).  

### Why Other Factors Are Secondary:
- **Physical inactivity**: While crucial, it’s often a *consequence* of sedentary lifestyles enabled by modern environments (e.g., desk jobs, urban sprawl).  
- **Genetics/Stress**: These play roles but don’t override consistent overconsumption of hyper-palatable foods.  
- **Exercise vs. Diet**: Even highly active people can gain weight if their diet is poor (e.g., "overtraining" with junk food).  

### The Bottom Line:
Processed foods create a **caloric surplus** with minimal satiety, making overconsumption inevitable. Pair this with reduced physical activity and poor sleep/stress, and obesity becomes a systemic issue. While other factors matter, processed foods act as the primary amplifier of weight gain.  

*Note: This doesn’t absolve individual responsibility—diet and activity are deeply interconnected—but the environment enabling processed food access is the largest-scale driver.*
