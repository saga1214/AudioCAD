Okay, so you're planning a diesel camper van road trip and want to make sure the vehicle is safe and reliable. That's a smart move. For brakes, a good first step is to listen carefully when you're driving. If you hear any grinding or squealing, that's a sign of wear or a problem. Also, if the car feels wobbly or you have to brake much harder than usual, that could mean the rotors or pads are worn out. Another thing is to check the brake fluid level; if it's low or looks dirty, that's a red flag. You should definitely get a professional inspection before you leave. For spark plugs, it's a bit more involved. Diesel engines use high compression, so you have to be careful. You'll need to remove the cylinder head, which is a big job. If you're not comfortable with that, it’s best to leave it to a mechanic. But if you do it yourself, you’ll need special tools, like a spark plug socket, and you’ll want to check the gap on the new plugs to make sure they’re set correctly. It's a good idea to replace them every 100,000 miles or so, but always check your owner's manual for the exact interval.
