### Why Design & Intent is Paramount:
1. **Guides All Other Steps**:  
   The blade's geometry, material choice, heat treatment, and finishing are all dictated by the sword's *purpose*. A rapier needs a thin, flexible blade, while a claymore requires a thick, strong core. Without this clarity, you’re just shaping metal blindly.

2. **Prevents Wasted Effort**:  
   Forging a blade for a broadsword’s weight profile onto a thin steel stock will fail. Designing first ensures your materials, tools, and techniques align with the goal.

3. **Safety & Functionality**:  
   A poorly balanced blade can shatter during use. Intent determines how you address balance, leverage, and durability—making it the "blueprint" for survival.

### The Vicious Cycle of Craftsmanship:
- **Heat Treatment** (most technically critical) *depends* on precise geometry.  
- **Materials** (critical for quality) must match the design’s demands.  
- **CNC vs. Forging** is irrelevant without understanding metallurgy.  
- **Skill & Experience** (the unspoken step) is what ties it all together.  

### Final Verdict:
**Design and intent is the root of a good sword.** It transforms raw materials into a functional tool. Without it, even perfect heat treatment or materials are wasted. But *all* steps—design, materials, heat treatment, fabrication—are equally vital in the *cycle* of craftsmanship.
