That's a very insightful question, and it gets to the heart of what drove the Age of Discovery. The search for a direct sea route to Asia was the fundamental engine.

The fall of Constantinople was a huge catalyst. It created a powerful sense of urgency and fear among European powers. It also helped shift support and funding towards the Portuguese, who were already making waves. So, while the underlying desire for new trade routes was always there, the event gave it a major push.

So, to put it simply: the Age of Discovery would have happened eventually because of the spice trade. But the collapse of the old system in 1453 acted as a powerful accelerant, making it happen much faster and on a larger scale than it might have otherwise.
