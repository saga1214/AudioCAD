Okay, let's break this down. Chronic redness has a ton of potential causes. It could be rosacea, which is common on the face, or sun damage that gets worse over years. It could also be an allergic reaction, or even a simple bacterial or fungal infection. Genetics definitely play a part, and environmental triggers like spicy food, alcohol, or even stress can set it off.

Now, about vasculitis. This is where your immune system mistakenly attacks your blood vessels. This inflammation can happen in large, medium, or small vessels. When it's small vessels, you often see rashes, sores, or swelling. When it's medium vessels, you might feel tired and have joint pain. For large vessels, it can be more serious, potentially causing aneurysms or blockages. So, while vasculitis can cause redness, it's usually part of a bigger picture of symptoms.

The key thing to remember is that chronic, lifetime-long redness needs a proper diagnosis. It's always best to see a doctor to figure out what's really going on.
