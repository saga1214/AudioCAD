You're very close! **Saccadic masking** is the brain's suppression of visual input during rapid eye movements (saccades). To clarify:

### Pronunciation:  
**Saccade** = *suh-KAH-dee*  
*(Emphasis on the second syllable: "KAH")*

### Eye vs. Brain Perception:  
1. **Eye (Sensor):**  
   - The eye detects light as *discrete packets* (photons) and motion changes.  
   - It has a **flicker fusion threshold** (~50-60 Hz for most people). Above this, flickering light appears continuous.  
   - **Saccadic masking** causes *brief moments of blindness* (microseconds) when the eyes jump between points.  

2. **Brain (Processor):**  
   - The visual cortex **fills gaps** between saccades using motion interpolation and predictive processing.  
   - This creates the *illusion* of continuous motion, even though the eye only "sees" discrete moments.  

### Key Difference:  
- **Eye:** Samples motion in short bursts (like a camera shutter).  
- **Brain:** Smooths these bursts into a seamless experience.  

### Why 24 fps Feels "Real":  
- 24 fps aligns with the brain’s temporal resolution. It’s close enough to our natural sampling rate to feel cinematic but not hyper-real.  
- Higher frame rates (60 fps+) feel more "video-like" because they better match the eye’s actual sampling speed during saccades.  

### Practical Take for Filmmaking:  
- Use **24 fps** for artistic, dreamlike motion.  
- Use **higher frame rates** (e.g., 60 fps) for extreme realism or slow-motion flexibility.  
- Remember: The *brain* creates continuity; the *eye* captures discrete data.  

This distinction explains why 35mm at 24 fps feels timeless—it taps into how our visual system *perceives* motion, not just how the eye *samples* it.
