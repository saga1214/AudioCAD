Okay, you're right. You want to be very specific but also respectful. Here's a way to structure your email.

Subject: Meeting Request: Last Week's Essay Exam

Professor [Professor's Last Name],

I hope you're doing well.

I scored a 64 on the recent essay exam and wanted to talk to you about my performance. I'm committed to doing well in this class and would really appreciate any feedback on how I could have improved in my essay.

Could we possibly meet briefly during your office hours or at another time that works for you? I'd like to understand what led to my score so I can apply that knowledge to future assignments.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Best regards,
[Your Name]

This email is direct and professional. It frames the request as a desire to learn, which should bypass his ego. It also gives him an easy way to respond by asking when he's available.

When you meet, continue with this theme. Ask open-ended questions like, "What was the most important aspect of the essay for you?" or "What could I have done differently to better meet the expectations?" This focuses the conversation on your growth.

And regarding imposter syndrome and scholarships: don't bring this grade up in scholarship materials unless it's directly relevant and necessary. Committees evaluate the whole picture. Your excellent grades and your proactive approach to seeking feedback are strong points in your favor. Apply if you believe you deserve it.
