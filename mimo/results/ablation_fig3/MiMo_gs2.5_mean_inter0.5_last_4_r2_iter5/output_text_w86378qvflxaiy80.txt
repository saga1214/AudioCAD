After considering all the factors, especially the direct link to vulnerable human populations through ecosystem services, the most critical aspect of preserving hotspots is preventing the permanent, and often irreversible, loss of unique species and the complex web of life they create.

While the services and resources are vital, the defining tragedy of a hotspot is watching a place that has existed for millions of years—home to countless species found nowhere else—be wiped out in a very short period due to human activity like agriculture or urbanization. This loss represents a unique evolutionary history that will be lost forever.

Therefore, preservation is fundamentally about preventing this extinction and maintaining the irreplaceable genetic library and ecological functions of our planet. It's about ensuring that this incredible biological heritage continues to exist for the future, for scientific discovery, for ecological balance, and for the communities who depend on it.
