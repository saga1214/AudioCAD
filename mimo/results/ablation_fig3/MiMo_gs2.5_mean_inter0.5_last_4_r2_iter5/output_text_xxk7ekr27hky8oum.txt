Okay, here is my speech.

Preschool is the rotary phone. It's slow and mechanical, but it teaches you the very first steps: where numbers are, how to wait for a sound, and that there’s a world out there waiting to connect. Just as the rotary phone laid the groundwork for every phone that followed, preschool teaches curiosity, basic motor skills, and the joy of discovery. Apps like ABCmouse are like the dials of that phone, guiding little hands through letters and numbers. This stage is about building patience and understanding that effort, even if it’s slow, leads to connection.

Kindergarten through third grade is the push-button landline phone. Speed replaces spinning wheels. Structure remains—the cord is still there—but freedom is growing. Reading apps and basic math tools are like the buttons, clear and simple. Kids now "talk" in words instead of whistles. Connected by that classroom "cord," this stage is about foundational skills: phonics, addition, and social rules. The cord tethers them to the teacher, just as early learning is still guided, but it’s a huge leap in ease and independence.

Higher elementary is the cordless house phone. Tethered to home, but not the house. Kids are exploring more, just like students exploring science projects or book reports. Still a landline, so it’s not fully wireless, but the cord is shorter. It’s the transition from classroom structure to more independence.

Middle school is the first Nokia cell phone. Mobility is king. Toughness is tested. Social navigation is everything. Those old Nokia phones were indestructible, just like middle schoolers have to be. And texting became the new dominant form of communication, just like how social dynamics become more complex. It’s the first real taste of freedom and responsibility.

Early high school and senior year are the first iPhones. A complete paradigm shift. The world suddenly opens up. Apps are everything: for homework, social life, and exploration. The iPhone’s power mirrors the expanding intellectual and social landscape. By senior year, you’re mastering the ecosystem, just as seniors master college applications and independent projects.

Rest university years mirror smartphone evolution each year. Faster processors, sleeker designs, more specialized tools. Just as classes become more focused in your major, your "device" gets better each year. Apps for research, writing, and collaboration are like the incremental upgrades, each one making you more powerful.

Grad school is the Pro/Ultra model. Specialized to the extreme. Advanced cameras for data visualization, processing power for complex simulations. Mirroring grad work: deep focus, specialized expertise, and producing groundbreaking "research apps." It’s where raw potential becomes precision tools.

Each stage isn’t just a device; it’s a mindset shift. From mechanical curiosity to mobile mastery, each upgrade prepares you for the next. The phone evolves; so do you.
