For your old diesel camper van, thorough pre-trip maintenance is **essential** for safety and reliability. Here’s a breakdown of key checks, DIY vs. professional tasks, and diesel-specific considerations:

### 🔧 **Critical DIY Checks**
1. **Brakes**  
   - **Test Pedal Feel:** Press pedal firmly. Spongy or soft pedal = air in system (requires professional bleed).  
   - **Exterior Inspection:** Look for cracks in calipers, leaks in fluid lines, or worn pads/discs.  
   - **Listen for Noise:** Grinding or squealing indicates pad/shoe wear.  
   - **Fluid Level:** Check brake fluid in reservoir (top off with **DOT 3 or DOT 4**).  
   - *Signs of Wear:* Grinding, vibration, or fluid leaks.

2. **Diesel-Specific Fluid Checks**  
   - **Engine Oil:** Check dipstick (level/viscosity). *Change if dirty or low.*  
   - **Diesel Fuel:** Ensure fresh, filtered fuel (water separation tank if equipped).  
   - **Coolant:** Full in reservoir, no air bubbles.  
   - **Transmission Fluid:** Check dipstick (red fluid; *change if dirty or low*).  
   - **Transfer Case Fluid** (if AWD/4WD): Check level (often requires draining/replacing).

3. **Exhaust System**  
   - Inspect for **rust holes, leaks, or white smoke** (excessive oil burning).  
   - *Diesel Note:* Glow plugs are critical for cold starts—test resistance (10–15 ohms).

4. **Tires & Tires**  
   - **Pressure:** Check cold (camper adds ~10% weight).  
   - **Tread Depth:** Use penny test (1/16" or less = replace).  
   - **Rim Condition:** Check for corrosion.

### ⚙️ **Professional Tasks**  
- **Brake Inspection:** Bleeding, caliper adjustment, rotor/shoe replacement.  
- **Engine/Transmission Diagnostics:** Compression test, oil filter change (if needed).  
- **DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) Cleaning:** Requires shop equipment.  
- **A/C System Check:** Refrigerant level and compressor function.  
- **Wiring/HVAC:** Test for leaks, electrical faults, or compressor issues.

### 🔧 **Spark Plugs?**  
Diesel engines use **compression ignition**—no spark plugs! Instead:  
- **Glow Plugs:** Inspect terminals/connections (critical for cold starts).  
- **Ignition Coil/Injector:** *Only if your model has direct injection*—requires professional testing.

### 🛠️ **Pre-Trip Pro Tips**  
1. **Visit a Diesel Specialist Shop** 2–3 weeks pre-trip. They’ll handle heavy lifts (transmission, turbo, fuel system).  
2. **Test Drive:** Check for unusual noises, vibrations, or fluid leaks.  
3. **Emergency Kit:** Include brake cleaner, tire repair kit, jumper cables, and a basic toolset.  
4. **Route Planning:** Use apps like **Roadtrippers** or **GasBuddy** to map fuel/rest stops.

> ⚠️ **Diesel Warning:** Old diesels are prone to **water pumps, timing belts, and fuel injectors** failing. Don’t skip professional checks—campers add extra strain.
