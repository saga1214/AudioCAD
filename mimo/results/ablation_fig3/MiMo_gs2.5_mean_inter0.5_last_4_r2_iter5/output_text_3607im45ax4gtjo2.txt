Dealing with a difficult person can be incredibly draining—here are some practical strategies to help manage the stress and protect your well-being:

### 1. **Document Everything**  
   - Keep a record of incidents (dates, times, details, witnesses, and calls to police/CPS). This creates a paper trail if the situation escalates or if authorities need more evidence.

### 2. **Set Clear Boundaries**  
   - Avoid direct confrontation if it risks making things worse. Instead, use non-engagement:  
     - Close blinds/curtains to reduce visibility.  
     - Park farther from their property.  
     - Keep kids indoors during playtimes if calling is a pattern.  

### 3. **Seek Support**  
   - Talk to trusted neighbors, school staff, or community leaders. If others share your concern, collective advocacy (e.g., through a neighborhood association) can amplify your voice.  
   - Consider contacting local authorities (non-emergency police line) to report harassment if calls to CPS/police are persistent and unwarranted.

### 4. **Focus on What You Control**  
   - Redirect energy toward positive activities (like Halloween prep/dress-up days) to help kids and yourself stay grounded.  
   - Use mindfulness or stress-relief techniques (e.g., deep breathing, short walks) to manage immediate anxiety.

### 5. **Legal Options**  
   - If CPS involvement continues without cause, contact their office to ask about reporting protocols. False reports can have consequences.  
   - Explore restraining orders or cease-and-desist letters if harassment becomes severe.

### 6. **Prioritize Your Mental Health**  
   - Stress is valid—don’t minimize it. Talk to a counselor or trusted friend.  
   - Limit exposure: If social media is triggering, mute keywords or take breaks.

### 7. **Community Action**  
   - Organize a peaceful neighborhood meeting to address concerns collectively. A united front can deter escalation.  
   - Support local initiatives (e.g., parenting groups) to build resilience.

> "You can’t control others, but you can control your response. Protecting your family’s peace starts with boundaries and support." — Remember, this stress isn’t your fault. Small steps add up. 💛
