### Difference Between What the Human Eye "Sees" vs. What the Brain Processes  
Let's separate these scientifically without conflating them:

1. **The Eye (Retina as a Sensor):**  
   - Acts as a biological camera capturing light and motion.  
   - Photoreceptors (rods/cones) detect rapid changes in light and motion.  
   - Can respond to visual stimuli up to **~60–75 Hz** (frames per second):  
     - If flashes occur faster than ~60 Hz, many perceive a continuous light (flicker fusion threshold).  
     - Rods detect fast motion (up to 100 Hz in low light), but cones process color/detail slower.  
   - **Limitation:** The eye is not a perfect frame-by-frame recorder. During saccades (eye jumps), it captures **blur and rapid detail**, but the signals are incomplete.  

2. **The Brain (Visual Cortex as Processor):**  
   - Takes retinal signals and constructs **perception**.  
   - Smooths jagged/blurred data and fills perceptual gaps.  
   - **Saccadic masking** is key here:  
     - During rapid eye movements (saccades), the brain **actively suppresses blur** and incomplete visual data.  
     - This means the brain *ignores* the eye’s raw "noise" from movement, preventing dizziness or motion sickness.  
   - **Perception of continuity:** The brain interprets the eye’s discrete data into a **continuous, stable flow of motion**. This is why you don’t consciously notice blur during head turns or saccades.

### Answer to Your Core Question:  
**No, the eye does NOT see continuously.**  
- The eye captures discrete, rapidly changing data (up to ~60 Hz).  
- **The brain processes this data into a continuous perception** using saccadic masking and other mechanisms.  

**Why 60 fps feels "real"?**  
- At ~60 fps, the brain’s processing matches the eye’s sampling speed. Motion appears smooth and lifelike.  
- **Saccadic masking explains why 24 fps feels cinematic:**  
  - At 24 fps, the brain *fills gaps* between frames to create continuity.  
  - This is why film feels "dreamy" or stylized—it’s the brain’s interpretation, not the eye’s raw data.

### Key Takeaway:  
- **Eye = Sensor:** Detects rapid discrete events (up to ~60 Hz).  
- **Brain = Editor:** Suppresses blur (saccadic masking), smooths gaps, and constructs continuity.  
- **Perception = Brain’s output:** What you "see" is the brain’s processed version of the eye’s data—not a direct feed from reality.
