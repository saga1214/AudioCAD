It's understandable to want to "cleanse" your body of these chemicals, but **juice cleanses or water fasts are ineffective at removing PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances)**. Here's why:

1.  **No Scientific Basis:** There's no credible evidence that juice cleanses or fasting can remove these persistent chemicals from your body. Your liver and kidneys naturally process and excrete them slowly over time.
2.  ️ **Focus on Prevention:** Your best strategy is to **prevent further exposure** by avoiding the sources, not trying to "flush" them out.

Here's a concise list of the **top 5 products to avoid** to minimize PFAS/PFOS exposure:

1.  **Non-Stick Cookware (Teflon, etc.):** Use stainless steel, cast iron, or ceramic-coated pots and pans instead.
2.  **Grease-Resistant Packaging:** Fast-food wrappers, microwave popcorn bags, takeout containers, paper bags from coffee shops.
3.  **Stain-Resistant/Waterproofing Treatments:** Carpets, upholstery, clothing (especially outdoor gear), furniture, some leather treatments.
4.  **Certain Cosmetics & Floss:** Some lotions, shampoos, sunscreens, deodorants, and especially certain types of dental floss (look for "PFAS-free" labels or brands like UniteDental Floss).
5.  **Untreated Tap Water:** Use a certified water filter (activated carbon is effective against many PFAS) for drinking and cooking water.

**Key Clarifications on Water:**

*   **Bottled Water:** Avoid because of microplastics *and* potential PFAS from the plastic bottle itself and manufacturing. An activated carbon filter pitcher or faucet filter is a much safer alternative.
*   **Filtered Water Removing Minerals:** While intensive systems like reverse osmosis remove *all* minerals, common **activated carbon filters screw onto faucets or come in pitchers. These certified to reduce PFAS typically leave essential minerals intact.** Look for NSF/ANSI Standard 53 certification for PFAS reduction on these filters.

**Focus on Manageable Actions:**

*   **Switch Cookware:** Stainless steel or cast iron is a simple, effective swap.
*   **Choose Packaging Wisely:** Opt for unpackaged foods or use reusable containers.
*   **Read Labels:** Look for "PFAS-free" claims on clothing, furniture, and personal care products.
*   **Filter Your Water:** Install an activated carbon filter on your tap for drinking water.
*   **Be Moderated with Fish:** Choose lower-mercury, lower-PFAS fish like salmon, shrimp, or canned sardines more often than high-fat fish like swordfish or king mackerel.

While PFAS are persistent, focusing on these high-impact avoidance strategies significantly reduces your ongoing exposure.
