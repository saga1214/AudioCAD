Well, based on the physics of it, at minus ten degrees, that thin layer of water isn't forming, so friction does tend to go up. But it probably doesn't jump all the way down to 0.005. More realistically, the coefficient of friction might increase slightly, maybe from 0.01 to something like 0.015 or 0.02. So it’s actually higher, not lower. The ideal temperature for the lowest possible friction is usually a bit warmer, around minus five to minus seven degrees, where you still get that beneficial water layer and the blade can glide smoothly.
