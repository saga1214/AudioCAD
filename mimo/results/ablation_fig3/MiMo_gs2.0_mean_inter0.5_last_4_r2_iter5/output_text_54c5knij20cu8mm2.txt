Okay, let's tackle this systematically. 

First, the brain hemisphere question. The left-right brain dichotomy is largely a myth. While there are some specialized functions in each hemisphere, the brain is highly integrated. Creative and logical processes happen in the same networks. So, no, people aren't strictly split into "creative" or "logical" hemispheres. 

Now, tinnitus again: it's almost always physiological, stemming from physical changes in the ear or auditory system. Stress can make it more noticeable, but it’s not post-traumatic in the psychological sense. 

For your writing issue—adding letters from subsequent words—that aligns with dysgraphia or simply orthographic processing difficulties during writing. Even if you know spelling, motor planning can get ahead of or interfere with your actual handwriting. 

Regarding your career dilemma: you want structure, measurement, creativity, and mobility—not a desk job. Here’s a concrete suggestion: 
- **Technical illustration or data visualization**: This merges measurement (data) with creativity (making it visually compelling), and the work can be portable. 
- **Architecture or design engineering**: Building things structurally with creativity, and the work often involves physical spaces, not just a desk. 
- **Scientific policy or tech writing**: Structuring complex information objectively while being creative in communication, with opportunities for fieldwork or client interaction. 

These fields offer the "indestructible" framework you like while leaving room for creative flow. Neuroscience might limit the flow you want, but applied fields like cognitive tech or human factors engineering could bridge both.
