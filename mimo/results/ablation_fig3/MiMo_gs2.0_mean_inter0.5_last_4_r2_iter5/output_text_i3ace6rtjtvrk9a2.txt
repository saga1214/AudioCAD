### The Difference: Eye vs. Brain Processing
1. **Eye (Eye/Retina): The Biological Sensor**  
   - The eye detects **rapid changes in light and motion** via photoreceptors (rods/cones).  
   - It has a **flicker fusion threshold** (typically 60 Hz for many people): Flashing lights faster than this threshold merge into a continuous perception.  
   - During **saccades** (rapid eye jumps between focal points), the eye *does not* see continuously. Instead, it senses rapid shifts in visual input.  

2. **Brain (Visual Cortex): The Interpreter**  
   - The brain **smooths, filters, and reconstructs** visual data from the eye.  
   - **Saccadic masking** occurs when the brain *suppresses blur* during saccades to prevent motion sickness or confusion. You consciously perceive stable, continuous motion despite the eye's intermittent sampling.  
   - The brain fills in gaps (e.g., between saccades) to create a coherent percept.  

### The Eye's "Continuous" Perception? Not Exactly.
- During **saccades**, the eye *avoids* continuous sampling. Saccadic masking actively *blurs out* blur during motion.  
- Between saccades, the eye samples **static or slowly moving** scenes.  
- Photoreceptors (rods/cones) respond to **changes in light**, not static images. They are "sampling" light rapidly, but the brain integrates these samples into a continuous percept.  

### Why 24 fps Feels "Natural"
- 24 fps is *close* to the flicker fusion threshold (~60 Hz) and the eye's sampling rate. The brain fills gaps between frames, mimicking the eye's behavior during saccades.  
- Higher frame rates (e.g., 60 fps) reduce gaps between frames, but the brain still smooths them into continuous motion. Saccadic masking remains active.  

### Conclusion
- **The eye does NOT see continuously during saccades**—saccadic masking suppresses blur.  
- **The brain creates the perception of continuity** by smoothing eye input.  
- **What feels "natural" (60 fps or 24 fps) is a result of the brain's processing**, not the eye's raw data.  

This hierarchy explains why films use 24 fps: it leverages the brain's natural motion-processing to create continuity, while saccades and saccadic masking ensure smooth perception despite the eye's intermittent sampling.
