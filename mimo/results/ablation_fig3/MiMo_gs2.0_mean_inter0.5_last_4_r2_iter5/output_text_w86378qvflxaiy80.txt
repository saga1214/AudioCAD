Based on everything we've discussed, I believe the most important aspect is that biodiversity hotspots are the very foundation of irreplaceable ecosystem services that vulnerable human populations depend on for their survival.

While the existence of unique species and their potential for undiscovered medicines are certainly important, the direct, tangible role these small areas play in providing clean water, pollination, and climate regulation for millions of people is arguably the most critical factor. Protecting them means safeguarding the immediate, life-sustaining functions of our planet's most fragile environments.
