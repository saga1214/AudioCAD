You're absolutely right. Public acceptance, weighted for resistance, becomes the deciding factor. Resistance in vaccination campaigns can completely derail them. Sugar campaigns might face criticism, but the resistance is not as extreme. So, if resistance is the biggest risk, a vaccination campaign becomes much riskier. In that case, I would definitely choose the sugar reduction campaign.

It might take longer and be harder, but at least its goals are not politically divisive. The vaccination campaign's success is too fragile to bet on in a polarized environment. It's better to make a change that people will accept, even if it's difficult, than to risk a campaign that could be shut down by controversy.
