Okay, let's break this down into a simple, step-by-step process for you. Think of it like building with LEGOs: each piece has its place.

First up is your Header. Center your name in about a fourteen to sixteen-point font. Right below that, on the same line, put your email, phone number, city, and state. Adding icons is optional and might cause automated system issues. A pro tip is to add a link to your LinkedIn profile here, too.

Next, create a Professional Summary. This is a two-to-three sentence paragraph at the top that highlights your most relevant skills and career goals. This is your chance to frame that long career gap.

Then, for Experience, create a section called "Relevant Experience." Here, you'll list the older jobs that directly match your target roles, with full descriptions. After that, create a section called "Additional Work History" where you can briefly list other roles, just with the job title, company, and dates.

For Education, list your degrees from the same institution, starting with the most recent. Include details like your expected graduation date, honors, and scholarships directly under the corresponding degree.

Then, you should definitely have a Skills Section. List your most important, relevant skills here. You can even organize them into categories like Technical Skills or Soft Skills to make them easier to scan.

If you have any, you can also add a Certifications section and an Awards section, which works best when tucked right into the Education section for academic awards.

Finally, at the very bottom, add a brief Objective Statement if you're targeting a very specific job.

So, to put it all together: your resume should flow from your name at the top, down to contact info, then your summary, followed by your most relevant experience, your additional work history, your education with honors, and then your skills. This structure tells a clear story of your career and makes it easy for a recruiter to find what they need.
