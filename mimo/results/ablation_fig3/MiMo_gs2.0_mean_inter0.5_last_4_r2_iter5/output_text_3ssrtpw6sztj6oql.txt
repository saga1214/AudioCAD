Here's a refined answer for your paper:

**For a modern-day art museum, the first line of defense is robust access control at all entry points. This combines a visible security presence (guards) with procedures like screening (bag checks, ID verification) to actively manage and restrict who enters the museum building and its sensitive areas.**

**Key reasons this is the first line:**

1.  **Prevention:** Its primary goal is to stop a threat *before* it enters the building.
2.  **Deterrence:** Visible guards and screening procedures deter potential attackers.
3.  **Detection Trigger:** It's the first point where an anomaly (wrong person, suspicious item) is detected and can be stopped immediately.
4.  **Foundation:** All other layers (surveillance, alarms, physical barriers) become ineffective if unauthorized personnel are already inside.

**Addressing Human Error:**

You're absolutely right that humans are vulnerable. To mitigate this, modern security integrates:
*   **Strict Training & Protocols:** Especially for guards on de-escalation, suspicion, and protocol adherence.
*   **Technology-Assisted Screening:** Automated bag checks, X-ray, or even AI-powered video analytics can supplement human judgment at the entrance.
*   **Multi-Factor Authentication:** Keycard systems often require a second factor (like biometrics or a PIN) for non-public areas, reducing reliance on a single human checkpoint.
*   **Behavioral Detection:** Training guards to identify subtle signs of distress or potential threats.

**In essence:** While secure displays, sensors, and cameras are vital *secondary* layers, the **first line of defense must be the active management of human access** at the very gates of the museum.
