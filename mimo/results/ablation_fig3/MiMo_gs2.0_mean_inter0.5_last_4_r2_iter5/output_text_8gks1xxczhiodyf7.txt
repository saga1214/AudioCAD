If I had to choose **one top driver** behind increasing obesity rates, I’d still say:  
**The widespread availability and marketing of highly processed, calorie-dense foods.**  

### Why this stands out:  
1. **Accessibility & Affordability**:  
   - These foods are often cheaper, longer-lasting, and require minimal prep, making them a default choice for many.  
   - They’re engineered to be hyper-palatable (high in sugar, fat, salt), triggering overconsumption.  

2. **Displacement of Nutrient-Rich Foods**:  
   - Even if portions are controlled, processed foods displace healthier options (like whole foods, legumes, or lean proteins) in diets, limiting satiety and nutrient intake.  

3. **Behavioral Impact**:  
   - They’re heavily advertised, shaping preferences and making unhealthy choices the norm.  
   - Convenience often trumps intentionality in modern lifestyles.  

### Nuance:  
While **physical inactivity** and **genetics** play roles, processed foods uniquely *enable* overconsumption at a societal scale. Even if someone exercises or eats "small portions," the sheer volume and design of these foods make it easier to exceed calorie needs without realizing it.  

> **Key Takeaway**: Obesity is driven by *caloric balance*, but processed foods make achieving that balance harder by design. Addressing food environments (e.g., reducing marketing, improving access to whole foods) is critical.
