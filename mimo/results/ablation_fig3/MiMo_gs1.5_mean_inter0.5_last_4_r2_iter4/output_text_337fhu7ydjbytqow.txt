Here's a clear, separated packing list for both pacing segments, optimized for your conditions:

### **20-Mile Overnight Segment (Night Running)**
1.  **Base Layer:** Moisture-wicking top & bottom (synthetic or merino wool).
2.  **Insulating Mid-Layer:** Fleece jacket or lightweight puffy/sweater (essential for warmth).
3.  **Outer Layer:** Windproof & water-resistant shell jacket (e.g., rain jacket).
4.  **Headlamp:** Bright, reliable headlamp with **extra batteries** (critical!).
5.  **Second Light:** Handheld or waist light (if headlamp battery is heavy).
6.  **Gloves:** Warm running gloves (hands get cold fast on trails).
7.  **Hat:** Warm beanie or fleece hat (cover ears/neck).
8.  **Buff/Gaiter:** Versatile for neck, face, or wrists.
9.  **Trail Shoes:** Sturdy, grippy trail running shoes (hills & uneven ground).
10. **Light Pack/Vest:** Small pack or vest to stash layers as you warm up.
11. **Extra Layers:** Pack spare base layer & mid-layer in your bag.

### **Final 12-Mile Segment (Daytime Running)**
1.  **Base Layer:** Moisture-wicking top & bottom (synthetic/merino wool).
2.  **Light Insulation:** Fleece or light jacket (if chilly morning).
3.  **Sun Protection (CRITICAL):**  
    *   **Sun Hat:** Wide-brimmed trail hat or baseball cap.
    *   **Sunglasses:** UV-blocking with strap (secure fit).
    *   **Sunscreen:** Reapply frequently (especially on face/hands).
    *   **Lip Balm:** SPF 30+.
4.  **Light Jacket:** If morning is cool, bring a packable jacket.
5.  **Headlamp/Handheld Light:** *Just in case* clouds roll in or it stays cool.
6.  **Trail Shoes:** Same as 20-mile segment (hills & terrain).
7.  **Light Pack/Vest:** To carry layers/sun protection.
8.  **Extra Layers:** Pack spare base layer & light jacket in bag.

### **Key Considerations for Both Segments**
*   **Packability:** Use a small backpack or vest – you'll need it for aid station drops and layer swapping.
*   **Visibility:** Add reflective gear (vest, ankle bands) if not in a well-lit area.
*   **Weather Check:** Review the forecast closer to race time – adjust layers accordingly.
*   **Comfort:** Wear your shoes and test gear before race day.
*   **Aid Station Strategy:** Plan what *you* need to drop off (water bottles, layers, energy gels) based on your pace.

This list covers all your needs – warmth, trail safety, sun protection, and packability – for both the challenging overnight segment and the decisive final miles. Good luck pacing your husband!
