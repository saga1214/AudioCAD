Dealing with a difficult person like your neighbor requires a mix of self-care, strategy, and boundaries. Here’s actionable advice tailored to your situation:

### 1. **Prioritize Your Well-being**
   - **Limit Exposure:** Avoid direct confrontation if possible. Use email or official channels for communication.
   - **Vent Safely:** Talk to trusted friends/family (like you’re doing here) to release stress—don’t bottle it up.
   - **Self-Care:** Engage in activities that reduce stress (e.g., listening to "Lemonade" or dress-up days at school).

### 2. **Document Everything**
   - **Keep Records:** Log dates, times, and details of incidents (e.g., "Kids playing bikes – police called at 3 PM"). Save texts/emails.
   - **Report Patterns:** If CPS involvement occurs, note how often she reports children. Documenting helps authorities take action.

### 3. **Set Clear Boundaries**
   - **Communicate Calmly:** If interaction is unavoidable, use "I" statements:  
     *"I’m concerned when police are called for children playing. Can we discuss solutions?"*
   - **No Engagement:** Don’t justify actions or argue. Respond with: *"We’re following school rules. Please stop contacting us."* then disengage.

### 4. **Involve Authorities**
   - **CPS Follow-Up:** If CPS is involved, request a case number and ask how to support resolution.
   - **Police Report:** If harassment escalates, file a formal complaint with your police department.
   - **Neighborhood Resources:** Contact your HOA, landlord, or local community board for mediation support.

### 5. **Protect Your Kids’ Normalcy**
   - **Reinforce Safety:** Teach kids to avoid the neighbor but reassure them it’s *not their fault*.
   - **Alternative Activities:** Encourage supervised play in safe spaces (parks, friends’ yards) to avoid her property.

### 6. **Seek Support**
   - **Legal Advice:** Consult a lawyer if threats or false reports persist (many offer free consultations).
   - **Community Support:** Reach out to other affected families—collective voices can prompt action.

### 7. **Long-Term Solutions**
   - **No Contact Order:** If feasible, explore restraining orders (requires evidence of harassment).
   - **Relocation:** If stress becomes unbearable, consider moving. Your family’s peace is non-negotiable.

> 💡 **Key Insight**: Your stress is valid, but *you can’t control her behavior*. Focus on what you *can* control: your responses, documentation, and seeking systemic help. Small victories (like your kids enjoying dress-up) keep hope alive. 🎃

If CPS or police involvement deepens, consider reaching out to organizations like [Childhelp](https://www.childhelp.org/) for guidance. You’re doing an amazing job protecting your family—keep leaning on those bright moments! 🌟
