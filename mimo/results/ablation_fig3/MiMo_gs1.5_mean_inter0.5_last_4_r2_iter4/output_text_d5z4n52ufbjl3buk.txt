Here are key pointers for your email, balancing respect with clarity:

**Email Structure & Content:**

1.  **Subject Line:** Clear and professional.  
    *Example:* "Request for Feedback on Recent Exam" or "Seeking Clarification on Exam Grade"

2.  **Opening - Respectful & Professional:**  
    *Example:* "Dear Professor [Last Name], I hope this email finds you well. Thank you for the opportunity to discuss my recent exam performance."

3.  **State Your Purpose Clearly & Neutrally:**  
    *Example:* "I scored a 64 on my latest exam and, while I understand the grade, I'd genuinely appreciate the chance to understand what aspects of my essay could have been improved to reach that score. My primary goal is to learn from this experience to enhance my performance on future assignments."

4.  **Focus on the Subjective Nature & Your Approach (Carefully):**  
    *Example:* "As the exam was essay-based, I recognize grading can involve subjective interpretation. I'm particularly interested in understanding how the content was evaluated, as I'd like to ensure my future work better aligns with expectations."

5.  **Request Specific Feedback (Crucial):**  
    *Example:* "If you have the time, I would be very grateful for any specific feedback you could share regarding areas where my argument, analysis, or structure fell short. This would be immensely helpful for my learning."

6.  **Suggest a Meeting & Be Flexible:**  
    *Example:* "Would you be available for a brief meeting during your office hours or at another convenient time? I understand your schedule is demanding and I'm happy to work around yours."

7.  **Closing - Respectful & Forward-Looking:**  
    *Example:* "Thank you for considering this request. I appreciate your time and guidance."

8.  **Signature:**  
    *Example:* "Best regards,"  
    [Your Full Name]  
    [Your Student ID]  
    [Your Class/Section if relevant]

**Key Principles to Uphold:**

*   **Focus on Learning, Not Challenging:** Frame the entire request around your desire to *improve*. Avoid words like "unfair," "question," or "why."
*   **Be Specific About What You Want:** Ask for *specific* feedback on *content*, structure, or argument, not just "what did I do wrong?"
*   **Acknowledge Subjectivity (Briefly):** Mentioning the essay nature and subjective grading *once* is okay to set context, but don't dwell on it. Immediately pivot to your request for learning.
*   **Avoid Blame or Emotion:** Keep the tone professional, respectful, and solution-oriented. No sighs, no "I feel," no accusations.
*   **Manage Expectations:** You are not necessarily asking for the grade to be changed. You are asking for the *reasoning* behind it to learn.
*   **Offer Flexibility:** Show you understand his time constraints by offering to meet outside office hours.

**Why this approach works with a difficult professor:**

*   **Minimizes Defensiveness:** It avoids direct confrontation or questioning his ego. It's about *your* growth.
*   **Focuses on the Task:** It presents a clear, actionable request (feedback) rather than an abstract complaint.
*   **Shows Professionalism:** It demonstrates maturity and a genuine interest in learning, which is usually respected, even if the professor is difficult.
*   **Creates a Record:** If he does provide feedback, you have it. If he doesn't respond, you've formally requested it.
