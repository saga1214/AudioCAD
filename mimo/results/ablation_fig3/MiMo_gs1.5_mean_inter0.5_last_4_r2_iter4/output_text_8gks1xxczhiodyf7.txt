Based on the evidence, the **single biggest top driver behind increasing obesity rates is the easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods.** Here's why:

1.  **Calorie Density & Low Satiety:** Processed foods (like chips, sugary drinks, fast food, packaged snacks) are often packed with calories from refined sugars, unhealthy fats, and salt, but are low in protein, fiber, and water. This combination makes them easier to overeat because they don't trigger fullness signals effectively.
2.  **Portion Size & Design:** They are often engineered to be hyper-palatable and come in portions much larger than traditional home-cooked meals, encouraging consumption without conscious awareness.
3.  **Affordability & Accessibility:** They are often cheaper per calorie than nutrient-dense whole foods and are ubiquitous, available 24/7, and heavily marketed, making them the default choice for many.
4.  **Impact on Behavior:** Their ease of consumption (no preparation, constant availability) directly facilitates overconsumption. Even if someone is active, consistently overeating these foods will lead to weight gain over time.
5.  **Synergy with Inactivity:** While physical inactivity is a major *contributor*, processed foods make it *easier* to become inactive in the first place (due to convenience) and make the inactivity factor even more potent (as the calories from processed foods are less satiating and more easily consumed).

**Why other factors, while important, are secondary in this specific question:**

*   **Physical Inactivity:** Crucial, but processed foods are a primary *cause* of both the calorie surplus and the energy needed for activity (making inactivity worse). They also make it easier to become inactive.
*   **Nutrient Quality:** Important for overall health, but the core driver of *weight gain* is the calorie surplus. Poor nutrient quality exacerbates health risks beyond weight.
*   **Portion Sizes:** Matter a lot, but processed foods are specifically designed to make large portions appealing and easy to consume.
*   **Genetics/Hormones:** Play a role in predisposition, but they don't explain the *global, rapid increase* in obesity rates. Environmental factors like food availability are primary drivers.
*   **Calorie-Dense Unprocessed Foods (e.g., nuts, oils):** While calorie-dense, these are harder to overconsume *as easily* as processed foods due to higher satiety factors (fiber, fat, protein). They are also less heavily marketed and consumed in larger default portions.

**In essence:** The combination of high calorie density, low satiety, massive availability, affordability, and constant marketing makes processed foods the most potent *environmental* driver of the *calorie surplus* that leads to obesity. While inactivity is a major *lifestyle* factor, processed foods are a fundamental *cause* of both the calorie surplus and the conditions that foster inactivity.
