Okay, let's break this down. Vasculitis is an autoimmune disorder where the body's immune system mistakenly attacks the walls of blood vessels, causing inflammation. This inflammation can occur in blood vessels of any size—large, medium, or small—which is why it can affect so many different parts of the body. The symptoms really depend on which blood vessels are affected and where they are in the body.

For example, if large blood vessels are inflamed, it can lead to things like joint pain, fever, and fatigue. If medium-sized vessels are affected, skin rashes and ulcers are common. And when it's the small blood vessels, it can cause problems with the nerves, kidneys, lungs, or eyes. Some people might even have a cough or shortness of breath from it.

The exact cause is often unknown, but it's usually triggered by an autoimmune issue, an infection, or sometimes it's just random. A proper diagnosis is key, and it usually involves a doctor taking a detailed history, doing a physical exam, and likely ordering some tests, like blood work or a biopsy of an affected area.

As for the redness you're seeing, it could be related to vasculitis, but it could also be from many other things, like rosacea, eczema, sun damage, or even allergies. It's really important to see a doctor to figure out what's causing it so you can get the right treatment. They can help address the root cause and also suggest things that might help with hair, nails, and skin, like specific supplements or topical treatments.
