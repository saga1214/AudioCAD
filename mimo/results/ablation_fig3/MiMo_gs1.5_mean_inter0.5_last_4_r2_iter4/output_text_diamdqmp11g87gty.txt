That's a fantastic attitude for winter! Here are some great outdoor winter activities to explore, building on your interests:

### 1. **Snowshoeing**  
   - **Gear Essentials**:  
     - **Snowshoes**: Rent or buy beginner-friendly frames (3–4 sq ft) with adjustable bindings.  
     - **Boots**: Waterproof, insulated boots with crampons for traction.  
     - **Poles**: For balance and conserving energy.  
     - **Layered Clothing**: Moisture-wicking base, insulated mid-layer, and a windproof/waterproof shell.  
   - **Tips**: Start on groomed trails (e.g., state parks or ski resorts), then explore backcountry paths. Apps like **AllTrails** filter for snowshoe-friendly routes.  

### 2. **Cross-Country Skiing**  
   - Similar to snowshoeing but more efficient. Rentals available at ski shops.  
   - **Ideal For**: Long-distance exploration with breathtaking views.  

### 3. **Ice Skating**  
   - **Public Rinks**: Many cities have outdoor rinks (e.g., Chicago’s Buckingham Park).  
   - **Frozen Lakes**: *Only* on naturally frozen lakes with safety guidelines (check ice thickness with a spud bar).  
   - **Learn Basic Strokes**: T-Wing and swan stances improve stability.  

### 4. **Winter Hiking**  
   - **Trail Safety**: Use a map/GPS (snow obscures paths). Pack a headlamp, ice axe, and shovel.  
   - **Scenic Spots**: Look for frozen waterfalls, animal tracks, or evergreen forests.  
   - **App**: **Gaia GPS** for offline trail navigation.  

### 5. **Snowmobiling**  
   - **Rentals**: Try trails near cities like Door County (WI) or Apostle Islands.  
   - **Beginner-Friendly**: Guided tours are great for first-timers.  

### 6. **Dog Sledding**  
   - **Experiences**: Join a guided mush tour (e.g., in Northern WI or Michigan’s Upper Peninsula).  
   - **Bonus**: Many sled dog kennels offer meet-and-greets!  

### 7. **Winter Wildlife Watching**  
   - **Birding**: Winter migrants like chickadees, nuthatches, and owls. Use apps like **Merlin Bird ID**.  
   - **Mammals**: Track deer, coyotes, or foxes in quiet woods.  

### 8. **Snow Caving**  
   - **Adventure**: Find snowdrifts >10 ft deep in open fields.  
   - **Safety**: Bring a headlamp, shovel, and a buddy. Never go alone!  

### 9. **Winter Camping**  
   - **Gear Up**: Insulated tents, heated sleeping bags (e.g., -20°F rated), and a stove for cooking.  
   - **Best Spots**: Mountain cabins or remote lakes.  

### 10. **Winter Sports for Kids**  
   - **Sledding**: Build jumps with logs or snowbanks.  
   - **Snow Tubing**: Some parks offer designated slopes.  

### ⚠️ **Safety Reminders**:  
- **Layer Up**: Hypothermia is real—avoid cotton clothing.  
- **Visibility**: Wear reflective gear and a headlamp for dawn/dusk outings.  
- **Avalanche Awareness**: If venturing into backcountry, check forecasts (e.g., **Avalanche.org**).
