Okay, let's put this all together into a clear, step-by-step structure for your resume. 

First, at the very top, you'll have your name in about a 14 to 16-point font. Right below that, in a smaller 9 to 10-point font, you'll list your contact info in this order: email, phone number, city and state, and optionally your LinkedIn profile URL. A professional email address is very important here. You can use icons, but as we discussed, it's often safer to just use plain text.

Next, you'll have a short professional summary, which is a two or three-sentence pitch about your career goals and key skills. This is where you can address a long career gap.

After that, the main part is your work experience. You can create a "Relevant Experience" section for roles that are most pertinent to the jobs you want now, where you'll list the company, your title, the dates, and just one or two bullet points highlighting your achievements. Then, you can have an "Additional Work History" section for all other jobs, also with just the company, title, and dates.

Following work experience, you'll have your education. Here, you'll list your degree, the school, and any honors or awards you mentioned, like "Graduated with Highest Honors" or scholarship details.

Then comes the skills section. It's crucial to tailor this to the job description you're applying for. You can list them as categories, like "Technical Skills," "Software Proficiency," or "Languages."

If you have any, you can add a brief "Awards and Certifications" section. 

Finally, at the very bottom, you can add optional sections for things like a link to your personal website or your volunteer work.

The most important thing to remember is to keep each section brief and focused. Use simple bullet points and avoid large blocks of text. This structure will help you create a professional resume that clearly showcases your relevant skills, no matter how long your career history might seem.
