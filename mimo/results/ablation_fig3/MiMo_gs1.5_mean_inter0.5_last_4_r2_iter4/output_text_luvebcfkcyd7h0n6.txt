Then there's entanglement. This is like having two actors whose performances are linked. The moment one is cast as the hero, the other instantly becomes the villain, no matter where they are on the set.

Budgets, or decoherence time, represent the amount of time your actors can hold all their potential roles at once. Outside noise, like a phone ringing, distracts them and makes them break character. You have to finish shooting the scene before that budget of focus runs out.

Editing is like quantum error correction. Even the best actor might make a small mistake, and editing is how you fix those errors in post-production to ensure the final answer is correct.

Qubits are your actors. Each individual actor can hold multiple roles. The more actors you have, the more complex the scene becomes, and adding just one more actor exponentially increases the potential complexity.

And finally, the final cut is the solution to your problem. It's the finished movie you get after all the acting and editing are done, representing the single, clear answer that the quantum computer gives you.
