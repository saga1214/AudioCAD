That's an exciting end-of-year event to plan! Here are a few suggestions keeping your situation and goals in mind:

1.  **The "Picnic & Patch Party" Celebration:**
    *   **Location:** A local park with a nice picnic area (check for permits if needed, especially if parents join). Have a backup plan (like the cafeteria) in case of rain.
    *   **Activities:**
        *   **Badge Ceremony:** Start with a simple but special ceremony where you award the badges the troop earned throughout the year. This is a great highlight!
        *   **Simple Games:** Keep the games light and fun – a relaxed version of the three-legged race (maybe using lighter material or having an adult help with setup), a sack race, or even a scavenger hunt around the park. Focus on participation over winning.
        *   **Food:** This is perfect for inclusivity! Organize a **potluck-style picnic**:
            *   **You:** Provide the main centerpiece – maybe a large ice cream sundae bar (ice cream, lots of toppings - ensure some are dairy-free/dairy-free options), or a pizza (check dietary needs, maybe a gluten-free option too). Or, you could organize a BBQ where you handle grilling burgers/hot dogs (dairy-free buns), and families bring sides/desserts.
            *   **Families:** Ask each family to bring one dish to share (e.g., chips & dip - *perfect nostalgia!*), a dessert, or drinks. This spreads the cost and effort.
    *   **Atmosphere:** Casual, relaxed, outdoorsy. Focus on celebrating the girls' achievements and having fun together.

2.  **The "Cafeteria Fiesta":**
    *   **Location:** The familiar cafeteria setting.
    *   **Activities:**
        *   **Patch Ceremony:** Still the star of the show!
        *   **Fun & Games:** Incorporate simple, easy-to-manage activities *inside* the cafeteria:
            *   **Craft Station:** Decorate their own sashes or badge holders with stickers, markers, etc.
            *   **Movie Time:** Show a fun, kid-friendly movie (or a short Brownie-themed video).
            *   **Simple Crafts:** Make friendship bracelets or decorate cookies/cupcakes (if baking is feasible).
            *   **Dance Party:** Put on some upbeat music and let them dance!
    *   **Food:** Keep it simple and fun! Maybe a pizza party (you handle grating cheese carefully!), or a "treat" like a sundae bar (ice cream, toppings) or cupcakes. This is easier to manage logistically than a full picnic with multiple families contributing.

3.  **The "BBQ Bash" (If you feel up to it and it fits your troop):**
    *   **Location:** A park with a designated BBQ area (again, permits!).
    *   **Activities:**
        *   **Badge Ceremony:** Essential!
        *   **BBQ Fun:** Focus on the social aspect. Set up simple games like cornhole, giant Jenga, or a bean bag toss near the grill area. Keep the cooking simple (burgers, hot dogs, maybe veggie burgers).
        *   **Food:** You could organize a BBQ where you are responsible for grilling the main items (ensure dairy-free buns/condiments are available). Families could bring sides (potato salad, coleslaw, chips & dip!) and desserts. This shares the food load.
    *   **Atmosphere:** Very casual, family-oriented, and fun. Great for celebrating together.

**Key Considerations for YOU:**

*   **Pregnancy:** Prioritize comfort and safety. Avoid heavy lifting, long walks in the sun, or strenuous games. Sit down! Have plenty of water. Delegate tasks if possible (ask a parent helper for setup/cleanup at a park event).
*   **Inclusivity:** Dietary needs (your dairy avoidance, others' preferences for ice cream/pizza) are crucial. A sundae bar, pizza, or BBQ with diverse options covers this well. Always ask about allergies.
*   **Cost:** A cafeteria event is usually the cheapest. A picnic/potluck or BBQ shares costs with families. A sundae bar or cupcakes is also relatively affordable.
*   **Rain Plan:** *Essential* if doing an outdoor event. Have the cafeteria or even a nearby community center as a backup.
*   **Keep it Simple:** Your first year, and especially while pregnant, is about celebrating their achievements and having fun. Don't overcomplicate it. A simple badge ceremony with a shared meal and some games is perfect.

**Recommendation:** The **Picnic & Patch Party** or the **Cafeteria Fiesta** feel like the most manageable, inclusive, and fun options for your first year and your current situation. They focus on celebrating the girls, are easy to execute, and allow for great food variety.

**Most importantly:** Your enthusiasm and willingness to step up is what will make this celebration special for your daughters. You've got this!
