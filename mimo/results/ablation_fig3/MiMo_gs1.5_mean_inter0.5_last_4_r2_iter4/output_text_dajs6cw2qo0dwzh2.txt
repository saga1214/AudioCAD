That's a really interesting counterfactual question. It's tempting to say yes, but the fall of Constantinople did play a specific role. It acted as a huge catalyst and a symbol of the instability in the traditional trade system. Even though the Portuguese were moving forward, that event confirmed their fears and gave them a massive competitive advantage. It also spurred other nations like Spain to invest heavily in exploration. So, while the underlying trends were there, 1453 was a major accelerant and a key milestone in the race.
