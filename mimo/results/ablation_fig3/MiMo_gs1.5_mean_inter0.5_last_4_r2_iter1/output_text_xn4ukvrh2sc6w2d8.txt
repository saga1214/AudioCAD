Okay, here are the recipes I'd recommend for your Art Deco airport reception, all with detailed measurements for your bartenders. 

First up, the Old Fashioned: You'll need two ounces of bourbon, like Maker's Mark or Woodford Reserve. Then, one sugar cube, or if you prefer, half an ounce of simple syrup. Add two to three dashes of Angostura bitters, and finally, an orange twist for the garnish. To make it, muddle the sugar cube with bitters and a splash of water until it dissolves, add the bourbon, stir it with ice, and then serve it over fresh ice in a rocks glass. You can garnish it with an orange twist or even an orange slice.

Next, the Paper Plane: For this one, you'll use two ounces of bourbon, like Bulleit Bourbon. Then, three-quarters of an ounce of Aperol, half an ounce of Amaro Nonino, and three-quarters of an ounce of fresh lemon juice. You'll shake all of that with ice and strain it into a coupe glass.

For your Whiskey Sour, you'll use two ounces of bourbon, three-quarters of an ounce of fresh lemon juice, and half an ounce of simple syrup. If you're using the egg white, you'll add one fresh egg white along with these ingredients. You dry shake everything first to foam the egg white, then add ice and shake again, and strain it into a rocks glass. For garnish, you can use a cherry or an orange slice.

The Aviation is a great choice too. It's made with two ounces of gin, like Beefeater or Tanqueray, half an ounce of Maraschino Liqueur, a quarter-ounce of crème de violette, and half an ounce of fresh lemon juice. You'll shake all of that with ice and strain it into a chilled cocktail glass.

And for the third gin cocktail, the French 75: You'll need one and a half ounces of gin, three-quarters of an ounce of fresh lemon juice, half an ounce of simple syrup, and you'll top it with about two ounces of champagne or prosecco. You can garnish it with a lemon twist.

These should give your bartenders a clear, classic recipe to follow. Let me know if you'd like any adjustments!
