That's a profound question, and it's really about balancing all the different values we place on nature.

If I had to pick the single most important aspect, it would be the preservation of irreplaceable genetic diversity and the potential knowledge it holds. 

Here’s why: The species within hotspots are genetically unique, and this genetic information is essential for things like developing new medicines, creating more resilient crops for our farms, and understanding fundamental biological processes. Losing a species in a hotspot means losing a potential source of future scientific breakthroughs and solutions to human challenges. This genetic library is unique to that specific place and cannot be replicated. While protecting ecosystem services is vital for immediate human welfare, and unique species are a core part of the concept, the underlying genetic diversity represents the ultimate, non-renewable resource for our collective future. It’s the foundation upon which all other benefits are built.
