That's a good question. While the mechanical steps for changing the oil are identical, **car ramps are not automatically safe for every identical vehicle.** Here's why:

1.  **Weight Rating:** Ramps are rated for specific vehicle weights. Even if the cars look identical, one might be slightly heavier than the other, or have a different engine type that adds weight.
2.  **Engine/Transmission Clearance:** The exact placement of the engine, transmission, and exhaust system might differ slightly between identical models. Ramps need enough clearance to get under the vehicle safely. A ramp designed for one car might be too low for another.
3.  **Tire Size & Spacing:** While unlikely to differ significantly between identical models, the exact tire size or spacing could theoretically affect how the ramp sits.
4.  **Manufacturer Specifications:** The ramp manufacturer has specific guidelines about which vehicles it is safe for. Just because two cars look the same doesn't mean they are on the manufacturer's approved list.

**What you should do:**

*   **Check the Ramp Manufacturer's Website/Manual:** Look for the specific vehicle model, year, and engine type that the ramps are rated for. **This is the most important step.**
*   **Call the Ramp Manufacturer:** If you can't find the info online, call their customer service. Tell them you have a [Your Car's Exact Year, Make, Model, and Engine Size] and ask if the ramps are safe for it.
*   **Test Fit (Safely):** If possible, carefully place the ramps under your car *without* lifting it onto them. Check that the front wheels lift easily onto the ramps and that there is sufficient clearance under the engine/transmission for you to get under safely. Look for any contact points.

**In summary:** While the *job* is the same, **the safety of the ramps depends entirely on the manufacturer's specifications for your specific car.** Don't assume compatibility just because the cars look identical. Always verify the ramp's rating for your exact vehicle.
