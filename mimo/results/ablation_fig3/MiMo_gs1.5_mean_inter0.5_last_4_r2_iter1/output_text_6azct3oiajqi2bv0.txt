That's a smart approach! Here are the safest ways to stick something to a jacket lining without damaging the fabric or the lining:

1.  **Removable Adhesive Patches:** This is often the best option.
    *   **Choose Carefully:** Use patches specifically designed for fabric, like iron-on patches (but see note below) or fabric glue patches.
    *   **Iron-On Patches:** **Crucially, DO NOT use an iron directly on the jacket lining.** Instead:
        *   Iron the patch *on a cotton cloth* (like a pillowcase or scrap) for 10-15 seconds according to the patch instructions.
        *   Place the patch *right side down* on the lining fabric.
        *   Iron the *back* of the patch (the wrong side) with *low heat* for 10-15 seconds, pressing firmly. The heat activates the adhesive.
        *   Let it cool completely before testing. Avoid high heat on synthetics.
    *   **Fabric Glue Patches:** Apply a small dot of strong fabric glue (like E6000 or Aleene's Tacky Glue) to the patch backing, press firmly, and hold for 30 seconds. These are removable with care.

2.  **Fabric Glue (Carefully):**
    *   Use a strong, flexible fabric glue (like E6000 or Aleene's Tacky Glue) designed for seams and patches.
    *   Apply a *tiny* amount *only* to the *back* of the item you want to stick (e.g., a button, a charm, a name tag).
    *   Press firmly onto the lining for several minutes, ensuring it's well-adhered.
    *   **Test First:** Apply a tiny bit to an inconspicuous seam or hem first to check for color transfer or stiffness.

3.  **Velcro (Hook & Loop):**
    *   This is fantastic for removable items like a name tag, pocket, or even a small pouch.
    *   Apply strong fabric glue (like E6000) to the *hook* side of the Velcro, press firmly onto the lining, and hold for several minutes.
    *   Apply glue to the *loop* side of the Velcro, press firmly onto the *outside* of the jacket, and hold for several minutes.
    *   **Ensure good contact** between hook and loop when attached.

4.  **Double-Sided Fabric Tape:**
    *   Find double-sided tape specifically labeled for fabric or upholstery. It's less bulky than glue.
    *   Apply a small strip to the *back* of the item and press firmly onto the lining.

**What to AVOID:**

*   **Strong Glues (like Gorilla Glue, Hot Glue):** These can become brittle, crack the fabric, or leave residue that's hard to remove. They also can't be removed cleanly.
*   **Permanent Tape:** Standard masking tape or duct tape will rip the fabric and leave residue.
*   **Direct Iron-On:** Never place a hot iron directly on the jacket lining. The heat and pressure will likely melt or scorch the synthetic fibers.
*   **Sharp Objects:** Don't try to staple, sew, or use super glue directly on the lining unless you're very confident and use the strongest, most flexible fabric glue possible (like E6000).

**Key Tip:** Always test any adhesive on a small, inconspicuous area of the jacket (like an inside seam) first to check for color transfer, stiffness, or damage to the fabric or lining before committing it to the main area. Good luck!
