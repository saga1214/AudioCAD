Based on the evidence, **easy access to highly processed, calorie-dense foods** remains the single biggest top driver behind increasing obesity rates. Here’s why it stands out:

### Key Reasons:
1. **Hyper-Palatability**: Processed foods are engineered to override natural satiety signals. High levels of sugar, fat, and salt trigger dopamine release, leading to overconsumption.
2. **Nutrient Imbalance**: They provide empty calories with minimal protein, fiber, or micronutrients—making it easier to overeat without feeling full.
3. **Portion Amplification**: Pre-packaged items often serve larger portions than home-cooked meals, increasing daily calorie intake without conscious effort.
4. **Cost and Convenience**: They’re cheaper and faster to access, especially in low-income areas, disproportionately affecting vulnerable populations.

### Why Other Factors Are Secondary:
- **Physical Inactivity**: While critical, it’s often a *consequence* of sedentary lifestyles (e.g., desk jobs, urban design) rather than the primary cause.  
- **Stress/Sleep**: These influence eating behavior but don’t directly cause overconsumption like processed foods do.  
- **Genetics**: They set individual susceptibility but don’t explain population-wide trends.  

### The Nuance:
If someone eats calorie-dense *unprocessed* foods (e.g., nuts, oils) **and** exercises significantly, obesity risk decreases. However, processed foods uniquely combine **overconsumption potential** with **nutrient deficiencies**, making them the most pervasive driver of modern obesity epidemics.  

In short: While diet, exercise, and environment all play roles, processed foods create a physiological "perfect storm" for weight gain at scale.
