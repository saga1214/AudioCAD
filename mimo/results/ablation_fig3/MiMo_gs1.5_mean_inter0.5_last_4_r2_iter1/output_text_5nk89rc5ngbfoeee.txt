For an end-of-year celebration, a hybrid approach usually works best – keeping it simple but special! Here are some ideas balancing fun, logistics, and your situation:

1.  **The "Simple Celebration" (Indoor Cafeteria Focus):**
    *   **Theme:** "Celebration of Our Troop Year!"
    *   **Activities:** Start with a quick recap video (photos/videos of badge work, cookie booths, field trips), a group sharing circle about favorite moments, and maybe a short, fun badge-related craft or game.
    *   **Food:** Keep it easy! Pre-order sundaes (vanilla/chocolate with toppings) or pizza boxes. Parents can bring simple side snacks if they wish, but you don't have to manage it. Avoid cooking anything complex.
    *   **Why it works:** Low stress for you, predictable location, minimal setup/cleanup. Focuses on the girls and celebrating *their* year.

2.  **The "Scavenger Hunt & Picnic" (Outdoor Park Focus - Optional & Weather-Dependent):**
    *   **Theme:** "Treasure Hunt at the Park!"
    *   **Activities:** Create a simple scavenger hunt based on Brownie badges (find something "Community Helpers," "Nature," "Science," etc.). Include teamwork challenges and maybe a final "treasure" (goody bags, small prizes).
    *   **Food:** Picnic! Pre-order sundaes or pizza, or have parents bring a potluck of simple finger foods (sandwiches, fruit, chips, dip). Set up a picnic blanket area.
    *   **Why it works:** Active, fun, reinforces badge learning. Picnics are classic celebration fare. Weather permitting, it's perfect. *Crucially:* Have a solid **Plan B** (like the cafeteria option above) in case of rain. Don't commit to an outdoor event you can't manage if it rains.

3.  **The "Mini Carnival Night" (Indoor or Outdoor Hybrid):**
    *   **Theme:** "Brownie Carnival Night!"
    *   **Activities:** Set up simple, badge-related game booths: Pin the Tail on the Animal (Science/Nature), Community Helpers Charades, a simple craft station (make friendship bracelets/flowers), maybe a "mini wheelbarrow race" (with safety precautions!).
    *   **Food:** Easy carnival snacks: Slime (just fruit/veg), sundaes, pizza slices, maybe a popcorn station. Parents can help set up/take down.
    *   **Why it works:** High energy, lots of fun, reinforces troop spirit. Can be done indoors or outdoors depending on space/conditions. Parents can easily participate.

**Key Considerations for *You* & Your Troop:**

*   **Pregnancy First:** Prioritize events that are easy on your body. Avoid overly physical activities or long hours standing. The cafeteria or park picnic (with plan B) are generally safer bets than a full-blown carnival if you're feeling tired.
*   **Keep it Manageable:** Don't try to do *everything*. Focus on 1-2 main activities (maybe the scavenger hunt + simple food, or the celebration circle + sundaes).
*   **Food Simplicity:** Pre-ordering sundaes or pizza is a huge time-saver. Avoid complex cooking. Offer simple, kid-pleasing options like chips, dip, fruit, sandwiches, and desserts.
*   **Parent Involvement:** This is your best friend! Send an email asking:
    *   *Parents:* "Can you bring a side dish or drinks for the picnic?" or "Can you help set up/clean up at the park/cafeteria?"
    *   *Everyone:* "Can you help spot for the scavenger hunt?" or "Can you help with the sundaes/pizza?"
*   **Focus on the Girls:** Keep the celebration centered on celebrating *their* achievements throughout the year. Short speeches, sharing, and fun activities are perfect.
*   **Safety:** Especially for outdoor activities, ensure adult supervision is adequate. Be mindful of any potential allergies (communicate food needs clearly).

**Recommendation:** Since you're pregnant, lean towards the **Cafeteria Celebration** or the **Scavenger Hunt Picnic with a strong Plan B**. Both allow for manageable food, incorporate fun elements, and focus on celebrating the girls' accomplishments. The scavenger hunt adds great activity, while the cafeteria is the safest, simplest fallback.

You've got a great plan for the year already! The end-of-year celebration is just the final, joyful punctuation mark. Choose something that feels doable and fun for *you* and the girls, and you'll have a wonderful time.
