To accommodate kosher dietary laws, you'll need to make a few adjustments to your snack and drink selections. Here's a clear breakdown:

### 🥦 **Healthy Snacks (No Changes Needed)**
- **Fruit skewers or vegetable platter with hummus**: Both are inherently kosher. Just ensure the hummus is kosher-certified (most are).

### 🍪 **Sweet Treats (Themed Option)**
- **Blood pudding**: *Not kosher* (contains non-kosher animal blood and fat).  
- **Alternative**: Opt for a fruit-based dessert (e.g., chocolate-dipped strawberries with dairy-free chocolate) or a non-animal protein option (e.g., oat-based energy bites).  
- **If using cookies**: Ensure they contain no pork, shellfish, or meat-dairy mixtures (e.g., avoid chocolate with casein milk protein).

### 🥓 **Savory Snacks**
- **Mini weenies in bacon**: *Not kosher* (meat + pork fat).  
- **Alternative**: Serve kosher-certified sausage (e.g., chicken or beef) or plant-based "sausage" options (like veggie dogs).  
- **Hummus or veggie platter**: Safe as-is.

### 🍷 **Wine**
- **Pinot Noir**: Must be **kosher-certified** (look for a "Kosher" seal on the label).  
- *Alternative*: Rosé or white wine (e.g., Sauvignon Blanc) are also easy to find kosher-certified options for.

### ✅ **Key Steps**
1. **Label snacks clearly** (e.g., "Kosher" or "Non-Kosher" on containers).  
2. **Check labels**: Verify packaged foods (hummus, cookies, wine) for kosher certification.  
3. **Avoid cross-contamination**: Use separate serving utensils for kosher/non-kosher items if needed.  
4. **Communicate**: Briefly mention the changes to Tony to show consideration.
