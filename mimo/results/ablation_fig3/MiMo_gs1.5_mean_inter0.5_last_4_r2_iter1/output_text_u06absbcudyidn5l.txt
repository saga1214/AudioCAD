### Common Causes of Chronic Skin Redness (Erythema)
1.  **Rosacea:** Chronic facial redness, often with visible blood vessels and bumps/pimples.
2.  **Sun Damage:** Long-term UV exposure causes persistent redness, sensitivity, and visible vessels.
3.  **Genetics:** Family history increases susceptibility to conditions like rosacea or flushing.
4.  **Allergies/Contact Dermatitis:** Reactions to irritants (soaps, fragrances) or allergens (pollen, foods) cause itchy, red, swollen patches.
5.  **Chronic Inflammation:** Conditions like eczema or chronic skin infections can cause persistent redness.

### Vasculitis: Symptoms, Cause, and Diagnosis
1.  **What it is:** Inflammation of blood vessels (autoimmune disorder).
2.  **Symptoms (Vary by Type & Location):**
    *   **General:** Fever, fatigue, weight loss, joint pain.
    *   **Skin:** Rashes (purple/purple-red), blisters, sores, visible blood vessels, swelling.
    *   **Hair/Nails:** Hair thinning, shedding, brittle nails, ridges.
    *   **Other Organs:** Lung inflammation (cough, shortness of breath), kidney issues, nerve pain.
3.  **Cause:** Autoimmune system mistakenly attacks blood vessel walls. Exact trigger unknown (genetics + environment).
4.  **Diagnosis:** Requires biopsy (tissue sample), blood tests, imaging (CT/MRI), and sometimes skin活检.
5.  **When Symptoms Appear:** Can start suddenly or develop gradually over weeks/months/years. Hair/nail changes and skin rashes are common early signs.

### Regarding Vasculitis & Inflammation
*   **Ibuprofen:** An anti-inflammatory pain reliever that *can* temporarily reduce localized inflammation (like a rash flare). **However:**
    *   It does not treat the underlying vasculitis.
    *   Long-term or high-dose use has risks (stomach bleeding, kidney damage).
    *   **Crucial:** **DO NOT start regular ibuprofen for chronic symptoms without consulting a doctor.** Vasculitis requires proper diagnosis and treatment (often immunosuppressants), not just painkillers.

### Next Steps
1.  **See a Dermatologist:** For evaluation of your skin redness, hair/nail changes, and potential rosacea or other skin conditions. They can recommend topical treatments.
2.  **Consult Your Primary Doctor or Rheumatologist:** To rule out underlying systemic conditions like vasculitis. Blood tests, potentially a biopsy, are essential for this.
3.  **Address Supplements:** Get blood work done (iron studies, Vitamin D) before starting new supplements. Collagen/keratin/zinc can be considered for hair/nail support under guidance.
