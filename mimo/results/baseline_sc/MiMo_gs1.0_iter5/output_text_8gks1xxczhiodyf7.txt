Based on the evidence, **the single most important factor driving the increase in obesity rates is the rise in calorie surplus**, primarily driven by two interconnected phenomena:

1.  **Increased Availability & Consumption of Highly Processed Foods:** These foods are often engineered to be hyper-palatable (hard to stop eating), calorie-dense, and low in satiating nutrients like protein and fiber. This makes it easy to consume large quantities of calories without feeling full, leading to a significant increase in daily calorie intake.
2.  **Decreased Physical Activity Levels:** Modern lifestyles involving more sedentary work, commuting, and leisure time mean people burn far fewer calories than they used to. This reduction in energy expenditure further contributes to the calorie surplus.

**Why this combination is the primary driver:**

*   **The Calorie Balance Principle:** Weight gain/loss fundamentally hinges on the balance between calorie intake and calorie expenditure. A consistent surplus, regardless of the source (processed or unprocessed foods), leads to weight gain.
*   **Synergistic Effect:** The rise in processed food availability and the decline in physical activity work together powerfully. Processed foods make overconsumption easy, while less activity means fewer calories are burned off.
*   **Scale of Impact:** The sheer volume of people consuming more calories than they burn, largely due to these modern dietary and lifestyle shifts, dwarfs the impact of individual genetic factors or hormonal variations (though these set individual baselines).
*   **Public Health Evidence:** Epidemiological studies consistently show strong links between high consumption of processed foods, sugary drinks, and fast food, and increased obesity rates, alongside correlations with sedentary behavior.

**Other factors (like stress, sleep, hormones, genetics) are significant contributors to *individual susceptibility* and *metabolic health*, but they are secondary to the fundamental driver of a sustained calorie surplus caused by modern food environments and lifestyles.** For example, stress might lead to comfort eating, but the *calories consumed* are the direct cause of weight gain.
