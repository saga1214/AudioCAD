That's an excellent question—and the answer is **yes, but with important nuances**. Let's break this down using precise terminology:

### 1. **Saccadic Masking & Perception**
   - **Saccades**: Rapid eye movements (typically 100–600 ms) between fixation points.  
   - **Saccadic Masking**: During saccades, the brain suppresses visual input to prevent motion blur. You experience a "blind spot" for ~200 ms as the eyes shift.  
   - **Key Insight**: The *eye* (retina) detects continuous light, but the *brain* edits out blur during saccades. Your conscious perception remains continuous because the brain interpolates missing frames.

### 2. **Eye vs. Brain Processing**
   - **Eye (Retina)**:  
     - Photoreceptors (rods/cones) detect light changes *instantly* (up to ~60 Hz).  
     - In bright light, flicker fusion threshold ≈ 60–75 Hz (you perceive continuous light beyond this).  
   - **Brain (Visual Cortex)**:  
     - Processes signals *after* saccades, stitching together gaps.  
     - Fills in motion "gaps" using predictive algorithms (e.g., "Where did that object go?").  
     - **Result**: You perceive continuous motion, but it’s a *constructed* experience.

### 3. **Implications for "Frames Per Second"**
   - **24 FPS Film**:  
     Matches the brain’s "preferred" frame rate for motion interpolation. Creates a dreamy, cinematic feel because it aligns with saccadic processing.  
   - **60+ FPS Video**:  
     Feels "hyper-real" because it captures motion too quickly for the brain to interpolate. Eliminates saccadic blur, making it feel like real-time observation.  
   - **1000+ FPS**:  
     Used for slow-motion (e.g., bullet impacts). Reveals details your eye *could* see but your brain *cannot* process smoothly.

### 4. **Scientific Evidence**
   - **Flicker Fusion Threshold**: Studies show humans fuse flicker at ~50–60 Hz in darkness, but this rises to 75–100 Hz in bright light.  
   - **Saccadic Masking**: Neuroimaging confirms the visual cortex deactivates during saccades ([Fuchs & Keepers, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0300-9488(90)90048-8)).  
   - **Perception vs. Reality**: Your brain constructs ~30–60 "snapshots" per second to create the illusion of fluid motion.

### Practical Takeaway
- **"Continuous" perception is a brain illusion**, not an eye function.  
- **Film at 24 fps feels natural** because it exploits saccadic masking.  
- **Higher frame rates feel "real"** because they bypass this biological editing.

This distinction is crucial for filmmakers: 24 fps creates artifice; 60+ fps creates realism. It’s not just about frames—it’s about *how the brain processes them*.
