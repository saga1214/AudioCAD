You've hit on a critical insight: while human error *can* be a vulnerability, the **first line of defense remains the physical access control system**—specifically, **guards and screening procedures at every entry point**.  

Here’s why:  
1. **Proactive Prevention**: Guards at doors, entrances, and perimeter gates act as a **human barrier** to deter, detect, or delay intrusion *before* it escalates.  
2. **Layered Screening**: Bag checks, ID verification, and visitor registers at public entrances create friction, forcing potential intruders to reveal themselves or abandon attempts.  
3. **Immediate Response**: Guards enable **real-time intervention** if someone bypasses security (e.g., confronting an intruder at the door).  

### Why Human Error Doesn’t Invalidate This Layer  
- **Redundancy**: The system is designed to *compensate* for human fallibility. For example:  
  - Guards *plus* motion sensors ensure that if a guard misses something, technology flags it.  
  - Regular training minimizes errors (e.g., the Gardner heist guards followed protocol but were deceived by a *social engineering* flaw).  
- **Focus on Entry Points**: The "first line" is about **controlling who enters**, not perfect execution. It’s the *initial checkpoint* where most threats are stopped.  

### The Gardner Heist Lesson  
The 1990 Gardner heist succeeded because the thieves exploited **social engineering**—not a physical breach. Guards opened the doors because they were convinced by faked police uniforms. This underscores that the "first line" must include:  
- **Strict protocols** (e.g., "no entry for police without ID verification").  
- **Procedural redundancy** (e.g., secondary checks after a guard’s decision).  

### Conclusion  
The **first line of defense is still human-controlled access points**, but it must be **reinforced by technology, training, and protocols** to mitigate human error. Without guards and screening, even the most advanced sensors would be useless at the threshold. As security experts emphasize: *"Control the door, control the threat."*
