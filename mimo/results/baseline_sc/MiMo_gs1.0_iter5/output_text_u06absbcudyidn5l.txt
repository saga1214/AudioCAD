Things like rosacea, which often shows up as redness on the face; sunburn; allergic reactions; or even just flushing from heat or spicy food. So, it's definitely a possibility, but not the only one.

Now, to explain vasculitis a bit more clearly. It's essentially when your immune system mistakenly attacks your blood vessels. This inflammation can affect them at different sizes—large, medium, or small—which is how they're often categorized.

The symptoms you'd look for depend on which vessels are affected. For example, large vessel issues can cause neck pain or jaw pain. Medium vessel problems are more common and can cause rashes, joint pain, or fatigue. Small vessel inflammation often leads to rashes, nerve pain, or issues with your eyes or kidneys.

The main cause is that autoimmune confusion, but it can be triggered by things like infections or even just happen without a clear trigger. To diagnose it, a doctor would look at your symptoms, do blood tests, and might need to take a small tissue sample for a biopsy to be certain.

And yes, because it affects the tiny blood vessels in your skin, hair follicles, and nail beds, it can absolutely lead to issues with hair thinning, brittle nails, and skin rashes. So it's a very plausible connection.
