You're absolutely right. Public resistance is a huge factor, and it directly affects both campaigns, but in different ways. Let's re-evaluate with that in mind.

For the sugar campaign, the public resistance is more about a general resistance to government telling people what to do, or feeling it's an infringement on personal liberty. But since it's a long-term health issue, people are generally more willing to accept it.

For the vaccination campaign, the resistance is much more intense and often politically charged. It can lead to widespread distrust in the government and even healthcare professionals. This is a bigger hurdle to overcome.

So, with that extra weight on public resistance, my thinking shifts. I would now lean more towards the sugar reduction campaign. It might be a safer bet because it doesn't have the same level of intense, polarized opposition that a vaccination campaign can face. It's a more gradual change, and people are generally more receptive to it, even if it's difficult. The vaccination campaign's effectiveness is high, but the political and social risks are much greater.
