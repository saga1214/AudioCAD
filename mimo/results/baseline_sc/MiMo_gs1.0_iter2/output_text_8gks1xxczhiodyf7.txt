Based on the conversation and scientific consensus, **the single most important factor driving rising obesity rates is the persistent **calorie surplus** (consuming more calories than the body burns).**  

### Why this is the core driver:
1. **Fundamental Principle of Weight Gain**:  
   Weight gain occurs when daily energy intake exceeds energy expenditure. Regardless of food type (processed or unprocessed), this surplus causes fat accumulation.

2. **Processed Foods Accelerate Surpluses**:  
   While processed foods aren't the *only* factor, they make surpluses easier to achieve:
   - **High Calorie Density**: A small volume of processed snacks (e.g., chips, soda) delivers excess calories quickly.
   - **Low Nutrient Density**: They lack satiating fiber and protein, leading to overeating.
   - **Additives**: Sugars, fats, and salt increase palatability, triggering overconsumption.

3. **Physical Inactivity Compounds the Issue**:  
   Sedentary lifestyles reduce calorie burn, making it easier to accumulate fat from any surplus.

### Key Nuance:
- **Processed vs. Whole Foods**:  
  Even calorie-dense whole foods (e.g., nuts, avocados) can cause weight gain if consumed in excess *without exercise*. However, processed foods make it *easier* to unintentionally overconsume calories due to design (high palatability, low satiety).

### Why Other Factors Are Secondary:
- **Genetics/Stress/Sleep**: These influence susceptibility but don’t override the physics of calories.  
- **Physical Activity**: Crucial for health and metabolism, but it can’t fully compensate for a severe calorie surplus.  
- **Diet Quality**: Important for long-term health, but the *calorie surplus* is the immediate driver of weight gain.

### Conclusion:
While processed foods, inactivity, and other factors create *conditions* for obesity, **the net calorie surplus remains the non-negotiable physiological cause**. Addressing both diet *and* activity is essential for prevention and management.
