You've hit on the most critical distinction in security philosophy. While technology and human vigilance are vital, the **absolute first line of defense for an art museum is physical access control at its primary entry points.** Here's why:

1.  **Prevention Over Response:** Its primary job is to *prevent* unauthorized access from happening in the first place. It's the gatekeeper for the entire building and its contents.
2.  **Mitigating Human Error:** As the Gardner heist showed, human error (like trusting the wrong people) is a major vulnerability. Robust access control (guards, screening, protocols) acts as a buffer. Even if guards make a mistake (e.g., letting the "officers" in), the system itself is designed to be a significant barrier.
3.  **Foundation for Other Layers:** The effectiveness of alarms, cameras, and internal sensors *depends* on preventing initial unauthorized entry. If someone gets in the front door without being challenged, those systems become less effective at stopping them.
4.  **Physical Barrier:** It leverages the most fundamental security principle: **make entry difficult.** This includes:
    *   **Visible Presence:** Guards at entrances create deterrence and can detect suspicious behavior before it escalates.
    *   **Screening:** Bag checks, metal detectors, and ID verification physically inspect and vet individuals before they enter the secured area.
    *   **Controlled Access Points:** Designing the main entrance to be visible, monitored, and physically imposing deters attempts to breach it.

**Why the other layers, while crucial, aren't the *first* line:**

*   **Alarms/Sensors:** These are vital *internal* deterrents and *detection* systems. They alert security *after* someone gets in or an action is taken (like breaking glass). They are reactive, not preventative, at the building's perimeter.
*   **Secure Displays:** These are the ultimate physical protection *for the specific objects*, but they rely on the building being secure *first*. They are a critical layer, but not the primary barrier to entry.
*   **Cameras:** Provide surveillance and evidence *after* entry, deterrence *inside* the building, and aid in investigation. They don't prevent someone from getting in the front door.

**In essence:** The physical security at the entrance (guards, screening, protocols) is the first, most critical layer because it aims to **prevent the event from happening at all.** It's the foundation upon which all other security measures (alarms, cameras, internal patrols, secure cases) are built to detect, deter, delay, and respond to incidents *if* access is breached. The Gardner heist succeeded because the *human* element at the gate was exploited; the *physical* security at the entrance failed.
