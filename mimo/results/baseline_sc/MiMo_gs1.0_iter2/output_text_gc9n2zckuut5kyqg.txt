Great point! Staying on budget is key. Here are some delicious, kid-friendly, and diabetes-aware dessert ideas that are also cost-effective:

1.  **Fruit Skewers & Salad:** The absolute easiest and cheapest.
    *   **Skewers:** Alternate melon balls, pineapple chunks, berries, grapes, and maybe some banana slices. Drizzle lightly with honey or use yogurt dip. Kids love holding and eating them.
    *   **Large Fruit Salad:** Chop up watermelon, cantaloupe, honeydew, grapes, berries, and maybe some orange or apple slices. Serve in a large bowl. Very refreshing and filling.

2.  **Baked Apples or Pears:** Simple and healthy.
    *   Core apples or pears, fill with a mix of oats, chopped nuts, cinnamon, and a *tiny* bit of brown sugar or honey. Bake until tender. Serve warm, maybe with a dollop of plain yogurt.

3.  **Homemade Cookies (Small Portions):**
    *   **Oatmeal Cookies:** Use oats, applesauce (instead of oil/butter), cinnamon, and raisins. Reduce sugar significantly. Cut into small shapes.
    *   **Sugar Cookie Bites:** Make mini sugar cookies using a recipe with less sugar. Frost with a little yogurt or sprinkle with cinnamon sugar.
    *   **Banana Bread Bites:** Make mini banana bread muffins. Reduce sugar and add nuts or chocolate chips sparingly.

4.  **Yogurt Parfaits (in cups):**
    *   Layer plain Greek yogurt (high protein, lower sugar) with homemade granola (oats, nuts, seeds, minimal honey) and fresh berries. Kids can assemble their own.

5.  **Frozen Yogurt Bark:**
    *   Spread plain Greek yogurt on a parchment-lined tray. Swirl in a *small* amount of fruit puree (like strawberry or mango) or melted dark chocolate. Top with berries, nuts, or seeds. Freeze solid, then break into pieces. Portable and healthy-ish.

6.  **Rice Cakes with Toppings:**
    *   Serve plain rice cakes. Let kids choose toppings like banana slices, avocado, a smear of peanut butter, or a sprinkle of cinnamon and honey.

7.  **"Fun" Fruit Popsicles:**
    *   Blend fruit (mango, berries, pineapple) with a little water or juice and pour into popsicle molds. Freeze. Naturally sweet and refreshing.

8.  **Dessert Donations:** This is your best budget-friendly strategy!
    *   **Partner with Local Bakeries/Cafes:** Ask if they'd donate cookies, brownies, cupcakes, or a cake in exchange for a mention in your event materials (signage, website, social media). Many are happy to do this for community events.
    *   **Ask Friends/Family:** See if relatives or neighbors have a special recipe or baked goods they can donate.

**Budget Tips for All Desserts:**

*   **Buy in Bulk:** Flour, sugar, oats, fruit, yogurt – buy larger quantities when on sale.
*   **Use Pantry Staples:** Rely on oats, nuts, seeds, dried fruit (use sparingly), yogurt, and seasonal fruit.
*   **Minimize Added Sugar:** Reduce sugar in recipes significantly where possible. Rely on the natural sweetness of fruit.
*   **Focus on Fruit:** Fruit is the star – it's cheap, nutritious, and naturally sweet.
*   **Simple Presentation:** Use clear containers, colorful bowls, or simple plates. A little creativity goes a long way.
*   **Label Thoughtfully:** Include a small tag explaining the dessert (e.g., "Oatmeal Cookies - Low Sugar," "Fruit Skewers - No Added Sugar") to subtly reinforce the awareness theme.

**Example Menu Snippet:**

*   **Main:** Grilled Burgers & Hot Dogs (use lean ground meat if budget allows), Baked Potato Bar (with toppings like broccoli, cheese, sour cream - watch portions)
*   **Sides:** Baked Beans, Coleslaw, Fresh Veggie Platter
*   **Beverages:** Water (plenty!), Lemonade (homemade concentrate), Iced Tea
*   **Desserts:** Large Fruit Salad, Baked Apple Slices, Mini Oatmeal Cookies, Rice Cakes with Nut Butter & Banana (donated by bakery?), Frozen Yogurt Bark

This approach keeps the focus on healthy, affordable options while still offering sweet treats, especially with the potential for donations. Good luck with your fundraiser!
