Here's a scenario demonstrating a key difference in how the "second-to-last defender" is interpreted between US high school rules and FIFA rules, using Red vs. Blue teams:

**Scenario:**  
*Red Team (Attacking)*: Striker (Red #9), Attacking Midfielder (Red #10), Goalkeeper (Red #1)  
*Blue Team (Defending)*: Center Back (Blue #2), Goalkeeper (Blue #1)  

**Play:**  
1.  Red #10 takes a long pass forward to Red #9, who is in an offside position.  
2.  As the ball travels, Red #9 is closer to Blue #2 (the last defender) than Red #10.  
3.  **Crucially, Red #1 is playing the ball forward.**  
4.  The ball bounces off Red #9's foot, deflecting into Red #10's path.  
5.  Red #10 quickly receives the deflection and shoots, scoring.  

**Question:** Is this an offside offense?  

**US High School Rule Interpretation (Counting Goalkeeper as Last Defender):**  
*   **Offside:** YES.  
*   **Reason:** Red #9 was in an offside position when Red #1 played the ball forward.  
*   **Second-to-Last Defender:** Under US HS rules, the goalkeeper (Red #1) is considered the **last defender**. Therefore, the player *behind* the last defender (Blue #2) is the **second-to-last defender**.  
*   **Offside Trigger:** Red #9 was in an offside position relative to the second-to-last defender (Blue #2) when Red #1 played the ball forward. Since the offense occurred from a play initiated by a player in an offside position, it's an offside offense. The fact the ball deflected off Red #9 doesn't negate the initial offside position when the play started.  

**FIFA Rule Interpretation (Goalkeeper not counted as Defender until played ball):**  
*   **Offside:** NO.  
*   **Reason:** The goalkeeper (Red #1) is **not** counted as a defender until he plays the ball. Since he was playing the ball forward (initiating the attack), he is *not* considered a defender for offside purposes.  
*   **Second-to-Last Defender:** The only defender present is Blue #2.  
*   **Offside Trigger:** Red #9 was in an offside position relative to Blue #2 (the only defender), but the ball came from Red #1 (a player in an onside position). Because the ball was played from a player *in an onside position*, the offside rule is **not** applied. The deflection off Red #9 doesn't create an offense because the initial play wasn't offside.  

**Key Difference Explained:**  
*   **US High School Rule:** Treats the goalkeeper as the **last defender** even when they are playing the ball forward. This means a player behind the goalkeeper is always the "second-to-last defender," creating a constant offside risk for attackers when the keeper plays the ball.  
*   **FIFA Rule:** The goalkeeper is **only** counted as a defender **after** they play the ball. During the attack, when the keeper plays the ball, they are *not* a defender, meaning the only defender for offside purposes is the last defender who hasn't touched the ball. An attacker behind the last *non-goalkeeper* defender is offside, but not behind the keeper who is initiating the play.  

**Why this matters for your certification:**  
This scenario highlights a fundamental difference in how the "second-to-last defender" is defined when the goalkeeper is involved in the attacking play. Recognizing whether the goalkeeper is considered a defender *before* or *after* they play the ball is critical for correctly identifying offside offenses in this specific situation. US HS rules apply the offside rule more strictly to keepers playing the ball forward, while FIFA rules create a distinct category for the goalkeeper during attacks.
