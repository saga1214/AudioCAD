Okay, let's walk through the final steps before you apply that primer. You've done all the heavy lifting, so you're almost there.

First, and this is super important, make sure everything is completely dry and clean. That degreaser you used earlier? It really needs to have worked its magic and evaporated. Any leftover grease will cause the primer to peel.

Next, give the sanded surfaces a quick vacuum. You want to get all that fine dust off the cabinets so it doesn't get stuck in the new paint.

Also, before you prime, use painter's tape to mask off any areas you don't want paint on, like the backs of the doors or the bottom edges of the cabinets. A little bit of tape residue is better than paint where it shouldn't be.

Now, for the primer itself. You'll want to use a primer specifically made for painting over dark colors. It's designed to absorb into the surface and really block that black from showing through. 

As for the application, you should use a brush or a roller. And definitely apply the primer in thin, even coats. Thick primer can bubble up or peel later. And remember, two thin coats are almost always better than one thick one.
