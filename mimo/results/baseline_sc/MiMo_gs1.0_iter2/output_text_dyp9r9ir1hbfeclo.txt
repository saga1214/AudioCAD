You're asking about the fourth component involved in shoulder rotation, which is **the rotator cuff tendons** (specifically their attachment to the humerus). While the three bones (humerus, scapula, clavicle) form the *structure*, the rotator cuff muscles and their tendons provide the **dynamic stability** for rotation. Here's how they work together:

1.  **Humerus (Upper Arm Bone):** The head rotates within the scapula's socket.
2.  **Scapula (Shoulder Blade):** Provides the socket (glenoid cavity) and articulates with the clavicle.
3.  **Clavicle (Collarbone):** Stabilizes the scapula and connects it to the sternum.
4.  **Rotator Cuff Tendons (Muscle Tendons):** The tendons of the four rotator cuff muscles (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) attach to the humerus. They:
    *   **Stabilize the humeral head** in the shallow glenoid cavity of the scapula during all phases of arm movement.
    *   **Enable smooth rotation** by dynamically adjusting tension as the arm moves.
    *   **Provide strength** to lift and rotate the arm.

**Key Point:** Rotation itself primarily occurs at the **glenohumeral joint** (where the humerus articulates with the scapula). The rotator cuff tendons are essential for *stabilizing* this joint and enabling the controlled rotation that the bones allow.

So, while the bones form the framework, the rotator cuff tendons are the crucial fourth component enabling efficient and stable rotation.
