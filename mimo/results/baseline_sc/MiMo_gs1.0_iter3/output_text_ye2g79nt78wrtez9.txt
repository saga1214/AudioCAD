You're right to ask for a re-evaluation. Let me think about it a bit more deeply. If the goal is to create an exhibit that is both educationally rich and a crowd-pleaser, then Egypt's strengths in storytelling and accessibility are very compelling. It can draw a large audience and teach them a lot about a fascinating ancient culture.

But Mesopotamia offers something different. Its educational value is arguably even more foundational. It represents the birth of writing, law, and city-states, which are the very building blocks of our modern world. It's a more unique and profound story. 

So, it really depends on the museum's primary goal. If the goal is broad public engagement, Egypt is a strong choice. But if the goal is to highlight a truly transformative moment in human history, Mesopotamia is the winner. 

Given that, I would still lean towards Mesopotamia. It offers a more unique and foundational story about the origins of civilization, which I think has a higher long-term educational value.
