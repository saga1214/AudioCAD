That's a very sophisticated argument, but it still doesn't quite work. Let's break down why.

First, a mutation in an egg doesn't mean the egg itself *contains* the new DNA. The mutation happens in the DNA of the egg *cell* that forms the embryo, and then that DNA is copied as the embryo develops. So the egg as an object existed before the DNA inside it was altered.

Second, the egg that contained the first chicken's DNA was laid by an animal that was not quite a chicken yet. It was a proto-chicken, or a very close relative. So that egg was not a chicken egg, even though it contained a chicken.

So, the first chicken emerged from the egg of a non-chicken ancestor. The chicken came first.

Let me try to explain it another way. Imagine a timeline. Let's call the creature that laid the egg 'Proto-Chicken X'. Proto-Chicken X laid an egg. Inside that egg, a mutation occurred, and a new DNA sequence appeared that defined the first true chicken. The first chicken then hatched from that egg.

So, the egg was laid by a non-chicken, the egg contained the DNA of a non-chicken, and the first chicken hatched from it. The chicken came first.
