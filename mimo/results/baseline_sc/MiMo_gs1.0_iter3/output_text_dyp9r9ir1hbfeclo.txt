You're right to ask for clarification – the fourth component that works with the humerus, scapula, and clavicle for shoulder rotation is actually the **Sternoclavicular Joint**.

Here's the breakdown:

1.  **Humerus:** The upper arm bone. Its head rotates within the shallow socket of the scapula.
2.  **Scapula:** The shoulder blade. It provides the socket (glenoid fossa) and muscles for rotation.
3.  **Clavicle:** The collarbone. It connects the arm to the axial skeleton (chest and neck).
4.  **Sternoclavicular Joint:** The **fourth key component**. This is the **only bony connection** between your arm and your axial skeleton. The inner surface of the clavicle meets the outer surface of the manubrium (top part of the sternum/breastbone).

**Why the Sternoclavicular Joint is Essential for Rotation:**

*   **Stability:** It anchors the clavicle firmly to the sternum, providing the stability needed for the arm to move freely in the socket of the scapula.
*   **Guidance:** It helps guide the movement of the clavicle and scapula together during arm rotation.
*   **Range of Motion:** While the primary rotation happens at the humeroscapular joint (humerus in scapula), the movement of the scapula itself on the chest wall relies heavily on the mobility and stability provided by the sternoclavicular joint.

**In Summary:**

The shoulder's rotation relies on the dynamic interplay between:
*   The **Humerus** rotating within the **Scapula**.
*   The **Clavicle** providing stability and connection.
*   The **Sternoclavicular Joint** anchoring the clavicle to the sternum, enabling the scapula to move and providing the overall framework for arm movement.

So, while the clavicle is the fourth bone, the **Sternoclavicular Joint** is the critical fourth *anatomical component* enabling the shoulder's complex rotational mechanics.
