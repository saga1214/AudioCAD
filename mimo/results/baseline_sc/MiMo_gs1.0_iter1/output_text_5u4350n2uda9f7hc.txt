You're absolutely right to focus on lightweight, compact plants that are easy to transport! Here's a breakdown of the best options for your space and situation:

**Perfect for Your Space & Transport:**

1.  **Bird's Nest Snake Plant (Sansevieria trifasciata 'Hahnii'):**
    *   **Size:** Stays very low and compact (usually 6-12 inches tall, with leaves 4-8 inches wide). Its "nest" shape makes it visually interesting on the floor.
    *   **Light:** Thrives in bright indirect light (like your east-facing balcony) and tolerates lower light better than most succulents. It's extremely adaptable to NYC sun and wind.
    *   **Care:** Very low water needs (water deeply only when soil is *completely* dry - maybe every 3-4 weeks, even less in cooler months). Nearly indestructible.
    *   **Transport:** Lightweight terracotta or plastic pot. Very easy to carry up stairs.
    *   **Best For:** The floor of your balcony. Looks great on its own or with a small succulent nearby.

2.  **Pothos (Epipremnum aureum):**
    *   **Size:** Can grow long, but you can easily keep it contained in a small pot. Mature size is still manageable (6-12 inches tall). Trailing or upright.
    *   **Light:** Tolerates a huge range, including bright indirect light (perfect for your balcony). Will grow slower but survive in lower light.
    *   **Care:** Water when the top inch or two of soil feels dry. Easy to propagate from cuttings.
    *   **Transport:** Lightweight pot. Very easy to carry.
    *   **Best For:** A hanging planter (if you have one) or placed in a small pot on the round table. Its trailing habit adds greenery.

3.  **Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum):**
    *   **Size:** Compact, upright plant (6-12 inches tall). Produces long "pups" (baby plants) you can propagate.
    *   **Light:** Adaptable to bright indirect light (your balcony is ideal). Avoid harsh direct sun.
    *   **Care:** Water when soil feels slightly dry. Very resilient.
    *   **Transport:** Lightweight pot. Easy to carry.
    *   **Best For:** A small pot on the table. Pups can be snipped off and shared or planted elsewhere.

4.  **Succulents (Choose Low-Growing Varieties):**
    *   **Examples:** Echeveria 'Lola' or 'Perle von Nürnberg' (small rosettes), Haworthia 'Zebra Plant' or 'Cobweb', Gasteria, small Hens & Chicks (Sempervivum).
    *   **Size:** All are inherently small and compact. Choose varieties known for staying low.
    *   **Light:** Need bright light (your east-facing balcony is excellent). Avoid intense afternoon sun if possible.
    *   **Care:** Very low water needs (water deeply then let soil dry out completely - could be every 2-3 weeks or less).
    *   **Transport:** Lightweight plastic or small terracotta pots. Easy to carry.
    *   **Best For:** A small pot on the table. Their low, rosette shape is perfect for tight spaces.

**Why These Work for Your Specifics:**

*   **Size:** All fit comfortably on your small table or floor space without needing a large pot or shelf.
*   **Transport:** Lightweight pots make carrying up five flights of stairs manageable. You'll easily lift them out when you leave.
*   **Hardiness:** They are all relatively tough, forgiving plants that won't stress easily in your NYC balcony environment (wind, sun, temporary watering needs).
*   **Care:** Low maintenance means less worry during your work week.
*   **East Light:** All thrive in the bright morning sun and filtered light typical of an east-facing balcony.

**What to Avoid:**

*   **Large Potted Plants:** Anything bigger than a small succulent pot or snake plant pot is too heavy.
*   **Fast-Growing Plants:** Plants like pothos *can* grow long, but they stay compact in a small pot. Avoid anything known for vigorous vining.
*   **Highly Water-Dependent Plants:** Avoid delicate ferns or tropicals that need constant moisture and more humidity – they'll struggle on a balcony.

**Recommendation for Your Balcony:**

1.  **Snake Plant (Bird's Nest):** Place on the floor. It's the most unique, sturdy, and low-maintenance option.
2.  **Small Succulent (e.g., Echeveria):** Place in a small pot on the round table.
3.  **Pothos or Spider Plant:** Place in another small pot on the table. Pothos adds trailing greenery; Spider Plant adds pups and a different look.

**Before You Buy:**

*   **Check the Pot:** Make sure it's a standard small pot (like a 4-inch or 6-inch nursery pot), not a giant one.
*   **Check the Weight:** Lift it before paying. It should feel light.
*   **Local NYC Options:** Home Depot, Lowe's, Walmart, Target, and especially local garden centers (like those in Queens, Brooklyn, or Manhattan) will have all these options. Trader Joe's often has good prices on snake plants, pothos, and spider plants.

You can absolutely make your balcony feel inviting with these choices! They'll be easy to move in and out of the apartment, and you can take them home with you at the end of your month. Enjoy your NYC stay!
