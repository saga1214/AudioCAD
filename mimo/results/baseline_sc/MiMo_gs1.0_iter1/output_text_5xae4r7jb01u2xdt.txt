Here's a scenario demonstrating the key difference between High School and College offside rules in the US, focusing on the **"interception"** interpretation:

**Scenario:**
*   **Teams:** Red Team (defending) vs. Blue Team (attacking)
*   **Situation:** Blue Team is attacking the Red Team's goal. Red Defender #5 is positioned behind the last defender (Red Defender #6), who is closer to the goal. Blue Team #7 is passing the ball forward to Blue Team #8 (the attacker).
*   **Action:** Red Defender #5 intercepts the pass from Blue #7 to Blue #8. Red #5 immediately controls the ball and takes a touch. Red #5 then turns and runs towards the Blue goal with the ball, eventually scoring.
*   **Question:** Is this an offside offense against Red Team #5?

**Explanation of the Difference:**

1.  **College Soccer Rulebook (NCAA):**
    *   **Rule:** An attacking player is offside if, at the moment the ball is played by a teammate, they are in an offside position.
    *   **Interception as an Offense:** If an attacking player is in an offside position and **intercepts** a pass or **plays** the ball before it has been touched by an opponent, it is an immediate offside offense.
    *   **Application:** Red Team #5 was in an offside position when they intercepted the pass from Blue #7. Since they intercepted the ball while in an offside position, **it is an immediate offside offense.** The play is stopped, and a free kick is awarded to Blue Team (or potentially a penalty kick if the ball was played directly from a restart, but here it was a pass in play). Red #5's goal is disallowed.

2.  **High School Soccer Rulebook (NFHS):**
    *   **Rule:** An attacking player is offside if, at the moment the ball is played by a teammate, they are in an offside position.
    *   **"Gain Possession" Requirement:** Crucially, for the offside offense to be called, the player in an offside position must also **"gain possession"** of the ball or **"play"** it before it has been touched by an opponent.
    *   **Interception vs. Gain Possession:** While Red #5 intercepted the pass, they only gained control of the ball *after* their first touch. The ball was not played *by* Red #5 before it was touched by an opponent (Blue #7 played it to Blue #8, Red #5 intercepted it). Red #5 did not gain possession of the ball until *after* the interception and the first touch.
    *   **Application:** Because Red #5 intercepted the ball but did not gain possession of it until *after* the interception, they **do not commit an offside offense.** The play continues. Red #5 is not in an offside position at the moment they gain control, so their subsequent run and goal are legal.

**Key Difference Summarized:**

*   **College (NCAA):** An **interception** by an attacking player in an offside position is an **immediate offside offense**, regardless of whether they control it later.
*   **High School (NFHS):** For an **interception** to be an offside offense, the attacking player must also **"gain possession"** of the ball *before* it is touched by an opponent. If they only intercept and control it *after* the initial pass, it is **not** an offside offense.

This distinction is crucial for referees working both levels. The "gain possession" requirement in the NFHS rulebook prevents referees from calling offside on every intercepted pass by a player in an offside position, as the player hasn't yet technically "played" the ball before it's touched by an opponent.
