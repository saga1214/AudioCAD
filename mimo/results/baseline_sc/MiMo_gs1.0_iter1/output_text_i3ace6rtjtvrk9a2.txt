That's an excellent and nuanced question. Let's clarify the distinction using precise terminology:

1.  **Pronunciation:** "Saccadic" is pronounced **sah-kah-dee-ik** (with emphasis on the first syllable).

2.  **The Eye's Role (Optics & Photoreception):**
    *   The eye is a biological camera. It *does* capture visual information continuously, moment by moment. Photoreceptors (rods and cones) in the retina detect incoming light and its changes (both intensity and movement) as fast as the phototransduction process allows.
    *   This process happens at a very high speed, often exceeding 100 frames per second (Hz) in bright conditions, and down to lower rates in dim light. The eye is constantly "sampling" the visual field.

3.  **The Brain's Role (Processing & Perception):**
    *   This is where the critical distinction lies. The brain does *not* perceive the visual world as a continuous, high-speed video feed.
    *   **Saccadic Masking:** As you correctly identified, during rapid eye movements (saccades), the brain actively suppresses visual processing in the visual cortex for a brief moment (roughly 100-200 milliseconds). This is an evolutionary adaptation to prevent motion sickness and prevent conflicting visual information from overwhelming the brain during movement.
    *   **Temporal Resolution & Flicker Fusion:** The brain has a limited "temporal resolution" – its ability to distinguish between discrete events or frames of motion. This is often described as the "flicker fusion threshold." For most people, this threshold is around **60 Hz** (60 frames per second) under normal viewing conditions. Anything flickering faster than this threshold appears continuous. Anything flickering slower appears as discrete flashes.
    *   **Perception of Continuity:** The brain doesn't see discrete frames. It constructs a *perception* of continuous motion by:
        *   **Blending:** Interpolating the information received during the brief moments *between* saccades.
        *   **Filling Gaps:** Using prior knowledge, expectations, and context to fill in the "missing" information during saccadic masking.
        *   **Temporal Integration:** Combining information received over a short time window (a few tens of milliseconds) into a single perceptual moment.

4.  **Answering Your Core Question: "Does the eye see continuously?"**
    *   **Yes, in terms of light capture:** The physical act of detecting photons and their changes happens continuously.
    *   **No, in terms of conscious perception:** Our *conscious experience* of seeing the world is **not** a continuous, high-speed video feed. It's a **constructed, continuous perception** generated by the brain based on:
        *   The continuous sampling by the eye.
        *   The brain's limited temporal resolution (~60 Hz).
        *   The brain's active suppression of conflicting data during saccades (saccadic masking).
        *   The brain's sophisticated ability to blend, interpolate, and fill in the gaps.

5.  **Connection to Film Frame Rates:**
    *   The magic of film (like 24 fps) relies on the brain's temporal resolution and its ability to blend discrete frames. The brain fills the gaps between the 24 frames per second, creating the perception of smooth motion.
    *   Higher frame rates (60 fps, 120 fps) provide the brain with more data points per second. This makes motion appear even smoother and more "real" because there are fewer gaps for the brain to interpolate and fill. It feels closer to the eye's *native* sampling rate during periods of relative stillness.
    *   Saccadic masking explains *why* we don't see the world in 1000 fps. The brain actively suppresses and ignores the visual input during the rapid jumps of the eyes, preventing us from perceiving the world as a series of very fast, discrete snapshots. We perceive the *result* of the eye's continuous sampling and the brain's processing.

*   The **eye** *physically* captures visual information (photons and their changes) **continuously**.
*   The **brain** *processes* this information and *creates the perception* of a continuous world. This perception is **not** a direct, unfiltered video feed.
*   This perception is constrained by the brain's **temporal resolution** (~60 Hz) and its active suppression of visual input during **saccadic movements** (saccadic masking).
*   Therefore, while the eye sees continuously in terms of light capture, our **conscious perception of seeing** is a **constructed, continuous experience** generated by the brain based on the eye's data and the brain's own processing limitations and adaptations.
