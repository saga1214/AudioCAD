First, a regular computer bit is like a light switch. It's either on, which is a one, or off, which is a zero. It can only be one thing at a time.

A quantum bit, or qubit, is like an actor who can be the hero, the villain, or a background character all at once. This is called superposition. The actor holds all these potential roles simultaneously.

When the director says "Action!", the actor instantly commits to one specific role. That's like measuring a qubit, which collapses its state to a single answer.

Entanglement is like having two actors whose performances are linked. If one is cast as the hero, the other instantly becomes the villain, no matter where they are on set. Their fates are connected.

The budget is the time limit for the actor to hold all their potential roles. Outside noise, like a phone ringing, is decoherence. It distracts the actor, and they lose their ability to be in multiple states at once. You have to finish the scene before that happens.

A quubit is just another name for a quantum bit, or an actor who can hold all their potential roles.
