You're right to double-check! Let's clarify the components involved in shoulder rotation:

1.  **Humerus (Upper Arm Bone):** This is the primary bone that *rotates* within the shoulder joint. It moves backward (external rotation) and forward (internal rotation) relative to the socket in the scapula.
2.  **Scapula (Shoulder Blade):** This forms the socket (glenoid cavity) where the humerus fits. It's essential for providing the *platform* and *stability* for rotation. Its movement helps position the humerus correctly for rotation.
3.  **Clavicle (Collarbone):** This bone connects the arm (humerus) to the main part of the skeleton (the sternum/ribcage). It plays a crucial role in *stabilizing* the shoulder joint and providing the connection point for the muscles that drive rotation.

**So, the key bones are Humerus, Scapula, and Clavicle – that's three.**

*   **The Humerus is the bone that actually performs the rotation.**
*   **The Scapula provides the socket and guides the movement.**
*   **The Clavicle connects the arm to the body and stabilizes the joint.**

There isn't a fourth bone involved in the fundamental mechanics of shoulder rotation itself. The complex movement relies on the coordinated action of these three bones. The rotator cuff muscles are essential for stabilizing the humeral head within this socket during rotation, but they are muscles/tendons, not bones.
