Based on the evidence, **the single most important factor driving rising obesity rates is the combination of increased calorie intake and decreased energy expenditure over time.** This means consuming more calories than the body burns.

Here's why:

1.  **Fundamental Principle:** Weight gain occurs when calorie intake exceeds calorie expenditure. This is the core biological mechanism of obesity.
2.  **Contribution of Other Factors:**
    *   **Processed Foods:** Are a *major contributor* to the increased calorie intake. They are often high in calories, low in satiating nutrients (protein, fiber), and engineered to be hyper-palatable, making it easy to overconsume them.
    *   **Physical Inactivity:** Contributes to decreased energy expenditure. Modern lifestyles involve significantly less movement than historical norms.
    *   **Other Factors (Stress, Sleep, Genetics):** These influence appetite hormones, metabolism, and energy expenditure, but ultimately impact whether someone takes in more calories than they burn.
3.  **Why it's the *Single* Most Important Factor:**
    *   **Direct Causality:** Without a calorie surplus, weight gain cannot occur, regardless of the source of the calories (processed or unprocessed) or the level of activity.
    *   **Ubiquity:** The calorie surplus exists for *everyone* who gains weight, even if the causes (like processed food consumption or inactivity) differ between individuals.
    *   **Interplay:** While processed foods and inactivity are critical *drivers* creating the environment where surpluses become common, the *fundamental cause* remains the imbalance between intake and expenditure.

**In essence:** Processed foods and inactivity are powerful *enablers* and *contributors* to the calorie surplus, but the **calorie surplus itself is the direct, unavoidable consequence and the primary driver of weight gain and obesity.** Addressing both intake and expenditure is crucial for prevention and management.
