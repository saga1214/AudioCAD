Here's why:

1.  **Irreplaceable Biodiversity:** The core definition of a hotspot is its high concentration of **endemic species** – species found nowhere else on Earth. Each one of these unique species represents a unique genetic library and evolutionary history. If a hotspot is destroyed, these species go extinct forever. No amount of conservation elsewhere can replace the loss of a species that evolved in that specific place and cannot be replicated.

2.  **Foundation for All Other Benefits:** While the ecosystem services (like water purification, pollination, climate regulation) and potential medicines derived from hotspot flora and fauna are critically important *reasons* to protect them, they ultimately stem from the **biodiversity itself**. The unique species *are* the source of these services and potential discoveries. Protecting the hotspot preserves the source of these benefits.

3.  **Permanent Loss:** Unlike a common species that might be found elsewhere and could potentially be reintroduced or bred, the extinction of an endemic species from a hotspot means that genetic information, ecological function, and evolutionary potential are lost **permanently**. This loss is irreversible on any human timescale.

4.  **Ethical Imperative:** Recognizing the intrinsic value of life forms and ecosystems that exist solely in specific places carries a strong ethical weight. We have a responsibility to prevent the extinction of species that have existed for millions of years simply because we are destroying their habitat.

**In essence:** Protecting biodiversity hotspots is fundamentally about preventing the **permanent, irreversible loss of unique life forms** that are irreplaceable. While the benefits to humans (ecosystem services, medicine) are vital practical reasons to prioritize them, the core reason they are designated as "hotspots" is their exceptional, localized biodiversity that cannot be duplicated elsewhere. Protecting them safeguards this irreplaceable heritage and the potential future benefits it holds.
