Okay, it sounds like you're making some good progress on the cabinets and also dealing with a tricky situation with your neighbor. Let's focus on the painting for now. Before you apply that first coat of primer, you'll need to do a few more things.

First, make sure the sanded surfaces are completely clean and free of any dust. You can wipe them down with a tack cloth or even just a slightly damp rag that you then dry thoroughly. This is really important so the primer will stick properly.

Next, you'll want to apply painter's tape to any areas you don't want to get primer on, like the edges of the upper cabinets where they meet the wall, or any cutouts for lights or outlets. 

Then, and this is a good tip, you can use a brush to apply a very thin 'skim coat' of the primer onto a small area first, like the back of the cabinet. This helps you check if the coverage is good and gives you a chance to adjust if needed before you commit to painting the whole thing.

Once you're happy with that test spot, you can go ahead and apply the primer to all the cabinets. Just be sure to use a high-quality primer that's designed for the type of paint you're using. And remember, it's usually best to apply it in a well-ventilated area.
