That's a very valid question, and it's understandable why it can be confusing. Let's break it down simply:

1.  **What is Vasculitis?**
    *   Think of your blood vessels as pipes carrying blood throughout your body. Vasculitis is inflammation of these pipes.
    *   **The Cause:** Usually, it's your immune system getting confused and mistakenly attacking the walls of the blood vessels, causing inflammation. This can be triggered by infections (like the flu or a cold), certain medications, or sometimes, it happens for no obvious reason. It's an autoimmune disorder.

2.  **What Does It Look Like?**
    *   Because these "pipes" are everywhere, symptoms depend on *where* the inflammation is and *how bad* it is.
    *   **Common Symptoms:**
        *   **Skin Rashes:** Red, purple, or white spots; bumps; sores (can look like hives, eczema, or psoriasis, but often worse).
        *   **Fever and Chills:** Often a sign the body is fighting something.
        *   **Extreme Fatigue:** Feeling constantly tired and weak.
        *   **Joint and Muscle Pain:** Aches and pains in limbs.
        *   **Nerve Pain:** Tingling, numbness, or pain in hands/feet.
        *   **Nosebleeds or Easy Bruising:** Blood vessels are leakier/inflamed.
        *   **Headaches:** Especially if large vessels are affected.
        *   **Vision Problems:** Blurred vision, double vision, or pain (if eye vessels are involved).
        *   **Swollen Glands:** Lymph nodes near the skin can swell.

3.  **Types & Vessel Size:**
    *   Doctors categorize vasculitis based on which size of vessel is primarily affected:
        *   **Large Vessels:** Affect arteries supplying the head/neck. Can cause jaw pain when chewing, vision issues, high blood pressure.
        *   **Medium Vessels:** Affect arteries supplying organs like kidneys, heart, lungs. Can cause high blood pressure, lung problems, nerve issues.
        *   **Small Vessels:** Affect capillaries and tiny veins (most common type). Cause skin rashes, joint pain, fatigue, nerve pain.

4.  **Other Causes of General Redness (Erythema):**
    *   **Rosacea:** Very common cause of facial redness ( cheeks, nose), often with bumps/pustules. Usually harmless.
    *   **Sun Exposure:** Sunburn causes redness.
    *   **Temperature:** Flushing from heat (hot tub, sauna), spicy food, alcohol, exercise.
    *   **Allergies:** Hay fever, contact dermatitis (allergic reaction to something touching skin).
    *   **Infections:** Viral (like chickenpox), bacterial (like staph infection).
    *   **Heat Rash:** Tiny red bumps caused by sweat.
    *   **Skin Conditions:** Eczema, psoriasis flare-ups.
    *   **Other Medical Conditions:** Some medications, thyroid issues, or even early signs of other systemic illnesses.

**Key Takeaway:** While vasculitis *can* cause skin redness and other symptoms you describe, it's not the only possibility. **The most crucial step is to see a doctor.** They can:
    *   Rule out common, less serious causes like rosacea or sunburn.
    *   Assess your specific symptoms, history, and physical exam.
    *   Order tests (blood work, urine tests, imaging like CT/MRI, sometimes a biopsy of the skin) to determine if vasculitis is present and which type.
    *   **Only then** can they diagnose the underlying cause and create a safe and effective treatment plan.

**Regarding Supplements & Inflammation:**

*   **Hair/Nail Support:** Collagen peptides, biotin, keratin, iron, zinc, and B vitamins *can* support hair and nail health, but they won't fix the root cause of inflammation or vasculitis.
*   **Inflammation:** **Do not start regular ibuprofen or any other anti-inflammatory medication without consulting your doctor.** While ibuprofen can help with inflammation *symptoms* like pain and fever, it does not treat the underlying cause of vasculitis. Long-term or high-dose use can have serious side effects. Your doctor can prescribe appropriate anti-inflammatory medications (like corticosteroids or biologics) that are specifically designed to target the immune system causing the vasculitis. They can also recommend other supportive measures like diet, stress management, or other supplements *if appropriate*.

**In summary:** Your symptoms warrant a medical evaluation to find the true cause. Vasculitis is a possibility, but many other common conditions cause redness. Only a doctor can provide an accurate diagnosis and the right treatment plan. Please schedule an appointment.
