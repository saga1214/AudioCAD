Okay, let me gather all the wines we've discussed and compare their ideal temperatures. First, we have the high-end Pinot Noir. It should be stored at 55 degrees Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius, and served slightly cooler, between 55 to 60 Fahrenheit, or 13 to 16 Celsius. It's a great choice for cooler weather or when you want a balanced red.

Next is the Japanese Hon-Mirin. For storage, it's best around 60 to 68 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you're sipping it, serve it near room temperature, around 68 to 75 Fahrenheit, or 20 to 24 Celsius. It's a unique option for a Japanese-inspired meal.

Then there's Italian Chianti. This one is stored similarly to Pinot Noir at 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius, and served a bit warmer, at 60 to 65 Fahrenheit, or 16 to 18 Celsius. It's a robust red that's great for a variety of meals.

For a crisp white, we have Pinot Grigio. It should be stored cool, around 50 to 55 Fahrenheit, or 10 to 13 Celsius, and served chilled at 45 to 50 Fahrenheit, or 7 to 10 Celsius. This is definitely the best choice for hot weather because it's light and refreshing.

And finally, the mulled wine, which is served hot. It's typically warmed to 140 to 160 Fahrenheit, or 60 to 70 Celsius. However, the wine itself is stored at cellar temperature, about 55 Fahrenheit, or 13 Celsius. This is the perfect choice for cold weather, providing warmth and comfort.

So, to sum it up: for hot weather, the Pinot Grigio is the top choice. For cold weather, the Pinot Noir or the mulled wine would be excellent. The Chianti is a good all-around red, and the Hon-Mirin is a unique option for a special occasion or a specific cuisine.
