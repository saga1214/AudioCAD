Here's a scenario demonstrating a key difference between US Soccer and FIFA/IFAB offside rules, using your teams:

**Scenario:**
The Red Team (Attacking) is on a counter-attack. Red Team Player 10 (the throw-in maker) is standing at the sideline boundary, preparing to make a throw-in. The ball is in Red Team possession. The Red Team forward, Player 9, is positioned directly behind the last defender of the Blue Team. Player 9 is also behind the ball that Player 10 is holding and is closer to the goal line than both the ball and the last defender. Player 10 throws the ball forward directly to Player 9, who controls it and scores a goal.

**The Rule Difference:**

1.  **US Soccer Rulebook:**
    *   **Rule:** Player 9 is **NOT** in an offside position when the throw-in is made. (Rule 12:10.c - "A player who is in an offside position at the moment the throw-in is made **shall not** be considered to be in an offside position when the throw-in is played." - *Emphasis added*).
    *   **Reason:** The throw-in is a specific type of restart. The rule explicitly states that a player in an offside position at the *moment* of the throw-in is **not** considered offside for that play. Player 9 meets the position criteria (behind the ball and last defender, closer to goal), but the throw-in rule overrides the standard offside position assessment.
    *   **Outcome:** The goal stands. **NO Offside Offense.**

2.  **FIFA/IFAB Rulebook:**
    *   **Rule:** Player 9 **IS** in an offside position when Player 10 plays the ball forward from the throw-in. (Rule 11:3.c - "A player in an offside position at the moment the ball is played by a throw-in, a corner kick, or a goal kick **shall not** be considered to be in an offside position when the ball is played by that kick." - *Emphasis added*).
    *   **Reason:** The FIFA rule states that a player *in an offside position* at the moment of a throw-in is **not** considered offside *if the ball is played by that kick*. However, the scenario describes Player 9 being in an offside position *at the moment the throw-in is made*. The ball is then played forward *from* that throw-in. FIFA's rule focuses on the *action* of the throw-in itself. Crucially, **FIFA does *not* have an explicit rule stating that a player *in an offside position* at the moment of a throw-in is automatically *not* offside.** Instead, the standard offside position rules apply *to the play initiated by the throw-in*. Since Player 9 was in an offside position when the throw-in occurred, and the throw-in itself constitutes a play (forwarding the ball), they are offside.
    *   **Outcome:** The goal is disallowed for offside. **OFFSIDE OFFENSE.**

**Key Difference Explained:**

*   **US Soccer:** Has a **specific, explicit exception** for players in an offside position *at the exact moment* a throw-in is made. They are automatically deemed *not* to be in an offside position *for that play*.
*   **FIFA/IFAB:** Does **not** have such an explicit exception. Instead, it relies on the standard offside position rules *applied to the play initiated by the throw-in*. Since the throw-in is a play (forwarding the ball), a player who is in an offside position *at the moment the ball is played* (which includes the moment the throw-in is made) is considered offside.

**In Summary:**

*   **US Soccer:** Throw-in made -> Player 9 is in offside position -> **NOT offside** for the throw-in play -> Goal stands.
*   **FIFA/IFAB:** Throw-in made -> Player 9 is in offside position -> **IS offside** because standard rules apply to the play (throw-in) -> Goal disallowed.

This distinction is crucial for referees officiating games governed by different rulebooks, especially when throw-ins are involved in counter-attacks.
