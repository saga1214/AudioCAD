Now, to answer your question: No, it wouldn’t be correct to say the eye sees continuously. It’s more accurate to say the brain perceives continuous motion, even though the eye itself is doing something very different. The eye is actually doing two things at once: it’s moving rapidly, like saccades, to scan for information, and it’s also detecting motion. The brain then takes all of this data and weaves it together to create the perception of continuous, stable vision.

So, to be precise: the eye is a sensor, but the brain is the interpreter. The brain’s role is what makes motion feel continuous, even though the eye is doing its own thing. It’s a bit like a video game where the frame rate is 60 frames per second, but your brain fills in the gaps to make it look smooth. The eye and brain work together to create the illusion of continuity.
