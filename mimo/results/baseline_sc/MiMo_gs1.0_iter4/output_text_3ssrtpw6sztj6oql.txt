You've hit on the critical tension in museum security: **human error is an inherent vulnerability, making physical barriers and technology essential *because* people can fail.**  

That said, the **first and most fundamental line of defense remains physical access control at every entry point.** Here’s why:  

1. **Prevention Over Reaction:**  
   Access control (guards, bag checks, ID verification) is designed to *prevent* unauthorized entry before a threat can even reach the art. It’s the first hurdle an intruder must overcome.  

2. **Human Reliability (Within Systems):**  
   While guards *can* make mistakes (as in the Gardner heist), robust protocols mitigate this:  
   - **Trained personnel:** Guards are vetted and drilled on security protocols.  
   - **Automation:** Electronic access control systems (keycards, biometrics) reduce human error in granting entry.  
   - **Layered Verification:** No single guard decision; multiple checkpoints (e.g., staff badge + guard inspection).  

3. **Limiting the "Attack Surface":**  
   Controlling entry points (doors, windows, service entrances) physically restricts where threats can enter. Even if a guard is deceived, the thief still needs to bypass the *physical barrier* at the entrance.  

4. **Enabling Secondary Systems:**  
   Without access control, sensors (alarms, cameras) and secure displays become reactive. For example:  
   - If a thief bypasses the front door, they can disable cameras or alarms before they trigger.  
   - Secure cases are only effective if the building isn’t already breached.  

### Why the Gardner Heist Highlights This:  
The thieves exploited human trust (posing as police), but their success depended on *gaining entry first*. They still needed to overpower guards *inside* the secured building. Access control failed at the entrance, but the art survived because *internal* defenses (secure cases, alarms) still functioned.  

### Conclusion:  
**Physical access control is the foundational first line of defense.** It’s the only layer that *must* physically stop unauthorized people before they can interact with security systems or the art. While human error is a risk, it’s managed through rigorous protocols, technology, and redundancy. Without it, even the most advanced sensors and displays become irrelevant.
