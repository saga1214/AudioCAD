### U-Turn Rules in BC & Penalties
1.  **General Rule:** U-turns are **not allowed** at intersections with traffic signals (stop signs, traffic lights, or yield signs) unless specifically permitted by a sign (e.g., "U-Turn Permitted").
2.  **Visibility Requirement:** If there is **no sign prohibiting a U-turn**, you can generally make one *if* you can see clearly in both directions for at least **150 meters** (about 500 feet). This is the key test.
3.  **Prohibited Locations:** U-turns are prohibited on:
    *   Curves in the road.
    *   Where traffic control devices are present (signals, stop signs, yield signs).
    *   On the approach to or within 50 meters of a pedestrian crossing or intersection.
    *   Near driveways, bus stops, or where traffic might be blocked.
    *   Where it would interfere with traffic flow.
    *   On bridges or overpasses unless specifically signed.
    *   On one-way streets (unless the signs permit).
4.  **Penalties for Illegal U-Turns:**
    *   **First Offense:** A fine of $167 plus 2 demerit points on your license.
    *   **Second Offense:** A fine of $250 plus 3 demerit points.
    *   **Third Offense:** A fine of $364 plus 4 demerit points.
    *   **Subsequent Offenses:** Fines increase progressively (e.g., $482 + 5 points, $645 + 6 points). Repeat offenses can lead to license suspension.
    *   **High-Risk Situations:** If the illegal U-turn poses a significant hazard (e.g., blocking traffic, causing danger), penalties can be much higher, potentially up to $500 and 5 demerit points.

**Key Takeaway:** Always check for signs! If there's no sign *allowing* a U-turn, assume it's prohibited unless you meet the 150-meter visibility requirement and it's safe. Use designated U-turn lanes or wait for a clear, safe opportunity.

### Spaghetti Carbonara Recipe (Traditional Egg-Based)

**Ingredients:**

*   **Pasta:** 400g (approx. 14 oz) Spaghetti (or Bucatini)
*   **Guanciale:** 150g (approx. 5 oz) (Diced, see note below) *Substitute Pancetta if Guanciale is unavailable.*
*   **Eggs:** 3 large eggs + 1 large egg yolk (Room temperature)
*   **Parmigiano-Reggiano Cheese:** 100g (approx. 1 cup) (Finely grated)
*   **Black Pepper:** Freshly ground, to taste (Generous amount!)
*   **Salt:** For pasta water
*   **(Optional)** Red pepper flakes (a pinch)

**Instructions:**

1.  **Cook Pasta:** Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a rolling boil. Cook the spaghetti *al dente* (check package time, usually 1-2 minutes less than stated). Reserve at least 1 cup of the starchy pasta water before draining. Drain the pasta.
2.  **Render Guanciale:** While pasta cooks, place diced guanciale in a large skillet over medium-low heat. Cook slowly, stirring occasionally, until the fat renders out and the meat is crispy (about 8-12 minutes). Do not brown the meat. Remove from heat and transfer to a separate bowl.
3.  **Temper Eggs:** In a large bowl, vigorously whisk together the 3 whole eggs and the 1 egg yolk until smooth. Gradually whisk in the finely grated Parmigiano Reggiano and a generous amount of freshly ground black pepper. This mixture should be thick and slightly runny.
4.  **Combine:** Add the hot, drained pasta to the skillet with the guanciale fat. Toss well to coat. **Immediately** remove the skillet from the heat. (This prevents scrambling the eggs).
5.  **Emulsify:** Working quickly, add about 1/2 cup of the reserved starchy pasta water to the egg mixture in the large bowl. Whisk constantly. Then, quickly pour the egg/cheese mixture over the pasta in the skillet, tossing and stirring vigorously with tongs. Add more reserved pasta water, a tablespoon or two at a time, as needed. The goal is a silky, creamy sauce that clings to the pasta without being soupy. The residual heat from the pasta will cook the eggs perfectly.
6.  **Serve Immediately:** Plate the carbonara right away. Top with an extra sprinkle of Parmigiano Reggiano and a generous grind of black pepper. Add a pinch of red pepper flakes if desired.

**Important Notes:**

*   **Guanciale:** This is traditional cured pork cheek. It has a rich, slightly smoky flavor. Pancetta (cured pork belly) is a very good substitute if you can't find guanciale. Avoid smoked bacon or thick-cut bacon.
*   **No Cream:** Authentic Roman Carbonara gets its creaminess from the emulsified egg yolks, cheese, starchy pasta water, and rendered fat from the guanciale. Never add cream.
*   **Speed is Key:** The process of combining the hot pasta with the egg mixture off direct heat and tossing vigorously happens quickly. Have everything prepped (eggs whisked, cheese grated, pasta drained) before you start step 3.
*   **Safety:** Ensure eggs are at room temperature before whisking. The residual heat cooks them gently.

Enjoy your delicious Spaghetti Carbonara!
