You're right, older comics are generally rarer and can be more valuable, but they can also be harder to find in good condition. Here are ten notable titles from the 1940s and 50s that are considered key issues and can be found under that price point.

First, there's *Captain America* number one from 1941, which is the first appearance of Captain America and Bucky Barnes. You can often find a graded copy in the mid-range, like a 5.0 or 6.0, for under five hundred dollars. It's a foundational comic, and demand is high.

Another one is *Tales of Suspense* number thirty-nine from 1964. This is Captain America's first appearance in a team-up comic, and it's also the first appearance of the Red Skull as an enemy.

From the 40s, *Marvel Super-Heroes* number one from 1944 is a great choice. It's the first appearance of the Human Torch and the Sub-Mariner, and it's one of the earliest Marvel titles from that era.

For something from the 50s, *Fantastic Four* number one from 1961 is a must. It's the first appearance of the Fantastic Four and Galactus, and a mid-grade copy is well within your budget.

Also, *The Amazing Adventures of Spider-Man* number one from 1963 is the first appearance of Spider-Man in his solo series.

From the 40s again, *The Avengers* number one from 1963 is the first appearance of the team. A mid-grade copy is a solid investment.

*Thor* number one from 1962 is the first appearance of Thor in Marvel's main line of comics.

From the 50s, *Secret Wars* number one from 1984 is a classic event, but a mid-grade copy is still under your price point.

*The Incredible Hulk* number one from 1962 is the first appearance of the Hulk.

And finally, *Iron Man* number one from 1963 is the first appearance of Iron Man.

Just remember, the price for any of these will depend heavily on the condition, which is why getting them professionally graded by a company like CGC is a good idea for investment purposes.
