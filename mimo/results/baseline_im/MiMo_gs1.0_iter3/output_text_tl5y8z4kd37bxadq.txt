### 🐝 Beekeeping Concerns (Sluggish Bees)
1. **Possible Causes:**  
   - **Weather:** Cooler temps or sudden drops can reduce activity.  
   - **Queen Inactivity:** If she’s not laying eggs, workers become idle.  
   - **Diet Issues:** Sugar water alone isn’t enough—ensure they have protein sources (pollen frames).  
   - **Pests/Disease:** Varroa mites or fungal infections (e.g., *Nosema*) can cause lethargy.  

2. **Action Steps Before Your Trip:**  
   - **Inspect the Hive:** Check for:  
     - Queen activity (look for eggs in cells).  
     - Brood pattern (evenly distributed = healthy queen).  
     - Pests (scrape frame—look for mite droppings or waxy debris).  
   - **Treat for Mites:** If mites are present, apply oxalic acid or formic acid (follow label instructions).  
   - **Boost Diet:** Offer a 1:1 sugar-water mix with pollen substitutes if brood is low.  

3. **Post-Trip Care:**  
   - Monitor honey supers—if they’re filling up, harvest to prevent swarming.  
   - If sluggishness persists, consult a local beekeeper for diagnostics.  

### ✈️ Packing Honey Jars Safely
- **Cushioning:** Wrap jars in bubble wrap or packing paper.  
- **Placement:** Nestle them between clothes, towels, or foam padding—**never near sharp edges**.  
- **Seal Tightly:** Ensure lids are secure (use jar straps if needed).  
- **Spot Check:** Before closing your suitcase, verify no jars are loose.
