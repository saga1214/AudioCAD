Here's a breakdown of treats and clicker training for your puppy:

### Treat Recommendations
1. **Best Flavors & Types**  
   - **Peanut Butter:** Use unsweetened, xylitol-free peanut butter (or make your own).  
   - **Plain Yogurt:** Small amounts of plain, non-fat yogurt (dairy-free if needed).  
   - **Commercial Training Treats:** Tiny, low-calorie options like **Ziwi Peak**, **Blue Buffalo**, or **Pam! Training Treats**.  
   - **Cheese Bits:** Hard cheese (like cheddar) cut into tiny cubes.  
   - **Avoid:** Chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, or table scraps (high fat/salt).

2. **Size Matters**  
   For a tiny puppy (3 lbs), use **pea-sized treats**. Gradually increase size as he grows.

### Clicker Training Explained  
Clicker training uses a **"marking" device** (the clicker) to bridge the gap between behavior and reward. Here’s how it works:  

#### Step-by-Step Process  
1. **Pair Clicker + Treat**  
   - Click the clicker once → Immediately give a treat. Repeat 10–15 times.  
   - Puppy learns: *Click = Treat is coming!*  

2. **Introduce the Behavior**  
   - Ask for a simple behavior (e.g., "sit").  
   - Wait for the puppy to *begin* sitting (even if just lifting his rear slightly).  
   - The *instant* you see the behavior, click → give a treat.  

3. **Build the Connection**  
   - Repeat daily. Gradually wait slightly longer for the behavior before clicking.  
   - Phase out the clicker after the behavior is solid (use only 20–30% of sessions).  

#### Why It Works  
- The clicker **marks the exact moment** the puppy does what you want.  
- Rewards are **immediately tied** to the click, reinforcing the behavior faster than verbal cues alone.  

#### Example: Teaching "Sit"  
1. Hold a treat near his nose, then slowly move it up over his head.  
2. As his bottom touches the ground → click → treat.  
3. Repeat until he anticipates the click when you raise the treat.  

#### Tips  
- **Consistency:** Use the same clicker sound/treat size every time.  
- **Patience:** Start with 5-minute sessions.  
- **Clicker Alternatives:** A **"target stick"** (for nose-tapping) or even a **hand clap** can replace the clicker.  

Clicker training builds trust and makes learning intuitive. For basic obedience (sit, stay, come), it’s one of the most effective methods! 🐾
