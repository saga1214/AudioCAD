Okay, let’s break this down. 

First, the left-brain versus right-brain idea is mostly a myth. The brain doesn’t neatly separate creative and logical functions that way. Both hemispheres work together, and creativity and logic are deeply intertwined in the same networks. For example, a lot of creative problem-solving actually happens in the prefrontal cortex, which is involved in both logic and innovation. But that doesn’t mean the left brain is only for logic and the right for creativity—it’s more about how different networks interact. 

Now, for careers that blend creativity, objectivity, and structure. You might like something in architecture, where you design in a way that’s both artistic and functional. Or maybe something like scientific illustration, where you translate complex data into visual stories. Another idea is data visualization or information design, where you turn abstract numbers into creative, meaningful visuals. Or even technical writing, where you take complex ideas and make them clear and engaging. These fields let you work with structure and measurement while also being creative. 

As for the tinnitus, it’s usually a physical thing—like noise exposure or hearing damage—but stress can make it worse. So, it’s not purely psychological, but the brain’s response to it can be.
