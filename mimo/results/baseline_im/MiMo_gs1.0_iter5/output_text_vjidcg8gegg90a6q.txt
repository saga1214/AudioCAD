For your winter chicks, **pine shavings** (or kiln-dried pine pellets) are an excellent, rodent-resistant bedding choice. Here’s why and how to use them:

### Recommended Bedding: Pine Shavings
1. **Safety & Insulation**:  
   - Pine shavings provide good insulation against cold air.  
   - They’re non-toxic and break down cleanly (unlike straw).  
   - Avoid cedar shavings—they contain aromatic hydrocarbons harmful to chickens.  

2. **Deep Litter Method**:  
   - Use a **thick layer (4–6 inches)** in the chicks’ brooder area.  
   - Add fresh shavings weekly and lightly fluff the existing layer to encourage decomposition.  
   - As it composts, it naturally generates **warmth** and reduces odor.  

3. **Moisture Control**:  
   - **Never** add wet shavings—they can harbor bacteria or mold.  
   - Spot-clean soiled areas daily.  
   - Ensure proper ventilation to prevent dampness.  

### Alternatives:
- **Paper Bedding**: Safe and absorbent, but less insulating. Ideal for short-term use.  
- **Wood Chips/Straw**: Use *only* if you can keep them dry and clean thoroughly (rodents *will* nest).  

### Critical Setup Tip:
- **Adjust your brooder plate’s legs** to allow a **2–3 inch gap** between the heat source and bedding. This prevents chicks from pecking at the hot plate and ensures heat rises evenly.  

### Ventilation Reminder:
Even with insulation, ensure airflow at the top of the coop to prevent condensation and frostbite.  

Pine shavings are a reliable, low-maintenance choice that aligns with your rodent-free goal. Pair this with your rubber mats and sand for a cozy, clean winter setup! 🐔❄️
