Based on your criteria (easy to train, good with kids/dogs, non-chasing, chill temperament, purebred or known hybrid), here's a list of dog breeds that often fit well:

**Top Recommendations:**

1.  **Golden Retriever:** The quintessential family dog. Extremely friendly, patient, eager to please, highly trainable, gentle with kids, and generally not prey-driven. They love people and other dogs.
2.  **Labrador Retriever:** Another top choice for families. Intelligent, outgoing, sociable, great with children and other pets, and famously easygoing. They enjoy play and training.
3.  **Poodle (Standard, Miniature, Toy):** Highly intelligent, trainable, and eager to please. Excellent with kids and other dogs. Poodles are generally not strong prey drivers. Their hypoallergenic coat is a bonus. Look for reputable breeders or rescue groups specializing in Poodles.
4.  **Cavalier King Charles Spaniel:** Small, affectionate, gentle, and loving companions. Very good with children and other dogs. They tend to be laid-back and enjoy being near their family. (Note: Prone to some health issues, so research breeders/rescues carefully).
5.  **Basset Hound:** Known for their incredibly laid-back, slow-moving, and friendly nature. Generally good with kids and other dogs. They have a strong sense of smell but are not typically high-energy or chase-driven. Can be stubborn.

**Other Good Options:**

6.  **Shetland Sheepdog (Sheltie):** Intelligent, alert, and generally good-natured. They are adaptable to apartment living (with exercise), trainable, and typically good with families and other dogs. They can be slightly more vocal than some breeds.
7.  **English Cocker Spaniel:** Similar to the Cavalier, they are intelligent, trainable, and affectionate. Generally good with children and other dogs. They are typically not strong prey drivers. (Note: Prone to some health issues).
8.  **French Bulldog:** Small, adaptable to apartments, low-energy, friendly, and generally good with kids and other dogs. They are not strong prey drivers. They can be sensitive to heat and prone to some breathing/health issues. Look for reputable breeders focusing on health.
9.  **Miniature Pinscher:** Small but surprisingly sturdy. Often described as "big dog in a small body." Can be very loyal and protective of families, good with children. Generally good with other dogs if socialized. They are alert but not typically high-energy or chase-driven. (Note: Can be sensitive to heat).
10. **Australian Cattle Dog (Blue Heeler):** While they *can* be very easygoing and affectionate with their family (the "chill" part), they are *highly* intelligent and *need* significant mental stimulation and exercise. If your lifestyle allows for this (training, walks, jobs, play), they are incredibly loyal and trainable companions. If not, they can become bored and reactive. **Crucial for your situation due to potential alone time.**

**Important Considerations & Next Steps:**

1.  **Research Individual Dogs:** Even within a breed, personalities vary. **Rescue is a fantastic option.** Shelters and rescues have dogs of all the breeds above. You can often find adult dogs whose personalities are known – they might be easier to place with a family matching your criteria. Ask rescues specifically about dogs known to be good with kids, other dogs, and non-chasing.
2.  **"Known Hybrids":** Many shelters and rescues have well-bred mixes (like Goldendoodles, Labradoodles, Cavapoos, Cockapoodles). These often combine traits from both parent breeds. Look for reputable rescues that screen for temperament and health.
3.  **Temperament Testing:** Reputable breeders and rescues will typically have you meet the dog and observe their temperament before adoption/purchase. Ask about their history and interactions.
4.  **Training & Socialization:** "Easy to train" means they *respond* well, but training *is* still essential for everyone's safety and happiness. Start early and be consistent. Basic obedience is crucial.
5.  **Energy Level:** While all these breeds are generally "chill," they still need appropriate exercise and mental stimulation (walks, play, training sessions, puzzle toys). A high-rise apartment means planning for this.
6.  **Health:** Research the common health concerns for any breed you're seriously considering (e.g., Cavalier heart issues, Basset Hound joint issues, Poodle skin allergies, French Bulldog breathing). Ask breeders/rescues about health clearances.
7.  **Spouse's Preference:** Reputable breeders exist, but shelters/rescues often have the best options for your specific criteria (temperament, health, cost). Discuss the pros and cons of both avenues with your spouse.

**Action Plan:**

1.  **Identify Top 2-3 Breeds:** Based on your criteria, pick breeds you're most drawn to (e.g., Golden, Lab, Poodle, Cavalier, Basset Hound).
2.  **Research Breed-Specific Rescues:** Look for shelters or rescue groups specializing in those breeds in your area. Many have online profiles for adoptable dogs.
3.  **Visit Shelters/Rescues:** Go in person to meet dogs and see which ones click with you. Ask questions about their history and temperament.
4.  **Talk to Breeders:** If pursuing purebreds, research and interview breeders thoroughly. Visit the facility, ask about health testing, and meet the parents.
5.  **Prepare Your Home:** Ensure you have the necessary supplies (crate, bed, leash, food/water bowls, toys, grooming tools, etc.). Consider pet-proofing your apartment.
6.  **Commit to Training & Socialization:** Enroll in puppy classes or find a good trainer from day one.

By focusing on rescues or reputable breeders and prioritizing temperament assessment, you'll find a wonderful, "chill," family-friendly dog that fits your lifestyle! Good luck!
