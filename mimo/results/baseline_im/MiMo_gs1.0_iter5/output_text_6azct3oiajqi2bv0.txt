That's a smart choice! Nordstrom Rack is a great place to find quality winter gear at a good price. Sticking something on a jacket lining without tearing it requires a bit of care, but it's definitely possible. Here are the best methods, starting with the safest:

1.  **Double-Sided Fabric Tape (Best for most situations):**
    *   **Why it's good:** Specifically designed for fabric, it's strong, non-removable, and won't damage the lining when applied correctly. It's easy to find (search online or in craft stores).
    *   **How to apply:**
        *   **Peel off one side first.** Peel back a small strip of the tape backing (about 1-2 inches) and smooth it onto the *wrong side* of the lining, *directly underneath* where you want the item to sit. Press firmly.
        *   **Peel off the remaining backing and press firmly.** Smooth the tape down completely over the item you're sticking on.
        *   **Key:** The tape *must* be bonded to the lining first, *then* the item. Never try to stick the item directly onto the lining with tape.

2.  **Fabric-Safe Glue (Use with Caution):**
    *   **Why it's good:** Offers a permanent bond.
    *   **Why it's risky:** Can be messy, can warp synthetics, and removal can be tricky.
    *   **Best Glue:** **E6000 Fabric Glue** is the gold standard for this. It's strong, dries clear, and is formulated for fabrics. Avoid regular craft glue or super glue.
    *   **How to apply:**
        *   **TEST FIRST:** Apply a tiny dab of glue to a *very inconspicuous* seam allowance or hem on the lining *and* the item you're sticking. Let it dry completely and check for color transfer, warping, or residue.
        *   **Apply Glue Sparingly:** Use a tiny dot or line of glue *only* on the *wrong side* of the lining, directly under where the item will sit. Press the item firmly into place.
        *   **Clamp/Grip:** Use clothespins or a hairdryer on low heat (held safely away) to gently heat and activate the glue while holding the item down for a few minutes.
        *   **Press Gently:** Once the glue starts to set, press down very gently over the area to ensure full contact without stretching or tearing the lining.

3.  **Heat-Activated Fabric Tape (For patches, labels, etc.):**
    *   **Why it's good:** Creates a strong, permanent bond when heated.
    *   **How to apply:** Similar to double-sided tape, but you peel off the backing and press the item onto the tape. Then, you use a fabric iron (set to the appropriate cotton/wool setting - *check the tape package*) to press firmly over the item for a few seconds to activate the glue. Remove the backing once heated.

**Crucial Tips for ANY Method:**

*   **Work on the Wrong Side:** Always apply the adhesive or glue to the *wrong side* of the lining, directly under the item you're sticking.
*   **Use Small Amounts:** Too much adhesive or glue is more likely to cause damage or peeling.
*   **Press Gently:** Avoid stretching or pulling the lining fabric while applying. Use firm, even pressure.
*   **Test, Test, Test:** Especially with glue, always test on an inconspicuous seam first.
*   **Consider Sewing/Stitching:** If the item needs to be very secure or you have access to a needle, **stitching is the most reliable method.** You can sew through the item and the lining layer to anchor it. This is often the best long-term solution.

**Methods to AVOID:**

*   **Regular Tape (Scotch Tape, Packing Tape):** Will almost certainly leave residue, tear the lining easily, and be removable (which you probably don't want).
*   **Super Glue (Cyanoacrylate):** Can warp synthetics, create bubbles, be hard to remove cleanly, and is very messy. **Generally not recommended for linings.**
*   **Fabric Glue Without Testing:** Especially on synthetics like polyester or nylon.

**Recommendation:** **Start with Double-Sided Fabric Tape.** It's the safest, easiest, and least risky option for most sticking tasks on a lining. If you need something more permanent or heavy-duty (like a large patch), and you're comfortable, use **E6000 Fabric Glue after thoroughly testing it.**

Good luck with your jacket project! Letting the fabric dry or cool down completely after applying the adhesive is key for a clean result.
