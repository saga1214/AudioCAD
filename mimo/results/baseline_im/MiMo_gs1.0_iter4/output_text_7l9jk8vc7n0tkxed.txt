### 🔧 Step-by-Step Plan:
1. **Gather Tools**  
   - **Drain Snake (Auger)**: Use a long-handled, flexible one (15–20 ft).  
   - **Plumber’s Putty or Wet/Dry Vacuum**: For water containment.  
   - **Gloves & Safety Glasses**: Protect your hands and eyes.  
   - **Bucket or Container**: For drainage.  

2. **Contain Water**  
   - Place a bucket under the drain to catch residual water.  
   - If the clog is severe, use a wet/dry vacuum to remove standing water first.  

3. **Try the Drain Snake**  
   - Insert the snake head deep into the drain.  
   - **Crank slowly**: Feel for resistance. When you hit the clog, **rotate clockwise** to break it up.  
   - **Pull back gradually**: If you hook something, ease it out slowly—don’t force it!  
   - **Clear debris**: Scrape off any stuck material with a pipe brush.  

4. **If the Snake Fails**  
   - **Slurp Method**: If the snake head is stuck, use a plunger *and* a wet/dry vacuum simultaneously.  
   - **Chemical Cleaners (Last Resort)**: For organic clogs (e.g., hair, grease), use a **non-toxic enzymatic cleaner**. Let it sit for 1–2 hours.  

5. **Advanced Tools (If Needed)**  
   - **Hydro Jetter**: For stubborn clogs, use a high-pressure water jetter. *Caution:* Wear eye protection!  
   - **Pipe Cutter or Wrench**: Only if you’re sure it’s a plastic pipe and you’re comfortable cutting it.  

6. **Dispose of Debris**  
   - Remove the clog from the snake head and dispose of it properly (e.g., trash, not down the drain).  

### ⚠️ Critical Safety Tips:
- **Never force a tool**: This can snap pipes or damage your wallet.  
- **Check for sewer backups**: If water is rising in other drains, you may need a **sewer line camera** ($100–$300).  
- **Call a pro if**:  
  - The clog persists after 30+ minutes.  
  - You suspect a broken pipe or tree roots.  
  - The job is outside your scope.
