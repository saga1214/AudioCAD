Okay, this is a fantastic opportunity to plan a truly unforgettable family vacation! Since you have a healthy budget and are traveling with your husband and daughter, I’d love to suggest a trip to either Big Sur or Monterey and the Central Coast. Both offer stunning scenery, unique experiences, and plenty of gluten-free dining options, which is perfect since your daughter has Celiac disease. I'll break down a sample 5-day itinerary for you.

First up, for a place to stay, I'd recommend the Ventana Big Sur Lodge. It’s in a really secluded, beautiful spot in Big Sur, and they have excellent accommodations and a dedicated gluten-free kitchen. It’s super private and luxurious. As an alternative, if you're more interested in Monterey, you could look into the Asilomar Conference Grounds. It’s a historic spot with a lovely beach view and great dining.

Now for the itinerary. Let's start with Day 1, which would be your arrival day. You can fly into Monterey, that's the closest major airport. From there, you could rent a car, which will be essential for exploring. You'll want to drive along Highway 1 to get to Big Sur. You can stop at some scenic overlooks like McWay Falls, but maybe save the full hike for another day. For dinner, I'd suggest the Ventana's own Ventana Restaurant, which has a dedicated gluten-free menu and incredible views of the ocean. Just be sure to call ahead to make a reservation.

On Day 2, you could spend the morning hiking at Pfeiffer Beach, which has those famous purple sand dunes. In the afternoon, maybe visit the iconic Bixby Bridge. For lunch, there's a great spot called Nepenthe, but it's high up on a cliff with amazing views, so you'll want to reserve a table well in advance. In the evening, you could take a short drive to Carmel-by-the-Sea for dinner. There's a wonderful restaurant there called The为一体叫“Carmel Valley Bistro”，它提供非常棒的海鲜和专门的无麸质菜单。你可以预订一个靠窗的座位，欣赏海滨风光。

Day 3 could be a full day exploring Big Sur. You could start with a walk along the Nepenthe Trail for those breathtaking cliff views. Then, maybe visit the Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park to see McWay Falls up close. For lunch, there's a charming café called Big Sur Bakery that has a dedicated gluten-free section. In the afternoon, you could explore some of the art galleries in Big Sur or take a scenic drive along the coast to see some sea caves. For dinner, Ventana has a special tasting menu you could try.

For Day 4, you could take a day trip to the Monterey Bay Aquarium. It's a must-see and they even have a small exhibit for your daughter, the SeaQuest, which is full of cool sea creatures. After that, you could explore Cannery Row and maybe even take a tour of a local farm to learn about sustainable farming. For dinner, I'd suggest The Sardine Factory in Carmel, which has a dedicated gluten-free menu and a fun, casual atmosphere.

Finally, on Day 5, you can have a relaxing morning at Big Sur. Maybe take a nice long walk on the beach at Pleasure Point, or enjoy a leisurely breakfast at the Ventana's restaurant. In the afternoon, you can drive back towards Monterey, stopping at any last-minute spots you want to see. Then you can fly home from Monterey.

Just a few extra tips: definitely rent a car, as public transport isn't great in this area. And for dining, always mention her Celiac diagnosis, as many places are very accommodating. Also, pack for all kinds of weather, as coastal areas can be unpredictable. And of course, bring plenty of sunscreen and a reusable water bottle. I hope this helps you plan a wonderful trip!
