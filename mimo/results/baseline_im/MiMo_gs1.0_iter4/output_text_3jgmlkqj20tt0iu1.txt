Okay, so you're looking for two distinct ideas on how to initiate a raise conversation with your boss. This is a high-stakes conversation, so it's smart to prepare carefully. Let's break down two different approaches you could take.

First, there's the 'Performance Review Link' approach. This is where you tie the raise request directly to your performance and how it aligns with the company's goals. You'd want to schedule a dedicated meeting specifically for this discussion, not just tack it on to an existing chat. In the meeting, you'd present clear, quantifiable examples of your achievements and how they've added value. You'd also want to demonstrate how your contributions have made a tangible difference to the company's objectives. You can frame it as a natural progression based on your proven performance. This approach is very structured and results-oriented.

Second, there's the 'Value Demonstration' approach. This one focuses more on the future and the value you plan to bring to the company. You'd start by summarizing your past achievements, but then pivot to outlining specific, ambitious goals you plan to achieve in the next year. You'd connect these goals directly to the company's objectives, showing how you'll contribute significantly. Then, you'd position the raise as an investment in your continued growth and your ability to deliver even more value. This approach is more forward-looking and strategic.

Both of these strategies are effective because they shift the focus away from you asking for something, and towards you demonstrating your worth and the value you provide. They give you a clear structure to follow, which can help reduce anxiety. Whichever path you choose, practice what you're going to say beforehand and remember to be confident in your contributions. Good luck!
