Before hitting the road, your diesel camper van needs a thorough check. Here’s a quick guide to key maintenance tasks:

**1. Brakes (Critical for safety):**
   - **Visual Inspection:** Check brake pads/rotors for uneven wear, cracks, or rust. If pads are less than 3mm thick or rotors are warped, replace them.
   - **Fluid Levels:** Ensure brake fluid is at the correct level (check the reservoir, usually near the firewall).
   - **Tire Pressure:** Verify tires are inflated to the manufacturer’s specs (found on the driver’s door jamb sticker).
   - **Warning Signs:** Squeaking during braking, vibration when turning, or a "spongy" pedal feel indicate urgent attention.

**2. Spark Plugs (For diesel engines):**
   - **Why Change?** Diesel engines use high compression, so spark plugs wear faster. Changing them every 30,000–100,000 miles (check your manual) ensures smooth operation.
   - **How to Change:**
     1. Park on a level surface, let the engine cool, and remove the battery’s negative terminal.
     2. Locate the spark plugs (access varies by van; some are under the valve cover).
     3. Use a socket wrench to remove the old plugs. Inspect the plug wires for cracks/fraying.
     4. Install new spark plugs, ensuring they’re torqued to the manufacturer’s specification (use a torque wrench).
     5. Reinstall the battery and test-drive the van.

**3. Additional Checks:**
   - **Fluids:** Top up engine oil, coolant, and transmission fluid.
   - **Battery:** Clean terminals and test charge if it’s older than 3 years.
   - **Tires:** Check tread depth (use the penny test) and inspect for cracks or bulges.
   - **Lights:** Verify all exterior lights (headlights, brake lights, turn signals) work.

**Pro Tip:** If you’re uncomfortable with mechanical tasks, visit a trusted mechanic for a pre-trip inspection. For brakes and plugs, it’s often worth the small cost to ensure reliability. Safe travels! 🚐💨
