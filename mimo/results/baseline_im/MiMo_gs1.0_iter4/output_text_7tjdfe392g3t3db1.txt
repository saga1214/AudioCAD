To tune your stringed instrument (like a guitar, ukulele, or banjo), you can use one of these tools:  

### 1. **Clip-On Tuner**  
   - **How to use**:  
     - Clip the tuner onto your instrument’s headstock.  
     - Play an **open string** (without pressing any frets).  
     - The tuner will display the note’s frequency (e.g., A4, D2).  
     - If the note is **too low**, tighten the string.  
     - If it’s **too high**, loosen the string.  
     - Adjust until the tuner shows a stable green/light.  
   - **Best for**: Precision and ease.  

### 2. **Tuner App**  
   - **How to use**:  
     - Open your phone’s tuner app (e.g., GuitarTuna, Fender Tune).  
     - Hold the app over the instrument’s soundhole/headstock.  
     - Play an open string.  
     - The app will display the pitch (e.g., "A# 442 Hz").  
     - Adjust until it’s within the app’s "tune" zone (usually green/yellow).  
   - **Best for**: Portability (free but requires phone mic access).  

### Key Tips:  
- **Tune one string at a time**.  
- **Start with the lowest string** (6th string on guitar) and move up.  
- **Use reference notes**: Many tuners show the target pitch (e.g., "A440 Hz") for comparison.  
- **Avoid over-tuning**: If the tuner’s needle swings wildly, you’re too far off.  

### Example Workflow (Guitar):  
1. Clip tuner to headstock.  
2. Play open 6th string (low E).  
3. Adjust until tuner stabilizes on "E".  
4. Repeat for 5th (A), 4th (D), 3rd (G), 2nd (B), 1st (high E).  

With practice, you’ll develop an ear for tuning! 🎸
