For your winter chicks, **wood pellet bedding** is an excellent choice—it's absorbent, dust-free, mold-resistant, and won't attract rodents if managed properly. Here’s why it works and how to use it:

### **Best Bedding Options:**
1. **Wood Pellet Bedding**  
   - **Pros**: Highly absorbent, resists mold, low dust, and decomposes into compost.  
   - **Cons**: Heavy (requires sturdy brooder), can clump if wet.  
   - **Tip**: Use a **deep litter method**—add fresh pellets weekly and fluff the bedding to prevent ammonia buildup.

2. **Pine or Cedar Shavings (if pellets aren’t available)**  
   - **Pros**: Affordable and effective.  
   - **Cons**: Dusty (can irritate chicks’ lungs) and less absorbent than pellets.  
   - **Tip**: Use **unmed, kiln-dried shavings** and add a **1-inch layer of sand** on top to absorb moisture and reduce dust.

3. **Straw or Hay (if rodents aren’t a concern)**  
   - **Pros**: Natural, cheap, and good insulation.  
   - **Cons**: Can mold if damp; rodents may nest in it if not secured.  
   - **Tip**: Use **only if your coop is very dry**, and secure with wire mesh to deter rodents.

### **Critical Tips for Chicks:**
- **Avoid Straw/Pine for Rodent Safety**: Stick to **wood pellets or sand** to minimize rodent attraction.  
- **Ventilation**: Ensure airflow under the brooder plate to prevent dampness.  
- **Hygiene**: Remove wet spots immediately to avoid chilling or disease.  
- **Layering**: Place a **1–2 inch layer of sand** under the bedding to absorb moisture and reduce dust.

### **Why Wood Pellets Win for You:**
- **No Rodent Risk**: Pellets are too bulky for rodents to nest in.  
- **Heat Efficiency**: They stay dry longer, keeping chicks warm near the brooder plate.  
- **Composting**: Breaks down into nutrient-rich soil for future gardens.

**Final Recommendation**: **Wood pellets + sand** is the safest, most effective combo for winter chicks. It’s rodent-proof, low-dust, and keeps them warm and dry. 🐥🔥
