Okay, let's tackle this step-by-step. Since plunging didn't work, it's likely a deep, stubborn clog – maybe from grease, hair, soap scum, or something stuck deep in the pipe. Here's the methodical approach:

**Step 1: Hot Water & Baking Soda/Vinegar (Last Resort First? No, let's try it!)**
*   **Why:** This can sometimes dislodge softer organic clogs if it's deep but not rock-solid.
*   **How:**
    1.  Pour **2 cups of boiling water** down the drain, slowly, to help soften the clog.
    2.  Wait 10-15 minutes.
    3.  Mix **1 cup baking soda** with **1 cup white vinegar** in a bowl. It will fizz.
    4.  Pour the mixture down the drain *slowly*.
    5.  Wait 30-60 minutes. The fizzing action can help break things down.
    6.  **Then, try plunging again.** If this doesn't work, we move to the snake.

**Step 2: Use a Drain Snake (Auger) - Your Most Likely Solution**
*   **Why:** This is the classic tool for deep clogs. It's a spring-like coil that you push in, hook the debris, and pull back.
*   **How:**
    1.  **Prepare:** Ensure you have a good quality drain snake (15-20 feet is standard). Wear gloves and eye protection. Protect your sink basin with towels.
    2.  **Insert:** Push the snake head into the drain opening. It might be tricky – use pliers to grip the end if needed. Don't force it, let it turn naturally.
    3.  **Hook:** Keep pushing and turning the handle (usually clockwise) until you feel resistance or the snake kinks. This is the clog.
    4.  **Hook & Pull:** Keep pushing the snake head slightly *through* the clog, then start pulling the snake back out slowly. You should feel the clog being pulled up with the snake coil. You might see the debris coming out.
    5.  **Clear:** Once you pull the snake out, look at the debris. Cut it up with pliers or flush it down with hot water. If you don't get it all, try again.
    6.  **Check:** Flush the drain with hot water. If water flows freely, you're done! If not, repeat the process.

**Step 3: Try a Small Hand Auger (If the Snake Isn't Deep Enough or Flexible)**
*   **Why:** If your standard snake is too long or the pipe bends too sharply, a smaller, more flexible hand auger might work better for tighter curves.
*   **How:** Follow the same principles as Step 2, but use the hand auger instead. It requires more manual manipulation.

**Step 4: Check for Damage & Consider Replacement (Last Resort)**
*   **Why:** If the clog is *really* deep and stubborn, or if you suspect a broken pipe *behind* the wall, you might need to cut out part of the wall or floor.
*   **How:**
    1.  **Inspect:** Carefully look for any signs of leaks or damage around the pipe. Is it leaking?
    2.  **Cut:** If necessary, cut out a section of drywall, tile, or flooring *above* the drain to access the pipe. Use a pipe cutter or saw to cut the pipe if it's damaged or the clog is lodged in a section that can't be reached otherwise.
    3.  **Replace:** If the pipe is damaged or the clog is truly inaccessible by snake, you'll need to replace a section of pipe. This requires cutting, cutting the new section, and joining them with a Teflon tape-wrapped pipe connector (like a slip-fit or compression fitting). This is more complex and might require calling a specialist if you're unsure.

**Key Tips for You:**

*   **Patience is Key:** Deep clogs take time. Don't get frustrated mid-step. Give each method its chance.
*   **Protect Yourself:** Gloves and eye protection are essential, especially when plunging hard or using tools.
*   **Don't Over-Pump:** Plunging too hard or too fast can crack the porcelain sink or damage the drain flange. Use steady, firm pressure.
*   **Know Your Pipe:** Is it PVC? CPVC? Copper? PVC is common and easier to work with, but don't overtighten fittings.
*   **When to Call a Pro:** If the clog persists after trying a good snake, if you see significant damage, if water is leaking, or if you just feel overwhelmed, call a qualified plumber. It's okay to ask for help!
