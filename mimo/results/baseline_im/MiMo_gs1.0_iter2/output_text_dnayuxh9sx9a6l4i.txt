Based on your criteria (easy to train, good with kids/dogs/non-chasing, high-rise living, dog park nearby, potential for mixed breeds), here are excellent dog breed options:

**Top Purebred Recommendations:**

1.  **Labrador Retriever:** The quintessential friendly, easygoing, and highly trainable family dog. Loves people, kids, and other dogs. Naturally curious but generally doesn't *chase* squirrels as much as fixate on them. Great for kids. (Note: Some individual Labs can be food-motivated).
2.  **Golden Retriever:** Very similar to Labs – exceptionally gentle, patient, intelligent, and eager to please. Excellent with children and other dogs. Generally calm and non-chasing. (Note: Can be prone to separation anxiety if left alone too long).
3.  **Cavalier King Charles Spaniel:** Known for their affectionate, gentle, and "chill" nature. Very good with kids and other dogs. Generally low-energy and adaptable to apartment life. (Note: Grooming needs are moderate, and they can be prone to certain health issues like heart problems).
4.  **French Bulldog:** Low-energy, adaptable, and affectionate. Generally good with kids and other dogs if socialized. Their unique flat face means they shouldn't be exercised excessively. Minimal chasing drive. (Note: Can be prone to breathing issues, spinal problems, and separation anxiety. High cost of care).

**Potential Mixed Breed Hybrids (Look for these traits):**

5.  **Labradoodle (or similar Lab mix):** Often bred for a "low-shedding" coat (though not guaranteed), combining the friendly nature of Labs with the smaller size of Poodles. Can be great with kids and other dogs if well-bred. Generally low chasing drive. (Research breeders carefully to avoid health issues).
6.  **Goldendoodle (or similar Goldie mix):** Combines the temperament of a Golden Retriever with the intelligence of a Poodle. Typically very friendly, trainable, and good with people/kids/dogs. Minimal chasing. (Again, breeder research is crucial).
7.  **Basset Hound:** Very laid-back, affectionate, and generally good-natured. Good with kids and other dogs. Low energy and minimal chasing drive. (Note: Very slow-moving, can be stubborn, and prone to drooling/ear issues).

**Other Considerations:**

*   **Border Collie:** *Extremely* intelligent and trainable, but **highly energetic**. Needs significant daily exercise and mental stimulation. If under-stimulated, they can develop obsessive behaviors, *including chasing*. Not ideal unless you can provide intense activity. (Can be great if your schedule allows).
*   **Basset Hound:** As mentioned above, a great "chill" choice if the energy level fits your life and you can manage their grooming/sensory needs.
*   **Poodle (Standard, Miniature, Toy):** Highly intelligent, trainable, and excellent with people/kids/dogs. Minimal chasing drive. Good for apartments. (Note: Can be aloof, and grooming is a major commitment, especially for Miniatures/Toy).

**Key Steps for Your Search:**

1.  **Rescue/Shelter First:** Consider adopting a mixed breed rescue dog that already has a known temperament (look for descriptions like "easygoing," "good with kids/dogs," "low prey drive"). This is often the best way to find a perfect fit.
2.  **Breed-Specific Rescues:** If you prefer purebreds, look for rescues specializing in the breeds you're interested in (e.g., Cavalier Spaniel Rescue, Golden Retriever Rescue, French Bulldog Rescue).
3.  **Reputable Breeders (If Adopting Purebred):** If going the breeder route for a purebred, ensure they are reputable. Ask about health clearances (especially for Frenchies, Cavoodles, etc.), temperament testing, and socialization. Avoid puppy mills.
4.  **Meet & Greet:** **Crucial!** Visit the dog in person. Observe how it interacts with you, your spouse, and other people. Ask to see how it reacts to gentle commands (sit, stay, come) and how it walks on a loose leash. Visit the nearby dog park to see how it behaves around other dogs.
5.  **Consider Adult Dogs:** Adult dogs often have a more settled temperament and known personality than puppies, which can be a great option for first-time owners.
6.  **Dog Park Test Drive:** If you adopt a dog, take it to the nearby dog park for a few sessions to see how it handles the environment, other dogs, and people.

By focusing on breeds known for temperament and choosing carefully (rescues or reputable breeders), you'll find a wonderful, "chill," family-friendly dog that fits perfectly with your lifestyle and high-rise living!
