That's a great approach. You want to be clear and professional, but not confrontational.

Here are a few key points to include in your email. First, start with gratitude. You could say something like, "Thank you for the opportunity to learn from you this semester." This sets a respectful tone.

Second, state your purpose clearly. You could write, "I'm writing to request feedback on my recent exam score of 64, as I'd like to understand how to improve."

Third, and this is important, frame it as a request for guidance. Something like, "Could you share specific areas where I could have done better? I'm committed to improving my skills in this subject."

Finally, offer to meet. You could add, "If you have a few minutes to discuss this, I would be grateful to hear your thoughts."

This structure keeps it professional and focused on learning, which is the approach you agreed on.
