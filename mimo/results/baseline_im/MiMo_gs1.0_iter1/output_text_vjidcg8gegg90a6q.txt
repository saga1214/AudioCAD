For winter chicks, here are the safest and most effective bedding options—**all rodent-resistant** and **safe for young birds**:

### 🐥 Best Bedding Options:
1. **Wood Shavings (Aspen or Pine-Free)**  
   - **Why**: Lightweight, fluffy, and affordable. Avoid cedar/pine (toxic oils).  
   - **Tip**: Use a 2–3 inch deep layer for insulation. Replace weekly.  

2. **Oat Hulls**  
   - **Why**: Cheap, absorbent, and insulating. Chicks burrow well in them.  
   - **Tip**: Mix with wood shavings to reduce dust.  

3. **Paper-Based Bedding (e.g., "PaperPad" or "PaperPads")**  
   - **Why**: Dust-free, biodegradable, and easy to handle.  
   - **Tip**: Layer 2–3 sheets thick. Replace daily to prevent moisture buildup.  

4. **Hay (Alfalfa or Timothy)**  
   - **Why**: Soft, warm, and mold-resistant. Ideal for chicks that like to nest.  
   - **Tip**: Fluff well to avoid compacting. Avoid baled hay (dust risk).  

### ⚠️ Critical Tips:
- **Avoid Straw/Straw Hay**: Attracts rodents and molds easily.  
- **Never Use Pine/Cedar**: Volatile oils irritate chicks' lungs.  
- **Monitor Moisture**: Wet bedding causes pneumonia. Ensure airflow under the brooder plate.  
- **Depth**: Keep bedding 1–2 inches deep—chicks should always be able to reach the heat source.  
- **Cleanliness**: Change bedding daily in the brooder to prevent bacteria.  

### 🔥 Safety Reminder:
The **brooder plate** is ideal—place it on a wire rack (not directly on bedding) to allow airflow underneath. Set the thermostat to **90–95°F** for 5-week-old chicks, then reduce gradually as they age.
