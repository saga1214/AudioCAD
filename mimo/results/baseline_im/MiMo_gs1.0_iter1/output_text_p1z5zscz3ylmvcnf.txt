Let’s brainstorm some iconic models that are both desirable and historically significant—perfect for an old-school guy who appreciates craftsmanship and legacy. Here are five that could spark his interest:

First, there's the Ford Mustang. It's an American icon, and the classic '67 to '73 models are timeless. They're affordable to start, and their value has been climbing steadily. Plus, the Mustang has a strong community around it, so he’d feel part of a club. 

Second, the Chevrolet Camaro. It's like the Mustang's rival, and the second-gen models from the late seventies are super collectible. It’s got that muscle car appeal, and Arizona’s sunny climate is perfect for keeping one looking great.

Third, the Porsche 911. This is a bit more upscale, but if he's got the taste for European engineering, the 993 or 964 models are amazing. They hold their value like gold, and the whole "driver's car" thing could appeal to his hands-on side.

Fourth, the classic muscle car, the Dodge Charger. The '68 to '70 models are legendary. They're big, bold, and their value is soaring. If he loves power and presence, this is the way to go.

And fifth, the Jaguar E-Type. It's a bit more niche, but it's stunning. The values have shot up recently, and it’s a conversation starter. If he enjoys the finer things in life, this could be his "secret indulgence."

The key is to frame it as a fun project or a way to pass down a legacy to the grandkids, not as a financial lesson. Maybe suggest a car meet or a restoration project to get him excited. It’s all about making him feel like a savvy investor while staying true to his old-school roots.
