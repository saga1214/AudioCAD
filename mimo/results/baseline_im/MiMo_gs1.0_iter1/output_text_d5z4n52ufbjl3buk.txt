Okay, let's craft a professional email that balances respect with your need for clarity. Here’s a structure you could follow. 

First, the subject line should be very clear. Something like: “Request for Feedback on [Exam Name] Grade.”

In the email, you can start by respectfully stating your purpose. For example: “Dear Professor [Last Name], I hope this email finds you well. I’m writing to request feedback on my grade of 64 on the [Exam Name] exam. I’m writing this to better understand how my response could be improved for future assignments.”

Then, you can mention your overall academic performance to provide context, but do it in a way that doesn't sound like you're complaining. You could say something like, “I’ve worked diligently throughout the semester and have consistently performed well in my other courses. However, this grade was unexpected and I’d really value your insight into the areas where I fell short.”

It's also a good idea to ask specific, constructive questions. For example: “Could you clarify the grading criteria for this exam? I’d appreciate any specific feedback on my essay’s structure, analysis, or other elements that could help me improve.” 

Then, you can politely ask about scheduling a meeting. You could say, “I’m happy to meet at your convenience to discuss this further. Please let me know if office hours would work, or if another time might be better for you.”

Finally, you can close the email professionally. Something like: “Thank you for your time and guidance. I look forward to your feedback.” Then sign off with “Sincerely,” and your name.

This approach keeps the focus on learning and avoids any accusations, which should make it more likely he'll respond positively.
