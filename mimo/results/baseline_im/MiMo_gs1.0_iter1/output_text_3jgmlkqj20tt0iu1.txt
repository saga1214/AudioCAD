Okay, so you're asking about the best way to initiate a raise discussion for the first time and how to manage the anxiety. Let's break this down into two key areas: preparation and communication strategies.

First, preparation is crucial. Before you even think about having the conversation, you need to build a strong foundation. This means tracking your accomplishments meticulously. You should document your contributions, any new skills you've acquired, and specific results you've achieved that go beyond your basic job description. It's also important to research market salaries for your role, experience, and location to understand what a fair increase would be. And finally, prepare your case. Think about how your contributions add value to the company and align with their goals. This helps you frame your request in a business context, not just as a personal want.

Now, for the actual conversation, there are a few strategies to help you feel more confident. One approach is to schedule a specific meeting with your boss. Don't just bring it up casually. Frame it as a professional discussion about your growth and contribution. For instance, you could say something like, "I'd like to schedule some time to discuss my performance and career development." This gives you control and allows you to prepare thoroughly.

Another strategy is to tie your request to your performance and future contributions. Emphasize your track record of meeting deadlines and exceeding expectations, and express your enthusiasm for continuing to contribute at a higher level. This shifts the focus from just asking for money to discussing a mutually beneficial investment in your continued growth.

To manage anxiety, practice is key. Rehearse what you want to say out loud, either by yourself or with a trusted friend. Also, focus on your breathing to stay calm. And remember to manage your expectations; the first conversation is about laying the groundwork, not necessarily getting an immediate yes. Think of it as starting a dialogue.

So, to sum it up: prepare with data and a clear case, choose the right time and setting, communicate your value professionally, and manage your nerves through practice and preparation. This approach will help you present a strong, confident case for your raise.
