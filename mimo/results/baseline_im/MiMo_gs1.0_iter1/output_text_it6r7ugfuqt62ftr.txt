Okay, you're looking for a 4 to 5-day vacation itinerary for you, your husband, and your daughter. Since your car is too small, you'll need to focus on places that are accessible by public transit, like a major city. Given your daughter's birthday wish for an adventure and the need for gluten-free dining, I think San Diego is a perfect choice. It has everything you're looking for: beautiful beaches, a mild climate, and a fantastic gluten-free food scene.

Here’s a sample 5-day itinerary for you. I’d suggest staying in the Gaslamp Quarter. It’s a central location with lots of dining options, and many places there cater to gluten-free diets.

For Day One, you could start with breakfast at The Mission Café. They have a great gluten-free menu and a lovely patio. Then, I'd recommend visiting the San Diego Zoo. It's world-famous and a perfect way to spend a day with the whole family. For lunch, you can grab something quick at the zoo, or head to a place like The Taco Stand for gluten-free tacos. In the evening, you could explore the Gaslamp Quarter, which has a lot of shops and bars. For dinner, you could try Café Gratitude, which is all about positive vibes and has a dedicated gluten-free menu. You could even get a gluten-free dessert from the store next door, Sprinkles Cupcakes.

On Day Two, you could take a day trip to Coronado Island. You can get there by taking a trolley or a short bus ride. You can spend the morning at Coronado Beach, maybe rent some bikes. For lunch, The Hotel del Coronado has a restaurant called The Del Mar Beach House that does gluten-free modifications. In the afternoon, you could take a guided walking tour of the historic hotel. For dinner, I'd suggest Seven Grand. It’s a speakeasy-style bar that has excellent craft cocktails and a great gluten-free menu. For a sweet ending, you can walk to the end of the island to see the famous lighthouse.

For Day Three, you could spend the morning at Balboa Park. It's an amazing spot with museums, gardens, and the San Diego Zoo Safari Park. For lunch, you can find some great gluten-free options right there in the park. In the afternoon, you could visit the San Diego Museum of Art or the Fleet Science Center, depending on your daughter's interests. For dinner, I'd recommend the Fishery. It's a very popular spot for seafood, and they do a great job accommodating gluten-free needs.

Day Four could be a relaxing day spent at La Jolla. You can take the trolley or a short bus ride to get there. You could go for a walk along the La Jolla Cove, where you might see sea lions. For lunch, The壳fish & Oyster Bar has a dedicated gluten-free menu. In the afternoon, you could take a kayak tour through the sea caves. For dinner, you could try Nobu Malibu, which is a short drive away. They have a fantastic sushi menu and can easily do gluten-free. Or, if you'd rather not drive, the Sea Ranch Restaurant in San Juan Capistrano is another great option.

And for your last day, Day Five, you could spend it at the Mission Beach Boardwalk. You can rent bikes or even try surfing lessons. For lunch, Mission Beach Boardwalk has a few food trucks with gluten-free options. In the afternoon, you could visit the Belmont Park amusement park. For a final dinner, you could go back to the Gaslamp and try the restaurant I mentioned earlier, Café Gratitude, again, or maybe try a new spot like Lucha Libre, which has a fun, themed dinner.

Just a few other tips: definitely use the Muni Metro or the trolley system for getting around. Most restaurants are very accommodating for gluten-free needs, but it's always a good idea to call ahead. And since you have a bigger budget, you could also consider renting a car for a day to explore places like Carlsbad or La Jolla more easily.

I hope this helps you plan a wonderful trip!
