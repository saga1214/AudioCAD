Here's a breakdown of brake inspection and spark plug replacement to ensure your camper van is ready for the road:

### Brake Inspection & Wear Signs
1.  **Visual Inspection:**  
    *   **Pads/Rotor:** Visually check pads for wear indicators (metal tabs). If worn thin (usually < 3mm), replace them. Look for uneven wear or glazing on rotors.  
    *   **Brake Fluid:** Check level in the reservoir (when cold). Low fluid often indicates leaks.  
    *   **Brake Lines/Hoses:** Look for cracks, bulges, or leaks near fittings.  
    *   **Calipers (if disc):** Ensure pistons move smoothly when compressed.  
    *   **Wheels:** Inspect for dust, debris, or unusual noises (grinding/screeching).  

2.  **Signs of Significant Wear:**  
    *   **Grinding/Squealing:** Harsh noises during braking often indicate worn pads or glazed rotors.  
    *   **Vibration/Pulsation:** Wobbling or vibration in the steering wheel when braking suggests warped rotors.  
    *   **Soft/Spongy Pedal:** Air in the system or failing components.  
    *   **Pulling to One Side:** Uneven pad wear or caliper issues.  
    *   **Reduced Braking Power:** Obvious loss of stopping ability.  
    *   **Visible Damage:** Severely worn pads, leaking fluid, or damaged rotors.  

**Action:** If you see significant wear, leaks, or experience any warning signs, get brakes inspected/repair by a professional mechanic *before* the trip.

### Spark Plug Replacement (General Steps - Diesel may differ)
*Note: Specific procedures vary by engine type (gasoline vs. diesel) and vehicle. Consult your owner's manual first!*

1.  **Tools Needed:**  
    *   Correct spark plug socket & ratchet  
    *   Torque wrench (critical!)  
    *   New spark plugs (correct heat range for your engine)  
    *   Gap tool (if plugs aren't pre-gapped)  
    *   Dielectric grease (optional, for boot connection)  
    *   Safety glasses & gloves  

2.  **Steps:**  
    1.  **Disconnect Battery:** Prevent accidental starts.  
    2.  **Access Plugs:** Locate plugs (access might require removing a cover or air filter).  
    3.  **Remove Old Plugs:** Use socket to loosen plugs *counter-clockwise*. **Do not pull the plug out yet.**  
    4.  **Check Gap:** Measure and adjust gap if necessary (use gap tool).  
    5.  **Install New Plugs:**  
        *   Apply a *small* amount of dielectric grease to the plug's rubber boot.  
        *   Hand-tighten plugs *clockwise* until snug.  
        *   **Torque:** Use a torque wrench to tighten to the specification listed in your manual (typically 15-25 ft-lbs for many engines). **Over-torquing can crack the head.**  
    6.  **Reassemble:** Reinstall any covers or filters.  
    7.  **Reconnect Battery.**  

**Diesel Specifics:** Diesel engines often use longer, coarser plugs. Gap requirements and torque specs are usually higher. Refer specifically to your engine manual.

### Key Safety Reminders
*   **Torque is Essential:** Always use a torque wrench to avoid damaging the cylinder head or plugs.  
*   **Gap Correctly:** Incorrect gap causes misfires, poor performance, or overheating.  
*   **Use Correct Parts:** Match plug type, heat range, and gap exactly to your vehicle's specs.  
*   **When in Doubt, Consult a Pro:** If you're uncomfortable with brakes or unsure about plugs, get professional help. A safe van is a happy van!
