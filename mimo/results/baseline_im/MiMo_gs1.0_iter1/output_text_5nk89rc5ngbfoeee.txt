That's a great question! The perfect end-of-year celebration depends on your troop's vibe, budget, and the girls' ages. Here are a few ideas weighing the pros and cons of each approach:

**Option 1: Simple Indoor Cafeteria Party (Lower Key, More Controlled)**

*   **Pros:**
    *   **Easy Logistics:** No permits, weather worries, or renting equipment. Your familiar space.
    *   **Lower Cost:** Minimal setup needed (decorations, maybe a table for food). Food can be simple.
    *   **Relaxed Atmosphere:** Less chaotic, easier for younger girls.
    *   **Inclusive:** Easy for everyone to participate without worrying about being left out of noisy games.
*   **Cons:**
    *   **Less Festive:** Might feel a bit "ordinary" compared to a special event.
    *   **Activity Limitations:** Fewer options for high-energy outdoor games.
*   **Idea:** **A "Brownie Bash" Pizza Party!**
    *   **Food:** Order a large pizza (or two!) from a popular place. Set out small plates with chips, fruit, and maybe a small dessert (cupcakes or cookies). Let girls make sundaes with ice cream and toppings.
    *   **Activities:** Simple crafts (like decorating mini flower pots or friendship bracelets), a "best badge" display, a "trophy" ceremony for achievements, maybe a simple group game like charades or a trivia quiz about Brownies. Play fun music! Focus on celebrating their year.
*   **Why it might be best:** If you want something reliably fun, low-stress, and budget-friendly, especially considering your pregnancy. It's all about celebrating their growth and friendship.

**Option 2: Park BBQ (More Festive, Requires More Planning)**

*   **Pros:**
    *   **Super Fun & Festive:** Picnics and barbecues feel like a special celebration!
    *   **Outdoor Games Galore:** Wheelbarrow races, sack races, three-legged races, sack-a-thon, relay races – all perfect for Brownies! Nature walks, bug hunts.
    *   **Scenic:** Enjoying the outdoors adds a special element.
*   **Cons:**
    *   **Weather Dependent:** Rain is a real concern! You *must* have a solid rain plan (tents, indoor backup location).
    *   **More Logistical Hurdles:** Permits, renting grills/bbq supplies, organizing food, cleanup, parking.
    *   **Potentially Higher Cost:** Food, supplies, rentals.
    *   **Can Get Messy/Chaotic:** Managing food, drinks, games, and cleanup outdoors with a group.
    *   **Parent Help Crucial:** You'll need significant parent volunteers for setup, cooking, games, and cleanup.
*   **Idea: "Brownie Bonanza" Picnic & BBQ**
    *   **Food:** Grilled hot dogs, burgers (if older girls), or veggie options. Serve with chips, fruit salad, and a big dessert table (ice cream sundaes, cupcakes, cookies). Parent volunteers can handle cooking and serving.
    *   **Activities:** Plan a mix: 30-45 mins of active games (wheelbarrow, sack race), 15 mins of craft or quiet time (decorate rocks, make friendship bracelets), maybe a simple talent show or "trophy" ceremony. Keep the schedule loose!
    *   **Parent Involvement:** *Essential!* Ask specific parents to bring specific food items, manage one game station, set up/tear down, or handle cleanup. A sign-up sheet is key!
*   **Why it might be best:** If you have a great park, reliable parent help, and a big budget, this can be incredibly memorable and fun, especially if the girls love outdoor activities.

**Recommendation & Tips:**

1.  **Consider Your Pregnancy:** An indoor event is likely the *least stressful* option for you right now. You don't want to be stressed about weather or heavy lifting.
2.  **Consider Your Troop:** Are they super energetic and love running around? A park event might be better. Are they younger or less mobile? Indoor might be safer.
3.  **Budget is Key:** The cafeteria party is almost guaranteed to be cheaper. A park BBQ requires significant investment. Be upfront with parents about costs (e.g., "We need 3 parents to bring pizza, 2 parents to rent grills, etc.").
4.  **Parent Volunteers are Your Lifeline:** *Especially* for a park BBQ, but also helpful indoors. Clearly communicate what you need and what parents can contribute.
5.  **Keep it Simple & Focused:** End-of-year should be about celebrating their year, not a huge production. A simple pizza party with some fun games and a badge display can be just as special as a big BBQ.
6.  **Hydration & Sun Safety:** If you do go outdoors, plan for plenty of water, sunscreen, hats, and a shaded area.

**My Suggestion:** Given your situation (pregnant, nobody else stepping up *yet*), I lean towards the **Simple Indoor Cafeteria Party**. It's manageable, budget-friendly, and focuses on celebrating the girls without overwhelming logistics or weather worries. You can make it festive with decorations, good food (pizza/sundaes!), and a mix of fun, relaxed activities.

If you *do* decide to go for the park BBQ, **make the parent sign-up sheet your top priority** and have a solid rain backup plan. Enjoy your first year as troop leader – it sounds like you're doing a great job!
