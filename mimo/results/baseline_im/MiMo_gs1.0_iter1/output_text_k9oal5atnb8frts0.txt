That's a fantastic choice! Retro Tech is super fun and easy to visualize. Here are two lunch ideas that tie into the theme, keeping it practical and inclusive:

**Theme:** **Retro Tech Lunch**

**Core Idea:** Embrace the neon, geometric patterns, and nostalgic tech aesthetic of the 80s and 90s.

**Option 1: "Pixel Power" Lunch**

*   **Main Dish:** **"Chip" Sandwiches or Wraps:** Create sandwiches or wraps that look like old computer chips or circuit boards.
    *   *Assembly:* Use green bread for the base (like a circuit board), yellow cheese (like a "chip"), and add elements like:
        *   **"Pixel" Pâté:** Spread a bright red or blue bean dip or hummus on the bread.
        *   **"Glitch" Veggie:** Add a small piece of red bell pepper (like a pixel) and a small piece of green broccoli or lettuce (like a glitch).
        *   **"Data" Deli:** Include a smear of mustard or ketchup (like data lines).
        *   **"Error" Crisp:** Add a tiny crouton or piece of bacon (like an error message).
    *   *Alternative:* Make mini "chips" by wrapping small amounts of tuna/chicken salad, hummus, or bean dip in tortilla or lettuce cups. Serve with "glitchy" veggie sticks (red peppers, broccoli).
*   **Snack:** **"Pixel" Cookies or Brownies:** Bake cookies or brownies with colorful sprinkles (red, blue, green, yellow - like pixels) and maybe some edible glitter. Serve alongside **"Glitch" Pretzels** (pretzel rods dipped in red or green candy melted over them).
*   **Drink:** **"CRT" Smoothie:** Blend a fruit smoothie (maybe green or red for color) with a splash of blueberry or blackberry juice (like the blue/green glow of an old CRT monitor).
*   **Presentation:** Use **"Glitch" Wrapping Paper** for the sandwiches/bento boxes – paper with geometric patterns, circuit boards, or old tech logos. Add a small **"Tech Trivia"** question to the lunchbox or table.

**Option 2: "Glow & Dial" Lunch**

*   **Main Dish:** **"Dial-Up" Pasta Salad:** Make a vibrant pasta salad reminiscent of the colorful, slightly garish designs of the era.
    *   *Assembly:* Use bright, geometrically shaped pasta (like shells, spirals, or macaroni). Mix with:
        *   **"Glow" Veggies:** Green peas, yellow corn, red bell pepper chunks.
        *   **"Data" Dip:** A tangy Italian dressing or a creamy pesto-based dressing.
        *   **"Modem" Crunch:** Add chopped olives, sun-dried tomatoes (like a modem icon), and maybe some finely chopped red onion (like a data stream).
*   **Snack:** **"Glow-In-The-Dark" Popcorn:** Make popcorn and sprinkle it with non-toxic glow-in-the-dark sand or dust. Serve alongside **"Retro" Candy** (like old-school candy bars, gummies, or fruit chews).
*   **Drink:** **"Commodore" Juice Box:** Serve classic fruit punch or lemonade in classic juice box containers (like the old round ones).
*   **Presentation:** Serve the pasta salad in a bowl decorated with **"Geometric" Place Cards** (simple triangles, squares, circles). Add a small **"Tech Trivia"** question. For the popcorn, use a bowl shaped like a retro tech gadget (if available) or just a plain bowl.

**Key Considerations for Inclusivity & Practicality:**

1.  **Make-Ahead:** Both options are perfect for assembling components ahead of time (dressing, cookie dough, pasta salad) and just assembling the final meal an hour or two before lunch.
2.  **Dietary Swaps:** Easily adapt:
    *   **Veggie Option:** Use hummus, bean dip, or tofu instead of deli meat. Swap pasta salad dressing for a vinaigrette. Offer veggie sticks as the main veggie.
    *   **Gluten-Free:** Use gluten-free bread/tortillas, GF pasta, GF cookies/brownies.
    *   **Dairy-Free:** Use vegan cheese alternatives, dairy-free yogurt dips.
3.  **Snack Simplicity:** Salted nuts are a great base! Pair them with the themed snacks for easy finger food.
4.  **Theme Execution:** Focus on the *colors* (neon greens, reds, blues, yellows, oranges) and *shapes* (geometric patterns, pixelated dots, circuit board lines) rather than trying to recreate complex tech. The "glitch" and "pixel" elements add fun without being overwhelming.
5.  **Tech Trivia:** A small, fun trivia question about the era (e.g., "What was the first computer to have a GUI?" or "What was the first widely used dial-up modem speed?") adds a nice interactive touch.

These ideas are designed to be fun, visually on-theme, practical for an office setting, and adaptable for everyone. Enjoy bringing the retro tech vibe to your team!
