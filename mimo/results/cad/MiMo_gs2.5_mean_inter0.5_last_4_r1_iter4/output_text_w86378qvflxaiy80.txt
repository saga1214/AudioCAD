That's a really important question. While all the reasons are valid, the most critical aspect is really the preservation of unique and irreplaceable life forms.

Here’s why: If a species goes extinct, it's gone forever. There's no do-over. That unique genetic information, that specific ecological role it played, and all the potential uses we might have discovered it for... they're all lost. Protecting hotspots is fundamentally about preventing this irreversible loss of unique biological heritage. It's about safeguarding the planet's incredible diversity of life, which is the very foundation of all other ecosystem services and potential benefits for humanity.
