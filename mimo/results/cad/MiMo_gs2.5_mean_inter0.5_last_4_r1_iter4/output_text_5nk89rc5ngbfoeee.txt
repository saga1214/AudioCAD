For your end-of-year celebration, a **park barbecue/picnic** strikes a great balance between fun, inclusivity, and manageable logistics. Here’s how to make it work smoothly:

### 1. **Venue & Setup**  
   - **Choose a Park with Cover**: Pick a spot with picnic tables, a gazebo, or a large tent rental (to avoid rain).  
   - **Parent Help**: Assign 2–3 parents to arrive early to set up chairs, tables, and games. You can handle the main setup yourself.  

### 2. **Food Strategy**  
   - **Potluck-Style Snacks**: Keep it simple with:  
     - **Main**: Pizza (easy cleanup) or BBQ sliders.  
     - **Sides**: Veggie platters, chips, fruit skewers.  
     - **Dessert**: Ice cream sundaes (let girls customize with sprinkles/chocolate).  
   - **Dietary Notes**: Include dairy-free options (vegan cheese, almond milk) since you need to avoid dairy.  
   - **Parent Coordination**: Use a sign-up sheet (Google Forms works well) for parents to bring one item (e.g., "Family A brings napkins, Family B brings drinks").  

### 3. **Activities**  
   - **Low-Key Games**:  
     - Sack races or three-legged races (no heavy equipment).  
     - Treasure hunt for hidden "patches" (decorated cardboard pieces) tied to badges earned this year.  
   - **Memory Book**: Have girls decorate a scrapbook with photos and badges.  

### 4. **Costs & Roles**  
   - **Sashes/Badges**: Clearly communicate upfront that parents cover these. Send a link to Brownie sash prices (often $15–$30 per girl) and badge costs ($5–$10 each).  
   - **Event Dues**: Charge a small fee ($5–$10 per girl) for the celebration costs (food, supplies). Parents can Venmo/Zelle you.  

### 5. **Pregnancy-Friendly Tips**  
   - **Delegate**: Let parents handle heavy lifting (setting up chairs, carrying food).  
   - **Rest**: Stay close to a car for quick escapes if tired.  
   - **Hydration**: Bring water bottles and a cooler for snacks.  

### 6. **Rainy Day Backup**  
   - **School Cafeteria**: If weather fails, pivot to a cozy end-of-year party there with cupcakes, badges, and a "trophy" photo op.  

**Key Takeaway**: Focus on **simple, collaborative planning**. Your troop’s first year is about bonding, not perfection. A park BBQ with parent teamwork, inclusive snacks, and badge-themed games will make it memorable—and you’ll be exhausted but proud afterward! 🎉
