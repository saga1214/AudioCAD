Here's how to tune your strings using either a tuner app or a clip-on tuner:

**For a Clip-On Tuner:**
1.  **Attach it:** Securely clip the tuner onto the headstock of your instrument.
2.  **Pluck a string:** Pluck one string at a time, starting with the lowest (6th) string.
3.  **Listen/Watch:** The tuner will display the string's pitch as a note (e.g., E, A, D) and a visual indicator (like a moving line or arrow).
4.  **Tune Up:** If the note is too low, *gently* tighten the string by turning the tuning peg *upward*. If it's too high, *gently* loosen the string by turning the peg *downward*.
5.  **Check:** Keep plucking the string while adjusting until the tuner shows the correct note and the visual indicator is centered or at the "tune" position. The tuner might also display the string's tension level (like "S" for stable).
6.  **Repeat:** Move to the next string and repeat steps 2-5 for all strings.

**For a Tuner App:**
1.  **Open App:** Launch your chosen tuner app (like GuitarTuna, Fender Tune, or GuitarTuna).
2.  **Select Tuning:** Choose the correct tuning pattern for your instrument (e.g., "Standard Guitar").
3.  **Pluck a String:** Pluck one string at a time.
4.  **Listen:** The app will display a visual representation of the pitch (like a pitchfork or a wave).
5.  **Tune Up:** If the wave is too low, tighten the string slightly. If it's too high, loosen it slightly.
6.  **Check:** Keep plucking while adjusting until the wave clearly centers on the correct note (e.g., E) and stabilizes. You might hear a "tune" sound when it's close.
7.  **Repeat:** Move to the next string and repeat for all strings.

**Key Tips for Tuning:**

*   **Start Low:** Always tune the lowest string (6th) first, then move up to the highest string (1st).
*   **Tune Up, Check:** Don't try to get it perfect in one go. Make a small adjustment, then pluck the string again to check.
*   **Tension Matters:** The tuner/app will tell you if the string tension is too high ("S" on a tuner) or too low ("L"). Loosen slightly if it's too high, or tighten slightly if it's too low. You want it to be stable ("S").
*   **Listen to Your Ear:** While the tuner is great, also practice tuning by ear. Pluck the string, listen to its pitch, and adjust until it sounds right relative to the other strings.
*   **Tuning Stability:** If a string keeps going sharp or flat quickly, the tuner might indicate it's unstable. This can happen with new strings or if the string is slightly loose. Give it a tiny bit more tension or loosen slightly.

By using either method consistently, you'll get your strings in tune!
