First, the old U.S. Soccer rule. In a game between the Red Team and the Blue Team, imagine a Red Defender deliberately controlling a bouncing ball with a touch. Under the old U.S. rule, the Blue attacker who was offside would remain offside. The play would likely still be called.

Now, under the current Law 11, which is used internationally, the situation is different. If that same Red Defender makes a deliberate, controlled play on the ball, it resets the Blue attacker's offside position. That means the Blue attacker is no longer considered offside when they get the ball, even if they were in an offside position when the pass was made.

So, the key difference is this: The old U.S. rule said a defender's touch doesn't necessarily negate an offside. The current international rule says a deliberate defender's touch can completely reset the offside situation for an attacker.
