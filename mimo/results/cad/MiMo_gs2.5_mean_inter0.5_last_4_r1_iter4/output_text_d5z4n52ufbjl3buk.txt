That's a great way to approach it. Here are a few tips for your email to strike that balance between respect and clarity.

First, start by stating your positive goal. For instance, you could say: "I'm writing because I'm very committed to doing well in your class and doing better on the next test. I'd appreciate any feedback on my essay that could help me improve."

Next, be specific about your confusion, but frame it as a question. You could say: "Based on the content I wrote, I'm a bit confused about how you arrived at the 64. Could you point me to specific areas I could have improved on?"

Then, close with another forward-looking statement. Something like: "Thank you for your time. I really value your feedback and am eager to learn from it."

This keeps the whole conversation focused on your growth as a student, rather than challenging his authority.
