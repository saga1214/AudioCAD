That's a really important question. While the ecosystem services and potential for new medicines are crucial, the most fundamental reason is the preservation of unique evolutionary history.

Norman Myers actually came up with the hotspot concept first and foremost to highlight the irreversible loss of unique evolutionary lineages. Each plant and animal in a hotspot represents millions of years of evolution that has occurred nowhere else on Earth. When a habitat is destroyed, that entire genetic story is lost forever.

So, really, the concept is a call to action. It's a way to prevent the permanent, irreversible disappearance of life's history before we even get a chance to understand it.
