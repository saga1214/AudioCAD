I'm so sorry you're going through this—it takes a lot of strength to manage neighborhood stress while keeping kids happy and safe. Here are some practical steps for dealing with difficult people like this:

1. **Document Everything**  
   Keep a detailed log: dates, times, incidents (bike calls/police calls, CPS call), witnesses, and outcomes. This creates a paper trail if you need to escalate.

2. **Set Clear Boundaries**  
   If she contacts you/your family:  
   - Respond neutrally: *"We understand your concerns. If safety is a risk, please notify us directly."*  
   - Avoid arguments—don't engage in debates.  
   - If she harasses kids directly, tell them firmly: *"We’re not discussing this with you. Please contact our parents."*

3. **Involve the Right Authorities**  
   - **Police/HOA:** If you feel safe, file reports about pattern-based harassment (e.g., kids playing). Request incident numbers.  
   - **School Counselors:** If dress-up/CPS stress affects kids emotionally, counselors can help.  
   - **CPS Advocates:** If involved, ask about support resources for families under scrutiny.

4. **Community Solidarity**  
   - **Anonymous Group Chat:** Parents can share experiences without her knowing. Document patterns together.  
   - **HOA/Neighborhood Association:** Request a meeting to address her behavior collectively. Many communities have policies against nuisance harassment.  
   - **Safety Plan:** If she approaches kids, have a code word (e.g., "trick-or-treat!" means "run to me").

5. **Protect Your Mental Space**  
   - Limit news/social media about her.  
   - Lean into positive routines: Halloween prep, dress-up fun, music (like Lemonade! 🍋).  
   - Talk openly with kids: *"Some people are stressed and act out. We’ll handle it together."*

6. **Legal Options (If Necessary)**  
   If safety is compromised:  
   - Consult a lawyer about nuisance laws or restraining orders.  
   - Report to local authorities if she violates stalking/harassment laws.

**Remember:** You can’t control others, but you *can* control your response. Focus on what matters most—your family’s well-being. The school dress-up days, Halloween, and music you mentioned show you’re already creating joy despite the stress. Keep nurturing those bright moments for the kids. 💛
