Of course. Here's a complete breakdown of the standard resume format that you can use as a template. Just follow these sections and you'll be done.

First is the Header. On the left side, centered under your large, bolded name, place a short professional summary that's just two or three sentences. To the right, on the same line down the page, put your contact info like email, phone, city, icons if you want, and any other professional links. The font for contact info is nine point, but your name stays at fourteen.

Next is the Professional Experience section. Start with your most relevant job first, even if it's older. For each job, list the company name, your job title, and the years you worked there. Then, add two or three bullet points that highlight your key achievements using strong action verbs.

After that, the Education section. List your degree, the university, and the graduation year. You can add honors or scholarships directly under the degree where you received them.

Then, you can have a Skills section. List your most relevant skills, like software you're proficient in or languages you speak.

If you want, you can add an optional Certifications or Awards section. Just list the name of the cert or award and the year you received it.

Finally, you can add a brief Volunteer Experience section if it's relevant to the job.

Remember, recruiters spend only a few seconds on each resume, so prioritize clarity and relevance. A clean, traditional layout is always the safest and most effective choice.
