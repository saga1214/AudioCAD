For modern-day art museum security, the **fundamental first line of defense** prioritizes **controlling human access** before threats even enter the building. This is the most critical layer because human vulnerabilities (e.g., deception, distraction) are often exploited in heists. Here’s why and how it works:  

### Core Principle: **Physical Access Control at Every Entry**  
This involves:  
1. **Visible Security Presence**: Guards at entrances to deter intruders and monitor behavior.  
2. **Screening Procedures**: Mandatory bag checks, ID verification, and metal detectors to eliminate weapons, tools, or unauthorized items.  

**Why this is the *first* line**:  
- It prevents threats from reaching internal systems (cameras, sensors, cases) before they can be triggered.  
- The 1990 **Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum Heist** (mentioned earlier) exploited human error—guards let imposters posing as police enter undisputed. This underscores that access control is the primary defense against deception.  

### Modern Enhancements (Still Secondary to Access Control):  
While other systems are vital, they serve as *supporting layers*:  
- **High-Tech Cameras**: Surveillance for deterrence and post-event analysis (a *second* line).  
- **Motion Detectors/Glass Break Sensors**: Alarms triggered *after* a breach occurs (*third* line).  
- **Secure Displays/Mounting Systems**: Physical barriers to protect art *if* an intruder breaches deeper layers.

Art museums must **design their first line of defense to be *uncompromisingly physical***.
