That's a great clarifying question! Let's break it down clearly:

1.  **Primary Bones for Shoulder Rotation:**
    *   **1. Humerus:** Upper arm bone.
    *   **2. Scapula:** Shoulder blade.
    *   **3. Clavicle:** Collarbone.
    *   **These three bones directly articulate and work together:** The head of the humerus (ball) fits into the shallow socket (glenoid fossa) on the scapula (joint). The clavicle connects the scapula to the rest of the skeleton (sternum/ribs), providing stability and anchoring the scapula. This trio enables the wide-ranging movement of the shoulder joint, including rotation (both internal and external).

2.  **The Fourth Component: The Rotator Cuff Muscles:**
    *   While the bones form the *joints* and provide the *articulating surfaces*, the **rotator cuff muscles** are the **soft tissue components** essential for stabilizing the humerus within the socket and enabling smooth, controlled rotation.
    *   **The four muscles/tendons of the rotator cuff are:**
        *   **Supraspinatus:** Starts on the top of the scapula, helps initiate lifting and external rotation.
        *   **Infraspinatus:** Starts on the bottom side of the scapula, primary external rotator.
        *   **Teres Minor:** Starts on the bottom side of the scapula, assists with external rotation and abduction.
        *   **Subscapularis:** Starts on the front of the scapula, primary internal rotator.
    *   **Their Role in Rotation:** These muscles contract in sequence to provide stability during movement and actively produce the rotational motions of the arm. They work *with* the bones to allow rotation.

**Summary:**

*   **Bones working together *at the joint for articulation*: Humerus, Scapula, Clavicle.**
*   **Bones working together *with the muscles for rotation*: Humerus, Scapula, Clavicle.**
*   **The "fourth component" enabling rotation is the Rotator Cuff:** Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres Minor, Subscapularis muscles. They stabilize the humerus in the scapular socket and actively produce rotational movements.

So, the bones form the skeleton and joints, the rotator cuff muscles provide the dynamic stability and active power for rotation. Your friend's soreness could definitely be related to strains or irritation within this muscle group, especially if he's lifting weights.
