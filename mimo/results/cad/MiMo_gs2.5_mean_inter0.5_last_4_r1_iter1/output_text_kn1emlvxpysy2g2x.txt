First, for High-End Pinot Noir: It should be stored at cellar temperature, which is 55 degrees Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. The serving temperature is slightly below room temp, so aim for 55 to 60 degrees Fahrenheit or 13 to 16 Celsius.

Next, Japanese Hon-Mirin: Store it at a cool place, around 60 to 68 Fahrenheit or 16 to 20 Celsius. If you sip it directly, room temp is best, which is about 20 to 24 degrees Celsius or 68 to 75 Fahrenheit.

Then there's Italian Chianti: For storage, it's the same as the Pinot Noir, at 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. You serve it a bit below room temp, around 60 to 65 Fahrenheit or 16 to 18 Celsius.

For Italian Pinot Grigio: You store this one a bit colder, from 50 to 55 Fahrenheit or 10 to 13 Celsius. You serve it chilled, at 45 to 50 Fahrenheit or 7 to 10 Celsius.

Finally, for the Hot Weather Mulled Wine: You store the wine itself at normal cellar temps, around 55 Fahrenheit or 13 Celsius. But when it's served, it should be warm, between 140 and 160 degrees Fahrenheit, or 60 to 70 Celsius.

So, to answer your question about the best wine for hot weather, it’s definitely the Pinot Grigio, since it’s served chilled. And for cold weather, the Pinot Noir or Chianti would be great served at a slightly warmer temperature.
