### Treat Recommendations:
* **Best flavors:** Chicken (your puppy loves chicken!), beef, salmon, or cheese are usually top picks. Avoid spicy or sugary human snacks.
* **Texture matters:** Small, soft, and easy to swallow. Options include:
  * Small kibble pieces (from his own food)
  * Soft boiled eggs
  * Cooked chicken or turkey
  * Freeze-dried liver or chicken (great for high-value rewards)
  * Commercial training treats (look for "small" size)
* **Quantity:** Use tiny pieces for quick rewards during training. A few pieces per session is enough.

### Clicker Training Explained:
Clicker training uses a "marker" (the clicker) and a reward to communicate precisely what behavior you want.

1.  **The Tools:** You need a clicker and high-value treats.
2.  **The Process:**
    * **Click:** As soon as your puppy *shows* the behavior you want (e.g., starts sitting), you click.
    * **Treat:** Immediately after the click, give a treat. The click *marks* the exact moment the behavior happened, and the treat is the reward for performing it correctly.
    * **Repeat:** Practice this many times. Click *only* when the behavior happens. The puppy learns that *click* + *treat* = good thing happens when I do X.
3.  **How it works:**
    * The clicker acts as a bridge, linking the behavior to the reward.
    * It provides clear communication: "That's the exact moment I want you to repeat!"
    * It helps build confidence and makes learning faster and more reliable.
    * **Example for "Sit":** Puppy looks up. You click. Give treat. Puppy sits. You click. Give treat. Repeat. Soon, he'll sit *on command* when he hears the click.

### Key to Clicker Training:
* **Timing is CRITICAL:** Click *immediately* after the behavior you want.
* **Be Consistent:** Use the clicker the same way every time.
* **Fade the Clicker:** Once the behavior is reliable, you can stop clicking and use just the verbal cue ("Sit!") or just the treat as a reward. The clicker was just a training tool.

**Combining this with your training plan:** Use clicker training for precise commands like "Sit," "Stay," and "Come." Pair it with consistent leash walks, crate training (as a safe space, not punishment), and regular training classes. You've got this!
