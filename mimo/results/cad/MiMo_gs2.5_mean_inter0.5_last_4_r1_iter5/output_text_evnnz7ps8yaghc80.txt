A weekly schedule is a fantastic idea to build consistency. Here’s a balanced plan you can follow for about 30 to 45 minutes each day.

On Monday, you could start with a five-minute warm-up, tossing two beanbags. Then, spend about fifteen minutes practicing adding the third ball from your other hand. Finally, use the last ten minutes to practice with scarves to work on your rhythm.

On Tuesday, you can focus on your dominant one-hand two-ball juggling, doing that warm-up for ten minutes. Then, spend fifteen minutes practicing just the first three throws we talked about earlier. Finish with five minutes of scarf work.

Wednesday is a good day for active recovery. You can do some light stretching and then spend ten minutes just watching juggling videos for inspiration. Then, spend twenty minutes doing some gentle one-ball tosses in each hand.

On Thursday, you can try juggling tennis balls. Spend fifteen minutes tossing two in one hand. Then, for another fifteen minutes, practice adding the third ball from your other hand. Finish with ten minutes of scarf juggling.

Friday is all about fun and music. Put on a favorite song and juggle along, focusing on keeping a steady beat. Spend about twenty minutes on this, and then do five minutes of relaxed one-hand beanbag juggling.

Saturday is for a challenge. Try to get through one full three-ball cascade without dropping any balls. If you drop one, just pause and reset. Then, spend ten minutes practicing with scarves to refine your timing.

Finally, Sunday is for rest. No structured practice, but you can spend about fifteen minutes just watching juggling tutorials or videos for some light mental rehearsal.

Just remember to listen to your body, take breaks, and celebrate the small wins. You've promised your son, and you will figure this out.
