### 1. **Dysgraphia & Letter Transposition**  
The phenomenon you describe—inserting letters from the next word into the current word—is often called **letter transposition** or a **dysgraphia-related error**. It’s common in individuals with dyslexia or dysgraphia, where working memory or sequencing skills during writing interfere with accuracy. It’s not a spelling error (you know the words), but a motor-planning challenge. If it persists, a formal evaluation could clarify if it’s linked to dysgraphia.

### 2. **Right-Brain/Left-Brain Dichotomy**  
This is largely a **myth**. While the brain has specialized regions (e.g., left hemisphere for language, right for spatial reasoning), creativity and logic aren’t strictly "left" vs. "right." Most complex tasks involve *both* hemispheres working together. For example:  
- **Creativity** requires logic (e.g., brainstorming within constraints).  
- **Logic** can be abstract (e.g., mathematical proofs).  
**Bottom line:** You likely have a *balanced* cognitive profile—creative and analytical—without needing to fit into rigid categories.

### 4. **Tinnitus (Ear Buzzy)**  
- **Physiological causes** are most common: noise exposure, aging, earwax, or jaw issues.  
- **Psychological factors** (e.g., stress) can worsen symptoms but rarely cause it.  
- **Action**: Rule out hearing loss with an audiologist. If persistent, consult an ENT.
