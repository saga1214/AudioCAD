For modern-day art museum security, **controlling access at every entry point remains the definitive first line of defense**. This foundational layer focuses on **preventing unauthorized entry before threats can even enter the building**, combining visible security presence and screening (e.g., bag checks, ID verification) to manage exactly who and what gains entry.  

While high-tech systems like surveillance cameras or internal sensors are critical, they operate as *secondary* layers to detect or respond to breaches *after* entry. The 1990 Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum heist underscores this: the thieves exploited human error (guards bypassing protocols to let imposters in) rather than bypassing physical barriers. Thus, the first line of defense prioritizes **prevention over reaction**—stopping threats at the threshold.  

### Key Elements of the First Line:
1. **Visible Security Presence**: Deters potential threats and establishes authority.  
2. **Screening Protocols**: Bag checks, metal detectors, and staff training to assess risks.  
3. **Controlled Access Points**: Keycard entry for staff-only areas complements public screening.  

This approach aligns with modern security frameworks (e.g., ISO 27001) that emphasize proactive perimeter security as the initial line of defense against external threats.
