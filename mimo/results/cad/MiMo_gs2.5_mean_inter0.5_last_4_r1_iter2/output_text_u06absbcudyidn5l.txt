Okay, let's break this down. Chronic skin redness, or erythema, can come from many places. Rosacea is a big one, sun damage over a lifetime adds up, genetics plays a part, and other autoimmune conditions can be a factor.

Now, vasculitis is another possibility. It's basically inflammation of your blood vessels. This can happen at different levels—large, medium, or small vessels—which is why the symptoms can be all over the map. Common signs are feeling generally unwell, with fever and fatigue, and you might see skin rashes or purple spots. To get a definite answer, a doctor usually needs to do a biopsy.

The diagnosis often involves a physical check-up, blood tests, and imaging. Sometimes, a biopsy is needed to confirm it. Vasculitis can show up at any age. The main danger is that the inflammation can narrow the vessels, which can lead to serious problems like organ damage because blood can't flow properly.

So, in short, your lifelong skin redness could be from a number of things, and vasculitis is one of them. Getting a proper diagnosis from a specialist is the most important step.
