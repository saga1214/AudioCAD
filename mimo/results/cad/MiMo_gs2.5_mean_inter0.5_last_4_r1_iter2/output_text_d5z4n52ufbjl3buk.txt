That's a great way to approach it. You can craft an email that's clear, respectful, and focused on improvement. Here are a few pointers to include.

First, start with your purpose. You could write something like: "I scored a 64 on my latest exam and wanted to understand what I could improve on in my essay to do better in the future."

Next, frame it as a desire to learn. Add a sentence such as: "I really value your feedback and would appreciate any guidance on areas where I could strengthen my responses."

Then, to be specific without being confrontational, you could ask: "Could you share what factors most influenced the grade? For example, were there specific content points or presentation aspects that I could work on?"

Finally, end with a clear call to action, like: "If you have 10 to 15 minutes sometime this week, I'd be grateful for the opportunity to learn from your feedback."

This approach keeps the conversation professional and focused on your growth, which gives you the best chance for a productive discussion.
