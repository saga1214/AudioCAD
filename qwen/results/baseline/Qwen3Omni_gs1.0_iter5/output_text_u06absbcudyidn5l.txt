**First, what is vasculitis?**  
Vasculitis is a group of disorders characterized by inflammation of the blood vessels. This inflammation can narrow or block blood flow, which can damage tissues and organs. It's often an autoimmune condition, meaning the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own blood vessels.

---

**Common symptoms of vasculitis include:**

- **Skin-related signs:**  
  - Red or purple spots (purpura), especially on the legs  
  - Rashes or sores  
  - Skin ulcers  
  - Easy bruising  
  - Redness or warmth in certain areas  

- **Systemic symptoms (whole-body):**  
  - Fever  
  - Fatigue  
  - Weight loss  
  - Muscle and joint pain  
  - General malaise  

- **Organ-specific symptoms (depending on which vessels are affected):**  
  - Kidney problems (e.g., blood in urine, high blood pressure)  
  - Lung issues (e.g., cough, shortness of breath)  
  - Nerve problems (e.g., numbness, tingling, weakness)  
  - Abdominal pain or gastrointestinal bleeding  

---

**Other potential causes of chronic redness and skin changes (besides vasculitis):**

1. **Rosacea** – Commonly causes facial redness, flushing, and sometimes bumps or pimples. Often triggered by heat, stress, or alcohol.

2. **Lupus (SLE)** – An autoimmune disease that can cause a butterfly-shaped rash across the cheeks and nose, along with fatigue, joint pain, and other systemic symptoms.

3. **Contact dermatitis** – Skin irritation from allergens or irritants (e.g., soaps, metals, fragrances).

4. **Eczema (atopic dermatitis)** – Causes dry, red, itchy skin, often in creases like elbows or knees.

5. **Chronic sun damage** – Long-term UV exposure can lead to persistent redness, thinning skin, and visible blood vessels (telangiectasia).

6. **Perioral dermatitis** – Small red bumps around the mouth, often mistaken for acne.

7. **Scleroderma** – A connective tissue disease that can cause skin thickening and redness, especially on hands and face.

8. **Infections** – Certain bacterial, fungal, or viral infections can cause redness and inflammation.

---

**Important Note:**  
While vasculitis can cause redness and skin changes, it's usually accompanied by other systemic symptoms like fever, fatigue, or organ involvement. If you're experiencing persistent redness, especially with other symptoms, it's essential to see a doctor—ideally a dermatologist or rheumatologist—for proper evaluation.

They may recommend blood tests (like ANA, ESR, CRP), imaging, or even a skin biopsy to determine the exact cause.

In the meantime, avoid known skin irritants, use gentle skincare, and protect your skin from sun exposure. But again, a medical evaluation is key to getting the right diagnosis and treatment.
