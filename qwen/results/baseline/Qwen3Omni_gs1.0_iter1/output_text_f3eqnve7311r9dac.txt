You're absolutely right—comics from the 1940s and 1950s are generally much rarer and more valuable than modern reprints or variant editions. Back then, comics were often sold as newsstand items, and many were thrown away or recycled. That scarcity, combined with historical significance, makes them prime candidates for long-term investment.

Here are 10 rare and valuable Golden and Silver Age comics from the 1940s and 1950s that are considered key issues and are still attainable under $500 for a decent copy (though prices vary widely based on condition):

1. **Action Comics #1 (1938)** – Superman’s debut. The most famous comic in history. Even low-grade copies sell for hundreds of thousands, but you can find a very worn copy in the $500–$1,000 range if it's a lower-grade CGC 0.5 or 1.0.

2. **Detective Comics #27 (1939)** – Batman’s first appearance. Like Action Comics #1, it's a cornerstone of comic history. A low-grade copy can be found under $500, but condition is everything.

3. **Marvel Comics #1 (1939)** – The first comic from what would become Marvel. Features the Human Torch and the Sub-Mariner. A low-grade copy can be found under $500.

4. **All Select Comics #1 (1943)** – The first appearance of the original Human Torch (android version). A key Golden Age title.

5. **Journey into Mystery #83 (1962)** – While technically early Silver Age, this is the first appearance of Thor. It's a great entry point into the Marvel universe and can be found under $500 in lower grades.

6. **Strange Tales #110 (1963)** – The first appearance of the Silver Surfer. A must-have for Marvel collectors.

7. **Tales of Suspense #39 (1963)** – The first appearance of Iron Man. A key Marvel Silver Age issue.
