Let’s break this down based on your earlier conditions:

You're investing **$10,000** in a stock priced at **$62.87 per share**.

### Step 1: How many shares can you buy?

$$
\frac{10,000}{62.87} \approx 159.13 \text{ shares}
$$

Since you can’t buy fractions of a share, you’d buy **159 shares**.

$$
\text{Total cost} = 159 \times 62.87 = 9,996.33
$$

So you’d have **$3.67 left over**.

---

### Step 2: Target prices based on 10% gain and 10% loss

- **10% gain target price**:  
  $$
  62.87 \times 1.10 = 69.157 \approx \$69.16
  $$

- **10% loss stop-loss price**:  
  $$
  62.87 \times 0.90 = 56.583 \approx \$56.58
  $$

---

### Step 3: Total gain or loss at those prices

#### ✅ If the stock hits **$69.16** (10% gain):

$$
\text{Total value} = 159 \times 69.16 = 11,000.44
$$

$$
\text{Gain} = 11,000.44 - 9,996.33 = \$1,004.11
$$

So you’d make about **$1,004.11**, or **10.04% return** on your $10,000.

#### ❌ If the stock drops to **$56.58** (10% loss):

$$
\text{Total value} = 159 \times 56.58 = 8,996.22
$$

$$
\text{Loss} = 9,996.33 - 8,996.22 = \$1,000.11
$$

So you’d lose about **$1,000.11**, or **10% loss** on your $10,000.
