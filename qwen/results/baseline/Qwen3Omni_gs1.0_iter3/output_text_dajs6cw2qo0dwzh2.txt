That's a really thoughtful question. The short answer is: probably not.

While the Portuguese were already exploring the African coast, their efforts were initially limited in scope and ambition. The fall of Constantinople in 1453 acted as a powerful catalyst. It made the risks of relying on overland or Ottoman-controlled routes much clearer and more urgent.

So, while the *technological* and *navigational* conditions were in place, the *political and economic* urgency to find a sea route to Asia was greatly intensified by the Ottoman conquest. It's likely that the Age of Discovery would have still happened, but it might have been delayed or taken a different path without that catalyst.
