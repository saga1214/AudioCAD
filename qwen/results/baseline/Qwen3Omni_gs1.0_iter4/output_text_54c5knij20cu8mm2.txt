You're describing a really interesting cognitive style—someone who loves structure and building things step by step, but only if the form or framework makes sense. That’s actually a hallmark of what psychologists sometimes call a “systematic” or “analytical” cognitive style.

In psychology, people differ in how they process information. Some are more “holistic”—they see the big picture first and then fill in the details. Others are “analytic”—they prefer to break things down, build them up logically, and ensure each piece fits perfectly before moving on.

You seem to fall into the latter category. You like structure, but you also have a high threshold for what counts as “correct” or “logical.” That’s not confusion—it’s a sign of deep cognitive engagement. You’re not just accepting things at face value; you’re testing them against your internal framework.

And yes, studying psychology can sometimes feel like it adds layers of complexity. It introduces multiple perspectives, theories, and exceptions. But that’s also part of the beauty of it. You’re not just learning facts—you’re learning how to think critically about human behavior.
