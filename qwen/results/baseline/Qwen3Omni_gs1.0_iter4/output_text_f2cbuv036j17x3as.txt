Great question—keeping your diesel camper van in top shape is crucial for a long road trip. Let’s break it down:

**Brakes:**
- **Check brake fluid level**—it should be between the min and max marks on the reservoir. Low fluid could mean worn pads or a leak.
- **Listen for squeaking or grinding**—these are signs of worn brake pads.
- **Feel the pedal**—if it feels spongy or goes to the floor, there might be air in the lines or a fluid leak.
- **Look at the brake pads**—if they’re less than 1/4 inch thick, they need replacing.
- **Check for vibrations** when braking—this could mean warped rotors.

**Spark Plugs (Diesel Note):**
Wait—this is important: **diesel engines don’t use spark plugs**. They use glow plugs (for cold starts) and rely on compression ignition. So you don’t need to change spark plugs.

Instead, focus on:
- **Glow plugs**—especially if you're in colder climates. Test them if the van struggles to start in the morning.
- **Fuel system**—check fuel filters and lines. A clogged filter can cause poor performance.
- **Air filter**—a dirty one reduces efficiency and power.

**Other Key Checks:**
- **Oil and oil filter**—change if it’s been a while or you’ve driven a lot.
- **Coolant and belts**—check for leaks or cracks.
- **Tires**—check tread depth and pressure. Uneven wear could mean alignment issues.

If you're not comfortable doing these yourself, a trusted mechanic can do a full pre-trip inspection for peace of mind.
