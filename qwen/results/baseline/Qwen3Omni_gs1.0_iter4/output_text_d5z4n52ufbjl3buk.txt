That's a great approach—being respectful while still seeking clarity.

Here are some pointers for your email:

1. **Start with appreciation**: Acknowledge that you value his time and expertise. For example:  
   *"Thank you for your time and for teaching this course. I appreciate the opportunity to learn from you."*

2. **Mention your concern**: Be specific but calm. You can say:  
   *"I recently received my grade on the most recent exam, and I was surprised by the score, especially given my performance on other assessments. I'm writing to better understand the feedback so I can improve."*

3. **Focus on learning**: Frame it as a desire to grow, not a challenge to the grade. For example:  
   *"Since this exam was essay-based, I'm curious about how the grading was applied. Was there a rubric or specific criteria I should have followed more closely?"*

4. **Ask for clarification**: Be direct but polite. You might say:  
   *"Could you please help me understand what areas I should focus on to improve my performance in future assignments?"*

5. **Suggest a meeting**: Offer flexibility:  
   *"I know your office hours are in the morning, and I'm happy to meet at a time that works for you."*

6. **Close respectfully**:  
   *"Thank you again for your time and guidance. I'm committed to doing my best in this course and appreciate any feedback you can provide."*

Keep it concise—this isn't a complaint, it's a request for help.
