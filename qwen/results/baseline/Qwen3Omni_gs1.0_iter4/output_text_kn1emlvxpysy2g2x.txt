Here are the wines we’ve discussed, along with their ideal storage and serving temperatures in both Fahrenheit and Celsius:

| Wine | Storage Temp | Serving Temp | Notes |
|------|--------------|--------------|-------|
| **Pinot Noir** | 55°F (13°C) | 55–60°F (13–16°C) | Light-bodied red, best served cool but not chilled. |
| **Hon-Mirin** | 60–68°F (16–20°C) | 68–75°F (20–24°C) | Sweet rice wine, often served at room temp. |
| **Chianti** | 55°F (13°C) | 60–65°F (16–18°C) | Medium-bodied Italian red, best slightly below room temp. |
| **Pinot Grigio** | 50–55°F (10–13°C) | 45–50°F (7–10°C) | Crisp white, served well chilled. |
| **Champagne / Sparkling Wine** | 45–50°F (7–10°C) | 45–50°F (7–10°C) | Best served cold to preserve bubbles. |

Now, to answer your questions:

**Best for hot weather:**
- **Pinot Grigio** and **Champagne** are your top picks. They’re crisp, refreshing, and served cold—perfect for beating the heat.
- **Chianti** is also a good choice if you're serving it slightly chilled, but avoid serving it too cold, as it can mute its flavors.

**Best for cold weather:**
- **Pinot Noir** and **Chianti** are excellent choices. They’re red wines with enough body and warmth to complement cooler temperatures.
- **Hon-Mirin** is also great in cold weather, especially if you're using it in warm dishes like stews or hot pots.

And if you're thinking of serving wine warm—like mulled wine—then you’d use a red like a **Barbera** or **Zinfandel**, stored at cellar temp (55°F), then gently warmed to 140–160°F (60–70°C) for serving.

So, in summary:
- **Hot weather?** Go for **Pinot Grigio** or **Champagne**—serve cold.
- **Cold weather?** Choose **Pinot Noir**, **Chianti**, or **Hon-Mirin**—serve slightly cooler or at room temp.
