The most important aspect is that these hotspots represent the last, best chance to preserve the planet's evolutionary heritage.

They're not just collections of rare species; they're living laboratories of evolution that have developed over millions of years. Once they're lost, that unique genetic and ecological information is gone forever.

From a practical standpoint, they're also our most strategic conservation priority. By protecting these relatively small areas, we can save a disproportionately large number of species and maintain the ecosystem services that support human well-being.

In essence, they're like the planet's most valuable natural assets. Protecting them is about preserving both the intrinsic value of life on Earth and the practical benefits that humanity depends on.
