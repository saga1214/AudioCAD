You're asking the right questions, and I want to be honest and reassuring.

First, yes, PFAS are called "forever chemicals" because they don't break down easily in the environment or in your body. But that doesn't mean you're powerless.

There's no proven way to "detox" or "flush" them out with juice cleanses or fasting. Your body naturally gets rid of them over time, but it can take years. The best thing you can do is reduce your ongoing exposure.

So here's a clear list of things to avoid:

1. **Non-stick cookware** – especially older Teflon pans. Switch to stainless steel, cast iron, or ceramic.

2. **Grease-resistant food packaging** – like fast food wrappers, pizza boxes, microwave popcorn bags, and some takeout containers.

3. **Stain- or water-resistant products** – including carpets, furniture, clothing, and outdoor gear. Look for PFAS-free labels.

4. **Certain cosmetics and personal care products** – especially some makeup, nail polish, and dental floss. Check for PFAS-free claims.

5. **Tap water** – especially if you're unsure of your local water quality. Use a certified water filter (look for ones certified to remove PFAS).

And yes, it's okay to be concerned, especially with young children. But focusing on these practical steps can make a real difference.
