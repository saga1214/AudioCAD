That’s a brilliant multi-use idea—using frozen water bottles to both cool the fog and serve as drinks later! You’ll save space and reduce waste. As for candy, here are some great options that work well for Halloween parties:

1. **Classic Halloween Candy**: M&Ms, Reese’s Cups, Snickers, Twix, and Skittles are always crowd-pleasers. You can buy them in bulk and put them in clear bowls or treat bags.

2. **Allergy-Friendly Options**: Since you mentioned some guests are allergic to artificial dyes, look for brands like **Enjoy Life** or **Hershey’s Special Dark** (which is often dye-free). Also, consider **fruit snacks**, **pretzels**, or **nuts** (if no allergies).

3. **Themed Treats**: Try **"Witch’s Fingers"** (pretzel rods dipped in white chocolate), **"Eyeball"** gummy candies, or **"Pumpkin"** shaped lollipops. These are fun and festive.

4. **DIY Candy Bar**: Set up a station with different candies, small bags, and fun labels like “Boo-tiful Treats” or “Spooky Snacks.” Guests can mix and match.

5. **Non-Candy Options**: For variety, include **chips**, **pretzels**, **trail mix**, or **mini cookies**. It’s always good to have non-sugar options.

6. **Candy Corn**: It’s a Halloween staple—just make sure it’s not too much for people who hate it!

You can also label the candy bowls with fun signs like “Trick or Treat” or “Spooky Snacks” to keep the vibe going.
