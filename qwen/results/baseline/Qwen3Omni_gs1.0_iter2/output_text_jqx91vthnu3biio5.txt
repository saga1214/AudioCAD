Great questions—let’s break them down.

**Color-coding and mental load:**  
You're right on the money. It’s not just about memory—it’s about reducing cognitive load. When your brain sees a consistent color for a specific project or task, it can quickly categorize and recall what’s what without having to think as hard. It’s like having a visual shortcut. For example, if all your work-related files are blue, your brain automatically knows, “This is work stuff.” That frees up mental space for actual thinking instead of decoding what each folder is. So yes, it helps with both memory *and* task switching.

**Switching between lo-fi and ambient sounds:**  
There’s actually some science behind this. Lo-fi tends to be great for focus because it’s predictable and non-intrusive—your brain can tune into it without getting distracted. Ambient nature sounds, like rain or forest sounds, can help with relaxation and creativity. The key is consistency. If you switch too often, your brain might start treating the sound as a new stimulus instead of background noise. So if you're using both, try pairing them with your tasks: lo-fi for deep work, ambient for brainstorming or winding down. Or just pick one that works best for you and stick with it—personal preference really does play a big role.

**Physical documents:**  
Tackling them on a weekend or a low-pressure day is smart. It gives you the mental space to be methodical without the pressure of the workday. You could even set a timer—say, 30 minutes to sort, then a 10-minute break. That way, it doesn’t feel overwhelming. And once you’re done, you’ll probably feel a real sense of accomplishment and clarity.

Bottom line: small, intentional changes like these add up. You don’t need to overhaul everything at once—just find what works for *you* and build from there.
