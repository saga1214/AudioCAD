1. **Gather your tools**: You said you only have a simple toolbox, but make sure you have a drain snake (also called a plumber's auger) or a flexible drain auger. If you don't have one, you can use a wire coat hanger bent into a hook shape as a temporary tool.

2. **Remove the P-trap**: This is the U-shaped pipe under the sink. Place a bucket underneath it to catch any water. Loosen the slip nuts on both ends of the P-trap with a wrench or pliers. Once loose, carefully remove the P-trap and check for any visible blockages. Clean it out thoroughly.

3. **Use the drain snake**: If the P-trap is clear, insert the drain snake into the drain opening. Turn the handle slowly to push the snake deeper into the pipe. When you feel resistance, keep turning and pushing gently. The snake will break up or hook onto the blockage. Once you feel it give, pull the snake back slowly.

4. **Flush the pipe**: After removing the blockage, run hot water through the drain to flush out any remaining debris.

5. **Reassemble and test**: Put the P-trap back in place, tighten the slip nuts, and turn on the water to test the flow.
