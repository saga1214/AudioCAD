That’s a really common challenge—when someone has a successful career and a strong sense of independence, they often don’t like the idea of involving lawyers, especially for personal matters like estate planning. But here’s the good news: you don’t have to present it as “we need to hire a lawyer.” You can frame it as “we want to make sure everything runs smoothly for you and the family, so we’re just going to have a quick chat with someone who knows how to handle these things.”

Since he already works with an attorney for his land deals, that’s a great starting point. You could say something like, “Hey, we know you work with [attorney’s name] for your business stuff. Would it be okay if we asked them to help us look at the estate side too? We just want to make sure everything’s set up the way you’d want it, and we know they’re familiar with your situation.”

That way, it’s not a new, scary lawyer—it’s someone he already trusts, just doing a different part of the job. And if he’s still hesitant, you could suggest a “family meeting” with the attorney, where it’s not just about him, but about making things easier for everyone involved.

The key is to keep it low-pressure and focused on his goals—like protecting his legacy, making sure his family isn’t burdened later, and keeping things simple for everyone.
