Here’s what to do:

**Step 1: Check accessibility**  
Look under the sink first. Can you see the P-trap (the U-shaped pipe)? If yes, that’s your best bet for a quick fix.

**Step 2: Place a bucket underneath**  
Put a bucket or container under the P-trap. This catches any water or debris when you remove it.

**Step 3: Loosen the slip nuts**  
Use your adjustable wrench or pliers to loosen the slip nuts (the ring-shaped connectors) on both ends of the P-trap. Turn them counterclockwise.

**Step 4: Remove the P-trap**  
Once loose, carefully slide the P-trap off. Be ready for water or gunk to come out—don’t panic, it’s normal.

**Step 5: Clean it out**  
Hold the P-trap over the bucket and flush it with water. Use your simple toolbox’s small brush or even a wire hanger (if you have one) to clear any debris stuck inside.

**Step 6: Reassemble**  
Once clean, slide the P-trap back into place and tighten the slip nuts by hand first, then give them a gentle snug with your tool—don’t overtighten or you could crack the pipe.

**Step 7: Test it**  
Turn on the water and see if it drains properly. If it still blocks, the clog might be further down the line.

If that doesn’t work, you might need to remove the sink drain flange (the metal ring under the sink) or check the cleanout plug on the main drain line (if accessible).

But for now, starting with the P-trap is your best move with a simple toolbox.
