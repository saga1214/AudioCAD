That’s a really important detail. If he already has an attorney who handles his land and business dealings, that’s a great starting point. You don’t need to bring in a completely new person—just make sure that attorney is the right kind of estate planner. Some real estate attorneys are great at property deals but not necessarily experts in estate tax planning or trusts.

So, here’s what I’d suggest: talk to your father-in-law and say something like, “We know you’ve got someone you trust for your business and land stuff, and we’d love to make sure that person can help with everything else—like making sure your wishes are clear and your family isn’t stuck with a mess later.” That way, it feels like you’re building on what he already trusts, not replacing it.

If he’s resistant to the idea of “estate planning,” you could frame it as “making sure your business and everything you’ve built can keep going smoothly for your family.” That’s usually a better sell than “we need to plan for taxes.”

And if he absolutely hates lawyers, maybe start with a financial advisor or CPA who can help you understand the numbers and then bring in the estate attorney as a next step. Sometimes people are more open to a financial professional first.

Bottom line: you want someone who knows both estate law and how to work with strong-willed, successful people like your father-in-law.
