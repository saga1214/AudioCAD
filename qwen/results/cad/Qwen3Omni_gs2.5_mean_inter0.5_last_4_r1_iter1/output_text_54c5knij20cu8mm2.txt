It sounds like you're experiencing what many psychology students go through—deep engagement with complex ideas can sometimes blur the lines between learning and confusion. That’s completely normal, especially when you're diving into abstract concepts and trying to connect them to your own experiences.

You mentioned liking structure and building things step by step. That’s a great cognitive style. It suggests you might be more of a **systematic thinker**—someone who values order, logic, and clear progression. In psychology, this kind of thinking often aligns with what’s called **sequential processing**, where information is taken in step by step, rather than all at once.

But when you're exposed to new, sometimes conflicting theories or overlapping ideas (like different types of dyslexia or phobias), it can feel overwhelming. That’s because your brain is trying to integrate a lot of new information into existing mental frameworks. It’s not that you’re confused—it’s that you’re actively processing and organizing.

To help manage this, try using **structured note-taking methods** like concept maps or outlines. Start with a broad idea (e.g., “phobias”) and branch out into subtypes. This way, you’re building on what you know, just like you like to do.

And remember: confusion isn’t a sign of failure. It’s a sign that you’re thinking deeply. Keep going—you’re doing great.
