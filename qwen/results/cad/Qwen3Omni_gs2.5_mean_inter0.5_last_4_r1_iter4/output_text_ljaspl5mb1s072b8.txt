Great question! Let's break it down with the timeline:

- **Siad Barre** took power in **1969** after a military coup.
- He ruled Somalia as a dictator until he was overthrown in **January 1991**.
- After that, the country descended into civil war with no central government.
- The **U.S. and UN intervened** in **December 1992** with Operation Restore Hope to deliver humanitarian aid.
- The infamous **Battle of Mogadishu**, shown in *Black Hawk Down*, happened on **October 3–4, 1993**.
- After that battle, the U.S. began withdrawing its forces.
- The **U.S. officially completed its withdrawal from Somalia in March 1994**.

So, from when Siad Barre first took power in **1969** to when the U.S. left Somalia in **1994**, about **25 years** passed.

But if you're asking how long it was between the **start of the civil war** (after Barre's ousting in **1991**) and the **U.S. withdrawal** (1994), that’s only about **three years**.
