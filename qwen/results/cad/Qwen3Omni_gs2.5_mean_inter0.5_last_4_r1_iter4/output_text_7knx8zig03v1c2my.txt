For treats, small, soft ones are best for tiny puppies like yours. Look for high-quality options like Zuke’s Mini Naturals, Greenies Puppy Bites, or even small pieces of cooked chicken or turkey (if he likes chicken!). Avoid anything too hard or large that could be a choking hazard.

Chicken, beef, or liver flavors are usually a hit with puppies, but since he likes chicken but not beef, stick with chicken-based treats.

Now, clicker training is a method that uses a small device that makes a “click” sound to mark the exact moment your dog does something right. Here’s how it works:

1. First, you “charge” the clicker by clicking it and giving a treat—repeat this a few times so your dog learns that the click means “reward.”
2. When your dog does something you want (like sitting), click the moment he does it.
3. Immediately give a treat.

The click is a clear signal that he did the right thing, helping him learn faster. It’s especially useful for tiny puppies because it’s precise and doesn’t rely on voice tone, which can be confusing.
