My apologies for the confusion earlier. Let me clarify:

The **classic Boulevardier** is traditionally made with **one and a quarter ounces of whiskey**, **one ounce of sweet vermouth**, and **one ounce of Campari**. That’s the ratio most bartenders cite as the original.

Your **pro bartender friend’s equal parts version**—one ounce of each—is a popular modern variation that balances the bitterness of Campari with the sweetness of vermouth more evenly.

As for the **Last Word**, it’s a completely different cocktail. It’s made with **equal parts** of:
- 3/4 oz gin  
- 3/4 oz green Chartreuse  
- 3/4 oz maraschino liqueur  
- 3/4 oz fresh lime juice  

Shake with ice and strain into a chilled cocktail glass. It’s bright, herbal, and refreshing—very different from the bold, bitter Boulevardier.

So to recap:
- **Boulevardier**: Whiskey (1.25 oz), sweet vermouth (1 oz), Campari (1 oz)
- **Your friend’s version**: Whiskey (1 oz), sweet vermouth (1 oz), Campari (1 oz)
- **Last Word**: Gin (0.75 oz), green Chartreuse (0.75 oz), maraschino (0.75 oz), lime juice (0.75 oz)

Hope that clears it up!
