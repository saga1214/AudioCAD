I'm so sorry you and your neighborhood are dealing with such a stressful situation. It's completely understandable that it's taking a toll on you and the kids. Here’s some advice for coping with a difficult person like the lady you're describing:

1. **Document Everything**: If she calls CPS or the police unnecessarily, consider keeping a record of what happened, when, and who was involved. This can be helpful if it escalates or if you need to speak with local authorities or community leaders.

2. **Stay Calm and Avoid Confrontation**: Engaging directly with her might make things worse. It's best to avoid interactions and keep your kids' activities within sight or in safe, supervised areas.

3. **Talk to Your Neighbors**: You're not alone. Chances are, other families are dealing with the same issue. Consider organizing a quiet conversation with trusted neighbors to share concerns and possibly approach local authorities or HOA together.

4. **Involve Authorities or Community Leaders**: If CPS calls are becoming a pattern, consider speaking with your local police non-emergency line or a community liaison. They may be able to offer guidance or intervene if there's a pattern of misuse.

5. **Create Safe Alternatives for the Kids**: If they can't play outside freely, try organizing bike rides or playdates in private spaces like backyards, parks during less busy times, or with trusted neighbors.

6. **Take Care of Yourself**: Stress like this takes a toll. Try to carve out small moments for self-care—whether it's listening to music, a short walk, or just a few minutes of quiet. Halloween dress-up days are a great distraction, so keep leaning into those joyful moments.

7. **Know Your Rights**: In many areas, calling the police or CPS without legitimate cause can be considered misuse. If you feel safe doing so, you may want to contact your local community board or even a legal aid organization for advice.

Dealing with someone who disrupts the peace of a neighborhood is exhausting, but you're doing your best by staying aware and keeping your family safe. Hang in there—you're not alone, and it's okay to seek support when you need it.
