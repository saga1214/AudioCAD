That's a great approach—respectful, focused, and solution-oriented.

Here are a few pointers for your email:

1. **Subject Line**: Keep it clear and professional. For example:  
   *"Request to Discuss Essay Exam Grade and Feedback"*

2. **Opening**: Start with a polite greeting and mention your desire to improve.  
   > "Dear Professor [Last Name], I hope this message finds you well. I recently received my grade on the most recent essay-style exam and would appreciate the opportunity to discuss my performance to better understand how I can improve in future assignments."

3. **Mention Your Other Performance (Optional but Helpful)**:  
   > "I want to mention that my grades in previous assessments have been strong, which makes this result particularly surprising to me. I'm eager to learn how I could have met the expectations for this particular format."

4. **Focus on Subjectivity**: Frame it as a learning opportunity.  
   > "Essay grading can be inherently subjective, and I'm curious to better understand the criteria used to arrive at my score. If you have any feedback on content or areas for improvement, I would greatly appreciate the chance to review them."

5. **Logistics**: Be flexible with scheduling.  
   > "I know your office hours are in the morning, so I'm happy to meet at a time that works for you, or if you prefer, I'm open to a brief virtual meeting."

6. **Close Politely**:  
   > "Thank you for your time and guidance. I value your course and am committed to doing my best moving forward."

**Key things to remember**:
- Avoid words like "unfairly."
- Don't accuse or challenge his judgment.
- Keep the tone humble and curious.
- Emphasize your desire to grow as a student.

This approach keeps the door open for a constructive conversation and shows maturity. Good luck!
