Since your camper van runs on diesel, you don’t have spark plugs—diesel engines use glow plugs instead to help ignite the fuel. But checking your brakes and overall condition is super important, especially for an older vehicle and a family trip.

Here’s what to do:

**Brakes:**
- Listen for squeaking, grinding, or screeching when you brake—those are signs of worn pads.
- Check brake fluid level in the reservoir (should be between min/max lines).
- Look for brake fluid leaks under the van.
- Have a mechanic inspect the brake pads and rotors if you're unsure.

**Glow Plugs (Diesel Specific):**
- If your van is slow to start in cold weather, or takes longer than usual to crank, it could be a glow plug issue.
- You can test them with a multimeter, but replacing them is best done by a mechanic unless you're experienced.

**Other Key Checks:**
- Oil level and condition—change it if it’s dirty or low.
- Coolant and radiator hoses—look for cracks or leaks.
- Tires—check tread depth and air pressure; uneven wear could mean alignment issues.
- Battery—test it, especially if it’s older than 4–5 years.

If you're not comfortable doing these yourself, take it to a diesel specialist or trusted mechanic for a full pre-trip inspection. It’s worth the peace of mind.
