Great questions! Let’s break it down:

**Color-coding and mental load:**  
You're spot on—color-coding isn’t just about memory; it’s about reducing cognitive load. When your brain sees a consistent color for a specific project or task, it can quickly categorize and process that information without needing to "think" about what it is. It’s like a visual shortcut. So yes, it helps with both memory *and* task switching by making it easier to mentally separate different types of work. But as you said, subtlety is key—stick to 2–3 colors max, and use them consistently.

**Switching between lo-fi and ambient sounds:**  
There’s actually some science behind this! Switching audio environments can help reset your brain’s attention, especially if you’re doing different types of work. For example:  
- **Lo-fi or chill beats** work well for focused, repetitive tasks (like coding or writing).  
- **Ambient or nature sounds** (like rain or forest sounds) can help during creative or brainstorming phases.  

But timing matters. If you switch *too* often, it can disrupt flow. A good rule of thumb:  
- Stick with one type of sound for 45–60 minutes, then switch if you feel your focus slipping.  
- Or, use a timer to switch *intentionally*—like “I’ll do 50 minutes of deep work with lo-fi, then switch to ambient for a lighter task.”  

It’s a mix of personal preference and rhythm. Some people thrive on consistency; others need variety to stay engaged.

**Physical documents:**  
Tackling them on a quiet day is a solid plan. Bonus tip: set a timer for 25–30 minutes and just dive in without distractions. You’ll likely get more done than if you spread it out over days. And once you’re done, take a moment to celebrate—clearing physical clutter can be surprisingly satisfying!

Bottom line: small, intentional changes—like color-coding, sound switching, and focused document time—can add up to a much smoother, less stressful home office experience.
