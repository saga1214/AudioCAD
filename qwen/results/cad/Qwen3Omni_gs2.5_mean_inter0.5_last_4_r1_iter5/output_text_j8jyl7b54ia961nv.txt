Yes, absolutely. If we increase the weight of public resistance, the vaccination campaign becomes much riskier to run nationwide.

Even though it's highly effective and cost-efficient, if public resistance is very high, the campaign could backfire. It might deepen mistrust in health authorities, lead to legal challenges, or even reduce trust in other health services. In countries with strong anti-vaccine movements, such a campaign could face serious social and political pushback.

On the other hand, reducing sugar faces less resistance. While people enjoy sweet foods, most people accept that too much sugar is unhealthy. Campaigns here can often be framed as empowering personal choice rather than restricting it—like promoting water over sugary drinks or reading nutrition labels.

So, if public resistance is a top priority to avoid, the sugar campaign becomes more favorable. It's still challenging, but the resistance tends to be lower and more manageable. In contrast, a vaccination campaign, while powerful, can become a flashpoint for broader societal debates.

Therefore, if minimizing public resistance is key, I would lean toward reducing sugar consumption. But if the nation is facing or preparing for a vaccine-preventable disease outbreak, then the risks of resistance might be worth taking.
