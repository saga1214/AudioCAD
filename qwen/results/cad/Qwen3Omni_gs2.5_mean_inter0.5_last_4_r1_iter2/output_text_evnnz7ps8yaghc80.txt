Absolutely. Here’s a realistic and encouraging weekly juggling practice schedule that builds gradually and includes both your beanbags and scarves. It’s designed to reduce stress, reinforce rhythm, and help you transition toward three-ball juggling.

**Weekly Juggling Practice Schedule**

**Monday:**
- **Time:** 10–15 minutes
- **Focus:** Two-handed juggling with beanbags
- **Goal:** Review the three-ball rhythm by practicing the first three throws slowly. Don’t worry about catching—just get the sequence right.
- **Bonus:** Listen to a song with a steady beat (e.g., “Clocks” by Coldplay or “Take On Me” by A-ha) to internalize the rhythm.

**Tuesday:**
- **Time:** 10 minutes
- **Focus:** One-handed two-ball practice (dominant hand)
- **Goal:** Perfect your timing until it feels automatic. Time your throws to a metronome app (set to 120 BPM) if you want extra structure.

**Wednesday:**
- **Time:** 15 minutes
- **Focus:** Scarf juggling
- **Goal:** Practice the full three-throw sequence with scarves. Since they fall slowly, use this time to focus on hand positioning and rhythm, not catching speed.

**Thursday:**
- **Time:** 10 minutes
- **Focus:** Back to two-beanbag juggling
- **Goal:** Increase speed slightly. Try juggling for 30 seconds straight, then rest. Repeat 3–4 times.

**Friday:**
- **Time:** 15 minutes
- **Focus:** Transition attempt + scarf review
- **Goal:** Try adding the third ball from scarf practice. If you drop it, that’s okay—just reset and try again. Use the scarf rhythm to guide your timing.

**Saturday:**
- **Time:** 10 minutes
- **Focus:** Fun practice
- **Goal:** Juggle with music, or try juggling with a different object (e.g., soft balls or even oranges if you're feeling adventurous!).

**Sunday:**
- **Time:** 5–10 minutes
- **Focus:** Reflect and celebrate
- **Goal:** Note what felt easier this week. Even one successful three-throw is progress!

---

**Tips for Success:**
- **Keep it short and sweet:** 10–15 minutes daily is better than one long, stressful session.
- **Celebrate small wins:** Every time you get the sequence right—even once—is a victory.
- **Reduce stress intentionally:** Before each session, take three deep breaths. Stress really *does* get in the way, so consciously calming yourself helps your brain learn.

You've already come so far—mastering one-handed juggling is a huge step toward three! Keep going—you’ve got this, Mom. And soon enough, teaching your son will be not just possible, but fun.
