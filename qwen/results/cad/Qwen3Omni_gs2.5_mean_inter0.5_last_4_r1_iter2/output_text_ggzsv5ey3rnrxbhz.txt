You're asking the right questions, and I want to be honest and reassuring.

First, yes, PFAS are called "forever chemicals" because they don't break down easily in the environment or in your body. But that doesn't mean you're helpless.

There's no proven way to "detox" or flush them out with juice cleanses or fasting. Your body naturally gets rid of some over time, but it can take years. The best approach is to reduce your exposure going forward.

So here are the five top products or items you should avoid to minimize getting these chemicals into your body:

1. **Non-stick cookware** – Like Teflon pans. Switch to stainless steel, cast iron, or ceramic-coated alternatives.

2. **Grease-resistant food packaging** – Avoid fast-food wrappers, microwave popcorn bags, and pizza boxes. Use paper bags or containers instead.

3. **Stain- or waterproof products** – Carpets, furniture, outdoor clothing, and even some shoes may contain PFAS. Look for PFAS-free labels.

4. **Certain cosmetics and dental floss** – Some brands now label products as PFAS-free. Avoid products with "fluoro" in the name.

5. **Tap water without filtration** – Use a certified water filter (look for ones certified to remove PFAS) to reduce exposure.

And yes, microplastics are a separate but related concern – avoid plastic bottles, single-use plastics, and microbeads in exfoliants.

You can't eliminate all exposure, but by avoiding these five key sources, you can significantly reduce it. And remember, your worry shows you care – that's a good thing. Focus on what you *can* control, and don't let fear overwhelm you.
