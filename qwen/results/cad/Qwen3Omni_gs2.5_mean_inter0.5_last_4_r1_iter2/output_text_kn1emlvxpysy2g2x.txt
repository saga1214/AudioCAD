Here is a comparative table of all the beverages we’ve discussed, including high-end Pinot Noir, Japanese Hon-Mirin, Chianti, Pinot Grigio, and Champagne, with their ideal storage and serving temperatures in both Fahrenheit and Celsius.

| Beverage               | Storage Temp (°F / °C) | Serving Temp (°F / °C) | Best for Hot Weather | Best for Cold Weather |
|------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| **High-End Pinot Noir** | 55°F / 13°C            | 55–60°F / 13–16°C      | ✅ Yes – delicate, light-bodied, refreshing | ❌ Not ideal – too light for cold weather |
| **Chianti**             | 55°F / 13°C            | 60–65°F / 16–18°C      | ✅ Yes – medium-bodied, food-friendly | ❌ Not ideal – better at room temp |
| **Pinot Grigio**        | 50–55°F / 10–13°C      | 45–50°F / 7–10°C       | ✅✅ Excellent – crisp, high acidity | ❌ Too cold for cold weather |
| **Champagne**           | 45–50°F / 7–10°C       | 45–50°F / 7–10°C       | ✅✅ Excellent – effervescent, refreshing | ❌ Too cold – can be too sharp |
| **Japanese Hon-Mirin**  | 60–68°F / 16–20°C      | Room temp (68–75°F / 20–24°C) | ✅ Yes – if used in summer dishes | ❌ Not ideal – too sweet |

**Best for Hot Weather:**

- **Pinot Grigio** and **Champagne** are top choices. Their crispness and acidity cut through heat beautifully.
- **Pinot Noir** and **Chianti** are also excellent, especially in lighter styles—great for warm evenings.

**Best for Cold Weather:**

- **Pinot Noir** and **Chianti** hold up well in cooler settings, especially when paired with richer dishes.
- **Hon-Mirin mulled wine** is a classic winter warmer, though it’s not typically served as a standalone wine.

So, for your outdoor party in 33°C, go with **Pinot Grigio** or **Champagne**—both will shine. Just keep them well-chilled and enjoy the warmth with a cool glass.
