Since pacing both a cool, hilly night segment and helping him through the final 12 miles (which often means pushing hard in low-light conditions), your needs overlap but have slight differences based on pacing intensity and likely fatigue. Here’s your tailored packing list broken into two parts:

---

### **Pacing the First 20 Miles (Cool, Mid 30°F, Hilly, Night, 3 Aid Stations)**

You’ll have time to adjust layers between aid stations and may walk or jog steadily. Prioritize warmth, visibility, and comfort.

**Clothing:**
- Moisture-wicking base layer (long-sleeve top + leggings or capris)
- Mid-layer (fleece or lightweight insulated jacket)
- Wind-resistant outer shell (lightweight trail jacket or softshell)
- Warm hat (beanie or balaclava)
- Gloves (lightweight, breathable, but warm)
- Buff or neck gaiter (for face/neck warmth)
- Trail running socks (moisture-wicking, comfortable)
- Trail running shoes (grippy, broken in)

**Gear:**
- Headlamp (bright, adjustable beam) + **2x spare batteries** or a backup light
- Small hydration pack or vest (to carry layers if needed)
- Optional: Lightweight pack or waist belt to carry layers
- Optional: Reflective vest or armbands (for visibility at night)

> 💡 Tip: Bring a thin insulated jacket or extra layer to stash at an aid station if you warm up.

---

### **Pacing the Final 12 Miles (Likely more intense, fatigued husband, night running)**

You’ll be pushing hard alongside him. Expect higher exertion, so you’ll generate more heat but still face cold temps. Visibility and reliability are key.

**Clothing:**
- Moisture-wicking base layer (lightweight, breathable)
- Light mid-layer (if needed, but you may stay warm from effort)
- Windproof shell (still important for wind chill)
- Warm hat + gloves (your extremities will freeze fast)
- Buff or neck gaiter
- Trail socks + shoes (same as above)

**Gear:**
- Headlamp (must be reliable—test it!) + **extra batteries**
- Small hydration pack or vest (to carry layers or essentials)
- Optional: Hand warmers (tuck in gloves or pockets)
- Optional: Lightweight reflective vest or armbands

> 💡 Tip: You may not need full insulation here—pace hard and stay warm. But be ready to add layers if you slow down or stop.

---

### **Items to Bring for Both Segments**
- Headlamp + spare batteries (non-negotiable)
- Gloves and warm hat
- Buff/neck gaiter
- Trail shoes and socks
- Small pack or vest to carry layers

---

You don’t need nutrition, so focus on comfort, warmth, visibility, and gear reliability. Good luck—you’ve got this!
