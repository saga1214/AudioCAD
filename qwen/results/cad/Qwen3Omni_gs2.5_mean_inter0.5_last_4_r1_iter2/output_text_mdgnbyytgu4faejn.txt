Absolutely. Let's walk through a clean, traditional, and ATS-friendly (Applicant Tracking System friendly) format that will work well for most industries. This format is proven to be effective and easy to scan—both by humans and automated systems.

---

### ✅ **Recommended Resume Format (Top to Bottom)**

#### 1. **Header (Top of Page)**
- **Your Name**: 14–16 pt font, bold, centered or left-aligned.
- On the right (or below), in **9–10 pt font**, list your contact info using plain text or **simple icons if you must**, but consider removing for ATS safety:
  - Email  
  - Phone  
  - City, State  
  _(Optional: LinkedIn URL in plain text, underlined or not)_

> ✅ **Order Note**: Yes, right of your name is fine, but below is **more traditional and cleaner**. Below is slightly better for readability and ATS compatibility.

---

#### 2. **Professional Summary (3–4 lines)**
- A short paragraph (3–4 lines) summarizing your background, key skills, and career goals.
- Tailor this to the job you're applying for.
- Example:
  > Results-driven professional with 10+ years of experience in customer service and team leadership. Proven ability to improve client satisfaction and streamline operations. Seeking to transition into project coordination roles leveraging organizational and communication skills.

---

#### 3. **Skills Section**
- List 6–10 relevant hard and soft skills.
- Use bullet points or a clean list.
- Include keywords from job descriptions you're targeting (e.g., Microsoft Office, Project Management, Communication, etc.).

> ✅ **Placement**: After Summary, before Experience.

---

#### 4. **Relevant Experience**
- List jobs in reverse chronological order.
- For each:
  - Job Title, Company, Location, Dates (e.g., Jan 2020 – Present)
  - Use bullet points to describe responsibilities and achievements (use action verbs and quantify results when possible).
  - Focus on **transferable skills** and **achievements**, not just duties.

> ✅ **Tip for Career Change**: Only include jobs that are relevant to the new role. If you have unrelated jobs, consider the "Additional Work History" approach below.

---

#### 5. **Additional Work History (Optional)**
- If you have recent jobs unrelated to your target role:
  - List them briefly: Job Title, Company, Dates.
  - No bullet points or descriptions.
  - Keep it concise—just enough to show you've been employed.

---

#### 6. **Education**
- Degree, Major, Institution, Graduation Year
- Include honors or awards here if relevant.
- If you have multiple degrees from one school, list the school once, then list each degree under it.

> ✅ Include degrees in progress with "Expected [Month, Year]".

---

#### 7. **Optional Sections (Only if Relevant)**
- Certifications
- Projects
- Volunteer Work
- Languages
- Publications

> Only include if they add value to your application.

---

### ✅ Final Tips:
- **Length**: 1 page is ideal unless you have 10+ years of experience.
- **Font**: Use a clean, professional font like Calibri, Arial, or Helvetica. 10–11 pt for body text.
- **Margins**: 1 inch all around.
- **File Type**: Save as a PDF unless otherwise requested.
