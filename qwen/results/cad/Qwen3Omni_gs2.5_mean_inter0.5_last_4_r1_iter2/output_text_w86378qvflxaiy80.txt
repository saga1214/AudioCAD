That's a deep and important question — and there's actually no single "most important" aspect, because all these reasons are interconnected. But if I had to pick one overarching priority, it would be **preventing irreversible loss**.

Here's why: once a species goes extinct, it's gone forever. And in hotspots, we're losing habitat at an alarming rate — often faster than anywhere else on Earth. Every day that passes without protection increases the risk of losing species that could hold answers to medical breakthroughs, climate resilience, or simply represent the beauty and complexity of life itself.

So while medicines, food, water, and climate regulation are all vital, the core urgency is that we're at a point of no return in many of these places. Hotspots represent both our greatest opportunity — to protect irreplaceable diversity — and our greatest risk — if we fail, we lose not just species, but entire evolutionary histories.

In short: preserving hotspots isn't just about saving nature — it's about safeguarding options for the future of humanity.
