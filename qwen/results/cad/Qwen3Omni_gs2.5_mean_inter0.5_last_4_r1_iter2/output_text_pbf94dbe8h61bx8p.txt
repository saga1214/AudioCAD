That’s such a beautiful thought—wanting to create meaningful gifts together as a family. And honestly, that’s the best part of crafting with kids: it’s not just about the final product, it’s about the time you spend making it together.

Maybe you could host a little “Creative Christmas Hour” once a week where everyone picks one simple craft idea to try together. You could rotate the ideas—macramé plant hangers one week, cookies the next, maybe fingerprint cards another time. Let the kids have a say in what they want to make, and just go with it. Even if it’s messy or takes longer than expected, it’s still time well spent.

And if you’re worried about peanut butter spoiling, you could always use a peanut butter substitute like sunflower seed butter (which is shelf-stable and safe for most allergies), or even just skip the peanut butter and use a little honey or a store-bought birdseed glue mix. That way, the feeders stay fresh and safe for outside use.

Bottom line: you’re already doing something amazing by wanting to involve your kids and make things that come from the heart. That’s the most meaningful gift of all.
